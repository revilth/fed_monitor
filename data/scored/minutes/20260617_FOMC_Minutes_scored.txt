GROWTH — PARTICIPANTS' VIEWS
──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Participants:
  "Participants generally observed that economic activity had continued to
   expand at a solid pace, despite elevated uncertainty, supported by strong
   business investment and resilient consumer spending." Business investment
   "remained concentrated in AI-related expenditures, which showed no signs
   of slowing." Some participants suggested these investments "would likely
   increase the growth of productivity and of potential output," though
   "considerable uncertainty remained regarding both the timing and
   magnitude" of the payoff.

  Compositional detail (NEW — the growth-section flag your methodology
  calls for): "stock market gains and federal income tax refunds sent
  earlier this year had provided support to consumer spending, particularly
  among higher-income households. They noted, however, that lower-income
  households were increasingly relying on credit to maintain spending and
  continued to face disproportionate pressures from elevated gasoline and
  grocery prices."

vs. June 17 press conference opening statement:
  Opening: "Economic activity is expanding at a solid pace despite elevated
  uncertainty that owes, in part, to the conflict in the Middle East.
  Productivity growth and capital investment are strong."
  → Aligned on solid pace / Middle East uncertainty / investment strength.
    The higher- vs. lower-income compositional split does NOT appear in the
    opening statement — richer detail than the committee's public framing.

vs. April 29 minutes:
  April: "Several participants noted energy price strains on lower-income
  households." A single, brief mention.
  June: explicit two-tier characterization — higher-income households
  supported by stock gains/tax refunds; lower-income households "increasingly
  relying on credit," facing "disproportionate pressures" from gas/groceries.
  → Escalation: the compositional concern is now more specific and more
    starkly two-sided than April's single-line mention.

vs. June 17 statement:
  Statement: "Economic activity is expanding at a solid pace despite
  elevated uncertainty... Productivity growth and capital investment are
  strong." No mention of the income-composition split.
  → The compositional concern did not make the public statement at all.

Direction: NEUTRAL-TO-HAWKISH — headline growth unambiguously solid; the
compositional concern is a watch-item, not yet a policy driver.

LABOR MARKET — PARTICIPANTS' VIEWS
──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Participants — hard data verdict:
  "Participants generally observed that the unemployment rate had remained
   relatively stable over the past year at a level near participants'
   estimates of its longer-run level. Participants generally remarked that
   payroll employment gains had strengthened this year and appeared roughly
   consistent with underlying labor force growth."
  → This is now a GENERAL characterization (not qualified to "most" as in
    April) — broader consensus on stabilization.

Participants — remaining caveats (minority):
  "Several participants noted, however, that declines in the job-finding
   rate and certain survey measures of job availability reflected a labor
   market with relatively low dynamism."
  "Many participants remarked that the labor market was not currently a
   source of inflationary pressures, or that nominal wage growth remained
   consistent with inflation moving toward 2 percent."

Near-term outlook — explicit self-correction:
  "Some participants remarked that their concerns earlier this year about
   labor market deterioration had eased with recent data, and several
   participants noted that the solid payroll employment data in recent
   months could signal increased labor market momentum."

vs. June 17 press conference opening statement:
  Opening: "Job gains have kept pace with the workforce, and the
  unemployment rate has changed little."
  → For the first time this cycle, the opening statement and the minutes
    AGREE on labor. In April, Powell's opening statement went further with
    "labor demand has clearly softened" — a phrase absent from the minutes'
    participants section. That divergence is now closed: the "kept pace
    with the workforce" reframe is fully consistent with the minutes.

vs. April 29 minutes — the key change:
  April risk balance: "Most participants judged... that risks to the
  employment side of the Committee's dual mandate were tilted to the
  downside."
  June (Monetary Policy section): "downside risks to achieving maximum
  employment had moderated a bit."
  → Real de-escalation of the labor-side risk case for cutting. The June
    minutes explicitly self-reference the shift ("concerns... had eased
    with recent data").
  April fragility language ("a few participants pointed to the possibility
  that the low rates of job growth were a sign of labor market fragility")
  has no equivalent in June — the minority-fragility framing has receded.

vs. June 17 statement:
  Statement: "Job gains have kept pace with the workforce, and the
  unemployment rate has changed little."
  → Well aligned with the minutes; no divergence to flag.

Direction: HAWKISH SHIFT — the labor-market insurance-cut rationale that
was live in April is fading.

INFLATION — PARTICIPANTS' VIEWS
──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Participants:
  "Participants generally noted that inflation had increased further and
   remained well above the Committee's 2 percent longer-run objective."
  Attribution: "lingering effects of tariffs, supply chain disruptions
   related to the closure of the Strait of Hormuz, and strength in demand
   for some goods and services stemming from robust AI-related investment."
  Breadth (NEW): "Several participants commented that price pressures had
   become more broad based, with a large share of goods and services—
   including transportation, airfares, petrochemical products, and
   agricultural inputs—experiencing substantial increases."
  "Several participants remarked that services price inflation excluding
   housing had declined little and remained high."

  Expectations:
  "The majority of participants commented that most measures of medium- and
   longer-term inflation expectations remained at levels consistent with
   the Committee's 2 percent objective." But: "The majority of participants
   highlighted the possibility that, after several years of inflation above
   2 percent, continued elevated inflation rates could begin to affect
   inflation expectations and wage- and price-setting decisions."

  New demand-side channel (NOT present in April): "Most participants
   remarked that growth in economic activity that exceeded that of
   potential output, owing in part to strong AI business investment, could
   contribute to more persistent inflationary pressures."

  Partial offset acknowledged: "Some participants remarked that
   productivity gains associated with AI adoption would eventually reduce
   production costs and increase aggregate supply... though they noted this
   effect would likely take time to materialize." "Several participants
   noted that the deceleration in housing services prices was likely to
   continue to be a source of disinflationary pressure."

vs. June 17 press conference opening statement:
  Opening: "Inflation remains elevated relative to the Committee's 2
  percent goal, in part reflecting supply shocks that have driven price
  increases in certain sectors, including energy. The Committee will
  deliver price stability." (Plus extended Q&A: "running well ahead...for
  more than five years"; "the recent past need not be prolog.")
  → The statement's terse "supply shocks... including energy" is a fraction
    of what's in the minutes. Broad-based sectoral pressure, the AI-demand
    channel, and the expectations-fragility risk (now "majority," up from
    April's "several") are all absent from the public opening statement.

vs. April 29 minutes — escalation on three axes:
  Intensity: April "inflation had moved up" → June "increased further and
  remained well above." (April minutes: "Participants observed that
  overall inflation had moved up.")
  Breadth: April was a narrow tariff/core-goods story (with a software-
  price look-through nuance from "a few" participants) → June is explicitly
  multi-sector ("transportation, airfares, petrochemical products,
  agricultural inputs").
  Mechanism: April was supply-only (tariffs) → June adds a genuine
  demand-side channel (growth exceeding potential via AI investment) not
  present at all in April.
  Expectations fragility: April "several participants highlighted the
  possibility" → June "the majority of participants highlighted" the same
  risk — "several" to "majority" is a clear escalation in how many
  officials worry about de-anchoring.

vs. June 17 statement:
  Statement mentions only "supply shocks... including energy." The
  demand-side channel (AI/above-potential growth) and the broad-based
  sectoral characterization are entirely omitted from the public document.
  → Widest comparison-3 gap of the three background sections.

Direction: HAWKISH ESCALATION — the largest directional move of the three
background topics.

──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
MONETARY POLICY — PARTICIPANTS' VIEWS
──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
On the hold decision — HIGH SIGNAL, present-tense hike case:
  "A few participants commented that, in light of these developments,
   there was a case for raising the target range for the federal funds
   rate, but those participants indicated that they supported maintaining
   the current target range at this meeting."
  → This is materially different from April's framing, which was purely a
    conditional/future reaction function ("a majority of participants
    highlighted that some policy firming would likely become appropriate
    IF inflation were to continue to run persistently above 2 percent").
    June documents an actual case for hiking NOW, declined only on
    process/timing grounds, not substance.

On restrictiveness — the neutral-rate hedge phrase is GONE:
  "Several participants remarked that they did not see the current policy
   stance as restrictive, while a few other participants commented that
   they saw the current policy stance as slightly restrictive."
  → CRITICAL: the vague hedge used in both March and April minutes —
    "participants generally judged that the current policy rate was
    within the range of plausible estimates of its neutral level" — does
    NOT appear anywhere in the June minutes (confirmed by full-text search).
    It has been replaced by an explicit, resolved split: the larger group
    ("several") says policy is NOT restrictive at all; the smaller group
    ("a few") says only "slightly restrictive." Per the CLAUDE.md
    neutral-rate precision rule: this doesn't just fail to resolve the
    ambiguity — it resolves it in the hawkish direction. No participant is
    quoted characterizing policy as meaningfully above neutral.

Named scenario framing — committee-wide, matching the language-cycle
methodology (dispersed → committee-wide, one stage short of the statement):
  "Most participants remarked on scenarios in which inflationary pressures
   would dissipate and inflation would soon begin to return to 2 percent.
   In such scenarios, almost all of these participants noted that it would
   likely be appropriate to maintain or eventually lower the target range."
  "Most participants, however, also pointed to scenarios in which... in the
   context of stable labor market conditions, inflation would remain
   elevated due to strong AI-related demand, the conflict in the Middle
   East, or the effects of tariffs. In such scenarios, almost all of these
   participants indicated that some policy firming would likely be
   warranted."
  → This is the Waller-style (Apr 17, "BENIGN/ADVERSE") explicit named
    two-scenario device, now appearing COMMITTEE-WIDE in the minutes for
    the first time. Bottom-up language cycle: dispersed (Waller, Apr 17) →
    committee-wide (minutes, June). Watch for it entering the statement.

Individual dot positioning — ties directly to the scored SEP:
  "Many participants indicated that the appropriate level of the federal
   funds rate would be within or slightly below the current target range
   at the end of this year. Many other participants, however, assessed
   that the appropriate level of the federal funds rate would be above
   the current target range at the end of this year."
  → Matches the SEP's quantified split: 9 of 18 participants pencil at
    least one 2026 hike (0 of 19 in March); median dot 3.4%→3.8%; the
    entire dovish tail (five sub-3.625% dots in March) is gone. Warsh's
    own Q&A quantified this verbally: "half of the members of the FOMC
    expect rate hikes as soon as this year."

Statement redesign rationale — confirms the April minutes' prediction:
  "A number of participants noted that it was an opportune time to
   consider significant changes to the FOMC's postmeeting statement. A
   majority of participants remarked that they saw advantages in
   shortening the statement. Most participants emphasized that they
   preferred not to repeat the language in the previous postmeeting
   statement that had suggested an easing bias."
  → Validates the April 29 minutes' scored prediction that easing-bias
    removal was "a single meeting away."

New institutional item:
  "The Chairman described plans to establish five independent task forces
   to examine issues related to the broad conduct of monetary policy."
  → Matches what Warsh's June 17 Q&A already flagged (communications,
    balance sheet, data sources, productivity/jobs, causes of inflation);
    now confirmed as discussed at the meeting itself, not just announced
    after.

vs. June 17 press conference opening statement:
  The opening statement is neutral in its forward language (no explicit
  tightening bias stated) even though the minutes reveal an actual,
  present-tense hike case was made and declined, and the restrictiveness
  view now tilts toward "not restrictive." The Q&A (Warsh, unscripted)
  went further than the opening statement — quantifying the hike risk at
  "half" the committee — but even the Q&A did not disclose the explicit
  "case for raising... at this meeting" language now visible in the
  minutes. The minutes are the most hawkish of the three documents.

vs. April 29 minutes — net-hawkish across the board:
  A. HIKE CASE: conditional/future (April) → present-tense, actually made
     and declined (June). Clear escalation.
  B. RESTRICTIVENESS: vague "within range of plausible neutral estimates"
     (April, unchanged from March) → explicit "not restrictive" (several)
     vs. "slightly restrictive" (a few) split (June). The hedge is gone.
  C. NAMED SCENARIOS: absent in April → committee-wide benign/adverse
     framing in June. New device.
  D. CUT CASE: April had an explicit near-term cut case — "several
     participants highlighted that it would likely be appropriate to
     lower the target range... once there are clear indications that
     disinflation is firmly back on track." June has NO equivalent
     language anywhere in the monetary policy discussion — the dovish
     minority case that existed one meeting ago has dropped out entirely.

vs. June 17 statement:
  By far the widest comparison-3 gap of the four topics. None of the
  following appear in the 130-word public statement: the hike-case
  discussion, the restrictiveness split, the named scenario framework, the
  individual dot positioning, or the five task forces.

Direction: HAWKISH — the strongest signal of the four topics, and the
most consequential finding in this release.

──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
SUMMARY
──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Net signal:    HAWKISH vs. April 29 minutes; HAWKISH vs. the June 17 opening
               statement; HAWKISH vs. the June 17 statement. Consistent
               across all three comparisons, with monetary policy carrying
               the strongest signal.

The most analytically significant findings:

  1. "A few participants commented that... there was a case for raising
     the target range... at this meeting" — the first present-tense hike
     case documented in minutes this cycle (previously only a conditional
     future reaction function).
  2. The neutral-rate hedge phrase ("within the range of plausible
     estimates of its neutral level") — present verbatim in both March
     and April minutes — is completely ABSENT from June, replaced by an
     explicit split that leans "not restrictive."
  3. The explicit April cut case ("several participants... appropriate to
     lower... once disinflation is firmly back on track") has NO
     equivalent in June — the dovish minority framing has disappeared
     from the discussion, not merely weakened.
  4. A committee-wide named benign/adverse scenario framework appears for
     the first time — the Waller-style device has moved from dispersed to
     committee-wide, one stage short of the statement itself.
  5. The labor-market risk balance eased ("downside risks... moderated a
     bit," down from April's "most... tilted to the downside") even as
     the inflation and monetary-policy sections hardened — labor is no
     longer providing a dovish counterweight.

vs. press conference opening statement:
  The minutes are more hawkish than either the opening statement or even
  Warsh's unscripted Q&A. The opening statement carries neutral forward
  language; the Q&A quantified the hike risk ("half the committee") but
  did not disclose that an actual hike case was made and declined at the
  meeting, or that the "not restrictive" framing had become the larger
  camp. The minutes are the richest and most hawkish of the three June 17
  documents.

On the neutral rate (precision note):
  Unlike March and April, the June minutes do NOT use the vague "within
  the range of plausible estimates" hedge. The explicit split — "several:
  not restrictive" vs. "a few: slightly restrictive" — resolves in the
  hawkish direction: nobody is quoted describing policy as meaningfully
  above neutral, and the "not restrictive" camp is described as larger
  than the "slightly restrictive" camp.

Language watch:
  • "Case for raising the target range" — first present-tense hike
    advocacy in minutes this cycle. Watch for this framing to appear in a
    Just Voter speech before the July 28–29 meeting.
  • Named benign/adverse scenario framing — now committee-wide in the
    minutes (Waller's Apr 17 device generalized). Watch for it entering
    the next statement — that would complete the bottom-up cycle.
  • Disappearance of "within the range of plausible estimates of its
    neutral level" — the vague neutral hedge is gone. If it does not
    reappear in the July minutes, treat its absence as durable, not a
    one-meeting fluke.
  • Disappearance of the April "firmly back on track" cut threshold
    language — no successor phrase for a near-term cut case appeared in
    June at all.

Analyst flag: YES
  → Is the "case for raising" minority (currently "a few") on track to
    become the median view by July 28–29, given the SEP already shows 9
    of 18 penciling a 2026 hike and the median dot (3.8%) sits above the
    current 3.625% midpoint?
  → The explicit disappearance of the near-term dovish cut case (present
    in April, absent in June) — is this driven specifically by the
    Middle East/AI inflation channel (potentially transitory pending a
    Strait of Hormuz resolution, which the staff review notes had already
    been reached by the end of the intermeeting period), or does it
    reflect a more durable hawkish consolidation under Warsh's first
    meeting? This distinction matters for how quickly the committee could
    pivot back toward cuts if energy prices retrace.
