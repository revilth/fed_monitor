  2. "MPR is blind to June data / May cutoff vs. weak June NFP" — FALSE. The MPR

  3. "117,000 jobs/month vs. 10,000 in 2025" — these figures are NOT in the MPR.

     difficult to measure the stock of money… 12-month rates of increase in M2

  5. "Prior MPR = February 2026 (Powell)." No Feb 2026 MPR exists. The genuine
     prior is June 2025 (Powell) — a much larger and legitimate diff (see below).
  6. MISSED: the five independent task forces — the actual signature Warsh

──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

GROWTH
──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Actual language:
  "real gross domestic product (GDP) grew at a moderate pace in the first quarter,
   with capital investment rising considerably but household consumption increasing
   only very modestly… Overall, economic activity is expanding at a solid pace
   despite elevated uncertainty that owes, in part, to the conflict in the Middle
   East." Q1 real GDP +2.1% (annual rate), "similar to last year's pace."
  Household consumption +1.3% annualized over first five months (modest).
  Manufacturing output "moved up strongly," partly AI/data-center demand.
  "Labor productivity growth is strong."

FOMC statement echoed (June 17, verbatim):
  "Economic activity is expanding at a solid pace despite elevated uncertainty that
   owes, in part, to the conflict in the Middle East. Productivity growth and
   capital investment are strong." → reproduced in the MPR near-verbatim.

Compositional note the report flags: growth is carried by capital investment (AI/
high-tech, data-center buildout) while household consumption is "only very modestly"
higher (1.3% annualized). Narrow, investment-led composition — a demand-breadth
caveat, not a headline-growth concern.

vs. June 17 statement:  CONSISTENT — verbatim "solid pace" language.
vs. June 2025 MPR:      Firmer. June 2025 said GDP growth "paused in the first
  quarter" with only "modest" consumer spending and declining sentiment. July 2026
  reads as steadier ("solid pace," productivity "strong"), though the consumption-
  vs-investment split persists.
Direction: NEUTRAL — stable vs. June 17 consensus.

──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
LABOR MARKET
──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Actual language:
  "the labor market has stabilized, with demand and supply roughly in balance. The
   unemployment rate, at 4.2 percent in June, was low and has been little changed
   since last summer. Layoffs have been subdued and job vacancies have been roughly
   flat, on balance, this year, though private payroll gains have picked up." Labor-
   supply growth slowed (immigration slowdown + aging/LFPR decline); "solid nominal
   wage growth… accompanied by strong growth in labor productivity."
  Figure note confirms labor data "extend through June 2026."

FOMC statement echoed (June 17, verbatim):
  "Job gains have kept pace with the workforce, and the unemployment rate has
   changed little." → reproduced in the MPR's Monetary Policy section verbatim.

Key correction: the report is NOT blind to June. It cites June UR 4.2% and payrolls
"picked up." The framing is a stabilization read (through June), consistent with the
June FOMC minutes ("concerns earlier this year about labor market deterioration had
eased with recent data"). No dovish-labor-risk floor.

vs. June 17 statement:  CONSISTENT — verbatim.
vs. June 2025 MPR:      Softer characterization of the level of tightness. June 2025:
  "at or near maximum employment." July 2026: "roughly in balance" / "stabilized…
  following a period of cooling." The 2025→2026 arc is cooling-then-stabilization,
  not further tightening.
Direction: NEUTRAL — stabilization framing removes the dovish labor floor present in
  the spring; no fresh hawkish push. (The media version's "NEUTRAL-TO-HAWKISH" rested
  on a stale-data tension that the actual text refutes.)

──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
INFLATION
──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Actual numbers (12 months ending May 2026):
  Headline PCE +4.1% (up from 2.5% a year earlier).
  Core PCE     +3.4% (up from 2.8% a year earlier).
  PCE energy   +24%.  Food +2.4% (up from 1.8%).
  Dallas Fed TRIMMED-MEAN PCE: 2.4% this May, DOWN from 2.6% last May.

Drivers (three channels, verbatim register): "earlier tariff hikes that pushed up
domestic prices of some imported goods, a surge in energy prices associated with
constraints on oil supplies following the start of the Middle East conflict in late
February, and increased demand for some high-tech products that support artificial
intelligence (AI) applications."

Expectations: short-term measures "moved higher" after the energy jump; long-term
measures "remained within the range… before the pandemic… broadly consistent with
the FOMC's… 2 percent" objective. Anchoring intact.

FOMC statement echoed (June 17, verbatim):
  "Inflation remains elevated relative to the Committee's 2 percent goal, in part
   reflecting supply shocks that have driven price increases in certain sectors,
   including energy." → reproduced verbatim.

Trimmed-mean offset (real, and quantified): the MPR explicitly flags that trimmed-
mean PCE DECLINED (2.6%→2.4%) even as headline/core rose — i.e., the Committee
distinguishes shock-driven headline inflation from underlying trend. Footnote 2 adds
a caveat that trimmed-mean "can provide misleading signals when the distribution of
price changes shifts." This is a genuine analytical hedge, not boilerplate — the
partial dovish anchor if the supply shocks retrace.

vs. June 17 statement:  CONSISTENT on the shock narrative; the trimmed-mean detail
  and long-run-expectations-anchored language are additions beyond the terse
  statement (context, not a directional change).
vs. June 2025 MPR:      Materially higher inflation regime. June 2025: PCE 2.1% /
  core 2.5%, "continued to moderate." July 2026: PCE 4.1% / core 3.4%, "stepped up
  further this spring." The firmer inflation posture reflects the data reversal, not
  only a committee lean.
Direction: NEUTRAL — reaffirms the June 17 structural-shock narrative; anchored
  long-run expectations + declining trimmed-mean prevent a hawkish read despite the
  4.1% headline.

──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
MONETARY POLICY
──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Interest-rate policy (verbatim, reproducing June 17 statement language):
  "the FOMC has maintained the target range for the federal funds rate at 3½ to 3¾
   percent since the beginning of the year… economic activity is expanding at a solid
   pace… productivity growth and capital investment are strong; job gains have kept
   pace with the workforce; and the unemployment rate has changed little. Inflation
   remains elevated relative to the Committee's 2 percent goal, in part reflecting
   supply shocks… The Committee will deliver price stability."

Cut/hike signal: NONE. No forward guidance, no easing bias — consistent with the
June 17 statement redesign that stripped forward guidance entirely. The MPR does NOT
break new policy ground; it formalizes the June 17 posture at the congressional-
document level.

Market rate path (report's own framing, high-signal): "the policy rate path moved
above the levels prevailing at the start of the year. Upward revisions… partly
reflected assessments that the Middle East conflict would lead to higher inflation
this year and increased confidence in the stability of the U.S. labor market."
Earlier in the year markets expected "the policy rate would fall further toward
estimates of its long-run neutral level"; that has reversed. The MPR reports the
hawkish repricing as an observation, not a Committee endorsement.

Balance sheet (a genuine pivot the media coverage buried): the Fed ENDED runoff
(Dec 2025, ample reserves reached) and is now BUYING shorter-term Treasuries —
"nearly $250 billion in Treasury bills" purchased since early January, balance sheet
back up to $6.7 trillion (reserves +$54bn to ~$3.1tn). This is the mirror image of
June 2025, which was still REDUCING holdings ("continued to reduce… slowed the pace…
in April"). Technical/plumbing, not a stance signal, but note the direction flip.

"The Committee will deliver price stability" — carried verbatim from the June 17
statement. Confirmed present. This is committee-consensus language being echoed into
the congressional record; per the Language Cycle it signals where consensus already
is (top-down), NOT a fresh Warsh personal signal.

vs. June 17 statement:  CONSISTENT — verbatim reproduction; no new directional
  content. FFR unchanged 3.50–3.75%.
vs. June 2025 MPR:      HAWKISH SHIFT IN TONE (legitimate, well-grounded):
    • June 2025: "The FOMC is strongly committed to supporting maximum employment
      and returning inflation to its 2 percent objective… the Committee will
      carefully assess incoming data, the evolving outlook, and the balance of
      risks" — symmetric dual-mandate, process/conditional language, "well
      positioned to wait for more clarity."
    • July 2026: "The Committee will deliver price stability." — terse,
      unconditional, price-stability-forward; no dual-mandate balancing sentence, no
      "returning inflation to 2 percent," no "well positioned to wait," no forward
      guidance.
  Caveat on level vs. tone: over the same span the FFR was CUT 75bp (4.25–4.50% →
  3.50–3.75%), so policy is EASIER in level terms even as the language hardened. And
  the inflation backdrop reversed (2.1%→4.1% PCE), so a firmer register partly
  reflects the data. The tone shift is real but should not be read as a tightening
  intention on top of an already-easier rate level.
Direction: HAWKISH-IN-TONE vs. June 2025 (unconditional price-stability commitment,
  no dual-mandate hedge); NEUTRAL vs. June 17 (verbatim echo, no new signal).

──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
STRUCTURAL / WARSH FEATURES (new this report)
──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
1. FIVE INDEPENDENT TASK FORCES (the signature Warsh feature; missed in the media
   writeup). "Over the remainder of the year, independent task forces led by the very
   best minds from inside and outside of the economics profession… will examine five
   areas… (1) communications, (2) balance sheet policy, (3) the quality of existing
   data sources, (4) productivity and jobs in an era of transformation, and (5)
   frameworks for analyzing the drivers of inflation. Each task force will start with
   first principles…"
   → Signals a structural review of Fed methodology under Warsh. Task force (1)
     communications "will explore… the Summary of Economic Projections, with the
     possibility of suggesting changes to current arrangements" — the SEP/dot-plot
     format itself is under review. Task force (5) frameworks for the drivers of
     inflation is the analytical home of the M2 material.

2. M2 / STOCK OF MONEY (real, but neutral — not the hawkish monetarist signal the
   media implied). "In the modern economy, it is difficult to measure the stock of
   money. One of a number of series traditionally used to provide an empirical
   approximation… is the M2 monetary aggregate. Over the first five months of the
   year, 12-month rates of increase in M2 were moderate and broadly similar to the
   pace typically observed during the 2010s." Hedged framing; moderate growth = no
   inflationary money signal. Language-cycle stage: Warsh methodological interest →
   MPR narrative box. NOT in the FOMC statement or minutes. Watch, don't over-weight.

──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
SUMMARY
──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Net signal: NEUTRAL vs. the most recent committee language (June 17); HAWKISH-IN-TONE
vs. the genuine prior MPR (June 2025).

The MPR breaks no new policy ground relative to June 17 — its entire interest-rate-
policy paragraph is a verbatim reproduction of the statement, and "The Committee will
deliver price stability" is carried over, not new. Its function is formalization: the
June 17 posture is now on the congressional record ahead of Warsh's debut Humphrey-
Hawkins testimony. Against the true prior (June 2025, Powell), the shift is real and
large — from a symmetric, conditional dual-mandate register to a terse, unconditional
price-stability commitment with no forward guidance — but it lands on a rate level
that is 75bp LOWER and against an inflation backdrop that roughly doubled, so it is a
tone/communication hardening, not a signal of intended additional tightening. The
only material analytical addition vs. June committee language is the explicit trimmed-
mean offset (2.6%→2.4%). The structural novelty is the five task forces (incl. an SEP-
communications review), not the money-supply box, which is neutral.

Language watch:
  • "The Committee will deliver price stability" — committee consensus now in the
    congressional record. Watch for Warsh to use verbatim in oral testimony (Jul 14–
    15) and whether other officials adopt it (top-down spread).
  • Five task forces, esp. (1) communications reviewing the SEP and (5) frameworks
    for inflation drivers — watch the July 28–29 statement/minutes and Warsh speeches
    for any procedural change (dot-plot format, inflation-framework language).
  • Trimmed-mean PCE (2.4%, declining) — watch whether Warsh deploys it as a dovish
    hedge under questioning, or distances from it (footnote 2 pre-loads the caveat).
  • Balance-sheet flip: Fed now BUYING T-bills ($250bn since January, $6.7tn) — a
    plumbing pivot from 2025 runoff; watch for questions on balance-sheet policy
    (task force 2).
