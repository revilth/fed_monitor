  ⚠ ATTRIBUTION DIVERGENCE: Barr retains supply-shock attribution language even
    though the September 16 statement DELETED the attribution clause:
    "Our economy has experienced a series of shocks over the past year and half:
     the imposition of tariffs, the conflict in the Middle East and continued
     disruptions from Russia's war on Ukraine, and, more recently, a surge in
     investment demand to support the artificial intelligence (AI) buildout.
     These shocks have contributed to upward price pressures."
    The committee REMOVED the attribution clause from the statement language at
    the September 16 meeting (the July 29 statement had "in part reflecting
    supply shocks ... including energy"). Barr is still running his own
    attribution framing independent of the updated committee language. This is
    the first post-meeting speech to exhibit this divergence. Compare to
    Williams's attribution position (CALIBRATION.md: Williams defends attribution;
    watch for his first post-meeting speech).

MONETARY POLICY

FOMC statement language echoed:

   rate to 3-3/4 to 4 percent." [Sep 16 stmt]
  → Barr: "The FOMC took important action to that end last week by increasing
    the policy rate, which I supported."
  → Endorses the September hike explicitly in the first person. "Important
    action" is Barr's evaluation, not statement language.

Own language — KEY:
  "We needed to recalibrate monetary policy to reflect the balance of risks to
   our mandate goals." — rationale for the hike.
  "In my view, given changes to the economy, we were out of position, and we
   made an adjustment in the right direction." — "out of position" is a stance
   admission; "right direction" is forward-looking (implicitly: more moves may
   be needed to correct the position).
  "In my base case, further policy adjustments are likely to be needed to
   ensure inflation comes down to target in a timely fashion." — KEY SIGNAL.
   "In my base case" qualifies the certainty; "likely" preserves optionality;
   but the directional claim is clear: more moves in the base case.
  "We want to support sustainable, durable growth in support of maximum
   employment, and price stability is crucial to that." — standard dual-mandate
   close; "price stability is crucial" echoes the statement's "will deliver
   price stability."

Cut signal status: ABSENT.
Hike signal status: PRESENT — base-case "further policy adjustments are likely
  to be needed." More than the Sep 1 conditional (which was still pre-hike and
  two-branched); this signals the base case is additional tightening.

Shift vs. prior speech (Sep 1):  HAWKISH — Sep 1's two-branch conditional

  case calls for "further policy adjustments." Sep 1: "act decisively to raise

Shift vs. pre-FOMC (Sep 1):      Same comparison — hawkish.

SUMMARY

Net signal:    HAWKISH LEAN
               Barr's first post-hike communication endorses the September 16
               increase in the first person ("which I supported"), characterizes
               the committee as "out of position" before it, and signals
               "further policy adjustments are likely to be needed" as his base
               case. Sequential upgrades on growth ("solidly" → "strong") and
               labor ("stable" → "solid") remove the last remaining cushioning
               language from his March–September arc. The inflation section
               adds an explicit risk-asymmetry statement (risks to inflation
               target "increased" / risks to labor "receded") that was not in
               the Sep 1 conditional framing.

               The embedded section is brief (three paragraphs), which limits
               the analytical granularity compared to Sep 1, but the directional
               read is unambiguous: Barr voted for the hike, explains it as a
               recalibration from "out of position," and his base case calls for
               more.

    statement's "timelier return" rationale; Barr's negative formulation

    labor market have receded" — explicit mandate-asymmetry framing. Stage:
    DISPERSED. Not yet in statement language.
  ⚠ Supply-shock attribution retained despite statement deletion — a potential
