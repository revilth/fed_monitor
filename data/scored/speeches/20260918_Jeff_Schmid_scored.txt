GROWTH
──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
FOMC statement language echoed:
  "Economic activity is expanding at a solid pace. While uncertainty remains
   elevated owing, in part, to geopolitical developments, domestic spending
   has been resilient. Productivity growth is strong, and capital investment
   is robust." [Sep 16 stmt]
  → Speaker version: "Overall economic growth is solid, supported by continued
    consumer spending and strong business investment."
  → A close paraphrase: "solid" (statement's word), consumer spending as
    support (statement's "domestic spending"), investment "strong" (the July 29
    statement's word; September upgraded it to "robust"). Consensus echo.

Own language:
  "Outside of inflation, the economy is performing well." ... "growth
   resilient" — "resilience" has been his own growth word since July 16
   ("Lately, the word I hear most regularly is 'resilience'") and Aug 4.
  No growth-composition content: the AI/data-center crowding-out argument he
  made on Aug 27 ("think about the machinery industry, steel, copper") is not
  in this text. [Inference: a five-paragraph outlook preface at a payments
  talk; read the omission as compression, not retreat.]

Shift vs. prior speech:   unchanged — Aug 27 "the economy is moving along
  nicely ... it's growing" / "pretty optimistic about the durability" →
  "Overall economic growth is solid." Same verdict.
Shift vs. pre-FOMC:       unchanged — Aug 4 "underlying consumption and
  investment remained very strong" → "continued consumer spending and strong
  business investment." Consumption loses its intensity adjective ("very
  strong" → "continued"); too thin to read as a directional change.

LABOR MARKET
──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
FOMC statement language echoed:
  "Job gains have kept pace with the workforce, and the unemployment rate has
   changed little." [Sep 16 stmt]
  → Speaker version: "While the pace of job growth over the past year has been
    slow, this is not unexpected given the aging of the workforce and an
    increase in the number of retirements."
  Press conference opening statement [Sep 16]: "Unemployment claims ... are
   running at levels consistent with full employment. So, the labor side of
   the Fed's congressional remit is in good shape."
  → Speaker version: "The unemployment rate, at 4.1 percent, is in the range of
    what most observers would consider to be full employment."

Own verdict:
  "The labor market appears to be in balance." — mandate met on the employment
  side.

Own qualifier / addition:
  "in the range of what most observers would consider to be full employment"
  — the first time in the scored/raw Schmid record that he labels the labor
  market "full employment." Aug 4 and Jul 16 used a neutrality formula
  instead: "about in line with what most economists estimate is consistent
  with a labor market that is neither too tight nor too loose." The new label
  matches the Chair's Sep 16 opening statement ("consistent with full
  employment"). Hawkish-leaning: it states the employment mandate as
  achieved, not merely balanced.
  "roughly" dropped: Aug 4 "The labor market also appears to be roughly in
  balance" → "The labor market appears to be in balance."
  Slow-payroll explanation re-sourced: Aug 4 "a labor force that is barely
  growing" (Jul 16 also cited "a sharp fall-off in immigration") → "the aging
  of the workforce and an increase in the number of retirements." Immigration
  no longer named; the retirement mechanism is the one he gave on Aug 27
  ("we're retiring at about 4 million people per year ... I'm signing more
  retirement letters").

Shift vs. prior speech:   unchanged-to-hawkish lean — Aug 27 "the labor force
  is in a pretty good place" → "in balance ... full employment"; same verdict,
  firmer label.
Shift vs. pre-FOMC:       hawkish lean (small) — Aug 4 "roughly in balance ...
  neither too tight nor too loose" → "in balance ... full employment";
  unemployment 4.2% → 4.1%.

INFLATION
──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
FOMC statement language echoed:
  "Inflation remains elevated. Today's policy action will support a timelier
   return to the Committee's 2 percent goal." [Sep 16 stmt]
  Opening statement [Sep 16]: "for more than five years, inflation has been
   running above target ... The plain fact is that inflation is too high and
   has been for too long."
  → Speaker version: "the FOMC was responding to inflation that has remained
    too high for too long. Inflation has run above the Committee's 2 percent
    price stability objective for over five years now."
  → Both phrases are also his own carry-over (Aug 4 "Inflation has been too
    high for too long"; Jul 16 "above the Fed's 2 percent objective for five
    consecutive years"). Shared language, now committee-level.

Own language — KEY:
  "Higher oil prices have been an important driver of elevated inflation, but
   it is important to acknowledge that our inflation problem is not just about
   energy. Inflation excluding energy has also been running hot and a broad
   range of goods and services are showing price growth inconsistent with our
   price stability target."
  — Near-verbatim carry-over of Aug 4 ("our inflation problem is not only
  about energy ... Inflation has been too high across a broad-based and growing
  cross-section of goods and services") and Jul 16 ("our inflation problem is
  not only about energy"). A breadth argument, the same test the Chair put in
  the opening statement ("Too many categories are still posting increases
  above 3 percent"). Note Aug 4's "growing" (cross-section) is not repeated.

  "While it might be tempting to focus on oil and other supply issues, I would
   argue for taking a broader view. Inflation always reflects both supply and
   demand developments ... High inflation is a signal that the economy is out
   of balance, and the Fed, through its influence on demand, always has a role
   to play when it comes to keeping inflation in check."
  — His signature anti-look-through position, carried over from Aug 4 ("would
  argue against the temptation to attribute our inflation problem to supply
  shocks alone ... The Fed, through its influence on demand, always has a role
  to play when it comes to keeping inflation in check, no matter the proximate
  cause") and Jul 16 ("I largely disagree with this analysis ... the Fed
  always has a role to play"). The September statement DELETED the supply-shock
  attribution clause ("in part reflecting supply shocks that have driven price
  increases in certain sectors, including energy"); Schmid's framing is the
  one the statement moved toward. [Inference: a bottom-up case — his July/August
  language preceded the statement change; he is now restating it as consistent
  with committee text rather than against it.]

  "the most recent readings suggest a pace that is trending above 3 percent"
  — Level claim. Softer register than the Chair's "around 3.6 percent" total
  PCE for August and than his own Aug 27 "a bit of a surge into the mid-3s"
  (ASR). Measures are unspecified, so not directly comparable; do not read as
  a dovish shift.

  Dropped vs. Jul 16/Aug 4: the June-print caveat ("encouraging deceleration
  ... premature to put too much weight on a single data point") and "Inflation
  shocks are not intrinsically transitory" / "I am uncomfortable ever assuming
  that a burst of inflation will be temporary." The first is stale data; the
  second is compression. No AI relative-price paragraph (Aug 4).

Shift vs. prior speech:   unchanged — Aug 27 "We haven't done our job yet on
  inflation" / "It has to be addressed" → "the Fed has work to do on inflation
  and this week's action was a step in that direction." Same verdict.
Shift vs. pre-FOMC:       unchanged — Aug 4 "my focus is squarely on inflation
  that remains too high" and the supply/demand argument carry over nearly
  verbatim.

MONETARY POLICY
──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
FOMC statement language echoed:
  "The Committee decided to raise the target range for the federal funds
   rate by 1/4 percentage point to 3-3/4 to 4 percent" / "Today's policy
   action will support a timelier return to the Committee's 2 percent goal."
   [Sep 16 stmt]
  → Speaker version: "On Wednesday the Federal Open Market Committee voted to
    raise the policy interest rate and to tighten the stance of monetary
    policy. I supported this decision."
  Jackson Hole (Aug 28, Warsh): "We must be confident that underlying
   inflation is moving to our objective, clearly and at sufficient speed.
   Otherwise, we have work to do."
  → Speaker version: "As Chairman Warsh said at the Kansas City Fed's Jackson
    Hole Economic Symposium last month, the Fed has work to do on inflation
    and this week's action was a step in that direction."

Own language:
  "I supported this decision." — non-voter endorsement in the first person;
  the earliest post-meeting endorsement on record (Goolsbee Sep 21 "that's why
  I supported this decision"; Barkin Sep 22 "That's why we raised rates").
  "to tighten the stance of monetary policy" — his description of the move.
  Not the Chair's "removed a dose of accommodation," and not "restrictive."
  He offers NO stance label for the new 3.75–4.00% range.
  "this week's action was a step in that direction" — "a step" frames the hike
  as partial progress on "work to do." [Inference: implies more than one step
  may be needed; he does not say so, names no path, and gives no condition.]
  "With the labor market in balance and growth resilient, inflation is my
  primary concern as I think about the correct course for monetary policy."
  — Near-verbatim carry-over of the Aug 4 close ("With the labor market in
  balance and growth resilient, my primary concern is inflation") and Jul 16
  ("My primary concern is inflation ... my focus remains on inflation in
  setting the correct course for policy").

  Cut/hike signal status: HIKE ask satisfied, next step UNSTATED. Aug 4: "I do
  not see the current stance of monetary policy as restrictive. As such, I
  believe that bringing inflation down to the Fed's 2 percent objective will
  require tighter policy." Aug 27: "I think we're at a fairly accommodative
  place for rates right now." Sept 18: the Aug 4 "not restrictive" and Aug 27
  "accommodative" verdicts are NOT restated for the post-hike rate, and
  neither is withdrawn.

Shift vs. prior speech:   unchanged (hawkish) — Aug 27 "fairly accommodative"
  → hike endorsed as "a step"; the diagnosis is consistent with his August
  position and the Committee acted on it.
Shift vs. pre-FOMC:       unchanged (hawkish) — Aug 4 "will require tighter
  policy" → "voted ... to tighten the stance of monetary policy. I supported
  this decision." His stated ask was delivered; no escalation beyond it.

SUMMARY
──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Net signal:    HAWKISH LEAN — unchanged vs. pre-FOMC.
               Schmid endorses the hike in the first person and describes it
               as "a step" on the "work to do" on inflation, with labor now
               labeled "full employment" and inflation framed as a demand
               imbalance the Fed "always has a role" in correcting. It is a
               confirmation document: the inflation and policy language is
               largely carried over from Aug 4, and his August ask ("will
               require tighter policy") has been met. What is missing is the
               path: he restates neither his "not restrictive" (Aug 4) nor
               "fairly accommodative" (Aug 27) verdict for the new rate, so
               whether one hike is enough in his view is unresolved.

Position relative to committee: with Goolsbee (Sep 21) and Barkin (Sep 22) in
  endorsing the hike, and earlier than both; on the supply-vs-demand question
  he is aligned with the post-September statement and the Chair's breadth
  framing and against Williams's attribution framework. A 2027 voter.

Language watch:
  • "our inflation problem is not just/only about energy" — Schmid Jul 16,
    Aug 4, Sep 18 (third consecutive scripted use). Stage: IN-STATEMENT in
    substance — the Sep 16 statement deleted the energy/supply-shock clause.
  • "the Fed, through its influence on demand, always has a role to play when
    it comes to keeping inflation in check" — Schmid's signature anti-look-
    through line (Jul 16, Aug 4, Sep 18). Related family: Kashkari's
    "series of successive supply shocks," Goolsbee's "persistent ones,"
    Hammack's demand-side attribution. Stage: COMMITTEE-WIDE.
  • "full employment" as the labor label — Schmid Sep 18, Chair's Sep 16
    opening statement. Stage: EMERGING. Watch whether Just Voters adopt it in
    place of the statement's "kept pace with the workforce."
  • "a step in that direction" — hike as first installment. Stage: DISPERSED
    (Schmid only; Barkin's "one 'talking-to' might not be enough" is the
    nearest analogue).

Analyst flag:  YES
  → (1) Does Schmid still see policy as accommodative at 3.75–4.00%? His
    Aug 27 "fairly accommodative" verdict (with a "higher base level of r-star"
    rationale) is neither restated nor withdrawn. "A step in that direction"
    reads as incomplete, but it is not a stated path. His next outlook speech
    should be probed for a stance label.
  → (2) Chronology correction for the record: this Sept 18 endorsement predates
    Goolsbee (Sep 21) and Barkin (Sep 22); Barkin's scored file describes
    "Why Hike?" as the first explicit defense of the hike by a participant.
    Barkin's is still the most detailed defense; Schmid's is the earliest.
