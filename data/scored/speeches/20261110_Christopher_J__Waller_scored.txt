GROWTH
──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Brief (growth is permissive backdrop, not a primary concern).

FOMC statement language echoed:
  "Economic activity is expanding at a solid pace despite elevated uncertainty...
   domestic spending has been resilient." [Sep 16 stmt]
  → Waller: "Economic output continues to grow at a healthy pace. The initial
    estimate was that real gross domestic product (GDP) rose 3.4 percent in the
    third quarter, supported by strong consumer spending and robust investment."

Own language:
  "I expect GDP growth is backing off a bit in the current quarter, as consumer
   spending slows to levels that I think are more consistent with household income
   and wealth, but output will continue to grow at a solid pace."
  — Q4 deceleration anticipated from the wealth-effect-driven Q3 surge, but no
    growth alarm. Removes mechanical urgency to pause.

Shift vs. prior speech (Oct 8):  UNCHANGED — Oct 8 had "economic activity is
  strengthening in the second half"; Nov 10 acknowledges Q4 backing-off, but
  "solid" verdict and no growth-driven case for pause are both continuous.
Shift vs. pre-FOMC (Sep 3):      UNCHANGED — Sep 3 had GDP 2% with AI investment.
  Nov 10 adds Q3 final at 3.4%; small hawkish directional (stronger outturn).

LABOR MARKET
──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
FOMC statement language echoed:
  "Job gains have kept pace with the workforce, and the unemployment rate has
   changed little. The labor market is consistent with maximum employment."
   [Sep 16 stmt]
  → Waller: "the labor market continued to be solid and stable in September.
    The unemployment rate remains relatively low and near the median of
    policymakers' projections of its longer-run level, while payroll gains are
    in the range of estimates of breakeven."

Own verdict:
  "What is important in the near term is that I see the labor market as stable
   and employment at a level close to the FOMC's maximum-employment goal."
  — The operative policy conclusion is stability near maximum employment. The
    extended "low hire, low fire" section does not alter this near-term verdict.

Own qualifier — METHODOLOGICAL SECTION ("low hire, low fire"):
  "Does a stable labor market necessarily mean it's a healthy labor market?"
  Waller raises two concerning indicators:
  (1) "the percentage of unemployed people who have been looking for work for
      more than six months has climbed steadily since 2022 and is now quite
      elevated for a period of economic expansion." (3-month MA: 18% → 27%+)
  (2) "the share of new labor force entrants in unemployment has been rising
      and has reached close to the highest levels of the past twenty years."
  He frames these as STRUCTURAL/RESEARCH concerns, not near-term policy inputs:
  "both these developments may reflect a labor market that is not as healthy as
   the headline unemployment rate indicates, with implications for the LONGER-TERM
   path of monetary policy" (emphasis added).
  → This is the most extensive public treatment of a possible soft-data/hard-data
    divergence within labor from any voter this cycle. The near-term policy
    conclusion is explicitly quarantined: "I will put aside this discussion... for
    now."

  Scenario framing (precision rule): DESCRIPTIVE. No named policy scenarios;
  the section is analytical, not a conditional commitment.

Shift vs. prior speech (Oct 8):  UNCHANGED in the near-term verdict ("solid and
  stable," labor excluded from decision function). The dynamism section is new
  material but Waller himself flags it as a longer-term research question, not a
  near-term input. Do not score the methodological section as a dovish lean.
Shift vs. pre-FOMC (Sep 3):      UNCHANGED — same "not a large factor" gatekeeper.

INFLATION
──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
FOMC statement language echoed:
  "Inflation remains elevated. Today's policy action will support a timelier
   return to the Committee's 2 percent goal." [Sep 16 stmt]
  → Waller: "By contrast, inflation is well above 2 percent and the focus of my
    attention." "Even if [October CPI improves], I would still support a 25 basis
    point increase... to return inflation to a path that will reach 2 percent in
    the medium term."

Own language — KEY:
  "September's inflation data were bad. There was no silver lining."
  → PCE 0.5% monthly, 3.7% 12m; core PCE 0.3% monthly, 3.1% 12m.
  → Breadth: "More than 70 percent of the components of core PCE service prices
    saw 12-month increases above 3 percent in September." — This directly echoes
    the breadth metric in Warsh's Aug 28 (54% of 199 PCE components) and Barkin's
    Sep 22 (60%+ of PCE); Waller's version uses a service-components cut.

  Three forward-looking concerns:
  (1) ISM textual analysis points to "possibility of more price increases ahead."
  (2) Energy pass-through research: "core inflation may be more responsive to
      energy shocks" post-pandemic; "especially if oil prices were to remain
      elevated, these pressures could be larger than previously thought and persist
      well into 2027."
  (3) AI-buildout inflation channel: "the artificial intelligence buildout was
      significantly driving up high-tech consumer prices." ISM October data confirm
      continuation.

  October CPI framing: "Inconveniently for today's event, we will get consumer
  price index (CPI) inflation for October in a few hours. While I am hoping for an
  improvement over September, even if this occurs, I would still support a 25 basis
  point increase... However, if inflation comes in substantially higher than I
  expect, more robust action by the FOMC may be called for."
  → Logan (also speaking Nov 10) states October CPI came in "higher than expected."
    That means Waller's escalation branch (50bp at December) is relevant.

Shift vs. prior speech (Oct 8):  HAWKISH LEAN — Oct 8 cited August PCE at 0.25%
  monthly/3.0% 12m as "not showing sufficient progress." Nov 10: September PCE
  0.5% monthly/3.7% 12m — worse. The breadth statistic is reintroduced (absent in
  Oct 8). Energy pass-through risk elevated with new research.
Shift vs. pre-FOMC (Sep 3):      HAWKISH — Sep 3 saw "signs of disinflation" in a
  three-month core reading (4.76%→3.05%); that read has been fully reversed by
  two months of worse-than-expected data.

──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
MONETARY POLICY
──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
FOMC statement language echoed:
  "Today's policy action will support a timelier return to the Committee's 2
   percent goal." [Sep 16 stmt]
  → Waller: "I believe further tightening of monetary policy will be needed,
    sooner rather than later, to return inflation to a path that will reach 2
    percent in the medium term."

Own language — BASE CASE:
  "Even if [October CPI shows] an improvement over September, I would still
   support a 25 basis point increase in the FOMC's policy rate at our December
   meeting."
  → 25bp at December is the stated base case regardless of October improvement.
    The only condition that would change the base case is improvement SUFFICIENT
    enough to offset the still-elevated level — and Waller explicitly says one
    improved month isn't enough.

Own language — ESCALATION BRANCH (new and notable):
  "If inflation comes in substantially higher than I expect, more robust action
   by the FOMC may be called for."
  "If the October CPI inflation data out later today are similar to the past two
   monthly readings—with core CPI inflation around 0.3 percent or even higher—
   then a stronger policy move may be needed. In that case, I believe a 50 basis
   point hike in the policy rate should be considered at the December meeting.
   Hiking only 25 basis points would put our credibility at risk, a risk I am
   not eager to take."
  → THIS IS NEW vs. Oct 8. Oct 8 established "additional hikes" as the base case,
    with timing flexible. Nov 10 explicitly names 50bp at December as the
    credibility-protection response to continued hot data. The trigger is stated
    quantitatively: core CPI ~0.3% or higher. The credibility framing (not merely
    an anti-inflation argument, but a credibility loss argument) is the strongest
    register he has used for escalation.

  October hold rationale (confirms consensus framing):
  "My rationale was that the Committee's communication about the likely tightening
   of policy had already led financial markets to price in some tightening...
   it is not necessary, for example, to raise rates at consecutive meetings."
  → Explicitly endorses the "skip" logic that Logan also defends in her speech.
    Both voters are calling the October hold a tactical pause, not a signal.

Hike signal status: PRESENT — 25bp December base case; 50bp if data hot.
Cut signal status:  ABSENT.

Scenario framing (precision rule): EXPLICIT AND NAMED. Two branches:
  BENIGN: October CPI improves → 25bp December.
  ADVERSE: October CPI ~0.3%+ → 50bp December, credibility explicitly invoked.
  This is the most pre-committed scenario framing Waller has offered since his
  Apr 17 "BENIGN/ADVERSE" split.

Shift vs. prior speech (Oct 8):  HAWKISH — Oct 8: "additional hikes... not
  consecutive... acceptable period of time." Nov 10: 25bp December is the floor;
  50bp is explicitly on the table with a numerical trigger. The upgrade from
  timing-flexible to timing-specified and quantity-escalated is the material move.
Shift vs. pre-FOMC (Sep 3):      HAWKISH — Sep 3 inclination was to hold; hike
  required "hot" August data. Nov 10: even an improvement doesn't prevent December
  hike; the question is only 25 vs. 50.

SUMMARY
──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Net signal:    HAWKISH LEAN vs. Oct 8; HAWKISH vs. Sep 3 pre-FOMC.
               Waller's December stance has hardened: 25bp is now the stated
               base regardless of October improvement, and 50bp is explicitly
               on the table if core CPI runs ~0.3%. This is the strongest
               near-term policy signal he has given post-September. The
               October hold is reframed as a tactical skip, not a signal —
               consistent with the committee consensus. The labor market
               dynamism section is analytically interesting but intentionally
               quarantined from the near-term policy decision.

               October CPI context: Logan's simultaneous speech (also Nov 10)
               states October CPI came in "higher than expected" — placing
               Waller's escalation branch (50bp) in live play for December.

               Position relative to committee: Waller now shares the Logan
               framework — "destination is clear; skip doesn't change it" —
               and has explicitly called out 50bp as a credibility-protection
               measure. Among post-September-16 voters, Logan, Waller, Paulson
               (Sep 24), Barkin (Sep 22), and Schmid (Sep 18) all point to
               further hikes. Waller's 50bp language is the most aggressive
               since Logan's July 31 dissent.

Language watch:
  • "50 basis point hike... should be considered" — first explicit 50bp call by a
    voter in this cycle. Stage: DISPERSED (first use). Watch for Logan or other
    voters to echo if December CPI data remain hot.
  • "Hiking only 25 basis points would put our credibility at risk" — credibility-
    protection argument for 50bp. Stage: DISPERSED. If this spreads to Troika, it
    has statement-language potential.
  • "low hire, low fire" labor market dynamism analysis — methodological framing;
    Stage: DISPERSED. Shares the analysis with Logan (who also raises it Nov 10).
    If this spreads, it may complicate the committee's "consistent with maximum
    employment" verdict in a future statement.
  • "September's inflation data were bad. There was no silver lining." — unusually
    blunt; Stage: DISPERSED (first use). Likely to be quoted by media.
  • "sooner rather than later" — echoes the speed language in Logan's Nov 10 and
    the committee's "timelier return" statement language. Stage: DISPERSED-to-
    SPREADING.

Analyst flag:  YES
  → (1) The October CPI release (Nov 10 morning) came in "higher than expected"
    per Logan. This triggers Waller's explicitly named branch: "50bp should be
    considered at the December meeting." Does the data meet his stated quantitative
    threshold (core CPI "around 0.3 percent or even higher")? Confirm October CPI
    details to assess whether the 50bp branch is live.
  → (2) This is the first explicit 50bp call from any voter for the December
    meeting. Watch for echoes from Logan, Paulson, Hammack, and Kashkari. If
    three or more voters endorse 50bp, that becomes the signal, not the base case.
  → (3) The labor market dynamism section (long-term unemployment, new-entrant
    unemployment) is worth monitoring as a future policy input. Waller explicitly
    shelves it for now, but names it as a productivity concern. If labor data
    weakens, this framework is pre-staged as his rationale for recalibration.
