THE STRUCTURAL FACT — WHAT KIND OF DISSENT THIS IS
──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
  Apr 29 (May 1 stmt):  "I supported the FOMC's decision to hold the federal
                        funds rate ... but I dissented against the FOMC's action
                        because I did not think it was appropriate to continue to
                        include the following phrase ... 'In considering the
                        extent and timing of additional adjustments'"
                        → a dissent about FORWARD GUIDANCE. He backed the rate.

  Jul 29 (Jul 31 stmt): "I dissented against the FOMC's policy action this week
                        because I preferred to raise the target range for the
                        federal funds rate by 0.25 percentage point."
                        → a dissent about the RATE, with a magnitude named.

Same escalation rung as Hammack, and again the language he objected to in April
was already gone by June 17. All three dissenters won the language fight and then
escalated to the rate — that pattern is the single most important structural fact
of this cycle and is treated in the daily report's cross-speaker note.

──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
INFLATION
──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
FOMC statement language echoed:
  "Inflation remains elevated relative to the Committee's 2 percent goal, in part
   reflecting supply shocks that have driven price increases in certain sectors,
   including energy." [Jul 29 stmt]
  → Kashkari: "Inflation has been elevated relative to our 2 percent target for
    more than five years. The initial cause of that inflation was, in part, a
    series of supply shocks ..."
  → He adopts the statement's attribution nearly word-for-word — "in part," the
    supply-shock framing, the same vocabulary — and then adds two modifiers that
    dismantle it: "the INITIAL cause" (i.e., no longer the operative cause) and
    "a SERIES of supply shocks" (i.e., not the single shock that look-through
    doctrine is built for). This is the statement's own language turned against
    the statement's own conclusion, and it is the most precise instance of that
    move in any document this cycle.

Own language — KEY (the doctrinal break):
  "In the face of individual supply shocks that temporarily elevate inflation,
   monetary policymakers should 'look through' the shock and allow it to pass on
   its own. I LARGELY SUBSCRIBE TO THAT VIEW, BUT I INCREASINGLY BELIEVE that
   monetary policy does have an important role to play in addressing a series of
   successive supply shocks that might lead to entrenched higher inflation."
  → This is the whole dissent. Kashkari is not disputing the data; he is
    withdrawing from the doctrine that the data would otherwise trigger. Note the
    tense: "increasingly believe" describes a view still in motion, which is
    weaker than Hammack's flat "did not see" and Logan's flat "does not appear
    to be on course."
  → CLAUDE.md flags "'sequence of transitory shocks' framing (raises the bar for
    looking through inflation)" as a hawkish signal. This is that signal in its
    fully developed form, stated as an explicit revision of the author's own
    prior position.

Own language — the 1970s analogy (new, and the hardest historical frame available):
  "monetary policymakers during the 1970s ALSO DIAGNOSED the extraordinary
   inflation of that period as largely the result of a series of successive supply
   shocks affecting commodities, food and energy markets. Policymakers ultimately
   concluded that tight monetary policy was necessary to bring inflation back down
   DESPITE THEIR ORIGINAL SUPPLY SHOCK DIAGNOSIS."
  → NEW. The May 1 article used the Ukraine-war oil shock as its historical
    parallel — a comparison about oil-price mechanics. The 1970s parallel is a
    comparison about POLICYMAKER ERROR, and specifically about the error of
    holding to a supply-shock diagnosis too long. Kashkari is telling his
    colleagues they are making the identifiable mistake of the canonical episode.
  → He immediately disclaims the comparison's severity ("the economy today is in
    a much, much better place ... Unemployment is lower and inflation is much
    lower"), which is a real hedge and should be quoted alongside the analogy.
    But invoking the 1970s at all, in a formal statement, from a president who
    held a cut dot as recently as March, is a substantial move.

Entrenchment:
  "a series of successive supply shocks that might lead to entrenched higher
   inflation" / "to manage against the risk that high inflation could become
   entrenched"
  → CARRY-OVER in concept from May 1, which ran an explicit expectations-
    unanchoring scenario ("the FOMC would have to take very seriously the risk of
    an unanchoring of long-run inflation expectations"). The vocabulary has
    shifted from "unanchoring" to "entrenched." Worth noting: Jefferson (Vice
    Chair) used "inflation becomes entrenched" on July 16. See language watch.

Duration:
  "elevated relative to our 2 percent target for more than five years"
  → CARRY-OVER — his May 13 remarks used "above our target now for more than 5
    years," and the formulation is shared with Hammack and Logan. NOT new.

Shift vs. pre-FOMC (May 13):  HAWKISH — May 13 was "a huge question mark" about
  the inflation path with confidence removed but no policy conclusion drawn.
  July 31 converts the uncertainty into a doctrinal argument for acting under
  uncertainty. The unresolved question became a reason to move rather than a
  reason to wait.

Direction: HAWKISH.

──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
MONETARY POLICY
──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
FOMC statement language echoed: none — Kashkari does not invoke "will deliver
  price stability" or the dual-mandate formula. Unlike Hammack and Logan, he does
  not argue from the Committee's own commitments.

Own language — KEY (the path, which is his distinctive contribution):
  "I would rather tighten policy incrementally as we gather more data on the path
   of inflation and employment. If inflation remains elevated, in my view, A
   POTENTIAL SERIES OF SMALL POLICY MOVES would be better than waiting and
   eventually concluding that even bolder actions were necessary. On the other
   hand, if inflation durably fades, a strategy of small policy steps would allow
   the FOMC to slow or pause subsequent adjustments without unnecessary impact on
   the real economy."
  → Kashkari is the only one of the three dissenters to describe a PATH rather
    than a single move. Hammack names no path; Logan asks for "modest action in
    the near term." Kashkari asks for a sequence, conditional and reversible in
    both directions.
  → CARRY-OVER WARNING: "a series of them" is NOT new language. The May 1 article
    already contained "Federal funds rate increases, potentially a series of
    them, could be warranted" — but there it sat inside the ADVERSE scenario (an
    extended Strait of Hormuz closure with expectations unanchoring), i.e. a tail.
    The change on July 31 is that the series has moved from the tail to the base
    case, with no triggering catastrophe required. That relocation is the signal;
    the phrase itself is not.

  Symmetry and reversibility:
  "if inflation durably fades, a strategy of small policy steps would allow the
   FOMC to slow or pause subsequent adjustments"
  → He builds in the exit. This is the same pre-emption Logan makes ("can always
    adjust policy if unanticipated shocks occur"), and it is aimed at the
    strongest objection to hiking into a supply shock — that you will have to
    reverse it. Kashkari's version is more explicit than Logan's.

  Cost-of-waiting:
  "would be better than waiting and eventually concluding that even bolder actions
   were necessary"
  → The bloc's shared template. Compare Hammack: "The longer that high inflation
    persists, the more challenging and costly it can be to bring it back down";
    Logan: "Modest action in the near term would reduce the likelihood of needing
    to take sharper action later." Three formulations of one argument, same day.

  Hike signal:  PRESENT AND EXECUTED, with a named magnitude (0.25pp) and an
                explicit multi-step path conditional on inflation remaining
                elevated.
  Cut signal:   ABSENT since Apr 29. His March SEP cut dot is long since void.

Scenario framing (per CLAUDE.md precision rule): DESCRIPTIVE-CONDITIONAL, not
  named. Two branches are specified ("if inflation remains elevated" / "if
  inflation durably fades") with a policy response attached to each, which is
  stronger than pure description — but he does not label them as scenarios the way
  his own May 1 article did (that one named two explicit Hormuz scenarios) or the
  way Waller did on Apr 17 ("BENIGN / ADVERSE"). Intermediate strength.

Shift vs. pre-FOMC (May 13):  HAWKISH — MAJOR. May 13 was "We just don't know
  right now," with no forward guidance and the cut signal withdrawn. July 31 is an
  executed hike vote plus a conditional sequence. Across the 77-day gap Kashkari
  has travelled from agnosticism to the most path-explicit hawkish position on the
  committee.

Direction: HAWKISH.

SUMMARY
══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
Net signal:    HAWKISH — the largest single-document move of the three dissenters,
               because his starting point was the least committed.
               (1) Dissent object moves from FORWARD GUIDANCE (Apr 29) to the RATE
                   (Jul 29), with a magnitude named;
               (2) Explicit doctrinal revision on look-through — "I largely
                   subscribe to that view, BUT I increasingly believe" — which is
                   the substance of the dissent;
               (3) The 1970s successive-supply-shock analogy: new, and a charge of
                   identifiable policymaker error, hedged immediately on severity;
               (4) The "series of small moves" relocated from May 1's adverse tail
                   scenario to the base case — the phrase is carry-over, the
                   placement is the signal;
               (5) Data centers named as a demand channel — converging with Logan.
               Weakest-hedged of the three: "increasingly believe" is softer than
               Hammack's "did not see" and Logan's "does not appear to be."

Position relative to committee:
  Kashkari is the path-maximalist of the dissent bloc — the only one asking for a
  sequence rather than a move — while being the most tentative in how he states
  his conviction. He offers no current data assessment at all, which makes his
  dissent the most theoretical and the most vulnerable to a couple of soft
  inflation prints. Against that, his argument is the one most likely to travel:
  it is about doctrine, and doctrine is what the minutes and the statement
  language actually encode.

Language watch:
  • "a series of successive supply shocks" — the look-through exit ramp, stated as
    doctrine rather than as a data read. Stage: EMERGING. This is the formulation
    most likely to surface in the July 28–29 minutes (Aug 19) as a participant
    view, and it is the direct antagonist of the statement's "reflecting supply
    shocks" clause. Highest-priority phrase to track this cycle.
  • "entrenched higher inflation" — shared with Jefferson's Jul 16 "inflation
    becomes entrenched" and Logan's Jul 16 "if higher inflation becomes
    entrenched." Stage: EMERGING, and notable because Jefferson is Vice Chair —
    a Troika member using a dissent-bloc frame is the classic bottom-up precursor.
  • "a potential series of small policy moves" — the only multi-step path on the
    committee. Stage: DISPERSED (Kashkari alone). If Hammack or Logan adopts a
    sequence framing, September stops being about one hike.
  • The 1970s analogy — stage: DISPERSED (first use). Watch for pushback; if a
    Troika member rebuts it explicitly, that tells you it landed.

Analyst flag:  YES
  → (1) The 77-day baseline gap (May 13 → Jul 31) is a genuine coverage hole.
    Kashkari's shift from "we just don't know" to a multi-step hike path is scored
    against a stale prior, and it is possible that intermediate appearances exist
    that were never recovered. Worth a targeted YouTube/Minneapolis Fed sweep for
    June–July before the magnitude of the shift is treated as settled.
  → (2) Kashkari is the only dissenter arguing for a SERIES. Whether the other two
    would follow him past one hike is unresolved and is the difference between a
    one-off September dissent and a genuine tightening faction.
  → (3) He offers no labor-market characterization while voting to tighten. If the
    August employment report is weak, his statement contains nothing to hold him
    to the hawkish position — his is the softest of the three commitments despite
    being the most expansive.
