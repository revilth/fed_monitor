GROWTH
──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Given fuller treatment than usual: Warsh volunteered a specific productivity
figure not disclosed June 17, directly relevant to the growth-slowdown-as-
cut-rationale debate.

FOMC statement language echoed: June 17 opening added "Productivity growth
and capital investment are strong" (first appearance of that sentence, per
the provisional press-conf score).
  → Speaker version, Sintra: extends it with an actual number — "over the
    last four quarters in the US structural productivity is in the high 2%
    range. So potential growth looks like it's trended up."

Own language:
  "This is a time that the labor markets hours worked are relatively flat.
  Um history says that we go from periods of low productivity to periods of
  high productivity. Nothing is in the bank at this time of consequence. But
  if the last four quarters are an indication, which is really largely before
  the advent of the new surge in what artificial intelligence can do, I think
  there's reason to be optimistic." — structural optimism, explicitly framed
  as pre-AI-surge (i.e., the "high 2%" number undercounts any future AI
  productivity contribution).
  "Now, does that optimism convey into policy in the next six or nine months?
  Still too soon to say." — deliberately non-committal; refuses to translate
  the growth optimism into a policy signal, consistent with the no-forward-
  guidance posture.

Shift vs. POST-FOMC PRIOR (June 17):    HAWKISH-LEAN — quantifies and
  reinforces the June 17 "productivity and capital investment are strong"
  line with a specific, previously undisclosed figure. Strengthens the
  "no growth-slowdown rationale for cuts" framing.
Shift vs. pre-FOMC:                     N/A (no pre-FOMC baseline).

LABOR MARKET
──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
FOMC statement language echoed:
  June 17 opening: "Job gains have kept pace with the workforce, and the
  unemployment rate has changed little."
  → Speaker version, Sintra: "when we met two weeks ago, I think the way we
    as a committee described the labor side of the mandate is we said labor
    markets are steady. We said the demand side of the economy was solid and
    we said the supply side of the economy, especially capex and
    productivity... was strong."

Own qualifier — KEY:
  "I don't want to sound like a Wall Street newsletter and update that with
  recent events because we follow trends." — an explicit methodological
  commitment to trend-based, not print-based, reading of labor data. Note
  [Inference]: this statement was made the day before the weak June NFP
  release (July 2); Warsh could not have been referring to that print
  specifically, but the framing signals how he is likely to treat it once
  released — as noise to be smoothed, not an immediate policy trigger, aligned
  with the data task force's stated skepticism of "government agencies with
  mismeasurement problems" and "surveys that are no longer relevant" (see
  SPECIAL TOPICS below).

Shift vs. POST-FOMC PRIOR (June 17):    UNCHANGED in substance ("steady" is
  Warsh's own shorthand paraphrase of "kept pace with the workforce") but ADDS
  an explicit anti-reactivity commitment absent June 17.
Shift vs. pre-FOMC:                     N/A.

INFLATION
──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
FOMC statement language echoed:
  June 17: "The Committee will deliver price stability."
  → Speaker version, Sintra: "We're going to deliver price stability in the
    US. That's what this committee has signed up to do and our objective is
    to do that." — near-verbatim continuation.

Own language — KEY:
  "Expectations of inflation over the first four weeks of this period, they've
  come down. Inflation risks have come down." — GENUINELY NEW since June 17;
  softer in degree, acknowledging near-term progress (confirmed against media
  coverage already logged in the July 3 report; full context now verified: the
  "four weeks" reference frames this as post-June-17 progress, likely tied to
  the oil-price retreat discussed elsewhere on the panel — "$72/barrel now vs.
  $120 in March," per Lagarde on the same stage).
  "If there were people in household or the business sector or the financial
  markets who thought that this central bank was going to be comfortable with
  an inflation objective above 2%. Well, I guess they'd be disappointed. We're
  going to deliver price stability in the US." — reaffirms the hard 2% target
  in substance, without repeating the literal "zero is to the right of the
  decimal point" phrase from June 17.

Notable non-answer: Sara Eisen asked directly, "So the market was right to
  interpret your first news conference as hawkish?" Warsh: "Nice try... I've
  just gotten advice from my senior statesman on this group not to do that."
  He declined to confirm or deny, then pivoted straight to the "inflation
  risks have come down" line — a soft signal at the margin without an actual
  retraction of the June 17 hawkish framing.

Shift vs. POST-FOMC PRIOR (June 17):    SLIGHT DOVISH-LEAN, tactical/near-term
  only — near-term inflation risk and inflation-expectations language has
  softened. STRUCTURAL STANCE UNCHANGED: the hard-2%, "deliver price
  stability" anchor is fully reaffirmed, not walked back.
Shift vs. pre-FOMC:                     N/A.

MONETARY POLICY
──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
FOMC statement language echoed: none literally new (forward guidance already
  dropped June 17); this appearance is a doubling-down and elaboration of
  that posture, in front of an audience of three other central bank heads
  who converged on the same view.

Own language:
  On the July 28–29 decision: "There's a lot of data that we received in that
  meeting [June 17]. I see many of my FOMC colleagues here in Sintra. I take
  their views very seriously. I want us to have a good family fight when we
  meet in four weeks. There's a lot of late-breaking news on a series of
  these things and when we get into that room and shut the door, we're going
  to have a good debate... I don't have much more for you than that." —
  refused to signal July outcome when asked directly ("Is July on the table
  for a rate hike?").
  On forward guidance philosophy — new, more personal framing than June 17:
  "My view is financial markets and the real economy work best when you look
  at what's happening in the real economy, you make your own judgments...
  There has been a tendency — and I take plenty of blame for this — [from]
  the '08 crisis, where we were trying to suppress volatility, where we
  thought we needed to spoon-feed markets to get out of that. That was the
  right policy for a crisis. It is not the right policy for the time that we
  have now." — GENUINELY NEW: a personal, self-implicating critique of his
  own earlier-career-era Fed (he was a Governor 2006–2011) that goes beyond
  June 17's more abstract "not well suited to the current policy conjuncture"
  framing.

Cut/hike signal status: ABSENT for July. The June 17 Q&A quantification
  ("half of the members of the FOMC expect rate hikes as soon as this year")
  was NEITHER reiterated NOR retracted here — the topic simply did not come
  up in this format. Do not read silence as a retraction.

SEP / dot plot — reform signal STRENGTHENED:
  "I'm going to let one panel discussion go without me sort of wagering on
  the dots. There will still be dots for a short time, at the very least,
  but we have a task force for that and we'll revisit it." — "for a short
  time, at the very least" is MORE explicit than June 17's "at least as
  currently structured"; raises the probability, in the analyst's read, that
  the dot-plot format changes as soon as the September SEP meeting.

Balance sheet — NEW content, not addressed June 17:
  Asked directly for a target balance-sheet level (currently $6.7T): declined
  a number, citing the new balance-sheet task force. But volunteered his
  standing personal framework: "There is no secret that from the 2011 period
  when I was leaving the Fed through now, I wanted the Fed's balance sheet to
  be smaller... interest rate policy [is] the fairest of the broad
  constellation... My four weeks at the Fed haven't disabused me of that
  idea." Process commitment: any change "will be well deliberated publicly,
  well understood and will not be implemented until financial markets have
  come to understand what those are... it won't take us more than 18 weeks
  to bring it down to size" (contrasted against the 18 years it took to build
  up). [Inference: this reads as Warsh pre-committing to a slow, telegraphed
  QT resumption or balance-sheet reduction later in his tenure, not an
  imminent move.]

Shift vs. POST-FOMC PRIOR (June 17):    HAWKISH structurally (no rate signal,
  reform program deepening) with the same caveat as June 17: this is a
  process signal, not a directional stance on rates. SEP-abandonment signal
  intensified; balance-sheet framework made explicit for the first time.
Shift vs. pre-FOMC:                     N/A.

SPECIAL TOPICS (Type B content)
──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
FED INDEPENDENCE — directly asked, not merely a passing reference (this
  resolves ANALYST FLAG #3 from the July 3, 2026 daily report, which
  speculated the topic was avoided; it was not):
  Asked about the Supreme Court ruling allowing Governor Lisa Cook to keep
  her seat: "We were doing so well. Before the Supreme Court the Fed acted
  independently and followed its remit. After the Supreme Court ruling the
  Fed will continue to do so. I read the opinion on the plane over here...
  I believe in Article 3 judges. I believe in the rule of law. We'll follow
  the Supreme Court decision, but day-to-day the decision reaffirms what
  President Lagarde already said. We are calling balls and strikes as best
  we can." — measured, legalistic, deferential tone; the wry "we were doing
  so well" deflection acknowledges the awkwardness without dodging the
  question. Also unprompted, earlier in the panel, apropos of nothing asked:
  "No matter what the president wants, we've been an independent central
  bank for a very long time. We're going to be an independent central bank
  at this moment, and you're going to see no changes on that." [Inference:
  the unprompted framing suggests independence messaging is a deliberate,
  rehearsed talking point Warsh is proactively deploying, not just reactive.]

AI / PRODUCTIVITY: "The AI shock is leading to a boom in capital expenditures.
  We see that first and foremost in demand, but I'm confident we're going to
  see it in supply at some point." — near-term demand-side effect (potentially
  inflationary via capex) vs. later supply-side effect (disinflationary) —
  an explicit two-stage timing view. Contrasted favorably against 1990s-2000s
  "financial engineering" (buybacks) as a better allocation of corporate
  capital. On jobs: "lump of labor fallacy... we are in the first or second
  inning of this revolution... jobs will be greater, prosperity will be
  stronger. The question... is timing, and we have to take that very
  seriously. We have a dual mandate." — long-run optimism paired with an
  explicit near-term monitoring commitment.

FINANCIAL STABILITY: Prompted on stretched valuations / AI-trade excess and
  leveraged basis trades in sovereign debt markets (a theme Bailey and
  Macklem led): Warsh declined an independent judgment — "I'm not prepared
  to sort of make a broad comment denoting risks that are available in the
  system" — deferring to the joint central-bank view rather than breaking
  from it. [Descriptive framing only; no named policy response — contrast
  with Waller-style explicit scenario pre-commitment per CLAUDE.md's
  scenario-framing distinction.]

TASK FORCES: First concrete timeline disclosed: "I can tell you likely next
  week who will be the outside experts" — task-force membership (the five
  groups announced June 17: communications, balance sheet, data, productivity/
  jobs, causes of inflation) to be named the week of July 6, 2026. Watch for
  this announcement as a near-term catalyst.

INTERNATIONAL ALIGNMENT: Explicit multilateral convergence against forward
  guidance — Warsh, on Lagarde's answer: "We have found common cause... I
  couldn't have said it better myself." All four central bank heads on stage
  endorsed a reaction-function-over-forward-guidance framework, reinforcing
  the June 17 U.S. domestic signal as part of a coordinated international
  shift, not an idiosyncratic U.S. move.

SUMMARY
──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Net signal: HAWKISH-ANCHORED CONTINUATION of June 17, with a narrow, tactical
  dovish note on near-term inflation trajectory that does NOT alter the
  structural stance.

  Warsh reaffirmed every load-bearing element of his June 17 debut — the hard
  2% target, no forward guidance, the reform task forces, skepticism of the
  SEP — and added two new hawkish reinforcements: a quantified productivity
  figure strengthening the "no growth case for cuts" argument, and a more
  explicit signal that dot-plot submission is on a short countdown. The one
  softening element — "inflation risks have come down" / "inflation
  expectations... have come down" — is real but narrow: it describes recent
  data/oil-price improvement, not a change in reaction function, and Warsh
  pointedly declined to characterize his own June 17 debut as hawkish or
  walk it back when asked directly. He also explicitly pre-committed to NOT
  reacting to "recent events" in labor data — a stance stated one day before
  the weak June payrolls report landed, worth revisiting once officials
  respond to that print.

  Two items newly resolved from the July 3, 2026 daily report's open
  questions: (1) growth/labor WAS discussed (contrary to the media-only
  read); (2) Fed independence WAS addressed directly, via the Lisa Cook
  Supreme Court question, with a measured/legalistic response. The "half of
  members expect hikes this year" June 17 quantification was neither
  reiterated nor retracted — it simply wasn't raised in this format.

Language watch:
  • "There will still be dots for a short time, at the very least" — SEP/dot
    plot; stronger version of June 17's "at least as currently structured."
    Watch for a September SEP-meeting format announcement.
  • "I take plenty of blame for this, the '08 crisis... spoon-feed markets" —
    new personal, self-implicating framing for the forward-guidance critique.
    Watch whether this self-referential note recurs in future Warsh remarks
    or stays a one-off Sintra aside.
  • "18 years... won't take us more than 18 weeks" — balance-sheet framing;
    watch for the balance-sheet task force's eventual output against this
    "slow and telegraphed" commitment.
  • "Inflation risks have come down" — watch whether this phrase persists or
    is walked back once the weak June payrolls print (and its wage-growth
    acceleration) is digested by the committee.

  → (1) Track the committee's actual response to the June payrolls miss
