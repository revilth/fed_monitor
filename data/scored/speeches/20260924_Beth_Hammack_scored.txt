INFLATION
──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
FOMC statement language echoed:
  "Inflation remains elevated." [Sept 16 stmt]
  → Hammack: "inflation remains elevated and it has now run above the FOMC's
    2% inflation objective for more than 5 years."
  → The echo is verbatim. The duration clause is her own carry-over (Jul 31:
    "stubbornly above 2 percent for more than five years").

Own language — KEY (risk balance stated, one-sided):
  "The inflation outlook continues to be highly uncertain with risks tilted to
   the upside."
  → Jul 31 had no risk language at all. May 1 had "upside risks to inflation
    AND downside risks to growth and employment." Here she names only the
    upside inflation risk and pairs it with "highly uncertain." The one-sided
    balance first seen by omission on Jul 31 is now stated outright.
  → Verified against Aug 27: the phrase does not appear in the published
    clip. The clip is truncated, so treat it as new with that caveat. "Continues
    to be" signals she regards the view itself as unchanged.

Own language — KEY (AI named as the demand shock):
  "the extended sequence of shocks that we've recently faced from both the
   supply side via tariffs and oil prices as well as from the demand side from
   the boom in capital spending related to AI."
  → Jul 31: "I see inflationary pressures coming from the demand side of the
    economy, as well," with no source named. Now the demand shock has a name,
    AI capital spending. Same day, Paulson called the AI buildout "one factor
    keeping underlying inflation stubbornly high." Two voters, two banks, the
    same attribution on the same morning.

Own language — the look-through argument:
  "The conventional view is that monetary policy should look through a supply
   shock. ... But when the environment is more prone to shocks or the shocks
   arrive one after another in a period when inflation has been elevated for
   years, there's a greater risk that an inflationary mindset could take hold."
  → Carry-over in substance from Aug 27: "We have had these layered shocks
    ... are they going to neatly roll off ... or are there going to be new
    shocks." Not new. It is also the successive-shocks doctrine Kashkari
    introduced on Jul 31 and
    the Chair folded into the Sept 16 opening statement ("relative price
    changes ... do not broaden"). Hammack now states it in her own voice. The
    phrase "inflationary mindset" is her own carry-over from Jun 2
    ("Preventing an inflationary mindset is critical").
  "The longer that high inflation persists, the more challenging and costly it
   can be to bring it back down."
  → Verbatim carry-over from Jul 31. NOT new.

Measurement emphasis (research framing, low weight):
  She highlights Cleveland Fed work on median and trimmed-mean inflation
  adjusted for skewness, expectations-anchoring measures that account for
  dispersion and uncertainty, and the firms' inflation expectations survey.
  → Conference welcome material. Read only as a signal of what she watches:
    underlying trend, dispersion of expectations, and firms' price plans.

SUMMARY
──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Net signal:    HAWKISH LEAN on inflation; no policy signal.

               Hammack's outlook is unchanged in substance from August 27.
               Growth is solid, labor is at her maximum-employment estimate,
               and inflation has been above target for more than five years.
               Two things sharpen. She now states the risk balance outright,
               "risks tilted to the upside," with no offsetting labor risk.
               And she names the demand shock as the AI capital-spending
               boom, which Paulson did independently the same morning.

               She says nothing about the hike she dissented for in July or
               about what comes next. The genre explains that. Her Friday
               panel is the real test.

Position relative to committee:
  The dissent bloc's framework (successive shocks, demand-side inflation,
  costly delay) is now committee language. The Sept 16 statement deleted the
  supply-shock clause, and Paulson, the median voter, made the same AI-demand
  argument today. Hammack no longer has to argue for the framework. The open
  question is whether she wants a second move and how soon.

Language watch:
  • "risks tilted to the upside" (inflation) — echoes the Chair's Sept 16
    press conference ("Inflation risks are to the upside while labor risks
    are roughly balanced"). Hammack drops the labor half. Stage:
    COMMITTEE-WIDE on inflation; her omission of labor risk is her own.
  • "demand side from the boom in capital spending related to AI" — AI capex
    as a demand shock. Stage: EMERGING (Hammack and Paulson on Sept 24;
    Goolsbee on Sept 21).
  • "the shocks arrive one after another" — the successive-shocks critique of
    look-through. Stage: COMMITTEE-WIDE (Kashkari Jul 31, Barkin, Goolsbee,
    the Chair on Sept 16).
  • "inflationary mindset" — Hammack's own recurring phrase since Jun 2.
    Stage: DISPERSED.

  → (2) Friday Sept 25 policy panel (afternoon, Cleveland, with ECB and Bank
    of Mexico officials, moderated by Wilcox) is the first chance to hear
    whether a July dissenter wants a second hike. Check the Cleveland Fed
