INFLATION
──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
FOMC statement language echoed:
  June 17 stmt: "The Committee will deliver price stability."
  June 17 stmt: "Inflation has been running somewhat above the Committee's 2 percent
  longer-run goal."
  → Testimony: "no tolerance for persistently elevated inflation"; "resolute commitment
    to restoring price stability"; "inflation surge of the last five years will be a
    thing of the past." All reinforce the June 17 stance with emphatic committee-level
    language in the prepared remarks.

Own language — KEY:
  "There might be some that look at this morning's data and say, 'mission accomplished.'
  That is not my view." — Warsh's direct response to the benign June CPI print, given
  in Q&A. Explicitly rejects the reading that one good month signals victory.

  "While monthly price fluctuations are inevitable – especially in an unsettled world –
  underlying inflation over longer time horizons is determined largely by monetary policy."
  — Structural framing that subordinates any single month's reading to the multi-period
  trend. The "unsettled world" clause acknowledges supply-shock volatility without
  using it as an excuse to declare mission accomplished.

  "The inflation surge of the last five years will be a thing of the past." — future-
  tense commitment rather than a present-tense observation. Signals Warsh views
  the fight as ongoing, not won.

  "Regime change" — (via CNBC coverage): Warsh described the Fed's new framework as
  a "regime change" to rid inflation as a "tax" on Americans. More populist/political
  framing than the technical June 17 language; calibrated for a congressional audience.

Comparison with Sintra (Jul 1) — NOTE:
  At Sintra, Warsh acknowledged "inflation risks have come down" and "expectations...
  have come down" — a tactical near-term softening. The testimony shows NO echo of
  that softening language; instead Warsh returns to full hawkish vigilance in the
  prepared remarks. The "not mission accomplished" Q&A response directly contradicts
  the dovish reading of the CPI print. Net interpretation: the Sintra softening was
  tactical (oil-price retreat framing) and has NOT consolidated into a more dovish
  structural posture. The testimony re-establishes the June 17 hawkish anchor.

Shift vs. prior speech (Jul 1, Sintra):   HAWKISH LEAN — returns to full hawkish
  vigilance; Sintra's "inflation risks have come down" tactical softening not echoed.

SUMMARY
──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Net signal:    NEUTRAL by design, HAWKISH by framing.

  Warsh delivered exactly what his stated policy dictates: no rate path signal.
  His prepared remarks maintained the June 17 hawkish anchor — "no tolerance,"
  "resolute commitment," "regime change" — without adding any conditionality.
  The critical unscripted moment was his "not mission accomplished" response to
  the benign June CPI print: it is a direct, real-time refusal to let one good
  month shift the committee's narrative. The structural vs. single-print framing
  ("underlying inflation... is determined by monetary policy") is the key message
  for portfolio managers: Warsh is explicitly in the multi-period, trend-over-
  print discipline he committed to at Sintra.

  Notably absent: any echo of Waller's July 13 "near-term tightening consideration"
  language. Warsh's silence is not a retraction of Waller — it is the Chair
  maintaining optionality while a more hawkish voter has set the upside boundary.
  The July 28–29 decision space remains: (a) hold if core readings continue to cool,
  (b) hike if inflation re-accelerates — Warsh has not narrowed that space.
