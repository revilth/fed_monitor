GROWTH
──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
FOMC statement language echoed:
  "Economic activity is expanding at a solid pace despite elevated
   uncertainty..." [Jun 17 stmt]
  → Waller version: "Economic activity continues to be solid" /
    "Spending by households and businesses has been resilient, despite
    higher goods costs generated by tariffs and the surge in energy
    prices from the Middle East conflict."

Own language:
  "private domestic final purchases, a good indicator of the underlying
   momentum of real GDP, likely rose strongly."
  → Firmer than the statement's "solid" — underlying momentum framing.

  "I expect that AI-related investment will continue at a strong pace
   in the near term and most likely next year as well."
  → Reaffirms the AI demand driver; explicitly dismisses "concerns on
    Wall Street about whether AI demand will continue."

  "So I feel the real side of the economy is in good shape."
  → Clears the growth leg out of the policy decision entirely.

Note: The May 22 income-bifurcation flag (upper-income spending masking
  a lower-income squeeze) and the April discretionary-spending softness
  watch item are NOT repeated. The growth read is unqualified this time.

Shift vs. prior speech:   n/a (Jul 6 was Type B — no growth content)
Shift vs. pre-FOMC:       unchanged, marginally firmer — solid growth
  affirmed; May 22's compositional caveats dropped; PDFP "rose strongly."

LABOR MARKET
──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
FOMC statement language echoed:
  "Job gains have kept pace with the workforce, and the unemployment
   rate has changed little." [Jun 17 stmt]
  → Waller version: "the story there is one of stability and a balance
    between supply and demand."

Own verdict:
  "I believe employment is close to its maximum sustainable level,
   neither a source of concern for the strength of the six-year economic
   expansion nor a source of inflationary pressure."
  → Two-sided neutrality verdict: labor is neither the recession
    trigger nor the inflation engine. Continuation of the May 22
    "rough balance" assessment, now stated with more confidence.

Own treatment of the weak June payroll print (looked through):
  "most people were surprised by the initial estimate of only 57,000
   jobs added in June and revisions that cut April and May by an average
   of 37,000 jobs a month. Despite this revision, the labor market has
   still created an average of 111,000 jobs a month for the past three
   months... a pace of 111,000 jobs a month reflects a strong level of
   labor demand relative to supply."
  → Explicitly discounts the June NFP surprise using his recalibrated
    break-even framework (low labor supply growth lowers the healthy-
    payrolls threshold). Consistent with the Warsh Sintra / Daly Jul 2
    pre-discounting of this print.

  On the prime-age participation drop: "I don't want to read too much
  into this one data point... this decline in participation could be
  reversed." — noise, concentrated in 25–34 cohort; broader
  underutilization measures declined.

Own gatekeeper line — KEY:
  "Unless I see evidence of a significantly weakening labor market, my
   focus will be on inflation."
  → Restates the May 22 double bar ("significant deterioration") and
    formally subordinates the employment mandate to inflation for the
    current decision window.

Shift vs. prior speech:   n/a (Jul 6 was Type B — no labor content)
Shift vs. pre-FOMC:       unchanged — "finally come into a rough
  balance" (May 22) → "stability and a balance" (Jul 13); the weak June
  payroll report arrived in between and did NOT move him.

──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
INFLATION
──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
FOMC statement language echoed:
  "Inflation remains elevated relative to the Committee's 2 percent
   goal, in part reflecting supply shocks that have driven price
   increases in certain sectors, including energy." [Jun 17 stmt]
  → Waller version: "no matter how you cut it, or what measure you want
    to use, inflation is up this year... running at levels inconsistent
    with the FOMC's 2 percent objective."
  → He goes beyond the statement in one critical way: he actively
    DISMANTLES the statement's supply-shock attribution for core.
    Tariffs: "the vast majority of the effect on inflation of that
    one-time adjustment in the price level is mostly over." Energy
    pass-through concerns "have greatly diminished." What remains is
    an unexplained core escalation — a more hawkish read than the
    statement's framing.

Own language — KEY:
  "I am concerned about the elevated pace of core inflation this year,
   which has steadily moved up... from 3 percent in December 2025 to
   3.4 percent in May."
  "we are past the point where we can attribute large price increases
   to earlier tariff hikes."
  → The look-through window is closed. Core at 3.4% and rising is now
    an un-attributable trend, not a shock residual.

Crossroads framing (title thesis):
  "will core inflation continue on its upward trajectory, or has it
   reached a turning point where it will begin to decline back toward
   our 2 percent target? The direction it takes has very different
   implications for the path of monetary policy."
  → Binary fork, with "equally plausible" weight on the adverse branch:
    "I am concerned about the equally plausible case that data in the
    coming weeks will show that inflation will remain at its elevated
    level or even trend higher, requiring tighter monetary policy in
    the near term."

Breadth signal (sharpened from May 22):
  "For core services, which accounts for 75 percent of core prices,
   nearly 70 percent of its categories have 3-month and 12-month
   inflation over 3 percent."
  → May 22's breadth metric (half of all categories above 3%) is now
    quantified within core services specifically — the component least
    explicable by tariffs or energy.

New inflation channel named — AI demand:
  "it is possible they could be a larger factor if the investment surge
   for AI continues... shortages of memory and storage chips and central
   processing units for servers... are driving up prices for retail
   goods that also use those components."
  → Third named driver alongside tariffs and energy; new since May 22.
    Notable because these categories "historically saw prices fall and,
    therefore, subtracted from inflation."

Expectations — SOFTENED vs. May 22 (dovish-side development):
  "the market-based measures of inflation compensation that I look at
   over the two- to five-year horizon are actually down a bit in recent
   weeks... it seems clear to me that inflation expectations are
   anchored near our 2 percent target."
  → May 22 flagged 1-to-5-year expectations as "moved up since the
    beginning of 2026" with a contagion watch. That concern is retired:
    TIPS 2.1% (2y) / 2.3% (5y), recently declining. The unanchoring
    hike trigger named on May 22 is therefore NOT active.

  But he immediately blocks the dovish inference:
  "I often hear people say that because inflation expectations are
   anchored, central bankers do not have to respond to above-target
   inflation. This view is wrong. When inflation is well above its
   target and the labor market is near full employment and stable, any
   serious policy rule calls for raising the policy rate... Sternly
   staring at inflation until it melts before our withering gaze is
   not an option."
  → Anchored expectations change the SPEED and PERSISTENCE of required
    tightening ("move more deliberately," "rate hikes to be less
    persistent"), not whether a policy-rule response is owed.

Shift vs. prior speech:   n/a (Jul 6 was Type B — no inflation content)
Shift vs. pre-FOMC:       hawkish lean on the trend, offset partially
  by the expectations retirement — core 3.3%→3.4% and characterized as
  a five-month "upward trajectory"; tariff/energy attribution formally
  closed; AI channel added. Expectations concern withdrawn (anchored,
  declining) — the single dovish-side update in the speech.

──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
MONETARY POLICY
──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
FOMC statement language echoed:
  "The Committee will deliver price stability." [Jun 17 stmt]
  → Waller version: "I am committed to returning inflation to the
    FOMC's 2 percent goal but also determined to avoid overtightening
    policy and risking a recession."
  → He renders the statement's unconditional pledge as a personal
    commitment WITH a two-sided qualifier. The overtightening guardrail
    is his own addition — verified absent from May 22, where "recession"
    appeared only in a 2022 retrospective.

Own scenario framing — EXPLICIT AND NAMED (both branches have
pre-committed policy responses tied to a specific data trigger):
  HOLD branch: "I would be very pleased to see a lower reading on core
    inflation, but after its escalation over the first half of this
    year, I will need to see several months of lower readings to feel
    that inflation is moving in the right direction... I would then
    continue to hold the policy rate at its current target range."
  TIGHTEN branch — KEY ESCALATION: "If we get another hot reading on
    core inflation this week, then the FOMC will need to consider
    tightening monetary policy in the near term."
  → Trigger: June CPI (Jul 14) + PPI (Jul 15), explicitly named
    ("Tomorrow's inflation data will be one of several data releases").
    "Near term" with the July 28–29 meeting 15 days away puts a July
    hike consideration formally on the table.

Hike signal trajectory across his 2026 arc:
  Apr 17: ADVERSE scenario = hold, not hike.
  May 22: "I can no longer rule out rate hikes further down the road."
  Jul 13: "the FOMC will need to consider tightening monetary policy
          in the near term" (conditional on this week's core data).
  → Timeline compressed from "further down the road" to "near term";
    subject upgraded from first person ("I can no longer rule out") to
    the committee ("the FOMC will need to consider").

New two-sided guardrails (NEW vs. May 22 — verified):
  "determined to avoid overtightening policy and risking a recession"
  "we need to avoid making the mistake of fighting the last war and
   reacting too soon... But we also must avoid repeating the same
   mistake we made in 2021 and 2022 by waiting too long to respond."
  → Symmetric error framing. He also lays out two structural
    differences from 2021–22 that argue for deliberate rather than
    aggressive tightening: (1) labor market not tight (V/U near 1 vs. 2
    in 2022; wage growth ~3.5% "consistent with 2 percent inflation") —
    "there is a much greater risk now that tighter monetary policy
    could drive up unemployment and even risk a recession"; (2)
    expectations anchored → hikes can be "less persistent."
  → [Inference: the guardrails read as calibration of HOW a hike would
     be done (small, deliberate, not a 2022-style campaign) rather than
     an argument against doing it — implied by their placement inside
     the tightening case, though he does not say this directly.]

2021-mistake anchor (carried from May 22):
  "I am cognizant of the mistake we made in 2021 by not responding
   sooner to the high inflation we observed, and I am determined to
   avoid repeating it."

Cut signal status: ABSENT — second consecutive outlook speech with no
  cut scenario of any kind. The word "cut" appears only in the May 22
  reference.

Hike signal status: ESCALATED — from conditional tail risk (May 22) to
  a live near-term consideration with a named, dated data trigger. This
  is the most explicit hike conditionality from any voter in 2026.

Shift vs. prior speech:   n/a (Jul 6 was Type B; note its forward-
  guidance skepticism — "one cannot simply take a weighted average of
  these scenarios as the 'base case'" — prefigures the crossroads
  refusal to give a base case here)
Shift vs. pre-FOMC:       HAWKISH — hike moved from "down the road"
  to "near term" on a named trigger; offset by new explicit
  anti-overtightening guardrails that bound the size/persistence of
  any tightening. Net direction unambiguous.

SUMMARY
──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Net signal:    HAWKISH lean — escalation from May 22 on the policy
               leg, with new symmetric guardrails.
               Waller moves the hike scenario from tail risk to a live
               near-term consideration explicitly conditioned on the
               June CPI/PPI prints landing this week. The real economy
               (growth solid, labor balanced, June NFP looked through)
               is cleared out of the decision; core inflation's
               five-month upward trend, no longer attributable to
               tariffs or energy, is the sole decision variable. The
               counterweights — anchored expectations, a not-tight
               labor market, the overtightening warning — govern the
               calibration of a potential hike, not its likelihood.

Language watch:
  • "the FOMC will need to consider tightening monetary policy in the
    near term" — FIRST voter to put tightening on the near-term table
    with a dated trigger. If Logan (Jul 16 Houston) or Warsh (Jul 14–15
    testimony) echo near-term tightening language post-CPI, a July
    28–29 hike moves from tail to live scenario. Stage: DISPERSED.
  • "at a crossroads" — binary-fork framing; watch for other officials
    adopting turning-point language pre-blackout.
  • "avoid... fighting the last war" / "avoid overtightening... and
    risking a recession" — the hawks' own symmetric-caution formula;
    if it spreads it signals a deliberate-pace hike consensus forming
    rather than a 2022-style campaign.
  • Anchored expectations ≠ no response ("any serious policy rule calls
    for raising the policy rate") — direct rebuttal of the dovish
    anchored-expectations argument; watch for counter-echo from Bowman
    or Goolsbee.
  • "Sternly staring at inflation until it melts before our withering
    gaze is not an option" — high media-pickup probability; track
    Bloomberg amplification per Task 4 (note: Waller is not Troika, so
    formal pickup tracking optional, but quotability is exceptional).
  • AI-demand inflation channel (chips/servers/memory → retail goods) —
    new driver; first voter articulation. Watch for spread into the
    Beige Book (Jul 15) prices section.

Analyst flag:  YES
  → (1) Waller has pre-committed to a decision rule ("hot" core reading
    this week → near-term tightening consideration) one day before CPI.
    June CPI printed July 14 (after this speech; before this scoring
    run was read by the analyst) — assess immediately whether the print
    meets his "hot" threshold; his reaction defines the hawk baseline
    into blackout.
    (2) "Near term" ambiguity: does it mean the July 28–29 meeting or
    September? He does not say. Warsh's Senate testimony (Jul 15) and
    Logan (Jul 16) are the remaining pre-blackout reads on whether
    near-term tightening language is spreading.
    (3) Note the succession context: Waller was a 2025 candidate for
    Chair. His shift from the committee's leading 2025 dove to its most
    explicit near-term hawk, now outflanking the statement's supply-
    shock framing, is worth an independent credibility read.
