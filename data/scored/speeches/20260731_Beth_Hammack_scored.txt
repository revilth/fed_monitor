THE STRUCTURAL FACT — WHAT KIND OF DISSENT THIS IS
──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Hammack has now dissented at two consecutive attended meetings, but the OBJECT of
the dissent changed:

  Apr 29 (May 1 stmt):  "I supported holding the federal funds rate steady. I
                        dissented from the post-meeting statement because I did
                        not believe it was appropriate to include an easing bias"
                        → a dissent about LANGUAGE. She backed the rate decision.

  Jul 29 (Jul 31 stmt): "I dissented in favor of raising the federal funds rate."
                        → a dissent about the RATE ITSELF.

That is the escalation rung, and it is unambiguous in her own words. A statement-
language dissent costs the Chair nothing structurally; a rate dissent is a
recorded vote against the policy action. Note also that the July statement was
the one she had wanted all along — the easing-bias language she objected to in
April was already gone by June 17. Having won the language fight, she moved the
objection to the level of the rate.

LABOR MARKET
──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
FOMC statement language echoed:
  "Job gains have kept pace with the workforce, and the unemployment rate has
   changed little." [Jul 29 stmt]
  → Hammack does not echo the job-gains clause at all. She renders the labor side
    purely as an unemployment-rate level judgment.

Own verdict:
  "Given the stability of the labor market, with the unemployment rate near my
   estimate of maximum employment, I view high inflation as the more pressing
   problem."
  → The labor market is not an object of concern; it is the PERMISSION SLIP. The
    sentence is constructed so that labor stability is the premise and
    inflation-primacy is the conclusion. This is the same "full employment"
    framing she has run since May 1 — the phrasing moves from "my definition of
    full employment" (Jun 2) to "my estimate of maximum employment" (Jul 31),
    which is the statutory term, not a change in substance.

  [Carry-over check: the near-full-employment characterization appears in the
   May 1 statement ("little changed near my estimate of full employment since
   last summer") and June 2 ("right around my definition of full employment").
   It is NOT new and must not be read as a shift.]

Shift vs. prior speech (Jun 2):  UNCHANGED — same level judgment, same absence
  of any softening or downside-risk language.
Shift vs. pre-FOMC (Jun 2):      UNCHANGED.

Direction: neutral-to-hawkish (labor deployed as headroom to tighten).

──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
INFLATION
──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
FOMC statement language echoed:
  "Inflation remains elevated relative to the Committee's 2 percent goal, in part
   reflecting supply shocks that have driven price increases in certain sectors,
   including energy." [Jul 29 stmt — verbatim unchanged from June 17]
  → Hammack accepts the first half and then explicitly refuses the sufficiency of
    the second half. This is the analytical core of the document (see below).

Own language — KEY (the demand-side bridge):
  "Supply-side factors, including energy prices, have boosted inflation this
   year, but I see inflationary pressures coming from the demand side of the
   economy, as well."
  → THE DECISIVE MOVE. The committee statement attributes inflation to supply
    shocks. A pure supply diagnosis implies look-through and argues AGAINST
    hiking. By locating pressure on the demand side, Hammack re-establishes the
    channel monetary policy can actually address — which is what makes a hike
    coherent rather than self-defeating. Everything else in the statement rests
    on this sentence.
  → Relative to June 2 this is a sharpening, not an invention: June 2 already ran
    the "services ex-housing has been stuck for two years, less impacted by
    tariffs and energy" argument, which is the same claim made structurally.
    July 31 states the conclusion directly and in the statement's own vocabulary.

Own language — duration and self-correction:
  "Inflation has remained stubbornly above 2 percent for more than five years,
   and I am not confident it will return to our objective on its own."
  → Two components. The duration argument ("more than five years") is carry-over
    — June 2 used "sixth year of elevated inflation," and the same framing runs
    through Logan, Kashkari and Barkin. NOT new.
  → "I am not confident it will return to our objective on its own" IS new as an
    explicit formulation from Hammack, and it is the precise negation of the
    look-through case. Compare Logan's July 31 "Inflation does not appear to be
    on course to sustainably achieve the ... 2 percent target" — the same
    proposition, arrived at independently, on the same day.

Own language — district evidence (new, and unusually vivid):
  "What I have heard from across the Fourth Federal Reserve District reinforces
   this view: Businesses describe pricing pressures as broadening rather than
   fading, and consumers are expressing despair over persistently higher prices."
  → "broadening rather than fading" is the on-the-ground version of the demand-
    side claim and directly contradicts a transitory-supply-shock read.
  → "despair" is the strongest affective word Hammack has used about inflation in
    any 2026 document. It carries the social-cost argument that Logan introduced
    on July 16 ("compounded the strain on Americans' budgets"). Anecdotal by
    construction — flagged as district color, not data — but it is doing real
    rhetorical work.

Cost-of-waiting:
  "The longer that high inflation persists, the more challenging and costly it
   can be to bring it back down."
  → Carry-over in substance from June 2's Volcker framing ("if we wait for
    definitive evidence ... it may require larger policy adjustments, at greater
    cost"), compressed to one line. NOT new; now shared near-verbatim with both
    other dissenters (see cross-speaker note in the daily report).

Shift vs. prior speech (Jun 2):  HAWKISH LEAN — June 2 was already alarmed
  ("too high and is moving higher"). July 31 does not escalate the intensity
  adjectives; it escalates the ATTRIBUTION, moving demand-side pressure from an
  implication of the services-ex-housing argument to a stated finding, and adds
  district evidence of broadening.
Shift vs. pre-FOMC (Jun 2):      HAWKISH LEAN — same, that being the same
  document.

Direction: HAWKISH.

──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
MONETARY POLICY
──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
FOMC statement language echoed:
  "The Committee will deliver price stability." [Jul 29 stmt]
  → Hammack: "deliver on our commitment to price stability for the American
    people" — she adopts the Committee's own verb ("deliver") and then uses it
    as the standard the Committee is failing to meet. The statement's language is
    turned into the indictment.

Own language — KEY (the hedge comes off):
  Jun 2:  "monetary policy MAY NOT BE sufficiently restrictive to bring inflation
          down to 2 percent in a timely fashion"
  Jul 31: "I did not see the current policy stance as APPROPRIATELY RESTRICTIVE."
  → Modal hedge ("may not be") → flat past-tense finding ("did not see"). The
    proposition is the same; the epistemic status changed from a concern she was
    weighing to a judgment she acted on.

  Jun 2:  "if recent data trends continue, it MAY SOON BE APPROPRIATE for policy
          to act"
  Jul 31: "NOW IS THE TIME for the FOMC to act to speed the return of PCE
          inflation to our 2 percent objective"
  → Conditional + deferred ("if ... may soon") → unconditional + present ("now is
    the time"). The June 2 condition was "if recent data trends continue"; the
    July 31 statement is the confirmation that, in her reading, they did.

  Magnitude and timing:
  "I preferred to move at our recent meeting" / [per the July 29 statement] a
  25bp increase.
  → Note the statement itself supplies the magnitude; Hammack's own text does not
    name 25bp. She names no path beyond this meeting — no series, no terminal
    rate. This is narrower than Kashkari, who explicitly contemplates a sequence.

  Hike signal:  PRESENT AND EXECUTED — no longer conditional. First affirmative
                hike dissent of Hammack's tenure.
  Cut signal:   ABSENT (and has been since Apr 29).

Collegiality close — CARRY-OVER, do not read as a signal:
  "A wide range of viewpoints is critical to any deliberative process, and I
   appreciate the robustness of our monetary policy discussions."
  → Near-verbatim from May 1: "A wide range of viewpoints is a cornerstone of our
    robust policy process." This is Hammack's standard dissent-statement closing
    formula. It is boilerplate and carries no directional content. Flagged here
    only because its similarity to Warsh's "good family fight" framing at the
    July 29 press conference could otherwise invite over-reading.

Scenario framing (per CLAUDE.md precision rule): NEITHER named nor descriptive —
  this document contains no scenario analysis at all, unlike June 2, which ran
  two explicit named scenarios. A dissent statement is a verdict, not a forecast.
  The absence is a feature of the genre, not a signal.

Shift vs. prior speech (Jun 2):  HAWKISH — conditional hike signal converted to
  an executed hike dissent; restrictiveness hedge removed.
Shift vs. pre-FOMC (Jun 2):      HAWKISH — same.

Direction: HAWKISH.

──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
WHAT DISAPPEARED — the highest-value comparison
──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Against the May 1 dissent statement, one clause is conspicuously gone:

  May 1:  "Uncertainty around the economic outlook is elevated, with upside risks
           to inflation AND DOWNSIDE RISKS TO GROWTH AND EMPLOYMENT."
  Jul 31: no two-sided risk language of any kind. No mention of uncertainty. No
           downside risk to growth or employment anywhere in the document.

Hammack's risk assessment has gone from explicitly two-sided to entirely
one-sided in three months. Given that both documents are the same genre, by the
same author, at the same length, written for the same purpose, this is a clean
comparison rather than an artifact of format. It is the single clearest evidence
in the statement that her dissent reflects a changed view of the balance of
risks, not merely impatience.

SUMMARY
──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Net signal:    HAWKISH — escalation from the June 2 pre-FOMC baseline, and a
               change in kind from the May 1 dissent.
               (1) Dissent object moves from statement LANGUAGE (Apr 29) to the
                   RATE ITSELF (Jul 29);
               (2) "may not be sufficiently restrictive" → "did not see the
                   current policy stance as appropriately restrictive" — hedge
                   removed;
               (3) "may soon be appropriate to act" → "now is the time ... to
                   act" — condition and delay both removed;
               (4) Demand-side inflation stated as a finding, which is what makes
                   hiking through a supply shock coherent;
               (5) Two-sided risk language present on May 1 is entirely absent.
               No path guidance beyond this meeting — narrower than Kashkari.

Position relative to committee:
  Hammack is one of three hike dissenters and shares the bloc's full argumentative
  template (five-year duration, cost of waiting, demand-side attribution,
  labor-as-headroom). Her distinctive contribution is district evidence —
  "broadening rather than fading" — which is the only bottom-up corroboration any
  of the three offers for the demand-side claim. She names no rate path, which
  places her between Logan (a single "modest action") and Kashkari (an explicit
  series).

Language watch:
  • "did not see the current policy stance as appropriately restrictive" — the
    unhedged restrictiveness verdict. Stage: DISPERSED within the dissent bloc
    (Logan's "not restraining the economy" is the same claim). Watch for any
    majority voter conceding the point; that is the leading indicator that the
    statement's characterization of policy is under pressure.
  • "pressures coming from the demand side of the economy, as well" — the
    supply-shock exit ramp. Stage: EMERGING — all three dissenters made a
    demand-side move on the same day by different routes. If a Troika member
    concedes a demand component, the statement's "reflecting supply shocks"
    attribution becomes the next thing to change.
  • "broadening rather than fading" — district-level pricing evidence.
    Stage: DISPERSED. Watch Barkin, whose business intelligence is the natural
    cross-check and who is not a voter.
  • "not confident it will return to our objective on its own" — the negation of
    look-through. Stage: EMERGING (Logan same day, different words).
  • "despair" — affective escalation; first use. Watch whether the social-cost
    register spreads beyond the dissent bloc.

Analyst flag:  YES
  → (1) The disappearance of May 1's "downside risks to growth and employment"
    is the cleanest like-for-like evidence of a genuine change in Hammack's risk
    balance. Worth confirming the analyst reads it the same way before it is
    treated as settled.
  → (2) Hammack names no path beyond July. Whether she wants one hike or a
    sequence is unresolved and materially changes the September math. Her next
    Type A speech is the place to look; she has no confirmed appearance yet.
  → (3) The demand-side attribution is now the load-bearing claim for all three
    dissenters, and it is the direct contradiction of the statement's own
    "reflecting supply shocks" language. The July 28–29 minutes (released
    August 19) will show how many non-dissenting participants share it — that is
    the highest-value read on whether 9–3 is stable or a floor.
