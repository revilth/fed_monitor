GROWTH
──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
FOMC statement language echoed: none explicit.

Own language:
  "Strong consumer spending, soaring corporate profits, accommodative financial
   conditions and AI investment continue to drive economic activity. Equity
   prices are up 20 percent over the past year. Credit spreads are close to the
   tightest they've ever been."
  → Same "accommodative financial conditions" framing as June 3, now quantified
    (equities +20%, credit spreads near record tights). Logan uses this as
    evidence that policy is not binding, not as a growth-risk flag.

  "The labor, consumption and financial data indicate that monetary policy is
   not restraining the economy. And that's a problem for sustainably achieving
   the FOMC's inflation goal."
  → The "not restraining" conclusion that was an [Inference] on June 3 is now
    STATED DIRECTLY and named across three data categories (labor, consumption,
    financial). She ties it explicitly to the inflation problem — the growth
    picture is deployed as an argument for restriction.

  "AI and other new technologies may eventually generate a surge in productivity
   ... But the potential size and timing of those gains are uncertain. The
   demand effects are here already. And when demand outstrips supply, the result
   is higher prices."
  → Same AI framing as June 3: disinflationary benefit uncertain/deferred,
    inflationary demand immediate.

Shift vs. pre-FOMC (June 3): NEUTRAL-to-HAWKISH — resilient growth read
  maintained; the "not restraining" judgment upgraded from inference to a direct,
  named statement. No growth-side risk cited.

Direction: neutral-to-hawkish.

──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
LABOR MARKET
──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
FOMC statement language echoed:
  June 17 statement reframed labor as adequate ("job gains have kept pace with
  the workforce").
  → Logan: consistent — she frames job gains as adequate for the labor force and
    removes labor as a dovish argument.

Own verdict:
  "the labor market is well balanced and perhaps even strengthening a bit."
  → Slightly MORE constructive than June 3's "stable and broadly balanced" — the
    "perhaps even strengthening" clause is new and leans marginally hawkish.

Own quantification:
  "The unemployment rate averaged 4.3 percent in the first half of this year.
   That is about the same as a year earlier and near most estimates of the lowest
   sustainable level. Employers added an average of 92,000 jobs per month in the
   first half of this year, modestly outpacing growth in the size of the labor
   force."
  → Unemployment 4.3% is unchanged from June 3. But the job-gains figure is
    materially higher: June 3 cited "about 50,000 jobs per month" (framed as
    merely in line with slow labor-force growth); July 16 cites a 92,000/month
    H1 average that "modestly outpac[es]" labor-force growth. Whether framing
    (recent monthly vs. H1 average) or genuine reacceleration, Logan now presents
    job growth as running ahead of the labor force — a stronger read that
    reinforces "not restraining."

Own qualifier (two-sided, but not a dovish pivot):
  "companies are both slow to fire and slow to hire. That dynamic is keeping the
   labor market in balance, but it's cold comfort for anyone who needs to find a
   new job. ... These are real difficulties, but not ones that easier monetary
   policy can fix."
  → Acknowledges worker-level pain but explicitly rules out easing as the remedy
    — closes the door on the dovish use of labor-market frictions.

Shift vs. pre-FOMC (June 3): HAWKISH LEAN — unemployment unchanged, but the
  job-gains figure and the "perhaps even strengthening" verdict are more
  constructive than June 3. The labor side is presented as leaving room to
  tighten, and easier policy is explicitly rejected as a fix for its frictions.

Direction: neutral-to-hawkish.

──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
INFLATION
──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
FOMC statement language echoed:
  June 17 statement: "Inflation remains elevated" / "sequence of supply shocks."
  → Logan does not echo the attribution framing; her approach is measure-by-
    measure and verdict-focused.

Opening framing — KEY (duration + cost-of-waiting):
  "Inflation has exceeded that target for more than five years. Over the 12
   months through May ... the PCE inflation rate was 4.1 percent. Every month of
   above-target inflation has compounded the strain on Americans' budgets ..."
  → The "more than five years" duration argument plus the household-cost framing
    ("every month ... has compounded the strain") is a cost-of-waiting argument
    not present on June 3. It reframes delay itself as a harm.

Explicit rejection of the June data:
  "Another metric, the Consumer Price Index (CPI), showed this week that the
   one-month inflation rate eased in June. But one month of relief is not enough.
   It is time to finish the job of restoring price stability."
  → Direct rebuttal of the June CPI print that drove Williams's "peaked" read
    (Jul 15) and sat below Waller's hot-print trigger. Logan looks at the same
    release and draws the opposite inference.

Path verdict — KEY:
  "inflation does not appear to be headed sustainably back all the way to 2
   percent." ... "my best judgment is that inflation appears to be heading toward
   the mid 2's—not all the way back to 2 percent."
  → The June 3 "mid 2's, not all the way back to 2 percent" forecast is
    maintained verbatim — a durable, explicit above-2% forecast across two
    speeches.

Measures cited (vs. June 3):
  • Core PCE: 3.4% (June 3: 3.3%) — "has risen four-tenths of a percent since
    December." Trend moving the wrong way.
  • Dallas Fed Trimmed Mean: 2.4% (June 3: 2.3%), with the same caveat that it is
    understated ("dropping too many increases right now ... lower than the true
    inflation trend").
  • NY Fed multivariate core trend: 3.4%, "hovered around 3 percent for three
    years."
  • Market-based non-housing core services: "made no progress" since mid-2024;
    "picked up a bit this spring."
  → Same analytical architecture as June 3; the numbers have moved marginally
    higher (core PCE 3.3%→3.4%), reinforcing her read.

Wage-channel note (newly two-sided):
  "Wage growth has been subdued enough that, adjusted for productivity, it is
   roughly consistent with 2 percent inflation. ... But ... business contacts are
   starting to report the opposite. CEOs at a few Texas companies have told me
   they are raising workers' pay to help them manage a higher cost of living."
  → Logan concedes the productivity-adjusted wage signal is benign, then flags
    on-the-ground evidence of wage pressure re-emerging — using her district
    intelligence to argue the benign wage read may not hold.

Risks:
  "downside risks to employment have faded ... And the inflation risks are mainly
   to the upside." — Middle East oil (refinery capacity constraints keeping fuel
   prices up "regardless of global oil supply"); AI investment "could trigger
   nonlinear price increases" beyond chips into "construction, power generation."

Shift vs. pre-FOMC (June 3): HAWKISH — same above-2% forecast, but core PCE has
  risen (3.3%→3.4%), the June CPI relief is explicitly dismissed, and the
  cost-of-waiting / duration argument is new. Data have moved further in her
  direction.

Direction: HAWKISH.

──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
MONETARY POLICY
──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
FOMC statement language echoed:
  June 17 statement: "The Committee will deliver price stability" / "attentive to
  the risks to both sides of its dual mandate."
  → Logan invokes the balanced-approach / dual-mandate structure but argues the
    current rate is FAILING the inflation side: "we should not perpetually
    achieve one goal while missing the other."

Own language — KEY (the signal of the speech):
  "Today, I'd like to tell you why I currently believe modestly higher interest
   rates would better balance the outlook and risks for the FOMC's dual mandate
   goals." ... "So, I currently believe modestly higher interest rates would
   better balance the outlook and risks for the FOMC's maximum employment and
   price stability goals."
  → Outright HIKE ADVOCACY as a current base case — stated twice, bookending the
    speech. Comparison vs. June 3:
    June 3:  "I am increasingly concerned that higher interest rates COULD BE
             NECESSARY LATER THIS YEAR to fully restore price stability."
             (conditional worry; deferred; single-mandate justification)
    July 16: "I CURRENTLY BELIEVE modestly higher interest rates WOULD BETTER
             BALANCE the ... dual mandate goals."
             (present conviction; not deferred; dual-mandate justification)
    Three shifts: (1) "concerned / could be necessary" → "believe / would" —
    worry to conviction; (2) "later this year" → "currently" — deferred to now;
    (3) single-mandate ("fully restore price stability") → dual-mandate ("better
    balance"), with a stable labor market argued to have room to absorb the hike.

  Deductive core:
  "If inflation is not heading all the way to 2 percent on its own, then at least
   some policy restriction is needed to help get it there."
  → An explicit if-then, not a conditional worry: her path forecast plus the
    principle yields "restriction needed."

  Pre-emptive dual-mandate case:
  "If higher inflation becomes entrenched, we'd need sharper rate increases to
   bring it back to target, with a larger cost for the labor market. Better
   modest restriction now than severe restriction later."
  → The early-action argument, framed as protecting employment: a small hike now
    beats a larger, more costly tightening later. Note this "entrenched" framing
    dovetails with Jefferson's Jul 16 "inflation becomes entrenched / expectations
    unanchored" language — see cross-speaker note in the daily.

Scenario framing (per CLAUDE.md precision rule): DESCRIPTIVE, not named — Logan
  does not label discrete scenarios (cf. Waller Apr 17 "BENIGN/ADVERSE"). But the
  logic is a deductive argument (if A then B), stronger than June 3's conditional
  "could be necessary." The hike has moved from her tail to her base case.

Committee-humility close (important for calibrating the signal):
  "Neither I nor any other single person makes monetary policy on their own ...
   When I go to the next FOMC meeting in a week and a half, I'll bring my best
   evidence, analysis and arguments about what we should do. I'll also listen
   carefully to my colleagues' evidence ... I may persuade them, they may
   persuade me ..."
  → Logan explicitly frames her hike view as her argument to bring to the table,
    not a done deal — she is positioning to persuade, and signaling she remains
    open to being persuaded. This tempers how hard to read the base-case hike as
    a dissent guarantee.

  Hike signal:  PRESENT — escalated from June 3's conditional concern to a
    current base-case conviction. First 2026 voter to advocate a hike as a
    current base case under the Warsh chairmanship. Stops short of naming July
    28–29 specifically; no magnitude beyond "modestly."
  Cut signal:   ABSENT.

Shift vs. pre-FOMC (June 3): HAWKISH — SIGNIFICANT ESCALATION. Conditional/
  deferred hike concern → current-conviction, present-horizon, dual-mandate hike
  advocacy.

Direction: HAWKISH.

SUMMARY
──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Net signal:    HAWKISH — significant escalation from the June 3 pre-FOMC
               baseline. Three structural shifts:
               (1) Hike conditionality → hike conviction: "could be necessary
                   later this year" → "I currently believe ... would better
                   balance";
               (2) June CPI explicitly dismissed ("one month of relief is not
                   enough; it is time to finish the job") — the direct counter
                   to Williams's "peaked" read and below Waller's hot-print
                   trigger;
               (3) "Not restraining" upgraded from inference to a direct,
                   named three-data-category statement, with job gains revised up
                   (~50K → 92K/month) and the labor verdict nudged to "perhaps
                   even strengthening."

Position relative to committee (entering July 28–29 blackout):
  → Logan: hike as CURRENT BASE CASE — the most hawkish stated voter position.
  → Cook: conditional "prepared to act" if disinflation absent "soon."
  → Waller: hike if a hot print; June CPI/PPI did NOT trigger it.
  → Jefferson: patient-hold base case + named conditional to "reconsider" if
    inflation does not cool soon (Vice Chair swing).
  → Williams: "inflation has peaked"; patient hold; no hike signal.
  → Warsh: full optionality ("leave alone, raise, cut").
  Logan sits to the right of Cook (conditional) and well right of Waller (trigger
  unmet). The stated hawkish wing entering blackout: Logan (base case) + Cook
  (conditional), with Kashkari/Hammack (Apr 29 dissenters) presumed sympathetic
  but silent post-June.

Language watch:
  • "modestly higher interest rates would better balance the ... dual mandate" —
    outright base-case hike advocacy. Stage: DISPERSED (Logan alone as a base
    case), but the argument for it is spreading (Cook conditional, Jefferson
    conditional). If a second voter adopts base-case hike language, the July/
    September dissent math changes.
  • "Better modest restriction now than severe restriction later" — clean
    pre-emptive one-liner. Watch for uptake by Kashkari/Hammack or in a formal
    dissent rationale.
  • "If higher inflation becomes entrenched" — shared with Jefferson's Jul 16
    "inflation becomes entrenched / expectations unanchored." Stage: EMERGING —
    a Just Voter and the Vice Chair using the entrenchment frame within the same
    day is a potential bottom-up leading indicator toward hawkish statement
    language. Watch Williams/Warsh.
  • "One month of relief is not enough" — direct rebuttal of the June CPI; the
    counter-signal to Williams's "peaked" call. Stage: DISPERSED.

Analyst flag:  YES
  → (1) Logan is the hawkish anchor of the voter coalition, to the right of
    Cook's conditional and Waller's unmet trigger. If she dissents for a hike at
    July 28–29 it would be her second consecutive dissent and the first
    affirmative hike dissent under Warsh. But her committee-humility close ("I may
    persuade them, they may persuade me") argues she is positioning to persuade
    rather than pre-committing to dissent — dissent probability is elevated but
    not certain. Flag for the analyst to weigh.
  → (2) Three voters — Williams (patient hold), Waller (trigger unmet → hold),
    Logan (base-case hike) — read the SAME June CPI and draw materially different
    conclusions, with Jefferson's conditional as the swing and Warsh holding the
    center. The committee enters blackout with no consensus on what the June data
    mean.
  → (3) The "compounded strain" + "time to finish the job" cost-of-waiting
    framing is the first explicit social-cost argument from a 2026 voter. If
    Logan files a formal dissent statement at July 28–29, watch for this language
    as the template for how the hawkish minority formalizes its case.
