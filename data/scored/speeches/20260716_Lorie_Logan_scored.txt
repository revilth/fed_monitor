Direction: neutral-to-hawkish — no growth risk cited; "not restraining"

INFLATION
──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
FOMC statement language echoed:
  June 17 statement: "Inflation remains elevated" / "sequence of supply shocks"
  → Logan does not echo this framing. Her approach is quantitative and verdict-
    focused, not attribution-focused.

Own language — KEY:
  "Inflation has been too high, for too long, and does not appear to be on track
   all the way back to 2%."
  → Three-part structure: (1) level ("too high"), (2) duration ("too long"),
    (3) path ("not on track all the way back") — the June 3 path verdict
    ("trending toward the mid 2's — not all the way back to 2 percent") is
    maintained, now stated as a more direct negative: "does not appear to be on
    track all the way back to 2%."

  "Every month of above-target inflation has compounded the strain on Americans'
   budgets."
  → Duration argument deployed as a cost-of-waiting argument — each additional
    month of inaction has a real household cost. This is NOT in the June 3
    scored file; it is new language in the post-FOMC window.

Measures cited (vs. June 3):
  - Core PCE: 3.4% (June 3: 3.3%) — RISEN since December 2025.
    → Trend is moving the wrong direction on Logan's watch.
  - NY Fed multivariate core trend: 3.4% (June 3: "moved above 3 percent this
    year") — the persistent component has continued rising.
  - Market-based non-housing core services: no progress since mid-2024 on a
    12-month basis. → The services stickiness argument unchanged.

Explicit dismissal of June CPI/PPI:
  "One month of relief is not enough. It is time to finish the job of restoring
   price stability."
  → Logan is explicitly rebutting the June 14 CPI (headline -0.4% MoM / +3.5%
    YoY; core flat MoM) and June 15 PPI (-0.3% MoM final demand) that drove
    Williams's "peaked" call and Waller's hold-branch activation. Her formulation
    is the direct counter-argument to the "data is going in the right direction"
    (Warsh) and "inflation has peaked" (Williams) readings from July 14-15.
    She is targeting exactly the same data release and drawing the opposite
    policy inference.

Shift vs. pre-FOMC (June 3): NEUTRAL on direction (same above-2% forecast),
  HAWKISH on framing — core PCE has risen further (3.3% → 3.4%); the persistent
  component has risen; June CPI/PPI explicitly dismissed. The analytical
  architecture is the same; the data have moved further in her direction.

Direction: HAWKISH — above-2% inflation forecast maintained; measures have
  worsened on the margin since June 3; benign June data explicitly rejected.

MONETARY POLICY
──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
FOMC statement language echoed:
  June 17 statement: "[Committee] will be attentive to the risks to both sides
  of its dual mandate." / "The Committee will deliver price stability."
  → Logan echoes the dual-mandate framing in structure but goes well beyond it:
    she argues the current rate is FAILING to deliver price stability.

Own language — KEY ESCALATION:
  "I currently believe modestly higher interest rates would better balance the
   outlook and risks for the FOMC's dual mandate goals."
  → Linguistic analysis vs. June 3:
    June 3: "I am increasingly concerned that higher interest rates COULD BE
    NECESSARY later this year to fully restore price stability."
    July 16: "I currently believe modestly higher rates WOULD BETTER BALANCE the
    dual mandate goals."
    Change: (1) "concerned/could be necessary" → "believe/would" — from worry to
    conviction; (2) "later this year" (deferred) → present tense ("currently") —
    the hike case is NOW, not deferred; (3) "fully restore price stability"
    (single mandate) → "better balance dual mandate" (both sides) — the framing
    broadens to the full dual mandate, with labor stable enough to absorb
    tightening.

  "If inflation is not heading all the way to 2 percent on its own, then at least
   some policy restriction is needed to help get it there."
  → Explicit logical deduction: her inflation path forecast ("not on track to 2%")
    + the general principle ("restriction needed if not going there on its own")
    = policy tightening required. No ambiguity.

  "If higher inflation becomes entrenched, we'd need sharper rate increases to
   bring it back to target, with a larger cost for the labor market. Better modest
   restriction now than severe restriction later."
  → Pre-emptive case, dual-mandate framed: a small hike now is better for
    employment in the long run than allowing entrenchment. This is the most
    sophisticated version of the early-action argument from any 2026 voter.
    [Note: This framing echoes the historical Volcker lesson — hesitation now
     leads to sharper action later — but Logan makes the cost-benefit case
     explicitly in dual-mandate terms.]

Scenario framing: DESCRIPTIVE (not named scenarios), but the logic is more
  explicit than June 3's general concern — Logan constructs a deductive argument
  (if A then B) rather than a conditional worry (could be necessary if...).

Hike signal: PRESENT — ESCALATED from conditional concern to current conviction.
  First voter under Warsh to publicly advocate for a rate increase (per media).
  Stopped short of committing to July 28-29 specifically; no magnitude indicated.
Cut signal: ABSENT.

Shift vs. pre-FOMC (June 3): HAWKISH — SIGNIFICANT ESCALATION.
  June 3: "increasingly concerned higher rates COULD be necessary LATER THIS YEAR"
  → conditional/deferred.
  July 16: "I currently believe modestly higher rates WOULD better balance the
  dual mandate" — current conviction, not conditional; present horizon, not
  deferred. The hike signal has moved from the far right tail of Logan's
  probability distribution to her base case.

Direction: HAWKISH — hike signal escalated from conditional to conviction;
  pre-emptive argument made explicit; June CPI/PPI dismissed as insufficient.
