GROWTH

──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
LABOR MARKET
──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
FOMC statement language echoed:
  "the unemployment rate has changed little" [Jun 17 stmt]
  → Cook: unemployment at 4.2% (down from 4.3% in April per May 27 speech);
    "nearly all indicators point to stability."

Own verdict — KEY HAWKISH SHIFT vs. pre-FOMC:
  "I see few reasons that today's labor market has more risk than a year earlier."
  → Explicit downgrade of labor downside risk. Compare May 27: "I view the
    downside risks to the labor market as being elevated" — explicitly named
    Middle East demand uncertainty, low-hire environment, AI displacement risk.
    July 15 reverses that: labor risk is now *not elevated* vs. a year ago.

Own verdict — balance of risks:
  "I see a notable shift in the balance of risks relative to a year or so ago,
   with inflation risks now outweighing employment risks."
  → Explicit risk-rebalancing statement. This is a formal June-17-framework
    adoption: easing bias is gone because labor risk no longer justifies it.
    Cook endorses the structural rewrite directly in her own words.

Shift vs. pre-FOMC (May 27):  HAWKISH — from "downside risks to labor are
  elevated" to "I see few reasons today's labor market has more risk."
  The AI-displacement risk and Middle East demand-disruption flags from May 27
  are absent. Employment risk explicitly de-prioritized vs. inflation risk.

──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
INFLATION
──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
FOMC statement language echoed:
  "Inflation remains elevated relative to the Committee's 2 percent goal"
  [Jun 17 stmt — "in part reflecting supply shocks"]
  → Cook: PCE 3.7% YoY (1.7pp above target). Three drivers: AI demand +
    tariffs + Middle East — identical to her May 27 framework and the
    June 17 statement framework.

Own language — LEVEL ASSESSMENT:
  "The initial assessment is easy: Inflation is simply too high."
  → "Simply too high" is a plain declarative level verdict — stronger than
    May 27's directional "clearly moving in the wrong direction." The May 27
    framing flagged trajectory; July 15 flags the state itself as unacceptable.
    [Note: PCE is lower (3.7% vs. 3.8% May), so the wording is a choice to
    emphasize the gap-to-target, not the trajectory.]

Own language — DURATION AND COMMITMENT:
  "I am fully committed to reaching our inflation target, and this commitment
   is unwavering."
  → Echoes the June 17 structural rewrite ("The Committee will deliver price
    stability"). The "unwavering" qualifier is own-language, not statement boilerplate.

Shift vs. pre-FOMC (May 27):  ROUGHLY CONSISTENT, slightly more emphatic —
  May 27: "inflation is clearly moving in the wrong direction" (directional);
  July 15: "simply too high" (level). PCE is lower (3.7% vs. 3.8%) so the
  tonal firmness is a deliberate framing choice. Three-driver framework unchanged.
  "Unwavering" commitment language is new; aligns with June 17 statement register.

MONETARY POLICY
──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
FOMC statement language echoed:
  "The Committee decided to maintain the target range for the federal funds
   rate at 3-1/2 to 3-3/4 percent" [Jun 17 stmt]
  → Endorsed implicitly.

Own language — RESTRICTIVENESS CHARACTERIZATION (new):
  Policy rate characterized as "mildly restrictive."
  → This is a new characterization not present in her May 27 scored file.
    "Mildly" is calibrated — it leaves open whether policy is sufficiently
    tight, which supports the patience framing.
  "The FOMC can take its time, I can take my time, to observe more data
   to understand whether it's really restrictive or not."
  → "Really restrictive" language pairs with "mildly" — acknowledging
    uncertainty about the transmission mechanism; more cautious about claiming
    policy is working than Waller's "at a crossroads" framing.

Own language — HIKE SIGNAL (conditional, explicit):
  "If we do not see signs of disinflation soon, I am prepared to act."
  → EXPLICIT CONDITIONAL HIKE SIGNAL. Condition: absence of disinflation "soon."
    Compare May 27: "I am prepared to raise rates, if the expected disinflation
    does not appear in a timely manner." July 15 version is substantively identical
    (slightly more colloquial). The hike signal is MAINTAINED across the June 17
    FOMC meeting with no weakening.
  [Inference: "act" = hike, given context — Cook names no other form of action
   and the speech has zero dovish content.]

Hike signal: PRESENT — conditional on no disinflation "soon." First post-June-17
  Type A from Cook confirms the May 27 hike signal survived the committee's
  12–0 hold. No cut language anywhere.

Cut signal status: ABSENT — no reference to employment backstop cut signal that
  was present in the May 27 scored file ("prepared to adjust my policy stance
  downward should the labor market deteriorate"). This conditional cut signal
  appears to have been dropped or is now considered secondary to the point of
  omission. [Inference: consistent with her explicit "inflation risks now
  outweigh employment risks" balance-of-risks statement.]

Shift vs. pre-FOMC (May 27):  HAWKISH NET — (1) "Mildly restrictive"
  characterization is new (May 27 did not characterize restrictiveness);
  (2) hike signal maintained in substantively identical form; (3) cut
  language/employment backstop appears DROPPED (present in May 27, absent
  July 15); (4) balance-of-risks explicitly tilted toward inflation mandate.
  The overall effect is a post-June-17 confirmation and slight hawkish
  consolidation: the hike stays, the dove leg recedes.

SUMMARY
──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Net signal:    HAWKISH — Cook's first post-June-17 Type A speech confirms and
               consolidates the committee's hawkish structural rewrite. The
               explicit hike signal ("prepared to act") is maintained from
               May 27 without weakening. The employment downside risk that was
               "elevated" in May 27 is now explicitly minimized ("I see few
               reasons today's labor market has more risk"). The cut backstop
               from May 27 appears dropped. Balance of risks explicitly tilted
               to inflation. "Mildly restrictive" characterization on the rate
               leaves the door open to more tightening if disinflation does not
               appear "soon." The benign June CPI (-0.4% headline) and PPI
               (-0.3% final demand, released same morning) are not addressed —
               Cook does not update her conditionality in response to the data.

               Cook is the first Board governor to give a full Type A post-June-17
               speech. She represents the committee's non-Troika hawkish wing.
               With Waller (Jul 13) and Cook (Jul 15) both carrying explicit
               conditional hike signals into the final pre-blackout window,
               the hawkish conditional branch now has two Just Voter voices.

Language watch:
  • "Inflation is simply too high" — own-language, first use; a level verdict
    rather than a directional one. Watch for committee spread. Stage: DISPERSED.
  • "I am prepared to act" — maintained from May 27's "prepared to raise rates";
    substantively identical. Cook + Waller (Jul 13) both carry explicit
    conditionality. Stage: EMERGING (two Just Voters; no Troika member yet).
  • "balance of risks... inflation risks now outweighing employment risks" —
    formal endorsement of June 17's easing-bias removal in Cook's own words;
    now on the record as a signed-off committee-wide view for at least one
    Just Voter. Stage: COMMITTEE-WIDE (aligns with statement structure).
  • "mildly restrictive" (rate characterization) — new language from Cook;
    watch whether other officials adopt this calibration or whether Waller's
    more hawkish framing ("at a crossroads") dominates. Stage: DISPERSED.
  • Absence of cut backstop ("prepared to adjust... downward") — present May 27,
    absent July 15. This absence, if permanent, signals Cook's balance-of-risks
    shift is a durable stance change, not a meeting-specific expression.

Analyst flag:  YES
  → (1) Cook drops the May 27 employment-softening cut backstop language.
    If this is a deliberate omission (not a venue/length artifact), it implies
    Cook now views the risk function as asymmetric: only act on inflation,
    not on labor. Does Troika member Jefferson adopt or reject this asymmetry?
  → (2) "Mildly restrictive" characterization alongside "prepared to act"
    creates a logical tension: if policy is only mildly restrictive, why
    would you wait and watch rather than act preemptively? This may be
    Cook's version of Warsh's deliberate patience — but watch whether she
    updates this characterization if inflation does not show signs of easing
    in August data.
  → (3) Cook and Waller both carry hike conditionality into the blackout.
    Is there coordination, or independent convergence? If coordinated, a
    third official (ideally Troika) would need to endorse it for it to
    become a statement-level signal. No Troika member has endorsed the
    conditional hike so far.
