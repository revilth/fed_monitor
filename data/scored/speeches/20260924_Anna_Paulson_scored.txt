GROWTH
──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
FOMC statement language echoed:
  "Economic activity is expanding at a solid pace. While uncertainty remains
   elevated owing, in part, to geopolitical developments, domestic spending
   has been resilient. ... capital investment is robust." [Sept 16 stmt]
  → Paulson: "output growth has been solid, perhaps even strengthening
    somewhat"; "I see a resilient economy that is showing some signs of
    increased momentum."

Own language:
  "Strong business investment, fueled by the artificial intelligence buildout,
   continues to power the economy." ... "manufacturing strength extends well
   beyond AI."
  "real consumption growth accelerated to an annualized rate of 3.4 percent in
   the second quarter, and the Atlanta Fed's GDPNow model is currently pointing
   to above 4 percent consumption growth in the third quarter. This momentum
   ... is likely being bolstered by the stock market. Although I'd note that
   this is not a boost that is being experienced evenly."
  → "strengthening" and "increased momentum" go one step past the statement's
    "solid pace." The consumer is quantified for the first time and is the
    growth engine, not a risk. The unevenness caveat (wealth-effect spending,
    footnoted to a JPMorgan Chase Institute study) is a compositional note, not
    a demand-side worry.
  → Aug 4 housing-as-evidence-of-restrictiveness line ("very subdued") is
    ABSENT. No sector is offered as proof the stance is biting.

Shift vs. prior speech (Aug 4):  HAWKISH LEAN — Aug 4 "pretty strong" with a
  soft Q2 headline set aside; Sept 24 "perhaps even strengthening," with
  consumption accelerating.
Shift vs. pre-FOMC (Aug 4):      HAWKISH LEAN — same document.

LABOR MARKET
──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
FOMC statement language echoed:
  "Job gains have kept pace with the workforce, and the unemployment rate has
   changed little." [Sept 16 stmt — unchanged, third statement]
  → Paulson: "the labor market is holding steady."

Own verdict:
  "conditions here are stable and seem to have improved a bit."
  "The unemployment rate ... currently sits at 4.1 percent — a rate consistent
   with what I consider to be maximum employment."
  → First time Paulson labels the current rate as maximum employment in her own
    voice. Matches the Chair's Sept 16 opening statement ("consistent with full
    employment"), Barr Sept 23 ("solid") and Barkin Sept 22 ("good grades").

Own qualifier / addition:
  "Since then, hiring has broadened to include a wider range of industries, and
   over the summer, total job gains averaged a solid 74,000 per month."
  → Hiring BREADTH is new evidence. It answers the concentration concern
    (healthcare carrying payrolls) that underpinned the dovish labor case.
  "Wage growth, though, has been modest, and employers report few pressures to
   raise wages. These are signs that labor costs are not driving inflation
   pressures."
  → Carry-over of the Aug 4 "wage growth ... very consistent with 2% inflation"
    and the May 19 exculpation of labor. NOT new.

  THE MAY 19 CAVEATS — now tested in a prepared text, and absent:

  → [Inference: in a prepared text, their absence can now be read as a real

Shift vs. prior speech (Aug 4):  HAWKISH LEAN — "stable" → "stable and seem to

INFLATION
──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
FOMC statement language echoed:
  "Inflation remains elevated." [Sept 16 stmt — attribution clause deleted]
  → Paulson: "Inflation, meanwhile, remains stubbornly elevated, and only in
    part because of those same shocks."
  → She keeps a partial attribution ("only in part") but inverts its emphasis.
    Where the July statement said "in part reflecting supply shocks," she now
    stresses the part that is NOT the shocks. Closer to the Sept 16 deletion
    than Barr (Sept 23), who restated the full shock list.

Own language — KEY (the underlying-inflation number moved UP):
  Aug 4:   "When I look at underlying inflation ... I see it somewhere in the
           range of 2.4, 2.8. Maybe there was a little bit of mild progress
           over the last several months"
  Sept 24: "Underlying inflation is running in a range of about 2.5 to 3
           percent, well above our 2 percent target, and that gap has shown
           little sign of closing. The best I can say about underlying
           inflation this year is that it hasn't gotten worse."
  → Both ends of the range rose: 2.4 → 2.5 and 2.8 → 3.0. Aug 4's "mild
    progress" is gone and replaced by "little sign of closing." This is the
    median voter's own decision variable, and it moved against her Aug 4 hold.
  → [Caution: Aug 4 was spoken and approximate; Sept 24 is a prepared text
    that cites "many ways to measure." The direction is clear. Do not read
    the size of the move precisely.]

Own language — AI as an inflation source (switch of sign):
  "The AI buildout is one factor keeping underlying inflation stubbornly high.
   While the impact of AI on productivity remains unclear, its effect on prices
   is not." ... "Import prices for computers and computer accessories are up 24
   percent over the last 12 months." ... "price pressures from the AI buildout
   are rippling through the entire electronics supply chain."
  → On Aug 4 AI capex sat on the not-restrictive-enough side of her ledger as a
    growth and neutral-rate consideration. Now it is a named DIRECT source of
    underlying inflation, with a data point and a contact anecdote. This lines
    up with Hammack's same-day "demand side from the boom in capital spending
    related to AI" and Goolsbee's AI-adjacent inflation watch.

Look-through doctrine — no longer defended:
  Aug 4: "I have definitely been of the view that you should look through
          supply shocks. I think that the June PCE inflation data kind of
          validate that."
  Sept 24: no look-through defense. Instead, "Tariff-related price pressures
          eased, but price pressures from the conflict in the Middle East and
          the AI buildout grew."
  → The Aug 4 timing argument ("I would have been too late") does not appear.
    [Inference: the first majority-side rebuttal of the dissent bloc's doctrine
    has been dropped by the voter who made it. She does not repudiate it; she
    simply no longer argues it.]

Shift vs. prior speech (Aug 4):  HAWKISH — underlying range raised, progress
  claim withdrawn, AI promoted to a direct inflation driver, look-through
  defense dropped.
Shift vs. pre-FOMC (Aug 4):      HAWKISH — same document.

MONETARY POLICY
──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
FOMC statement language echoed:
  "Today's policy action will support a timelier return to the Committee's 2
   percent goal." [Sept 16 stmt]
  → Paulson: "This recalibration brings policy closer to what I believe is
    needed to return inflation to 2 percent at a pace that appropriately
    balances inflation risks with risks to the labor market."
  → "At a pace" is her rendering of "timelier." She also reuses her own Aug 4
    word "recalibrating" ("we have to be open to recalibrating monetary
    policy") — the conditional she named on Aug 4 is now the action she
    describes.

Own language — the vote, in her own words:
  "By September, it was clear that inflation risks were growing. Underlying
   inflation showed little to no progress. ... Against this backdrop, the risk
   of persistently elevated inflation had increased. That's why I supported
   raising the federal funds rate by 25 basis points at last week's meeting."
  → A clean statement of which Aug 4 branch fired. Aug 4: "if we don't see that
    progress, then we have to be open to recalibrating ... Could be higher
    rates, could be same rates for longer." Sept 24: no progress, so higher
    rates.

Own language — the STANCE LABEL is gone:
  Aug 4: "I think we need policy that's mildly restrictive. And I think policy
          has been mildly restrictive."
  Sept 24: "Coming into the summer, I was asking myself whether policy was
          restrictive enough to deliver 2 percent inflation — or whether a
          somewhat higher federal funds rate might be needed." ... "were growth
          and the labor market consistent with some modest monetary policy
          restrictiveness?" ... "brings policy CLOSER to what I believe is
          needed."
  → "Mildly restrictive" — her label in every outlook communication since March
    6 — does NOT appear. "Closer to what is needed" says, in her own words, that
    policy before the hike was NOT restrictive enough and is still short of the
    required setting after it. That is the dissenters' July diagnosis.
  → She joins Warsh, Goolsbee and Barkin in not stating a stance label. The
    "mildly restrictive" bloc (Paulson, Collins, Daly, Waller) has lost its
    anchor member.

Own language — KEY (forward path):
  "Looking ahead, if conditions evolve as I expect, some modest further
   tightening may be warranted."
  → An explicit forward TIGHTENING lean from the median voter, conditional only
    on her own baseline ("if conditions evolve as I expect"). This is the
    base case, not a tail scenario. "Modest" bounds the size. It is a stronger
    path statement than the Chair gave on Sept 16 ("not in the forward guidance
    business") and parallels Barr Sept 23 ("further policy adjustments are
    likely to be needed").
  "returning inflation to 2 percent is non-negotiable, and I will support the
   policy path that gets us there while carefully weighing risks to the labor
   market along the way."
  → "Non-negotiable" is new, strong commitment language from Paulson. The labor
    clause is a guardrail, not a counterweight.

  Hike signal: PRESENT — base-case, forward-looking ("some modest further
               tightening may be warranted"). Upgraded from Aug 4's
               first-person conditional.
  Cut signal:  ABSENT.

Scenario framing: DESCRIPTIVE — a single baseline ("if conditions evolve as I
  expect") with no named alternative branch and no specified trigger data.

Shift vs. prior speech (Aug 4):  HAWKISH — conditional option ("could be higher
  rates") → executed hike plus a base-case lean toward more; "mildly
  restrictive" dropped; "non-negotiable" added.
Shift vs. pre-FOMC (Aug 4):      HAWKISH — same document.

SUMMARY
──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Net signal:    HAWKISH — the median voter now leans toward a second hike.

               Paulson explains her September vote as the Aug 4 conditional
               firing: underlying inflation made "little to no progress," so
               she chose higher rates over "same rates for longer." Her own
               underlying estimate rose from 2.4–2.8 to about 2.5–3 percent,
               and the "mild progress" she saw on Aug 4 is gone. She drops the
               "mildly restrictive" label she has used since March and says the
               hike only brings policy "closer to what I believe is needed."
               Her base case is "some modest further tightening."

               Labor is at maximum employment in her own words. Growth is
               "perhaps even strengthening." AI is now a direct source of
               inflation in her account. Nothing in the text argues for
               patience.

Position relative to committee:
  Paulson is the third official since the hike to lean toward more tightening,
  after Barr ("likely") and Barkin ("we'll see," "one talking-to might not be
  enough"). Goolsbee (Sept 21) left the path open but named no second move. She is the first of the "mildly
  restrictive" bloc to speak after the hike, and she left it. If the median
  voter sees a further modest hike as her baseline, the October 28 meeting is
  live. [Inference: October is live on her words alone. Whether it happens
  depends on the September and October data against her 2.5–3 range. She
  names no date.]

Language watch:
  • "some modest further tightening may be warranted" — base-case path lean
    from the median voter. Pairs with Barr's "further policy adjustments are
    likely to be needed" (Sept 23). Stage: EMERGING (two voters in two days).
  • "brings policy closer to what I believe is needed" — implicit admission
    that the pre-hike stance was not restrictive enough. Stage: DISPERSED.
  • "Underlying inflation is running in a range of about 2.5 to 3 percent" —
    the median voter's number, now higher. Stage: DISPERSED (only Paulson
    publishes a range).
  • "its effect on prices is not" (AI) — AI capex as a direct inflation source.
    Stage: EMERGING (Paulson, Hammack same day; Goolsbee Sept 21).
  • "non-negotiable" — commitment language. Stage: DISPERSED (first use).
  • "mildly restrictive" — DROPPED by its anchor user. Watch Collins, Daly and
    Waller. If they drop it too, the label has left the committee.

Analyst flag:  YES
  → (1) The median voter has a base-case lean toward "some modest further
    tightening." Is October 28 now a live meeting? She gives no date and no
    trigger. The next PCE release is the first data point against
    her new 2.5–3 range.
  → (2) The underlying range moved up from 2.4–2.8. Is this the same measure
    with new data, or a different basket? Aug 4 was spoken and approximate.
    Worth confirming the direction is not a measurement change.
  → (3) Her Aug 4 look-through-on-timing defense is not repeated. Does she
    still hold it for energy while treating AI as a demand shock? The text
    lists the Middle East conflict alongside AI as sources of growing
    pressure, which suggests look-through is no longer her operating rule.
