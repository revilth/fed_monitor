THE STRUCTURAL FACT — WHAT KIND OF DISSENT THIS IS
──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
  Apr 29:  dissented against the easing-bias LANGUAGE (with Hammack and Kashkari).
  Jul 29:  "I would have preferred to increase interest rates by one-quarter
           percentage point at this week's meeting."  → the RATE itself.

Logan is the one dissenter whose July vote was fully pre-announced: on July 16 she
had already stated hike advocacy as a current base case. The dissent is the
execution of a position taken in public 13 days earlier, not a new development —
which makes what CHANGED between the two documents the only interesting question.

GROWTH
──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
No standalone growth section. Growth appears once, folded into the policy-stance
argument:

  "Labor, consumption and financial market conditions indicate that monetary
   policy is not restraining the economy."
  → CARRY-OVER, near-verbatim from July 16: "The labor, consumption and financial
    data indicate that monetary policy is not restraining the economy." Same three
    categories, same conclusion. "data" → "conditions" is not a substantive edit.
    NOT a new claim and must not be scored as one.

  Dropped vs. July 16: the quantified financial-conditions evidence (equities
  +20% over the year, credit spreads "close to the tightest they've ever been")
  and the AI-productivity discussion are both absent. Compression, not retreat —
  the conclusion they supported is retained verbatim.

Shift vs. pre-FOMC (Jul 16):  UNCHANGED.

Direction: neutral-to-hawkish (unchanged from Jul 16).

──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
LABOR MARKET
──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
FOMC statement language echoed:
  "Job gains have kept pace with the workforce, and the unemployment rate has
   changed little." [Jul 29 stmt]
  → Logan does not echo it. She substitutes her own verdict.

Own verdict:
  "In addition to its inflation target, the FOMC has a mandate to achieve maximum
   employment; the job market is solid and perhaps strengthening a bit."
  → CARRY-OVER from July 16: "the labor market is well balanced and perhaps even
    strengthening a bit." The distinctive "perhaps ... strengthening a bit" clause
    is identical. "Well balanced" → "solid" is a marginal firming at most and
    should not be read as a directional shift on its own.
  → What IS different is the placement: on July 16 the labor verdict opened a
    section of its own; here it is a subordinate clause inside the sentence that
    names the employment mandate. Logan raises maximum employment precisely in
    order to dispose of it — the labor side is introduced as an obligation and
    answered in the same breath.

Dropped vs. July 16: the two-sided qualifier is gone. July 16 contained "companies
  are both slow to fire and slow to hire ... it's cold comfort for anyone who needs
  to find a new job. These are real difficulties, but not ones that easier monetary
  policy can fix." The acknowledgment of worker-level pain does not survive into
  the dissent statement, though its conclusion (easing is not the remedy) is
  implicitly retained.
  → [Inference: this is compression consistent with the genre rather than a change
    of view — she reached the same policy conclusion on July 16 with the qualifier
    present. Flagged as an inference, not scored as a hawkish shift.]

Shift vs. pre-FOMC (Jul 16):  UNCHANGED — same verdict, same distinctive clause,
  subordinated in position.

Direction: neutral-to-hawkish.

──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
INFLATION
──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
FOMC statement language echoed:
  "Inflation remains elevated relative to the Committee's 2 percent goal, in part
   reflecting supply shocks ..." [Jul 29 stmt]
  → Logan engages the supply-shock attribution head-on and dismisses it by
    concession (see "Even after accounting for" below) rather than, like Kashkari,
    by re-theorising it.

Path verdict — CARRY-OVER, now in the first sentence:
  "Inflation does not appear to be on course to sustainably achieve the Federal
   Open Market Committee's 2 percent target."
  and
  "inflation appears to be trending toward the mid-2's, not all the way to 2
   percent, and the risks are to the upside."
  → The "mid-2's, not all the way to 2 percent" forecast is now in its THIRD
    consecutive document — June 3, July 16, July 31 — in nearly identical words.
    This is the most durable individual forecast on the committee. NOT new.
    Its promotion to the opening sentence of the dissent is a framing choice: the
    forecast, not the vote, is presented as the reason.

Duration and social cost — CARRY-OVER:
  "More than five years after the post-pandemic surge, prices have continued to
   rise too rapidly. Every month of above-target inflation compounds the strain on
   the budgets of American families and businesses."
  → July 16: "Every month of above-target inflation has compounded the strain on
    Americans' budgets." Present tense now, and extended from households to "and
    businesses." A small widening; the argument is unchanged. The "more than five
    years" duration frame is shared with both other dissenters.

NEW — the concession-and-dismiss construction:
  "EVEN AFTER ACCOUNTING FOR PRODUCTIVITY GAINS AND TEMPORARY SUPPLY SHOCKS,
   inflation appears to be trending toward the mid-2's ..."
  → This clause is new. It pre-empts the two strongest arguments for holding —
    that AI productivity will disinflate, and that the energy shock is transitory
    — by granting both and asserting the forecast survives them anyway. On July 16
    she argued against the productivity case at length (uncertain timing, demand
    effects arrive first); here she simply nets it out. Rhetorically stronger and
    much shorter.

NEW — the unanticipated-shock rebuttal (the most substantive addition):
  "Without any policy restraint, inflation will likely continue to trend above
   target UNTIL THERE'S AN UNANTICIPATED SHOCK. The FOMC CANNOT COUNT ON
   UNANTICIPATED SHOCKS to achieve its goals and can always adjust policy if
   unanticipated shocks occur."
  → Genuinely new; no antecedent in July 16 or June 3. It names and refutes the
    implicit majority position — that something will come along to cool inflation
    without the Committee having to act — and characterises holding as relying on
    luck. The second half ("can always adjust policy if unanticipated shocks
    occur") is the reversibility argument, pre-empting the overtightening
    objection. Compare Kashkari's more explicit version of the same pre-emption.

Dropped vs. July 16: all of the measure-by-measure detail — core PCE 3.4%, Dallas
  Fed trimmed mean 2.4%, NY Fed multivariate core trend, market-based non-housing
  core services, the June CPI rebuttal ("one month of relief is not enough"), the
  Texas CEO wage anecdotes. The dissent statement asserts the conclusion those
  measures supported without restating them.

Shift vs. pre-FOMC (Jul 16):  UNCHANGED in direction, SHARPENED in construction.
  Same forecast, same risk skew; the new material is defensive (pre-empting the
  productivity, transitory-shock and overtightening objections) rather than an
  escalation of the inflation read itself.

Direction: HAWKISH.

──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
MONETARY POLICY
──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
FOMC statement language echoed:
  "The Committee will deliver price stability." [Jul 29 stmt]
  → Not quoted. Logan instead reaches past the statement to the framework document
    (see below) — a more binding reference.

Own language — the vote:
  "To better balance the outlook and risks for the Fed's dual mandate goals, I
   would have preferred to increase interest rates by one-quarter percentage point
   at this week's meeting."
  → CARRY-OVER of the operative formulation, now with a magnitude. July 16: "I
    currently believe modestly higher interest rates would better balance the
    outlook and risks for the FOMC's maximum employment and price stability
    goals." The "better balance the outlook and risks ... dual mandate" phrasing is
    hers across both documents. What is new is only the quantity — "modestly" →
    "one-quarter percentage point" — and the tense.

Cost-of-waiting — CARRY-OVER:
  "Modest action in the near term would reduce the likelihood of needing to take
   sharper action later."
  → July 16: "Better modest restriction now than severe restriction later." Same
    argument, restated in flatter prose. Shared near-verbatim with Hammack and
    Kashkari on the same day.

NEW — the framework invocation (institutional escalation):
  "The FOMC's policy framework, as described in the statement on longer-run goals
   and monetary policy strategy, ALSO CALLS FOR A BALANCED APPROACH to our
   Congressionally established price stability and maximum employment mandates."
  → New, and the most consequential addition in the document. Logan grounds the
    dissent in the Committee's own standing framework rather than in her forecast,
    which converts "I read the data differently" into "the majority's decision is
    in tension with our stated strategy." She also invokes Congress. This is a
    materially higher-stakes register than anything in her July 16 speech, and it
    is the sort of argument that is designed to be quoted back in the minutes.

GONE — the committee-humility close:
  July 16 ended: "Neither I nor any other single person makes monetary policy on
  their own ... I'll bring my best evidence, analysis and arguments ... I may
  persuade them, they may persuade me." That passage was the reason the July 16
  score treated her hike advocacy as positioning-to-persuade rather than a dissent
  guarantee.
  → It has no counterpart on July 31. There is no collegiality close at all —
    a notable contrast with Hammack, whose closing formula ("a wide range of
    viewpoints is critical to any deliberative process") is standard for her, and
    which Logan does not offer. She did not persuade them, and the statement does
    not soften the disagreement.

  Hike signal:  PRESENT AND EXECUTED, magnitude named (25bp), meeting named.
                No path beyond this meeting — "near term" is as far as she goes.
  Cut signal:   ABSENT.

Scenario framing (per CLAUDE.md precision rule): DESCRIPTIVE. No named scenarios;
  the closest thing is the unanticipated-shock passage, which is a rebuttal rather
  than a scenario with an attached policy response.

Shift vs. pre-FOMC (Jul 16):  HAWKISH in execution, UNCHANGED in stated position.
  The July 16 advocacy became a vote; the escape hatch she had left herself
  ("they may persuade me") was not used and is not renewed.

Direction: HAWKISH.

──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
SUMMARY
──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Net signal:    HAWKISH — but this is CONFIRMATION, not escalation, and the
               distinction matters for forecasting. Logan's substantive position
               is where it was on July 16; the news is that she executed it.
               (1) Hike advocacy → recorded dissent, with 25bp named;
               (2) The "mid-2's, not all the way to 2 percent" forecast now runs
                   three consecutive documents unchanged — the committee's most
                   durable individual forecast;
               (3) NEW: "cannot count on unanticipated shocks" — names and refutes
                   the hold case as reliance on luck, with reversibility built in;
               (4) NEW: the framework document invoked — recasting the dissent as
                   a fidelity-to-strategy argument rather than a forecast dispute;
               (5) The July 16 "they may persuade me" humility close is gone.
               Most of the rest is verbatim or near-verbatim carry-over and
               carries no independent signal.

Position relative to committee:
  Logan remains the hawkish anchor and is the only dissenter who had publicly
  pre-committed to the position before the meeting. Her statement is the most
  institutionally framed of the three: Hammack argues from district evidence,
  Kashkari from doctrine and history, Logan from the Committee's own strategy
  document. Like Hammack and unlike Kashkari, she asks for one move, not a series.

Language watch:
  • "cannot count on unanticipated shocks to achieve its goals" — the cleanest
    one-line attack on the hold position produced this cycle. Stage: DISPERSED
    (first use). Watch for uptake by Hammack or in the minutes; it is quotable
    enough to travel.
  • "the statement on longer-run goals and monetary policy strategy ... calls for
    a balanced approach" — framework-fidelity argument. Stage: DISPERSED, but
    high-consequence: if a second dissenter adopts it, the disagreement is being
    escalated from a policy judgment to a question about whether the Committee is
    following its own strategy. Track closely.
  • "trending toward the mid-2's, not all the way to 2 percent" — third
    consecutive document. Stage: DISPERSED but entrenched as Logan's signature.
    The proposition (though not the words) is now shared with Hammack's "not
    confident it will return to our objective on its own."
  • "modest action ... rather than sharper action later" — cost-of-waiting, shared
    near-verbatim with both other dissenters. Stage: COMMITTEE-WIDE WITHIN THE
    DISSENT BLOC; absent from the statement. This is the bloc's common template
    and the most likely candidate to surface in the Aug 19 minutes.

Analyst flag:  YES
  → (1) The framework invocation is the escalation to watch, not the vote. Logan
    is arguing the majority is out of step with the Committee's own strategy
    statement — a qualitatively different claim from "I forecast differently," and
    one that is harder for the Chair to absorb quietly. Worth deciding whether
    this reads as a genuine institutional escalation or as boilerplate framework
    citation before it is treated as the former.
  → (2) Logan's substantive view did NOT move between July 16 and July 31. Any
    read that treats the dissent statement as fresh hawkish information would be
    double-counting a position already priced from July 16. The new information is
    Hammack's and Kashkari's, whose pre-FOMC baselines were June 2 and May 13.
  → (3) She asks for one move and stops. Kashkari asks for a series. Whether the
    bloc converges on a path is the September question, and nothing in Logan's
    statement resolves it.
