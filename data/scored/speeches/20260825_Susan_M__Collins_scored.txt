GROWTH
──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
FOMC statement language echoed:
  "economic activity has been expanding at a solid pace"
  [July 29 stmt, verbatim from June 17; activity paragraph FROZEN]
  → Collins: "I see recent economic activity as expanding at a
    near-trend pace"
  → "Near-trend" is a subtle step down from the statement's "solid."
    May 13 used "resilient." Collins has softened the modifier by
    one level.

Own language:
  "I see recent economic activity as expanding at a near-trend pace"
  — Positive but bounded: growth is adequate but not a policy concern.

  [AI demand channel, raised in the inflation section]:
  "the AI build-out appears to be putting upward pressure on core
   goods inflation" — implies AI-capex investment is generating
   demand-side goods inflation. Growth has an AI-driven momentum
   component that Collins views as inflationary, not just productive.

Shift vs. prior speech:   NEUTRAL LEAN — "near-trend" is softer than
  May 13's "resilient," but growth remains a non-concern for policy.
Shift vs. pre-FOMC:       NEUTRAL — directionally unchanged; the growth
  characterization has not moved in a way that motivates a policy signal.

LABOR MARKET
──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
FOMC statement language echoed:
  "Job gains have been low, on average, and the unemployment rate
   remains low." [July 29 stmt, verbatim from June 17; FROZEN]
  → Collins: "unemployment rate just above 4 percent and fluctuating
    within a narrow range since mid-2024"; "Layoffs continue to be
    limited"; "hiring has picked up relative to late 2025 and become
    somewhat more broad-based across industries"
  → Her read is more constructive than the statement's "job gains
    have been low." She emphasizes hiring improvement and low layoffs.

Own verdict:
  "I see labor market conditions as broadly consistent with maximum
   or full employment, one part of the Fed's dual mandate."
  — Most positive Collins labor characterization in 2026. May 13
    led with "labor market has clearly softened"; Aug 25 leads with
    "maximum or full employment."

Own qualifier / addition:
  "I view risks to the labor market as two-sided."
  — Upside risk: stronger-than-expected growth → tighter labor →
    more price pressure.
  — Downside risks: (a) AI-related stock-market repricing → reduced
    business and consumer spending → lower labor demand; (b) AI
    displacing (not complementing) labor in affected sectors;
    (c) low job-finding rate in low-hire, low-fire environment →
    potential discouraged-worker exit from labor force.
  — "I see labor market conditions as broadly balanced – although
    it is an unusual balance and not without risks."
  — "Unusual balance" framing retained from May 13. The downside
    risk framing has not disappeared, but it is now paired with
    explicit upside labor risks, moderating the net read.

Shift vs. prior speech:   NEUTRAL — "unusual balance" retained; risks
  shifted from downside-skewed (May 13) to explicitly two-sided.
  Hiring improvement noted positively. Net: mild hawkish lean within
  the labor section.
Shift vs. pre-FOMC:       HAWKISH LEAN — May 13 led with softened labor
  framing and AI skill-mismatch downside. Aug 25 leads with "maximum
  or full employment" and two-sided risks. The labor channel no longer
  supports a patience argument for holding.

INFLATION
──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
FOMC statement language echoed:
  "Inflation remains elevated relative to the Committee's 2 percent
   goal." [July 29 stmt, verbatim from June 17; FROZEN]
  → Collins: "inflation still too high" + "running above the FOMC's
    2 percent target for over five years"
  → "Still too high" reinforces "elevated"; duration emphasis ("over
    five years") is Collins' own addition, not in the statement.

Own language — KEY:
  "with inflation still too high, I remain particularly concerned
   about the price stability portion of the dual mandate."
  — Price-stability-first framing leads the piece.

  "In the coming months, I will be looking for evidence that
   inflation is durably returning to 2 percent, consistent with my
   modal, or most likely, outlook for the remainder of this year."
  — Modal scenario is conditional on: (1) limited additional tariff
    increases; (2) some degree of Hormuz reopening. Neither is assumed.

  Three stated disinflation factors:
  (1) "A balanced labor market, with economic activity growing near
      trend, should not be a source of additional price pressures."
  (2) "mildly restrictive monetary policy, together with the recent
      rise in longer-term interest rates, should mitigate, at least
      to some extent, a possible re-acceleration in household and
      business spending"
  (3) "solid productivity growth – if it continues – should put some
      downward pressure on prices"
  — NEW ELEMENT: "the recent rise in longer-term interest rates"
    named as a co-tightening factor alongside the policy rate. Echoes
    Warsh's July 29 framing that higher bond yields are a credibility
    signal, not a constraint.

  "upside risks to inflation from both additional adverse supply
   shocks, and a stronger-than-expected pace of economic activity.
   With regards to the latter, I'll note that the AI build-out
   appears to be putting upward pressure on core goods inflation."
  — NEW: AI demand channel named explicitly for goods inflation.
    Aligns with Waller's July 13 analysis (chips/servers/memory →
    retail goods). First Collins use of this channel.

  "While June and July inflation reports were mildly encouraging,
   monthly readings can be volatile. It remains to be seen whether
   the recent improvements will be sustained"
  — "Mildly encouraging" is the most positive inflation data language
    Collins has used in 2026. Heavily qualified ("volatile," "remains
    to be seen").

Shift vs. prior speech:   UNCHANGED — May 13's explicit look-through
  fatigue ("five years has reduced my patience for looking through")
  is not repeated verbatim, but the duration concern is present.
  "Mildly encouraging" data acknowledgment is slightly dovish; AI
  demand channel is hawkish offset. Net: broadly unchanged on
  inflation.
Shift vs. pre-FOMC:       UNCHANGED — Same structural concern over
  above-target duration. May 13 was explicit on look-through fatigue;
  Aug 25 reframes this as conditional: "maintaining the current
  federal funds rate target range will require continued evidence."

MONETARY POLICY
──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
FOMC statement language echoed:
  No forward guidance in July 29 statement. Collins does not echo
  any cut-oriented language. "Well positioned" is her own
  construction, not verbatim statement language.

Own language:
  "I was comfortable with July's FOMC decision to maintain the
   current target range for the federal funds rate."
  — Non-voter explicitly validating the hold vote.

  "For now, this mildly restrictive policy stance should leave the
   Committee well positioned to address evolving economic conditions
   and return inflation to target in a reasonable amount of time."
  — Rate characterization: "mildly restrictive" (same as May 13's
    "slightly restrictive" — stable descriptor in Collins' vocabulary).
  — "In a reasonable amount of time" is her inflation-return bar.

  "maintaining the current federal funds rate target range will
   require continued evidence that inflation is indeed coming down."
  — Hold is explicitly conditional on evidence. Without it, the hold
    is not justified.

  "Should evidence of sustained inflation progress not materialize,
   I believe it will be appropriate to tighten policy soon to ensure
   we deliver price stability in a reasonable time frame."
  — HAWKISH ESCALATION vs. May 13. Comparison:
    May 13: "I can also envision a scenario that requires some policy
            tightening" — hypothetical framing
    Aug 25: "I believe it will be appropriate to tighten policy soon"
            — pre-commitment + time dimension ("soon")
  — Upgrade: (a) from "I can envision" to "I believe it will be
    appropriate" (from hypothetical to pre-commitment); (b) "soon"
    introduces urgency absent from May 13.

  "I particularly value the range of perspectives expressed around
   the FOMC table. I look forward to the on-going robust, substantive,
   and data-driven discussion with my fellow Committee members"
  — Diplomatic nod to the 3-dissenter dynamic; confirms she is not
    calling for the immediate hike the dissenters preferred.

Cut signal status:  ABSENT
Hike signal status: CONDITIONAL, TIME-PRESSURED.
  [Inference: "tighten policy soon" if evidence does not materialize
  positions Collins within the hold-but-primed camp, directionally
  aligned with Hammack/Kashkari/Logan without joining their immediate-
  hike preference. Given she is a non-voter, her influence is
  informational — but high-quality non-voters (Collins, Goolsbee)
  signal where rotating-voter sentiment may be heading.]

Shift vs. prior speech:   HAWKISH — May 13 named tightening scenario
  in hypothetical terms. Aug 25 pre-commits with a time dimension
  ("soon"). This is a meaningful escalation in urgency.
Shift vs. pre-FOMC:       HAWKISH — The arc from March 6 (neutral-
  patient) → May 13 (conditional tightening scenario) → Aug 25
  (time-pressured pre-commitment) is consistently hawkish.

SUMMARY
──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Net signal:    HAWKISH LEAN
  Primary escalation is in monetary policy: "tighten policy soon"
  is a pre-commitment upgrade from May 13's conditional "I can
  envision a scenario." Labor improved ("maximum or full employment"
  vs. "clearly softened"), removing the dovish offset that existed
  in May. Inflation is broadly unchanged with a new AI demand channel
  adding to upside risk. "Mildly encouraging" June/July data note is
  the only dovish element, heavily qualified.

  Published the day before Jackson Hole (Aug 27–29), this piece sets
  Collins' public posture ahead of Warsh's keynote (Aug 28). Her
  framing — comfortable with the July hold, but "soon" if progress
  stalls — is consistent with a September tightening if upcoming data
  disappoint. Her acknowledgment of "the recent rise in longer-term
  interest rates" as a co-tightening factor (echoing Warsh July 29)
  indicates committee-level language propagation.

Language watch:
  • "tighten policy soon" — first Collins use of time-pressured
    tighten language. Language cycle stage: EMERGING (dissenters in
    hike camp; Collins now in conditional-soon camp from non-voter
    bench). Watch for spread to Just Voter ranks.
  • "mildly encouraging" re data — most positive data characterization
    in 2026 from Collins. If repeated post-Jackson-Hole, track shift.
  • "AI build-out appears to be putting upward pressure on core goods
    inflation" — AI demand channel for goods. DISPERSED (Waller Jul 13,
    Collins Aug 25). Watch for Troika adoption.
  • "the recent rise in longer-term interest rates" as tightening co-
    factor — echoes Warsh July 29. EMERGING downward spread from Chair.

Analyst flag:  YES
  → Collins pre-commits to "tighten policy soon" if sustained
    progress does not materialize. By her own read, June and July
    were only "mildly encouraging" — a bar that may not stay met
    through September data. This piece was timed for maximum impact
    ahead of Jackson Hole. Track whether Warsh's Aug 28 keynote
    echoes, softens, or clarifies the "soon" timeline. If Warsh does
    not push back on the tightening-soon framing, September becomes
    a live hike meeting, and Collins would be among the first non-
    dissenter voices to have put that in writing.
