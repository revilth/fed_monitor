GROWTH
──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
FOMC statement language echoed:
  "Economic activity is expanding at a solid pace" [Jun 17 stmt]
  → Williams: "Growth in the economy is solid and on trend."

Own language:
  Real GDP: "around 2 percent to 2.25 percent this year and over the next two
  years." Growth running "modestly above its potential rate of 2 percent."
  Unemployment expected to "edge down gradually to 4 percent in 2028."
  → Identical forecast to June 25 and consistent with May 4. The growth
    path is stable: slightly above potential, AI-investment-led.

Shift vs. prior speech (June 25):  unchanged — same "solid," "around 2.25%,"
  AI-investment-led framing; no directional change.
Shift vs. pre-FOMC (May 4):        unchanged — consistent across three speeches.

LABOR MARKET
──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
FOMC statement language echoed:
  "the unemployment rate has changed little" / "kept pace with the workforce"
  [Jun 17 stmt]
  → Williams: "The labor market is likewise solid and stable." Unemployment
    edging toward 4.0% by 2028.

Own verdict — NOT a source of inflation pressure:
  "we are not seeing evidence that the labor market is adding to inflationary
   pressures." [Teller Window; direct quote]
  → Consistent with June 25: "labor market not compounding the supply-shock
    inflation." This is Williams's stable formulation — labor is a non-issue
    for inflation, a holding formula that removes the labor leg as a two-sided
    risk driver.

Inflation expectations anchor:
  Inflation expectations "well-anchored" — giving the Fed "policy breathing room."
  → Maintained from June 25 ("medium-term inflation expectations … remained
    well anchored").

Shift vs. prior speech (June 25):  unchanged — "solid and stable"; labor not
  adding inflation pressure; anchored expectations. June 25's "largely
  stabilized, albeit at low levels" language has upgraded to "solid and
  stable" — a minor tonal improvement, not a directional shift.
Shift vs. pre-FOMC (May 4):        HAWKISH LEAN (carried forward from June 25) —
  May 4's "increasing labor market slack" soft-data concern is gone; "solid
  and stable" is the new holding formula.

INFLATION
──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
FOMC statement language echoed:
  "Inflation remains elevated relative to the Committee's 2 percent goal,
   in part reflecting supply shocks including energy" [Jun 17 stmt]
  → Williams: "Inflation is unquestionably too high" at approximately
    3.7 percent (or stated as "about 4 percent" — sources vary on whether
    he uses headline or core; June CPI/PPI context: headline PCE likely
    ~3.7%, with core lower; the "about 4%" may refer to a broader or
    earlier reading).
  Three drivers: (1) tariffs on imported goods; (2) Middle East energy/
    supply disruptions; (3) robust AI investment demand for technology goods
    and electricity. Identical three-driver framework to June 25.

Own language — KEY DOVISH SIGNAL (NEW vs. June 25):
  "There are encouraging reasons to expect that inflation has peaked and
   should edge down in coming quarters."
  → "PEAKED" is new language not present in June 25. June 25 had "I expect
    inflation readings to edge down" — a forward path. July 15 adds "peaked"
    — claiming the high point has already passed. This is a more confident
    disinflation claim. After the benign June CPI (core flat MoM) and June
    PPI (-0.3%, core +0.2%, also released July 15 morning), Williams is
    calling the peak explicitly.

Glide path (updated):
  Expected to "decline to approximately 3.25 percent by year-end 2026"
  → June 25 forecast was "3-1/2 percent by year-end" — downward revision of
    0.25 percentage points for 2026. The benign June CPI/PPI data allowed
    Williams to revise the near-term path dovish. Then glide toward 2% in
    2027 and "land on target" in 2028 — timeline unchanged from June 25.

Shift vs. prior speech (June 25):  DOVISH — "peaked" is new and stronger than
  June 25's "edge down"; year-end 2026 forecast revised down 0.25pp (3.5%→3.25%).
  Both reflect the June CPI/PPI prints incorporated into his assessment.
Shift vs. pre-FOMC (May 4):        MIXED, net mild dovish — "peaked" call is
  new; May 4 had "inflation risks tilted to the upside" with no peak claim;
  but return-to-2% timeline slips (2027→2028) from May 4. June 25 bridged
  the two; July 15 adds the peak claim.

MONETARY POLICY
──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
FOMC statement language echoed:
  "The Committee decided to maintain the target range … 3-1/2 to 3-3/4
   percent … in support of the Federal Reserve's dual mandate." [Jun 17 stmt]
  → Williams: "The current stance of monetary policy is well positioned
    to do that." [Note: this is his identical formulation from June 25.]

Own language:
  "well positioned" — stable Troika holding formula; unchanged from June 25.
  No cut signal. No hike signal. No forward guidance.
  "my views, as always, will be based on the evolution of the totality of
   the data, the economic outlook, and the balance of risks."
  → Pure data-dependence closing formula; unchanged.

NOTABLE ABSENCE: Williams does NOT echo Cook's "prepared to act" conditionality
  or Waller's near-term tightening consideration (Jul 13). He maintains
  the patient "well positioned" framing while asserting inflation has "peaked"
  — i.e., the "peaked" call functions as his justification for why the
  current rate is sufficient, not as a dovish pivot signal.

Cut signal status: ABSENT — unchanged from June 25 and June 17 Q&A. Williams
  provides the disinflation glide path without attaching rate-cut timing to it.
Hike signal: ABSENT — "well positioned" explicitly rejects the need for
  additional action in the near term.

Shift vs. prior speech (June 25):  unchanged on stance — patient hold, "well
  positioned," no guidance. "Peaked" call and revised year-end forecast are
  informational updates, not stance changes.
Shift vs. pre-FOMC (May 4):        unchanged on stance.
