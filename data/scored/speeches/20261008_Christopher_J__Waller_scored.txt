GROWTH
──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
FOMC statement language echoed:
  "Economic activity is expanding at a solid pace despite elevated uncertainty
   ... domestic spending has been resilient" [Sep 16 stmt, new phrase]
  → Waller: "With evidence that economic activity is strengthening in the
    second half of this year, I am not greatly concerned that tighter monetary
    policy threatens a damaging slowdown in the economy."

Own language:
  "With evidence that economic activity is strengthening in the second half
   of this year, I am not greatly concerned that tighter monetary policy
   threatens a damaging slowdown in the economy." — growth provides the cover
   for tightening; it removes the brake on further hikes. Stronger framing
   than Sep 3's "solid pace" — "strengthening" is directional, not level.

  Rationale for 2025 cuts vs. current pivot: "When the FOMC cut rates from
   September through December 2025, we did so with inflation fairly close to
   our 2 percent target, after accounting for tariff effects... At the same
   time, there was significant evidence of a weakening labor market." The
   switch: by 2026 the labor market "stabilized, while progress on inflation
   stalled."

Shift vs. prior speech (Sep 3):   UNCHANGED — Sep 3 had GDP 2%, PDFP 3%,
  wealth-effect sustaining consumption, AI investment not discounted. Oct 8
  carries no new data but restates "strengthening" directionally. Growth is
  still the permissive backdrop for further tightening.
Shift vs. pre-FOMC (Sep 3):       Same comparison — unchanged.

LABOR MARKET

  "Job gains have kept pace with the workforce, and the unemployment rate has

   [Sep 16 stmt]
  → Waller: "the labor market continued to be solid and stable in September.
    The unemployment rate remains relatively low and near the median of
    policymakers' projections of its longer-run level, while payroll gains are

Own verdict:

   and stable in September" — identical register to "satisfactory" in Sep 3.
   Payroll gains "down overall" but the breakeven-framing mechanism from Sep 3

   "policy will be focused on the inflation side of our mandate." This is the

Shift vs. prior speech (Sep 3):   UNCHANGED — same "solid and stable" verdict;

INFLATION
──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
FOMC statement language echoed:
  "Inflation remains elevated. Today's policy action will support a timelier
   return to the Committee's 2 percent goal." [Sep 16 stmt — supply-shock
   attribution DELETED vs. Jul 29]
  → Waller: "August inflation data... showed monthly core PCE inflation of
    0.25 percent and the 12-month change at 3 percent... not showing
    sufficient progress." He echoes "timelier return" explicitly: "anticipate
    additional hikes to support a timelier return of inflation to our 2 percent
    goal."

Own language — KEY (decision rationale for September hike):
  "Three forces that undermined progress":
  (1) "hopes for a quick ending to the Middle East conflict faded, and experts
      warned that low inventories and damaged infrastructure could keep oil
      prices high through 2027"
  (2) "evidence mounted that the artificial intelligence buildout was
      significantly driving up high-tech consumer prices, and projections for
      the size of that buildout ballooned"
  (3) "continuing trade conflicts threatened new tariffs that could put upward
      pressure on inflation yet again"
  → These are the three persistent forces he names as swamping the "fleeting
    signs of progress." The AI buildout is now an inflation source — same as
    Sep 3's upside risk, but more definitively framed.

  "When the first inflation reading for August came in hot just before the
   FOMC's September meeting, it was impossible to deny that inflation was
   still too high and not making sufficient progress toward our target."
  → The hot August CPI is stated as the trigger. He explicitly rebuts the
    narrative that this was one-data-point-dependent: "it was the culmination
    of factors that developed over the past year."

  Current state: "August inflation data... showed monthly core PCE inflation
   of 0.25 percent and the 12-month change at 3 percent... Looking at the
   history of 12-month core inflation, it has been between roughly 2.5 percent
   and 3.0 percent since the spring of 2024. This is obviously higher than we
   want, above our target, and not showing sufficient progress."

  Expectations concern (new emphasis vs. Sep 3):
  "I am concerned that the recent acceleration in inflation—after what soon
   will be five and a half years of it above the FOMC's target—will lead
   consumers, investors, and price-setting businesses to revise up their
   expectations for future inflation." — Duration-framed expectations risk,
   more prominently stated than Sep 3. Sep 3 named expectations as a risk
   to "acknowledge" if data reversed; Oct 8 names it as an active concern NOW.

Comparison with Sep 3 — REVERSAL of the disinflation read:
  Sep 3: "signs of disinflation," three-month core 4.76%→3.05% "encouraging,"
    nonmarket services flattering core, pending Commerce revision "a few tenths."
  Oct 8: August PCE 0.25%/3.0% 12m, "not showing sufficient progress."
  The three-month series is not cited; the pending revision is not mentioned.
  The Sep 3 hold branch ("continued progress") did not materialize.

Shift vs. prior speech (Sep 3):   HAWKISH — The Sep 3 disinflation signal did
  not survive the August data. "Not showing sufficient progress" replaces "signs
  of disinflation." Expectations concern elevated from contingent risk to active
  concern. The three-month-core momentum measure and the nonmarket-services
  adjustment (the two novel items in Sep 3) are both silently dropped.
Shift vs. pre-FOMC (Sep 3):       Same comparison — hawkish.

MONETARY POLICY
──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
FOMC statement language echoed:
  "Today's policy action will support a timelier return to the Committee's 2
   percent goal." [Sep 16 stmt]
  → Waller: "I anticipate additional hikes to support a timelier return of
    inflation to our 2 percent goal." — Verbatim adoption of "timelier return."

Own language — KEY:
  "If the economic data continue to come in as expected, I anticipate additional
   hikes to support a timelier return of inflation to our 2 percent goal. But
   there is some flexibility about when those hikes will occur. The hikes do
   not need to come at consecutive meetings, but they should be in place in an
   acceptable period of time."
  → "Additional hikes" (plural) is now the base case, conditioned on "as
    expected." This is a materially firmer posture than Sep 3's "I would be
    inclined to support holding... if inflation comes in hot, I would consider
    a rate hike." The conditional has resolved: September was the hot print;
    more hikes are anticipated.

  "For at least the near term, policy will be focused on the inflation side of
   our mandate." — Asymmetric focus stated explicitly; labor is out of the
   decision framework for now.

SEP signaling framework (the special topic):
  Waller argues the SEP functions as a "signaling option" — communicating the
  likely terminal direction/quantum without committing to pace or meeting-level
  decisions. He presents three options: silence (too volatile), full forward
  guidance (too rigid), and the signaling option (SEP + speeches, the actual
  approach).

  Key SEP facts he cites:
  "16 of the 18 participants who submitted dots anticipated at least one more
   hike during the two remaining meetings this year. Four of those 16 expected
   two additional hikes. ...eight participants expect that rates will be 50
   basis points higher at the end of 2027 than they are today."

  Market interpretation as of Oct 7: "85 percent chance of at least one hike
   by the end of the FOMC's December meeting and nearly 20 percent chance of
   two hikes. And, by the March 2027 meeting, markets see nearly an 80 percent
   chance of at least two hikes and a 33 percent chance of three or more hikes."

  Analytical note on SEP defense: This is a DIRECT DEFENSE of the SEP's
  usefulness at a moment when Chair Warsh has signaled SEP reform ("for a
  short time, at the very least," Jul 1; withheld his own dot twice; footnote
  citing anti-dot paper in his Jackson Hole text). Waller argues it is
  "serving that signaling role... The SEP and policymakers' outlook speeches
  that encompass their interpretation of additional economic data" explain why
  markets are correctly pricing the hike path. He does not address whether the
  Chair will reform it.

No stance label appears. "Only slightly restricting aggregate demand" from
Sep 3 is NOT repeated. The path argument is framed as "removed a dose of
accommodation" (the Chair's Sep 16 framing) is not used either — Waller does
not characterize whether 3.75–4.00% is restrictive.

Cut signal status:  ABSENT (no cut discussion in any form).
Hike signal status: PRESENT — explicit base case; "anticipate additional hikes"
  conditioned on data coming in "as expected"; timing flexible (not consecutive).

Shift vs. prior speech (Sep 3):   HAWKISH — Sep 3: hold is the stated inclination;
  hike is a named branch requiring "hot" August data. Oct 8: the September hike
  has been delivered; additional hikes are now the base case. The transition from
  conditional to anticipated is the key move.
Shift vs. pre-FOMC (Sep 3):       Same comparison — hawkish.

SUMMARY
──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Net signal:    HAWKISH LEAN (vs. Sep 3; vs. the committee's current center)
               Waller's first post-September-16 speech confirms the hike was
               driven by a "preponderance of evidence over several months," not
               a single print. The three-month disinflation trend he cited in
               Sep 3 did not survive the hot August data (0.25% monthly core
               PCE; 3.0% 12m). Additional hikes are now the anticipated base
               case, conditioned on data "as expected." Timing is flexible
               ("not consecutive meetings"), but the "acceptable period of time"
               qualifier prevents indefinite deferral.

               The SEP framework section is both analytically substantive and
               politically pointed: by arguing the SEP is functioning as
               designed ("signaling option"), he implicitly defends the tool
               against the Chair's reform signals. The markets-are-reading-it-
               correctly argument (85% for one hike by December) works only if
               the SEP continues publishing useful dots.

               Position relative to the committee: his "additional hikes"
               framing matches the September SEP median (two more to ~4.25%–4.50%
               through 2026), and is consistent with the Paulson (Sep 24),
               Barkin (Sep 22), and Schmid (Sep 18) post-hike endorsements.
               All four name a second hike as a base-case or likely path. Logan
               and Hammack likely lean harder; Goolsbee's show-me-state caveat
               is softer.

Language watch:
  • "anticipate additional hikes... not consecutive meetings, but... acceptable
    period of time" — most explicit near-term hike path of any post-September-16
    speaker. Stage: DISPERSED (first use of this specific formulation).
  • "timelier return of inflation to our 2 percent goal" — VERBATIM ADOPTION of
    the September 16 statement phrase; confirms it is committee-level language,
    not just the Chair's. Stage: IN-STATEMENT (committee-wide).
  • "five and a half years" duration clock — UNCHANGED from Sep 3; also in
    Barkin Sep 22 ("five-year overshoot"). Stage: SPREADING (Waller, Barkin;
    watch for Logan and Paulson to use it).
  • "SEP serving that signaling role" / defense of the dot plot — Stage:
    DISPERSED (first explicit defense of the SEP as a mechanism). Directly
    counters Warsh's sunset signal. Watch for other officials to echo or avoid.
  • "impossible to deny" (re hot August print) — Stage: DISPERSED (first use).
    The idiom frames the September hike as evidence-forced, not discretionary.

Analyst flag:  YES
  → (1) "Additional hikes... not consecutive... acceptable period of time":
    this is the most explicit near-term path statement of any post-FOMC speaker.
    Does "acceptable period of time" signal the October 27–28 meeting is
    under consideration, or only December? Markets price 85% for one by December
    (not October) — verify whether Waller's formulation differs from that.
  → (2) SEP defense vs. Warsh reform signal: Waller argues the SEP is "serving
    that signaling role" well. Warsh has withheld his dot twice and signaled
    potential reform. This is the most direct public counter to the Chair on
    communications infrastructure. Watch whether other voters echo Waller's
    defense or stay silent.
  → (3) The Sep 3 three-month momentum measure (4.76%→3.05%) and the nonmarket-
    services adjustment — both items he used in Sep 3 to argue "underlying
    inflation is doing better" — are completely absent from Oct 8. The Aug PCE
    data evidently negated them. Verify: what were the updated three-month
    figures for August?
  → (4) The foreign audience context (Istanbul Economic Forum, Central Bank of
    Türkiye) is relevant: the international spillover from a US hiking cycle is
    a natural political pressure point, and Waller explicitly defends the path.
    No unusual deference in the text — treat it as a domestic policy speech.
