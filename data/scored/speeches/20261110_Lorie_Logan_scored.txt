GROWTH
──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Brief (growth is the permissive backdrop; not a primary policy concern for Logan).

FOMC statement language echoed:
  "Economic activity is expanding at a solid pace despite elevated uncertainty...
   domestic spending has been resilient." [Sep 16 stmt]
  → Logan: "Economic growth is strong. Real gross domestic product (GDP) increased
    at an annual rate of 3.4 percent in the third quarter. And the Dallas Fed's
    Weekly Economic Index is consistent with real GDP continuing to expand at around
    a 3 percent annual rate."

Own language:
  Two pillars: (1) consumer spending "solid, especially among wealthier households"
  that benefited from stock market gains; (2) business investment "robust" — real
  business investment in equipment +14% Q3 YoY; computers/peripherals (AI data
  centers) >80% of that growth; broadening into industrial machinery, semiconductor
  mfg equipment, gas turbines; defense/manufacturers ramping up. "Technological
  innovation and changes in global trade and security alliances appear poised to
  continue driving strong investment in coming years."
  — The broadening-investment narrative is an upgrade from prior Logan speeches.
    Growth providing strong cover for continued tightening.

Shift vs. pre-FOMC (Jul 31):  HAWKISH LEAN — Jul 31 had growth subsumed into
  the policy stance argument ("not restraining the economy"). Nov 10 gives full
  sectoral growth analysis; explicitly calls growth "strong" and investment
  "robust." The upgrade in texture, not in direction.

LABOR MARKET
──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
FOMC statement language echoed:
  "Job gains have kept pace with the workforce, and the unemployment rate has
   changed little. The labor market is consistent with maximum employment."
   [Sep 16 stmt]
  → Logan: "A growing economy creates jobs, and the labor market is balanced and
    even tightening a bit. Unemployment continues to hover just above 4 percent.
    That is down slightly from late last year and close to most estimates of the
    lowest sustainable level."

Own verdict:
  "My assessment is that the economy is achieving maximum employment and, supported
   by strong aggregate demand, remains on track to continue to do so."
  → Directly echoes the Sep 16 statement's "consistent with maximum employment."
    "Tightening a bit" is her signature qualifier — present since July 16 ("well
    balanced and perhaps even strengthening a bit"). Here downgraded from
    "strengthening" to "tightening a bit," marginally softer word choice.

Own qualifier / addition (new):
  "Today's labor market is not especially dynamic. Rapid technological change and
   geopolitical volatility have increased employers' uncertainty... It's a tough
   market for anyone seeking employment."
  → This "low dynamism" observation directly parallels Waller's "low hire, low
    fire" analysis in his simultaneous Nov 10 speech. Both Just Voters are raising
    the same structural concern on the same day — appears coordinated in timing or
    reflects shared awareness of the same data pattern.
  → Logan's policy disposal: "Monetary policy, however, addresses cyclical
    mismatches between supply and demand, not structural challenges such as
    employers' uncertainties." She explicitly quarantines the concern from
    near-term policy, as Waller does.
  → "Latest data show hints of increased dynamism, including more job openings and
    more workers quitting their jobs." — The hint of re-acceleration is noted.

Shift vs. prior speech (Jul 31):  UNCHANGED in direction and near-term verdict.
  Jul 31: "job market is solid and perhaps strengthening a bit." Nov 10: "balanced
  and even tightening a bit." The dynamism observation is new but explicitly
  quarantined from policy. The maximum-employment conclusion is unchanged.
Shift vs. pre-FOMC (Jul 31):       Same — UNCHANGED.

──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
INFLATION
──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
FOMC statement language echoed:
  "Inflation remains elevated." [Sep 16 stmt — supply-shock attribution DELETED vs.
   Jul 29]
  → Logan: "inflation has now exceeded the Federal Open Market Committee's 2 percent
    target for nearly six years." She echoes the duration language but does not
    quote the statement's exact phrase. She engages the question of TREND vs.
    temporary at length.

Own language — PATH VERDICT:
  "Unfortunately, inflation is trending meaningfully higher than 2 percent."
  "Looking ahead, inflation appears set to trend meaningfully above that target."
  → This is a significant escalation vs. her July 31 "trending toward the mid-2's,
    not all the way to 2 percent." "Meaningfully above" is broader and less
    pinpointed than the prior "mid-2's" framing; the direction of travel is the
    same (above target), but the word "meaningfully" suggests a wider miss.

Own language — BREADTH DASHBOARD:
  Core PCE: 3.1% (12m Sept); NY Fed multivariate core trend: 2.8% (Sept);
  Dallas Fed trimmed mean: 2.3% (12m Sept) BUT "our research finds that a change
  in the mix of price increases and decreases is causing the trimmed mean to drop
  too many increases right now. This effect likely makes the trimmed mean lower
  than the true inflation trend." — The key: Logan explicitly says her own bank's
  signature trimmed mean is currently misleading to the downside. She provides
  cover for discounting a measure that might look relatively benign.

  Non-housing, non-transportation core services: 3.2% (12m Sept) — "eight tenths
  of a percentage point above the pace historically consistent with achieving the
  FOMC's inflation target." Explicitly: "these services aren't particularly
  sensitive to tariffs, conflict in the Middle East or surging demand for computer
  chips. While I expect inflation to moderate as price pressures dissipate in
  energy and manufactured goods, the broader inflationary trend is more likely
  to persist."
  → This is her clearest statement that the breadth of inflation — beyond the
    identifiable shocks — is the signal. Directly parallels Warsh's Aug 28 breadth
    test and Barkin's Sep 22 "60% of PCE above 3%."

  October CPI: "came out this morning. It was higher than expected. I am concerned
  that PCE inflation could rise when we receive those data for October in a couple
  of weeks."
  → Live data point; confirms the data as of this speech.

  Expectations framework:
  "Market- and survey-based measures of long-run inflation expectations are near
   2 percent, indicating the public trusts the FOMC to deliver price stability."
  — Expectations noted as anchored. The CEO anecdote (wages for 2027 rising to
    offset 2026 inflation) illustrates the second-round risk embedded in anchored-
    but-stretched expectations.

Shift vs. prior speech (Jul 31):  HAWKISH LEAN. Jul 31 "mid-2's, not all the way
  to 2 percent" (precise forecast) → Nov 10 "trending meaningfully above 2 percent"
  (less precise but more emphatic). Explicit dismissal of the trimmed mean as
  currently understated is new and hawkish. The breadth section (non-housing, non-
  transportation core services 3.2%) is the most detailed inflation analysis since
  her July 16 speech.
Shift vs. pre-FOMC (Jul 31):       Same comparison — HAWKISH LEAN.

──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
MONETARY POLICY
──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
FOMC statement language echoed:
  "The Committee will deliver price stability." [Jul 29 stmt] /
  "Inflation remains elevated. Today's policy action will support a timelier
   return to the Committee's 2 percent goal." [Sep 16 stmt]
  → Logan: "Congress charged the FOMC with delivering price stability as well as
    maximum employment. We must fulfill this responsibility." — Not verbatim, but
    obligation framing consistent with both.

Own language — REAL RATE CALCULATION (most detailed stance analysis of the cycle):
  Nominal fed funds rate: 3.88%; Expected PCE inflation (13 surveys): ~3.1%;
  Real fed funds rate: 78bp = "a quarter-percentage-point below the lowest estimate
  of neutral." (Neutral real rate estimates from 8 models: 1.0% to 1.85%.)
  "In my view, accommodative monetary policy is adding to inflationary pressures."
  → Logan explicitly concludes current policy (3.75–4.00%) is ACCOMMODATIVE,
    not restrictive, not neutral. This is the clearest stance label in the cycle:
    not "mildly restrictive," not "slightly restricting" (Waller Sep 3), not
    "hard-pressed to describe as restrictive" (Warsh Aug 28/Sep 16). Logan says
    outright it is adding to pressures.
  → "Broad financial conditions are currently providing a tailwind" — equities up,
    credit spreads compressed, banks eager to lend. Long-term rate rise explained
    as a signal of stronger nominal growth expectations, not a tightening force.

Own language — DESTINATION AND PATH:
  "I currently estimate that the fed funds target range is at least 50 basis
   points—half a percentage point—below the level that would create some modest
   restriction and begin to bring the inflation trend back to target."
  → This is a PRECISE ESTIMATE of the gap to even modest restriction: at least
    50bp from current 3.75–4.00% → implies a "modestly restrictive" rate of at
    least 4.25–4.50%.

  "It remains clear to me, given the current outlook, that the FOMC needs to
   implement at least two more 25-basis-point rate increases."
  → "AT LEAST TWO MORE" is the clearest and most specific path statement from any
    voter this cycle. After September hike +25bp and October skip, Logan's base
    case is December (+25bp, to 4.00–4.25%) and at least one more. Waller says
    25bp December with 50bp possible — Logan says at minimum two additional moves.

  "The exact pace of increases could depend on the near-term economic and financial
   environment. It may be possible to move more rapidly this winter than the FOMC
   has moved so far this fall."
  → This "move more rapidly" language is the strongest speed signal in the cycle.
    "So far this fall" references the September 25bp and the October skip — she
    implies the skip-plus-hike cadence could accelerate.

  "After two more increases are in place, it would make sense in my view to pause
   and examine economic and financial developments to determine what further action
   may be appropriate."
  → Provides a terminal-level framework: two more hikes (minimum), then assess.
    The pause is conditional on what the economy looks like at that point; "what
    further action" keeps the door open.

  October skip rationale:
  "Skipping one meeting does not change the monetary policy destination... by
   making the destination clear, the FOMC can better help the economy adapt and
   obtain the promised benefits of a skip."
  → Fully endorses the skip as a tactical instrument that works only IF the
    destination is clear. The November 10 speech IS the destination-clarification.
    Consistent with Waller's simultaneous speech framing.

Hike signal status: PRESENT — "at least two more 25bp"; "may be possible to move
  more rapidly this winter." Most explicit forward path of any voter this cycle.
Cut signal status:  ABSENT.

Scenario framing (precision rule): DESCRIPTIVE. The speech frames the path as a
  base case, not a named scenario structure with discrete branches. The "may be
  possible to move more rapidly" is directional optionality, not a named branch.

Shift vs. prior speech (Jul 31):  HAWKISH — Jul 31 asked for "one-quarter
  percentage point" at the July meeting (one move). Nov 10 says "at least two
  more" from the current 3.75–4.00% level. This is a substantial escalation: her
  dissent position was one move; her post-hike position is at least two additional
  moves. The "at least" qualifier leaves the door open for more.
Shift vs. pre-FOMC (Jul 31):       Same comparison — HAWKISH.

SUMMARY
──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Net signal:    HAWKISH — the most hawkish and most analytically detailed
               post-reference-FOMC speech from any voter. The key escalation
               from her July 31 dissent: she asked for one move; she now says
               "at least two more" with the possibility of moving "more rapidly
               this winter." The real rate calculation (78bp — below lowest
               neutral estimate) and the explicit "accommodative" stance label
               are the most precise public assessments of the policy gap from
               any voter. The inflation section's dismissal of the Dallas trimmed
               mean as currently understated removes a potential data safety valve.

               The simultaneous Logan–Waller pair on November 10 is the most
               coordinated hawkish signal from two Just Voters since the July 16
               pre-FOMC alignment. Both:
               (1) explicitly endorse the October skip as tactical;
               (2) raise the "low dynamism" labor concern and quarantine it from
                   policy;
               (3) point to December as the next hike with an escalation option
                   if October CPI is hot (which Logan confirms it is).

               Position relative to committee: Logan is the hawkish anchor of
               the voter coalition. Her "at least two more" baseline, combined
               with Waller's 50bp escalation branch, brackets December at 25bp
               (minimum) to 50bp (if data confirm). Among previously scored
               voters, Paulson (Sep 24), Barkin (Sep 22), and Schmid (Sep 18)
               all name a second hike as likely. No voter has publicly dissented
               from that direction.

Language watch:
  • "at least two more 25-basis-point rate increases" — most specific path statement
    from any voter. Stage: DISPERSED (first use in this form). Watch for Paulson
    or Hammack to echo.
  • "accommodative monetary policy is adding to inflationary pressures" —
    explicit stance label; Stage: DISPERSED. The hardest stance label in the cycle.
    "Accommodative" vs. "mildly restrictive" (Paulson) vs. "slightly restricting"
    (Waller Sep 3) vs. "hard-pressed to call restrictive" (Warsh). The gap
    between Logan's label and the committee median is wide.
  • "move more rapidly this winter" — speed escalation signal; Stage: DISPERSED
    (first use). If multiple voters echo, the December meeting debate is 25 vs. 50.
  • Trimmed mean dismissal ("causing the trimmed mean to drop too many increases")
    — methodological caution about the Dallas Fed's own signature measure; Stage:
    DISPERSED. Undercuts a bearish inflation data point; watch for media pickup.
  • CEO wage anecdote (wages for 2027 rising to offset 2026 inflation) — human
    evidence of second-round pass-through risk; Stage: DISPERSED. The anecdote
    is the most memorable illustration of de-anchoring risk in this cycle.

Analyst flag:  YES
  → (1) "At least two more 25bp increases" is a materially more aggressive path
    than her July 31 dissent (one move). After the September hike was delivered,
    her stated base case grew from one move to at least two. This is NOT a
    carry-over of her prior position — it is a genuine escalation that follows
    the September hike but does not reset to neutral.
  → (2) The real rate calculation (78bp below lowest neutral) is the analytical
    backbone of her path argument. If expectations data revise (the 3.1% survey
    expectation she uses is elevated vs. TIPS breakevens), the gap would narrow.
    Confirm the survey sources she cites (13 surveys from "Baker's dozen" of
    household, business, and market participants) and assess whether this measure
    is accepted by other voters.
  → (3) The Dallas trimmed mean dismissal is analytically important: Logan is
    saying her own bank's signature measure is currently misleading. If other
    trimmed-mean watchers (including those at the Board) accept this interpretation,
    it broadens the case for continued tightening. Verify the composition shift her
    staff identifies.
  → (4) Both Logan and Waller raise "low dynamism" labor concerns on the same day.
    This is either parallel independent analysis of the same data or deliberate
    coordination. Watch for a third speaker to use the same framing.
