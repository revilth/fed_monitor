September 1, 2026 | Michael S. Barr | Governor | Just Voter
"Unlocking Opportunities for Workers and Entrepreneurs with a Criminal Record"
Second-Chance Lending Forum, Developing Evidence-Based Policy on
Creditworthiness and Criminal History, Washington, D.C.
SOURCE: https://www.federalreserve.gov/newsevents/speech/barr20260901a.htm

CLASSIFICATION: Type B (financial inclusion / second-chance lending) WITH
  EMBEDDED ECONOMIC OUTLOOK. The outlook is four short paragraphs at the
  opening ("Before I proceed, I wanted to share a few thoughts about the
  economy"), covering growth, labor, inflation and a two-branch policy
  conditional for the September meeting. Scored with the Type A structure.
  The remainder of the speech (financial inclusion, entrepreneurship, AI
  tools for small business) carries no policy signal and is not scored.

REFERENCE FOMC:    July 29, 2026  (HOLD 3.50–3.75%; 9–3; Hammack, Kashkari,
                   Logan dissented preferring +25bp; statement substantive
                   text frozen vs. June 17; no forward guidance)
PRE-FOMC BASELINE: March 26, 2026  "Brief Remarks on the Economic Outlook and
                   Monetary Policy," Brookings Institution — Barr's most
                   recent outlook remarks. June 6 ("Deregulating in a
                   Financial Boom") and July 14 ("Will Artificial Intelligence
                   Broadly Raise Living Standards?") contain no outlook
                   section (checked against the raw texts).
POST-FOMC PRIOR:   N/A — first Barr outlook language since the July 29 FOMC.
                   March 26 serves as both comparators; the shifts below span
                   five months and two FOMC meetings (April 29, June 17).

GROWTH
──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
FOMC statement language echoed:
  "Economic activity is expanding at a solid pace ... Productivity growth and
   capital investment are strong." [Jul 29 stmt]
  → Barr: "The economy has been growing solidly, powered in part by the boom in
    AI-related business investment and the buildout of AI-related capabilities.
    Productivity and new business formation have been strong for a number of
    years."
  → Close paraphrase of the statement's activity and productivity/investment
    sentences — consensus language.

Own language:
  "Consumer spending to date has been largely resilient."
  → "To date" and "largely" are his own qualifiers on the consumer; the
    business side is unqualified. Growth is not a policy concern in this text.

Shift vs. prior speech (Mar 26):  HAWKISH LEAN — March 26 offered no
  affirmative growth characterization (it went straight to labor); solid,
  AI-investment-led growth is now stated up front.
Shift vs. pre-FOMC (Mar 26):      Same comparison — hawkish lean.

LABOR MARKET
──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
FOMC statement language echoed:
  "Job gains have kept pace with the workforce, and the unemployment rate has
   changed little." [Jul 29 stmt]
  → Barr: "The labor market is stable, with relatively low unemployment."

Own verdict:
  "The labor market is stable, with relatively low unemployment."
  → One sentence; no data cited. "Stable" is the minutes' word ("labor market
    conditions appeared stable").

Own qualifier / addition:
  None. The March 26 qualifier — "Low levels of hiring likely leave the labor
  market vulnerable to shocks, so continued vigilance on labor market
  conditions remains warranted" — and the "low hire, low fire" description are
  absent. The −23K July payroll is not mentioned.

Shift vs. prior speech (Mar 26):  HAWKISH LEAN — the vulnerability qualifier
  is gone; the verdict is "stable" without a fragility clause.
Shift vs. pre-FOMC (Mar 26):      Same comparison — hawkish lean.

──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
INFLATION
──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
FOMC statement language echoed:
  "Inflation remains elevated relative to the Committee's 2 percent goal, in
   part reflecting supply shocks that have driven price increases in certain
   sectors, including energy." [Jul 29 stmt]
  → Barr: "But inflation remains too high—and has been for over five years."
    "A series of shocks—from tariffs and then the conflict in the Middle East,
    as well as from the rapid AI buildout—pushed us off course."
  → "Too high" replaces the statement's "elevated." The shock attribution is
    echoed but extended: the statement names supply shocks; Barr adds "the
    rapid AI buildout" as a third shock, which is a demand-side source not in
    the statement.

Own language — KEY:
  "We made enormous progress from inflation's peak of more than 7 percent in
   2022 to a bit above 2 percent in 2024, but that progress stalled in 2025."
  "And core non-housing services inflation remains elevated."
  → Same component focus as March 26 ("Nonhousing services inflation has also
    remained elevated") and as the minutes ("price increases remained elevated
    in core services excluding housing").
  "With inflation above target for a protracted period, there is a risk of
   broader price pressures taking hold, a risk I am watching closely."
  → Broadening risk. March 26 framed the same concern as expectations: "yet
    another price shock could increase longer-term inflation expectations."
    The September 1 framing is about price-setting behavior rather than
    expectations, and "watching closely" is a calmer register than March 26's
    "We need to be especially vigilant."

Duration: "for over five years" — carry-over of March 26's "five years now of
  inflation at elevated levels." (Warsh: "65 months"; Waller Sep 3: "five and
  a half years.")

Shift vs. prior speech (Mar 26):  UNCHANGED — same structure (duration,
  services, broadening/de-anchoring risk). "Series of shocks" and the AI
  buildout as a shock are new elements; the urgency register is slightly
  lower. Net unchanged.
Shift vs. pre-FOMC (Mar 26):      Same comparison — unchanged.

──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
MONETARY POLICY
──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
FOMC statement language echoed:
  None. The statement carries no guidance; Barr does not restate "will deliver
  price stability."

Own language — KEY (explicit, named two-branch scenario for September):
  "At our September FOMC meeting, we will again discuss the outlook for
   inflation and our policy stance."
  HOLD branch: "If trends in the data give me some confidence that inflation is
   moderating on a path to 2 percent, then I think we can take a bit more time
   to assess our policy stance."
  HIKE branch: "However, if inflation appears not to be moderating
   sufficiently, then I think we should act decisively to raise rates."
  → Scenario framing is EXPLICIT AND NAMED: both branches carry a specified
    policy response. The trigger ("trends in the data" on whether inflation is
    "moderating sufficiently") is not quantified.
  → Note the hold branch's wording: "a bit more time" — the hold is described
    as a deferral, not a settled stance.
  → "Moderating sufficiently" is the same test-form as Warsh's Aug 28 standard
    ("moving to our objective, clearly and at sufficient speed"). Two officials
    in four days conditioning policy on a sufficiency judgment about the pace
    of disinflation.

Cut signal status:  ABSENT.
Hike signal status: PRESENT — conditional, named, unquantified trigger.

Shift vs. prior speech (Mar 26):  HAWKISH — March 26: "Our current policy
  stance puts us in a good place to hold steady while we evaluate incoming
  data" and "it makes sense to take some time to assess conditions" — a hold
  with no hike branch. September 1 names a hike branch for a specific meeting
  with an unhedged verb ("act decisively").
Shift vs. pre-FOMC (Mar 26):      Same comparison — hawkish.

──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
SUMMARY
──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Net signal:    HAWKISH LEAN
               Barr's first outlook language since March 26 removes the two
               qualifiers that had cushioned his hawkish inflation read (no
               labor vulnerability clause; growth now affirmatively solid) and
               converts a pure hold into a two-branch conditional with a
               named hike for September. The inflation read itself is
               unchanged in structure. The hold branch is framed as "a bit
               more time," not as a stance he is settled in.

               Position relative to the Committee: with Cook (Aug 5: "I am
               prepared to act by raising rates, if necessary") he is one of
               two majority-side voters with a named hike conditional since
               July 29. His trigger is broader than Waller's (Jul 13: a hot
               core reading; Sep 3: the August inflation print) and his hold
               branch is weaker than Cook's ("It may not" be needed). He does
               not take the dissenters' base-case position.

Language watch:
  • "act decisively to raise rates" — unhedged hike verb from a majority
    voter. Stage: DISPERSED (Cook's "prepared to act by raising rates" is the
    nearest). Watch the September minutes for the distribution of participants
    carrying a hike conditional.
  • "moderating sufficiently" — sufficiency test on the pace of disinflation;
    pairs with Warsh's "at sufficient speed." Stage: EMERGING (two officials).
  • "a series of shocks ... as well as from the rapid AI buildout" — extends
    the successive-shocks framing (Waller Apr 17 "sequence of transitory
    shocks"; Kashkari Jul 31) and adds AI capex to the shock list. Stage:
    EMERGING.
  • "risk of broader price pressures taking hold" — broadening framing rather
    than expectations framing. Stage: DISPERSED.

Analyst flag:  YES
  → Barr voted to hold on July 29 and now names a September hike branch with
    an unquantified trigger. Is this a stance shift toward the dissenters, or
    optionality-preserving language ahead of a projection meeting? The verb
    ("act decisively") is stronger than Cook's; the trigger is vaguer than
    Waller's. The August CPI/PCE prints — which land inside blackout — are the
    only data that can resolve which branch he takes.
