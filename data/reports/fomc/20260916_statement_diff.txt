HEADLINE
─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
The Committee raised rates by 25bp, unanimously, and rewrote the inflation
paragraph to remove the supply-shock attribution that had framed inflation as
exogenous since June. The three July dissenters got their hike; the nine who
held in July joined them, including every voter who had been on record with a
"mildly restrictive" or "only slightly restricting" stance characterization.
No dovish dissent.

Three textual changes carry the signal, all in the same direction:
  1. "maintain" → "raise ... by 1/4 percentage point" — the action.
  2. The inflation paragraph loses "relative to the Committee's 2 percent goal"
     and loses the entire clause "in part reflecting supply shocks that have
     driven price increases in certain sectors, including energy." What is left
     is a bare "Inflation remains elevated." The Committee has stopped
     explaining inflation and started acting on it.
  3. A new purpose clause: "Today's policy action will support a timelier
     return to the Committee's 2 percent goal." "Timelier" is the operative
     word. It concedes that the July path was too slow, and it is the closest
     thing to guidance this Committee has published since Warsh removed it.

The labor sentence is unchanged for the third consecutive statement. The
demand side gets its first explicit characterization ("domestic spending has
been resilient"). The Middle East conflict becomes "geopolitical developments."

DIRECTION: HAWKISH. The action, the deleted attribution, and the new purpose
clause all point the same way. Nothing in the text points the other way.

SENTENCE-BY-SENTENCE DIFF (Prior = July 29 | New = Sept 16, both verbatim)
════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

─── PARAGRAPH 1 — Policy decision + reserves ───
JULY 29:  "The Committee decided to MAINTAIN the target range for the federal
           funds rate AT 3-1/2 to 3-3/4 percent, in support of the Federal
           Reserve's dual mandate. The Committee is continuing its policy of
           maintaining ample reserves in the banking system."
SEPT 16:  "The Committee decided to RAISE the target range for the federal
           funds rate BY 1/4 PERCENTAGE POINT TO 3-3/4 to 4 percent, in support
           of the Federal Reserve's dual mandate. The Committee is continuing
           its policy of maintaining ample reserves in the banking system."
CHANGE:   The decision. Reserves sentence unchanged (second consecutive use of
          "is continuing").
DIRECTION: HAWKISH — the first increase in the target range since July 26,
          2023. The implementation note moves every administered rate by 25bp
          (IORB 3.65 → 3.90; standing repo 3.75 → 4.00; ON RRP 3.50 → 3.75;
          primary credit 3.75 → 4.00) and changes nothing else: the $160bn ON
          RRP per-counterparty limit, bill purchases to maintain ample
          reserves, and full reinvestment are all unchanged. No balance-sheet
          action. The balance sheet — a formal meeting question on July 29 —
          is absent from the statement, the implementation note, and the
          press conference.
          Factual note: the primary-credit increase was approved on requests
          "submitted by the Board of Directors of the Federal Reserve Banks of
          Cleveland, Richmond, Atlanta, Chicago, Minneapolis, Kansas City, and
          Dallas" — seven of twelve. [Inference: the other five boards had not
          filed a request as of the meeting; this is a bank-board matter and
          may be timing, not a signal. Low priority.]

─── PARAGRAPH 2 — Economic activity + labor ───
JULY 29:  "Economic activity is expanding at a solid pace DESPITE ELEVATED
           UNCERTAINTY THAT OWES, IN PART, TO THE CONFLICT IN THE MIDDLE EAST.
           Productivity growth AND capital investment ARE STRONG. Job gains
           have kept pace with the workforce, and the unemployment rate has
           changed little."
SEPT 16:  "Economic activity is expanding at a solid pace. WHILE UNCERTAINTY
           REMAINS ELEVATED OWING, IN PART, TO GEOPOLITICAL DEVELOPMENTS,
           DOMESTIC SPENDING HAS BEEN RESILIENT. Productivity growth IS strong,
           AND capital investment IS ROBUST. Job gains have kept pace with the
           workforce, and the unemployment rate has changed little."
CHANGES:  (a) "the conflict in the Middle East" → "geopolitical developments."
              Broadened, not dropped. Warsh in Q&A: geopolitics is one of the
              three things that changed since July — "there's no hiding from
              hot spots around the world, and our judgment about what is the
              most likely, or least likely of the geopolitical situation has
              changed." The uncertainty is now a standing condition rather
              than a single conflict.
          (b) NEW: "domestic spending has been resilient." First explicit
              demand-side characterization in a Warsh-era statement. The
              prior statements described supply (productivity, capex) and
              labor only.
          (c) "Productivity growth and capital investment are strong" →
              "Productivity growth is strong, and capital investment is
              robust." Capex upgraded one step.
          (d) Labor sentence: UNCHANGED, third consecutive statement.
DIRECTION: HAWKISH LEAN. (b) is the substantive addition: resilient demand
          alongside strong supply removes the last growth-side argument for
          patience. Read with the SEP, where the 2026 unemployment median fell
          to 4.1 and the count of participants seeing upside unemployment
          risk went from 7 to 0, the labor market has moved from "no case for
          cuts" to "no obstacle to hikes." (a) is neutral-to-hawkish: the
          uncertainty that in July was a reason to wait is now described as
          persistent, and the Committee hiked through it.

─── PARAGRAPH 3 — Inflation ───
JULY 29:  "Inflation remains elevated RELATIVE TO THE COMMITTEE'S 2 PERCENT
           GOAL, IN PART REFLECTING SUPPLY SHOCKS THAT HAVE DRIVEN PRICE
           INCREASES IN CERTAIN SECTORS, INCLUDING ENERGY. The Committee will
           deliver price stability."
SEPT 16:  "Inflation remains elevated. TODAY'S POLICY ACTION WILL SUPPORT A
           TIMELIER RETURN TO THE COMMITTEE'S 2 PERCENT GOAL. The Committee
           will deliver price stability."
CHANGES:  (a) DELETED: "relative to the Committee's 2 percent goal" as a
              qualifier on "elevated." The goal is relocated into the new
              second sentence. The first sentence is now three words.
          (b) DELETED: "in part reflecting supply shocks that have driven
              price increases in certain sectors, including energy." This
              clause had been in every statement since June 17. It was the
              Committee's stated reason inflation could be looked through, and
              it was the framework Williams defended publicly on Aug 3 and
              Sep 2 ("broader commodity prices are the biggest drivers," "not
              seeing second round effects or broadening").
          (c) NEW: "Today's policy action will support a timelier return to
              the Committee's 2 percent goal."
          (d) RETAINED: "The Committee will deliver price stability" — third
              consecutive statement.
DIRECTION: HAWKISH, and this paragraph is the document.
          (b) is the largest textual change since Warsh's June rewrite.
          Removing the supply-shock attribution removes the Committee's
          published reason for patience. It is consistent with Warsh's Aug 28
          framework — underlying inflation measured by BREADTH (share of PCE
          components above 3%), where the source of a price increase is not
          the test — and inconsistent with Williams's ATTRIBUTION framework.
          The breadth test won the drafting; the press conference opening
          statement then put it in committee language ("Too many categories
          are still posting increases above 3 percent"). The supply-shock
          story does survive in one place, inverted: the opening statement
          lists as an aim "that relative price changes in some sectors of the
          economy do not broaden." The shocks are no longer a reason to wait;
          their broadening is the thing the hike is meant to prevent.
          (c) "timelier" is a comparative. It says the July path — the one the
          nine-vote majority chose seven weeks ago — was not timely enough.
          That is the Committee adopting the July dissenters' premise
          ("finish the job," "now is the time") in its own text. It also
          operationalizes Warsh's Aug 28 standard ("moving to our objective,
          clearly and at sufficient speed"), which the opening statement then
          reports as unmet: "Today, the FOMC decided that this standard has
          not been satisfied."
          Note what "timelier" is NOT: it is not a path. There is no "further
          adjustments" language, no "additional firming," nothing that names
          a next step. Whether it survives into October unchanged, after
          another hike, or is dropped, will tell you whether it was a purpose
          clause or a guidance device.

─── FORWARD GUIDANCE ───
Absent in June. Absent in July. Absent in September, in the formal sense.
Warsh in Q&A, asked directly whether the usual sequence of hikes applies:
"I'm not in the forward guidance business ... I'm not going to pre-judge any
future decisions we make. You heard from other people in the dots effectively,
what their forecasts are." The path signal has been delegated to the SEP —
where 16 of 18 participants see at least one more hike by year-end — and
disowned by the Chair in the same breath ("those aren't my forecasts. Those are
the forecasts of my 18 colleagues").

WHAT THE STATEMENT RESOLVED FROM JULY — and what it did not
════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
The July diff posed two readings of the frozen text: (a) Warsh holding the
center deliberately, absorbing dissents rather than conceding language; (b) no
majority for anything else. September answers it: (a). Seven weeks after
absorbing three dissents with the text frozen, the Chair delivered the
dissenters' rate with the text rewritten and twelve votes. Warsh in Q&A: "a
good majority of my colleagues seven weeks ago thought seven weeks is a good
investment, it's a way to buy time so we could make a wise decision." The hold
was a delay, not a disagreement.

The DOTS-VS-STATEMENT GAP flagged in June and July has now partly closed from
the statement side (the action) and re-opened from the dots side (16 of 18 see
another hike; the statement names none). See the SEP scored file.

NOT RESOLVED: the stance characterization. The Chair says policy was
accommodative ("we removed a dose of accommodation ... This view was widely
shared by the Committee"; "hard-pressed to describe broad financial conditions
as restrictive," and his colleagues "were hard-pressed to describe it that way
too"). The scored record has Paulson, Collins, Daly and Waller calling the
PRE-hike stance "mildly restrictive" / "only slightly restricting." Both cannot
be right about the same rate. If the Just Voters re-label 3.875% as
"restrictive" in their first post-meeting speeches while the Chair will not,
that is the next fault line; if they adopt "accommodation," the hiking path is
longer than one more move.
