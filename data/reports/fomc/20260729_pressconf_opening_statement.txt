CONSEQUENCE FOR THE READ: The opening statement is the COMMITTEE-CONSENSUS half
of a press conference — approved language, not personal signal. It is therefore

HEADLINE
─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Warsh spent his opening statement doing two things: hardening the inflation-
credibility commitment to its most explicit form yet, and welcoming the bond
market's tightening as a vindication of his communications regime.

The most consequential passage is a flat rejection of a soft target — "There is
no soft inflation target, there is no soft implicit target—not on this
Committee's watch" — paired with an explicit dismissal of the benign June
inflation print. The most novel is his treatment of a top-decile rise in
Treasury yields as "a change for the better."

Put together, they explain the frozen statement text and the 9–3 vote. The Chair
is rhetorically aligned with the hawks on inflation credibility while letting the
CURVE, not the policy rate, deliver the tightening. That is a coherent position
that can hold three dissents without moving a word of the statement.

NEW vs. JUNE 17 OPENING STATEMENT — verified absent from the June text
════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

─── 1. THE ANTI-DRIFT PASSAGE (the headline; genuinely new) ───
JULY 29: "Uncertainty, however, does not mean a lack of clarity. For some
          households, businesses, and market professionals, five years of high
          inflation have left a mistaken impression that is hard to shake: that
          the Fed's implicit inflation target was somehow above 2 percent. Let
          me reiterate: There is no soft inflation target, there is no soft
          implicit target—not on this Committee's watch. There is only a target,
          and it is 2 percent. Not one of my FOMC colleagues is under any
          illusion."
JUNE 17:  No equivalent. June asserted the commitment ("This Committee will
          deliver price stability") but never addressed the perception of an
          implicit higher target.
READ:     HAWKISH. This is a credibility intervention aimed at expectations, not
          at the rate path. He is naming and rejecting a specific belief he
          thinks is embedded in household, business and market pricing. Note the
          audience — "market professionals" is called out by name.
          Watch this as an expectations-anchoring device: if Warsh believes the
          implicit-target perception is live, that raises his bar for tolerating
          any above-target print, independent of the data.

─── 2. THE SINGLE-MONTH DISMISSAL — the Chair adopts the hawks' argument ───
JULY 29: "We have begun a new chapter, and we understand that the five-plus
          years of inflation above target cannot be cured in nine weeks—or by a
          single month of modest price decreases."
JUNE 17:  Absent. June acknowledged inflation "running well ahead... for more
          than five years" but had no benign print to discount.
READ:     HAWKISH, and the single most important line for positioning. This is
          the Chair explicitly refusing to bank the June CPI/PPI improvement.
          It is Logan's July 16 argument almost verbatim in substance: "one
          month of relief is not enough; it is time to finish the job."
          It also completes an escalation in Warsh's own device for handling
          good data: Jul 14–15 testimony discounted favorable prints as
          "imperfect measures" (withholding the conclusion); Jul 29 dismisses
          the month outright.
          THIS IS WHY THE STATEMENT'S INFLATION PARAGRAPH WAS FROZEN. The
          drafting choice flagged in the statement diff now has the Chair's
          stated rationale behind it.
          [Inference: "nine weeks" reads as the span of his chairmanship / the
          "new chapter," not a data window. Not stated. Low stakes either way.]

─── 3. THE YIELD MOVE — most novel content, and genuinely two-sided ───
JULY 29: "The first is a very notable change since our last meeting 42 days ago:
          nominal and real yields are materially higher across the Treasury
          curve. In fact, some of the increases in market interest rates between
          FOMC meetings are among the most significant in the last two decades,
          ranking around the top decile or so."
          "...market attention centered on real data and real economic
          developments. Prices reacted in real time to incoming information, and
          the reduction in forward guidance may have been a factor. Market
          participants are learning to play the ball, not the referee—and market
          prices will continue to respond in the direction and magnitude they
          see fit. This is, in my view, a change for the better—and we are just
          getting started. After all, the central bank need not always and
          everywhere be the center of attention."
          "I want to stress, of course, that decisions by this Committee matter a
          great deal. And where necessary and appropriate, we will not hesitate
          to act."
JUNE 17:  Absent. June announced the removal of forward guidance; it could not
          yet evaluate the consequence.
READ:     MIXED, and the most decision-relevant passage in the document.
          (a) DOVISH FOR THE POLICY RATE: a top-decile inter-meeting rise in
              real and nominal yields is tightening that the Committee did not
              have to deliver. The Chair volunteering this as development number
              one, unprompted, is a strong hint at how the hold was rationalized
              against three hike dissents — financial conditions did the work.
          (b) HAWKISH IN REGIME: he is not merely tolerating the move, he is
              endorsing it ("a change for the better," "just getting started")
              and disclaiming any intent to lean against market pricing. There is
              no Fed put in this framing.
          (c) THE GUARDRAIL: "we will not hesitate to act" is the counterweight,
              and it is deliberately undirected — it does not say in which
              direction.
          ACTIONABLE ASYMMETRY: if the yield move retraces, the implicit
          tightening unwinds and the case for an actual policy-rate hike
          strengthens mechanically. Warsh has effectively made the curve a
          substitute for the funds rate. Track real yields as a live input to
          September odds.
LANGUAGE: "play the ball, not the referee" — a variant of his Jul 15 Senate
          formulation "play the ball, don't play the Fed." Second use, mutated
          slightly. Stage: DISPERSED → CONSOLIDATING (Warsh only, but now his
          fixed idiom for the communications regime).

─── 4. BALANCE SHEET FRAMED AS ACCOMMODATION — a formal agenda item ───
JULY 29: Fourth of four enumerated meeting questions: "If, as the Fed has long
          held, interest rate policy should be its primary monetary policy
          instrument, how much accommodation are we getting from the balance
          sheet?"
JUNE 17:  The balance sheet appeared only as one of five TASK FORCE subjects —
          a governance/review item. It was not framed as a live source of
          current accommodation.
READ:     HAWKISH-LEAN, and a promotion. The balance sheet has moved from
          "under review" to a question about how much easing it is currently
          providing — which presupposes it may be providing too much. This
          extends the Jul 15 Senate framing (balance sheet as one of "two
          instruments" relevant to INFLATION RISKS, "leaner, meaner balance
          sheet" preferred) from personal view into a formal Committee agenda
          item.
          This is the most plausible route to tightening that does NOT require
          moving the funds rate — and therefore the most plausible way to
          accommodate three hawkish dissenters without a hike. HIGH PRIORITY to
          track. Note the July implementation note contained no operational
          change, so this remains discussion-stage.

─── 5. THE FOUR MEETING QUESTIONS (new disclosure format) ───
Warsh enumerated the four questions the Committee debated — a level of process
disclosure June did not provide:
  1. "the implications of the past five years of high inflation on the current
     policy conjuncture. To echo an old phrase, has the past really passed?"
  2. Whether the recent shocks (pandemic supply chains, military conflicts,
     energy disruptions, tariff increases, AI investment) "differ in their
     effects on output and employment."
  3. Price increases arising from shocks — memory/logic chips, AI
     infrastructure: "Do those changes indicate a broader inflationary dynamic,
     or do we just focus on them just because they are under the bright
     streetlight?"
  4. Balance-sheet accommodation (above).
READ:     Questions 1 and 3 are the tell. Question 1 ("has the past really
          passed?") is the hawks' framing — whether five years of above-target
          inflation has changed the policy problem. Question 3's "bright
          streetlight" is dismissive: it pre-loads the answer that AI-driven
          chip prices are a relative-price story, not broad inflation.
          This preserves the split flagged on Jul 15, where Warsh broke with
          Waller and Williams on the AI-inflation channel. He has now carried
          that skepticism into the Committee's formal agenda.
          NOTE THE TENSION, it is real and worth watching: Warsh is maximally
          hawkish on the 2% target's credibility while discounting the
          Committee's newest inflation driver. Reconciles as fighting
          UNDERLYING inflation, not relative price shifts — but if AI capex
          prices keep feeding core, that distinction gets expensive.

─── 6. AI CAPEX QUANTIFIED ───
JULY 29: "the most striking feature of the economy is the strong growth of
          business investment... In the A.I.-related category of high-tech
          equipment and software, the most recent data shows four-quarter growth
          rates of nearly 20 percent. This is helping to sustain the healthy
          momentum of manufacturing output... Nonetheless, the precise timing
          and magnitude of effects on the supply side remain hard to predict."
JUNE 17:  Not in the opening statement.
READ:     HAWKISH-LEAN on the growth leg — reinforces that there is no
          growth-based case for easing, consistent with his Jul 1 Sintra
          "structural productivity in the high 2% range." The supply-side
          hedge is new and honest.

CARRY-OVER — present in BOTH, therefore NOT a signal
════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
Flagging these explicitly because each could be misread as a fresh hawkish
addition; all were verified present in the June 17 text:
  • "we will deliver price stability" — June: "This Committee will deliver price
    stability." July: "we will deliver price stability." Carry-over. Warsh even
    marks it himself: "You've heard this before." NOT new.
  • "the policy statement conveys just the facts" — June: "That statement just
    gives you the facts, as best we can judge it." Rephrased carry-over.
  • Forward-guidance absence — announced in June. July's addition is the
    EVALUATION of its market effect (item 3), not the fact of it.
  • "Job gains have kept pace with the workforce, and the unemployment rate has
    changed little" — verbatim statement language in both.

NOTABLE ABSENCES
════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
  • THE DISSENTS ARE NEVER MENTIONED. Warsh reports the "9 to 3 vote" as a fact
    and never addresses it. June's boast — "members of the FOMC are unambiguous
    and unanimous" — is necessarily gone, but note how he replaced it: "Not one
    of my FOMC colleagues is under any illusion" about the 2 percent target.
    The unity claim was RELOCATED from the policy decision to the inflation
    objective. Unity on the goal, open dispersion on the path. That is a
    deliberate and rather skilful piece of drafting.
    He also pre-empts the "nothing happened" read: "there was nothing inertial
    about our discussions, our policy, or our strategy."
  • NO TASK FORCE UPDATE. June devoted roughly a third of the opening statement
    to launching five task forces and promised "quite a bit more... in the
    coming weeks"; Jul 15 gave a hard timeline (September briefings, year-end
    conclusions). The July 29 opening statement says nothing about them. Most
    likely deferred to Q&A — FLAG for recovery.
  • NO SEP. Correct — July is not a projection meeting. Next is September 15–16.
  • NO RATE-PATH SIGNAL WHATSOEVER. Consistent with two days of testimony in
    which he kept "leave alone, raise, cut" all open and twice declined to
    engage Waller's hike conditionality by name.

NET SIGNAL (PROVISIONAL — opening statement only)
════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
HAWKISH LEAN ON CREDIBILITY, NEUTRAL ON THE PATH.

Warsh hardened the inflation commitment to its most explicit form to date, took
the hawks' side of the argument on the benign June print, and put balance-sheet
accommodation on the formal agenda — while giving zero guidance on the funds
rate and welcoming the bond market's tightening as a substitute for Committee
action.

The coherent story: the Chair agrees with Logan, Hammack and Kashkari about the
inflation problem and disagrees with them about the instrument. He is content to
let a top-decile yield move do the tightening, and he has a second lever
(balance sheet) in development before he would need to touch the funds rate.
That is how a hold survives three hike dissents with the statement text frozen.

WHAT WOULD CHANGE THE READ: a Q&A concession on near-term tightening — the thing
he declined to engage twice in testimony. That is precisely what is missing here,
so hold this as provisional.
