HEADLINE
─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
The September projections raise the rate path far more than they raise the
inflation forecast. Headline and core PCE medians move up one tenth for 2026
and are unchanged for 2027; the fed funds median moves up +0.3 for 2026, +0.5
for 2027 and +0.5 for 2028. The Committee is responding to roughly the same
inflation outlook it had in June with roughly two more quarter-point hikes.

What changed is not the inflation forecast but the rest of the frame: the
unemployment path was revised DOWN two tenths to 4.1 across every horizon, the
count of participants seeing upside risk to unemployment went from 7 to 0,
and the count seeing downside risk to growth went from 5 to 0. The labor-market
argument for patience did not weaken; it disappeared. With inflation risks
still skewed to the upside by 17 of 18, the Committee re-priced its reaction
function, not its inflation view.

Against the NEW 3.875% midpoint, 16 of 18 participants project at least one
more hike by year-end, 2 project a hold, and 0 project a cut — the first SEP of
the cycle with no dot below the current rate. The 2027 median equals the 2026
median (4.1): hike once more, then hold for a year.

KEY READ ON THE RATE PATH:
• Read against the NEW midpoint (3.875% after today's hike), not the old one.
  The 2026 median of 4.125 is one quarter-point above it: the median
  participant expects one more hike at one of the two remaining meetings
  (October 27–28 or December 8–9).
• 2027 median = 2026 median = 4.125. The modal path is hike-then-plateau, not
  a hiking cycle: the median sees the rate reached by year-end held through
  all of 2027, one cut in 2028 (3.875), another in 2029 (3.625).
• Longer-run median 3.0625 → 3.25 (published 3.1 → 3.2). A small upward
  re-estimate of neutral, NOT the explanation for the near-term move: the
  2026-minus-longer-run gap widened from +0.69 to +0.875 (published +0.7 →
  +0.9), and the 2027 gap from +0.56 to +0.875. The Committee is signaling
  MORE restriction relative to a slightly higher neutral, i.e.
  higher-for-longer, not neutral-repricing. [Per the CLAUDE.md neutral-rate
  precision rule: the near-term move is a restrictiveness call; the neutral
  drift is real but second-order — see the longer-run histogram below.]

The median rose 0.1, but the distribution changed more than the median
suggests — in the direction of LESS dispersion, not more inflation. The three
dots at 3.9 or above in June are gone; twelve of eighteen now sit on 3.7–3.8.
The Committee is more certain about 2026 inflation than it was, and the upper
tail came DOWN. Core PCE 2026 tells the same story (3.3–3.4: 10 → 12; 3.5–3.6:
4 → 3; nothing above 3.6 either time).

PCE 2027: essentially unchanged (2.1–2.2: 5 → 6; 2.3–2.4: 6 → 7; 2.5–2.6: 4 →
4). PCE 2028: 1.9–2.0 went 12 → 6, 2.1–2.2 went 4 → 11 — the median moved from
2.0 to 2.1 and target achievement slipped to 2029.

Risk assessments (Figure 4):
  PCE inflation risk weighted to upside:   June 17/18 → Sept 17/18 (unchanged)
  Core PCE risk weighted to upside:        June 17/18 → Sept 15/18
  Unemployment risk weighted to upside:    June  7/18 → Sept  0/18  ← the change
  Unemployment risk broadly balanced:      June 10/18 → Sept 17/18
  GDP risk weighted to downside:           June  5/18 → Sept  0/18  ← the change
  GDP risk weighted to upside:             June  3/18 → Sept  5/18
The inflation risk picture is identical to June. The labor and growth risk
pictures flipped. That is the mechanism behind the headline: the rate path
rose because the downside risks that justified waiting were withdrawn, not
because the inflation forecast worsened.

THE TENSION WORTH FLAGGING: "Today's policy action will support a timelier
return to the Committee's 2 percent goal" (statement), yet the median PCE
path reaches 2.0 in 2029 rather than June's 2028. Michael McKee put this to
Warsh; the answer was "those aren't my forecasts." [Inference: the medians
are set by different participants per variable and per year, so the composite
is not one participant's internally consistent path. Read "timelier" as
relative to the counterfactual of not hiking, not relative to the June SEP.
But the analyst should note that the Committee's own central forecast does
not show the higher rate path buying faster disinflation.]

DOTS-VS-STATEMENT GAP (Language Cycle):
In June the gap was "dots say hike, statement says nothing." In September the
statement closed half of it by hiking, and the dots re-opened it: sixteen
participants see another hike; the statement's only forward-looking word is
"timelier." The individual positioning is still running ahead of the
collective text by one move. Per the bottom-up cycle, the October statement is
where that consolidates or does not. The Chair has explicitly delegated the
path signal to the dots ("You heard from other people in the dots") while
disowning them ("those aren't my forecasts") — which makes the dots the
Committee's de facto guidance in a regime that has formally abolished it.

NET READ
════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
HAWKISH SEP, on the rate path, with the mechanism worth stating precisely:
the inflation forecast barely moved and became more certain; the labor and
growth risk assessments flipped from skewed-down to balanced; the Committee
therefore withdrew the patience it had been extending and moved its central
path up two steps against a neutral estimate that drifted up one tenth. The
result is one more hike this year, a plateau through 2027, and the first SEP
of the cycle with no participant below the current rate.

The two watch items for October: (1) whether "timelier" survives a second
hike as guidance, and (2) whether the Just Voters who called the pre-hike
stance "mildly restrictive" now adopt the Chair's "removed a dose of
accommodation" framing — if they do, 4.125 is a floor, not a ceiling.
