FOMC PRESS CONFERENCE — September 16, 2026 (Type E) — OPENING STATEMENT + Q&A
════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
Kevin Warsh, Chair | Troika | Third press conference as Chair
Reference FOMC: September 16, 2026 (HIKE +25bp to 3.75–4.00%, 12–0)
Prior press conference: July 29, 2026 (HOLD, 9–3)
Chair's prior speech: August 28, 2026 (Jackson Hole, Type G)

Written September 17. No provisional alert was generated on decision day.

HEADLINE
─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
The opening statement moves Warsh's August 28 personal framework into
Committee language: his standard for action ("clearly and at sufficient
speed") is reported as the FOMC's finding — "Today, the FOMC decided that this
standard has not been satisfied" — and his breadth measure of underlying
inflation appears as consensus text ("Too many categories are still posting
increases above 3 percent"). The hike is characterized not as a move into
restriction but as the removal of accommodation: "we removed a dose of
accommodation," a view "widely shared by the Committee."

The Q&A adds three personal signals. He will not own a path ("I'm not in the
forward guidance business"), but points twice to the dots as where the path
lives. He says the August CPI was not the trigger — trends, a stronger economy
and a changed geopolitical judgment were. And he refuses to call 3.875%
restrictive or to use a neutral rate operationally ("Do I think it has any
operational effect on decisions that we make today? No, I don't"), which
leaves the door to the next hike formally open.

NET: HAWKISH. Opening statement committee-level hawkish; Q&A declines every
opportunity to bound the path.

OPENING STATEMENT (committee-approved language; consensus signal)
════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
Carry-over check: every "new" item below was verified absent from the July 29
official opening statement (data/raw/pressconferences/20260729_pressconf_
transcript.txt). Items that first appeared in the Aug 28 Jackson Hole speech
are marked as such — they are new to a PRESS CONFERENCE but not new language.

GROWTH
  Statement language: "Economic activity is expanding at a solid pace ...
    domestic spending has been resilient. Productivity growth is strong, and
    capital investment is robust." (read verbatim)
  Own additions (new vs. July 29):
    "the American economy appears to be strengthening. New hiring,
    private-sector earnings, business capital investment—each of these
    markers has improved in recent months and is pointing in a good
    direction. Credit flows have been robust, particularly for businesses."
    "an attitude of optimism is exactly what I heard inside the FOMC these
    last two days."
  Absent vs. July 29: the AI-capex quantification ("nearly 20 percent") and
    the supply-side hedge. Growth is now stated as a settled fact, not a
    two-sided development.
  Direction: HAWKISH LEAN — "strengthening," "optimism," robust credit flows.
    No growth-side reason for patience is offered.

LABOR MARKET
  Statement language: "Job gains have kept pace with the workforce, and the
    unemployment rate has changed little." (verbatim; third consecutive)
  Own additions (new vs. July 29 — the July opening had no labor detail
    beyond the statement line):
    "The jobless rate remains low at around 4.1 percent, and both job
    openings and weekly hours have been increasing. Unemployment claims, on a
    four-week moving average, are running at levels consistent with full
    employment. So, the labor side of the Fed's congressional remit is in
    good shape."
  → "consistent with full employment" was Aug 28 language (personal); here it
    is the Committee's opening-statement verdict. Openings and hours
    "increasing" is new evidence not cited before.
  Direction: HAWKISH LEAN — a full-employment verdict in consensus language
    removes the labor argument for patience. Matches the SEP: unemployment
    median 4.1 through 2029; upside-risk count 7 → 0.

INFLATION (the section that moved)
  Statement language: "inflation remains elevated. Today's policy action will
    support a timelier return to the Committee's 2 percent goal. This
    Committee will deliver price stability." (read verbatim, with "But"
    prefixed)
  New vs. July 29:
    (1) "our predominant focus is on the price-stability side of our mandate.
        The plain fact is that inflation is too high and has been for too
        long." — Aug 28 had "predominant focus right now should be on prices"
        (personal); now consensus.
    (2) "This summer's inflation readings do not tell me that underlying
        trends have meaningfully improved." — Aug 28 verbatim, carried into
        the opening statement. The two benign summer prints remain
        discounted; the August data did not change the verdict.
    (3) The data: "the 12-month change in total PCE prices likely was around
        3.6 percent in August. Core PCE and CPI prices are running at about
        3.2 percent and 2.4 percent respectively."
    (4) THE BREADTH MEASURE, NOW COMMITTEE TEXT: "Too many categories are
        still posting increases above 3 percent, on both a 6- and 12-month
        basis." — Aug 28 disclosed this as his personal lens (54% / 49% of
        199 PCE components). It is now in the consensus document without the
        numbers. Language-cycle stage: personal (Aug 28) → committee-level
        (Sept 16) in nineteen days.
    (5) Commodities: "over the inter-meeting period, the prices of many of
        these key inputs have risen." — Aug 28 said they "bear watching";
        now they have moved.
    (6) THE STANDARD, EXECUTED: "Last month in Wyoming, I expressed my
        commitment to a monetary policy discipline, not to a decision. I
        defined the standard for action: We must be confident that underlying
        inflation is moving to our objective, clearly and at sufficient
        speed. Today, the FOMC decided that this standard has not been
        satisfied." — The Chair's personal standard is reported as the
        Committee's finding. That is the mechanism of today's decision stated
        in one sentence.
    (7) FOUR AIMS (new enumeration): "ensure that credit and financial
        conditions are consistent over time with our mandate, that relative
        price changes in some sectors of the economy do not broaden, that
        inflation compensation in market prices stays low, and that inflation
        expectations remain well-anchored."
        → "relative price changes ... do not broaden" absorbs the
        supply-shock story the statement just deleted, and inverts it: the
        shocks are no longer a reason to wait; their broadening is what the
        hike guards against. Williams's "not seeing broadening" test has
        become a policy aim rather than a reason for patience.
  ABSENT vs. July 29 (verified present in July, absent now):
    • "There is no soft inflation target, there is no soft implicit
      target—not on this Committee's watch." Not repeated. [Inference: the
      action made the declaration unnecessary; not a softening.]
    • "cannot be cured in nine weeks—or by a single month of modest price
      decreases." Replaced by "This summer's inflation readings do not tell
      me..." — same function.
  Direction: HAWKISH — committee-level adoption of the breadth test and the
    speed standard; the summer data dismissed a second time.

MONETARY POLICY
  Statement language: "raise the target range ... by ¼ percentage point to 3¾
    to 4 percent, in support of the Federal Reserve's dual mandate." (verbatim)
  New vs. July 29:
    (1) STANCE: "as I said at the policy symposium in Jackson Hole, I would
        be hard-pressed to describe broad financial conditions as
        restrictive. This view was widely shared by the Committee. So we
        removed a dose of accommodation." — The Aug 28 personal LEVEL claim
        is now attributed to the Committee, and the hike is named as
        accommodation-removal, not tightening into restriction. This is the
        single most consequential framing in the document for the path: a
        Committee that describes 3.875% as "less accommodative" has not
        reached the point where it must justify further moves.
    (2) THE JULY HOLD RE-DESCRIBED: "At our July meeting, we all agreed that
        inflation remained too high, and we expressed our joint readiness to
        act ... a good majority of my colleagues and I thought the wiser
        course then would be to await new information." — Aug 28 language,
        carried. The 9–3 is retold as a timing choice.
    (3) UNANIMITY AS RESOLVE: "The committee's unanimous vote shows our
        resolve to achieve price stability on a timelier basis." — July 29
        relocated the unity claim from the decision to the goal ("Not one of
        my FOMC colleagues is under any illusion"); September has it back on
        the decision.
    (4) SEP: "as in June—I have not offered a projection of my own." Second
        consecutive withheld dot (confirms the 18-participant count is
        19 minus Warsh). He reads the medians and adds: "Inflation risks are
        to the upside while labor risks are roughly balanced."
    (5) INTERNATIONAL (new): Jackson Hole, "Asheville at the G-20 meeting,
        which the U.S. hosted," "a central bank conference in Basel" — "most
        advanced economies are facing price pressures. Their central banks
        are making their own judgments." — Global tightening context offered
        unprompted; the ECB has hiked twice this year (raised by AFP in Q&A).
  ABSENT vs. July 29:
    • The bond-market section ("play the ball, not the referee"; top-decile
      yield move; "a change for the better"). In July the curve was doing the
      work; in September the Committee did it. Yields appear only when asked
      (Q&A 7 below).
    • The four meeting questions / deliberation-framework disclosure. Gone.
    • THE BALANCE SHEET. Not mentioned in the opening statement, the
      statement, the implementation note, or any Q&A answer. The July 29
      fourth meeting question ("how much accommodation are we getting from
      the balance sheet?") has no successor. Consistent with Aug 28 principle
      5 (rates "the predominant tool") — the rate was used, the balance sheet
      was not.
    • "where necessary and appropriate, we will not hesitate to act." —
      superseded by the act.
  Cut signal status:  ABSENT.
  Hike signal status: the hike itself; no forward path. See Q&A 1.
  Direction: HAWKISH.

Q&A ANALYSIS (Chair's personal, unscripted signals — where he went beyond
the opening statement)
════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

1. THE PATH — delegated to the dots, disowned by the Chair
   Asked (Colby Smith, NYT) whether the usual sequence of hikes applies:
   "I'm not in the forward guidance business. The decision we made today was
   a sober decision, serious decision, responsible decision, one that we have
   been preparing for and thinking about in my 110 or 20 days here. You heard
   from other people in the dots effectively, what their forecasts are. I'm
   not going to pre-judge any future decisions we make."
   Asked (McKee, Bloomberg) how "timelier" squares with the SEP reaching 2%
   in 2029: "those aren't my forecasts. Those are the forecasts of my 18
   colleagues, and I've tried to represent them dutifully to you ... when we
   continue our discussions over the course of the next several weeks and
   months, we'll have more to say about it. But I'm ill-prepared to pre-judge
   those future actions."
   Beyond the statement: twice he routes the path question to the SEP. In a
   regime with no guidance, the dots — 16 of 18 above the new midpoint — are
   the Committee's de facto forward signal, and the Chair has pointed at them
   without endorsing them. "We'll have more to say about it" is the only
   forward-looking phrase of his own, and it is undirected. [Inference: "more
   to say" may refer to the communications task force (SEP reform) as much
   as to policy; not resolvable from the text.]

2. WHAT CHANGED SINCE JULY — not the CPI
   Asked (Rugaber, AP) what changed and whether today's retail sales mattered:
   "I'm not a data point dependent guy ... a good majority of my colleagues
   seven weeks ago thought seven weeks is a good investment, it's a way to
   buy time so we could make a wise decision. I'll highlight three things
   ... There's been a pretty wide-ranging set of data, including in the labor
   markets, that the economy has strengthened ... My judgment some weeks ago
   was the inflation summer trends weren't passing the test. I've seen very
   little information since that would make me reverse that decision, so
   I've stuck with it. And the third thing that's changed in seven weeks are
   geopolitics ... our judgment about what is the most likely, or least
   likely of the geopolitical situation has changed."
   Asked (Guida, Politico) about the market's focus on August CPI: "I was not
   waiting breathlessly on what any particular data was, whether it was
   retail sales this morning, or a CPI print last week ... trends matter.
   Data points are noisy. Data point dependence is a dangerous
   preoccupation."
   Beyond the statement: the Chair's stated reasons are (i) a stronger
   economy, (ii) an unchanged verdict on inflation trends, (iii) geopolitics.
   Note (ii): he did not say inflation got WORSE; he said the summer data did
   not pass the test in August and nothing since reversed that. The hike is
   the execution of a judgment already formed at Jackson Hole, not a reaction
   to the August print. This is the opposite of Waller's Sep 3 framing, which
   made the August data the explicit trigger; both reached the same vote.
   Also new: the July hold retold as buying time ("seven weeks is a good
   investment"), settling the July diff's open question — the center was
   there; it was waiting.

3. STANCE — "removed a dose of accommodation"; no neutral rate
   Asked (Jones, FT) whether rates are now restrictive: "I found it difficult
   to describe financial conditions as restrictive. I think I said I was
   hard-pressed. What I heard around the table in the last couple of days is,
   my colleagues were hard-pressed to describe it that way too. We removed a
   dose of accommodation ... we'll continue to evaluate that prospectively."
   Asked (Liesman, CNBC) where the rate sits vs. neutral, short-run vs.
   long-run: "In a word, no ... I've always been interested in a neutral rate
   as an academic matter ... Do I think it has any operational effect on
   decisions that we make today? No, I don't."
   Beyond the statement: he declines to call 3.875% restrictive and rejects
   neutral as an operational anchor. The scored record has the majority's own
   stance language at "mildly restrictive" (Paulson, Collins, Daly) and "only
   slightly restricting" (Waller) BEFORE the hike. The Chair says the
   Committee was "hard-pressed" to call it restrictive. If policy was
   accommodative at 3.625, it is at best neutral at 3.875, and there is no
   stated point at which the Chair would have to justify stopping. This is
   the hawkish content of the Q&A: not a path, but the absence of a ceiling.

4. THE HIKE AGAINST SUPPLY SHOCKS — broadening is the target
   Asked (Escobedo, CBS) how a hike helps when it "does not reopen the Strait
   of Hormuz": "We cannot affect any individual price ... But what we can do,
   and will do, is ensure that any change in relative prices don't broaden
   out. Don't have second and third order effects in the economy."
   Asked (Schonberger, Yahoo) whether he must push growth below potential:
   "we believe that the unemployment rate is basically running consistent
   with full employment. I don't believe that we need to do harm to the labor
   markets to achieve our objective. I don't believe that the two parts of
   our mandate ... are working at cross purposes over the medium term."
   Beyond the statement: the rationale for hiking into a supply shock is
   second-round prevention, which is what the deleted statement clause and
   the new "do not broaden" aim encode. The no-labor-sacrifice position is
   carried from June 17 ("I don't believe we have a cruel choice") — not new,
   but now stated after an actual hike.

5. MARKET-LED? THE PRESIDENT? — independence framing, unanimous hike
   Asked (Lawrence, Fox) whether a 90%-priced hike was market-led: "the Fed
   has an enormous amount of power. These are decisions we make ... Sometimes
   the market tries to pre-judge our outcomes. I'll observe market prices and
   see what they have to say. But today was our decision."
   Asked (Schulze, ABC; Halaschak, Examiner) about the President's calls for
   cuts and the trade threat: "I've got nothing for you on the discussion
   with the president." "Part of the independence of the Federal Reserve is
   we stay in our lane. Independence is a two-way street. We'll let people
   that do trade policy and fiscal policy stay in their lane too."
   Beyond the statement: a unanimous hike delivered against explicit
   presidential pressure for cuts, with the Chair declining to engage. The
   institutional signal is the 12–0, not the words.

6. YIELDS — three causes, none of them "the Fed"
   Asked (Irwin, Axios) what the bond market is saying: "these things tend to
   be overdetermined ... first is economic strength ... Second reason,
   competition for capital. The surge in capital expenditures ... the
   so-called hyperscalers are out in the market raising funding ... The third,
   is geopolitics ... the difference between those spot prices and so-called
   crack spreads."
   Beyond the statement: July's "yields are doing the tightening" argument is
   gone. Yields are explained by growth, capex funding and geopolitics, and
   the policy consequence is left unstated. Consistent with Williams's Sep 2
   read ("the economy affecting financial conditions") — a rare point of
   Troika convergence.

7. DISTRIBUTION — "inflation is a choice"
   Asked (Cheung, NBC) who the "least well-off" are: "people that don't own
   financial assets, call that a bit less than 50 percent of the country ...
   living off their paycheck ... As I've said before, inflation is a choice,
   and today we took a step in delivering it."
   Beyond the statement: Aug 28's distributional argument, now attached to
   the action. "Inflation is a choice" is new phrasing for the tracked
   commitment.

8. AI — task force "by the end of the year" (Egan, CNN). Housekeeping; no
   policy content. Confirms the AI task force timeline; nothing on the
   communications / SEP task force.

vs. CHAIR'S PREVIOUS SPEECH (August 28, 2026 — Jackson Hole)
════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
Jackson Hole set out a standard, a measure and a level claim, all personal:
"clearly and at sufficient speed"; the share of PCE components above 3%;
"hard pressed to describe broad financial conditions as restrictive";
"Otherwise, we have work to do." September 16 executes all four and
attributes each to the Committee: "the FOMC decided that this standard has
not been satisfied"; "Too many categories are still posting increases above 3
percent"; "This view was widely shared by the Committee"; and the hike. The
personal-to-committee migration took nineteen days, and it ran top-down —
the Chair's framework became the Committee's text, not the reverse.
Genuinely new since Aug 28: "removed a dose of accommodation" (the
characterization of the move); geopolitics as a stated decision input; the
"do not broaden" aim; "inflation is a choice." Retracted or dropped since
Aug 28: nothing. The balance sheet, narrowed on Aug 28, is now absent.
Unchanged: no reaction function, no path, dot withheld, "discipline, not a
decision."

════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
vs. COMMITTEE SINCE THE PREVIOUS FOMC (July 29 → September 16)
════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
Anticipated the decision: Hammack, Kashkari, Logan (Jul 31 dissent
statements); Collins Aug 25 ("tighten policy soon"); Barr Sep 1 (raise rates
"decisively" if not moderating); Waller Sep 3 (hike branch on August data);
Cook Aug 5 ("prepared to act by raising rates"); Daly Aug 6 (scenario two
"much more likely"); Paulson Aug 4 ("could be higher rates"). Seven voters
and two non-voters were on record with a conditional or outright hike case
going into blackout.
Diverged from the decision's language: Williams (Aug 3, Sep 2) — attribution
framework, tariffs "in the rear view mirror," "trend moving slowly down." He
voted with the majority; the statement deleted his framework's clause.
Diverged from the decision's STANCE language: Paulson, Collins, Daly
("mildly restrictive"), Waller ("only slightly restricting") — all described
the pre-hike stance as restrictive; the Chair says the Committee was
"hard-pressed" to. Watch every first post-meeting speech for which label
they use at 3.875%.
Not on record since July 29: Jefferson, Bowman, Powell. All three voted to
hike.

NET SIGNAL: HAWKISH — a unanimous hike framed as accommodation-removal, the
Chair's personal inflation framework adopted as Committee text, the supply-
shock excuse retired, no ceiling on the path and the path itself delegated to
a dot plot in which sixteen of eighteen see another hike.

    Just Voters adopt it, "restrictive" is retired and the path lengthens.
  • "clearly and at sufficient speed" / "this standard has not been
    satisfied" — Aug 28 personal → Sept 16 Committee finding. Stage:
    committee-level. Watch for echo in Paulson / Jefferson.
  • "Too many categories are still posting increases above 3 percent" — the
    breadth test in consensus text. Stage: committee-level. Williams's
    attribution test is now the minority language.
  • "relative price changes ... do not broaden" — supply-shock story
    inverted into a policy aim. Stage: opening statement.
  • "timelier" — in-statement. Test in October.
  • "inflation is a choice" — personal; new phrasing.
  • DROPPED from the Chair's repertoire this meeting: "play the ball, not the
