HEADLINE
─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
The Committee said nothing new and did something loud.

The substantive text of the statement is FROZEN. The economic-activity, labor,
and inflation paragraphs are verbatim identical to June 17 — not similar, not
lightly edited, character-for-character identical. The only textual change in
the entire document is two words of housekeeping ("reaffirmed" → "is
continuing"). The implementation note is likewise identical in every operative
provision; no balance-sheet change.

Against that frozen text, THREE voters dissented in favor of a 25bp HIKE. These
are the first hike-preferring dissents of the cycle. The vote went 12–0 → 9–3 in
a single meeting with no change in the language being voted on.

That combination is the signal. When the text does not move and the dissents do,
the dispersion is not being absorbed into the statement — it is being left
visible. Read the vote line, not the paragraphs.

THE VOTE — the entire directional content of this meeting
════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
July 29:   9–3. "Voting against the monetary policy action were Beth M.
           Hammack, Neel Kashkari, and Lorie K. Logan, who preferred to raise
           the target range for the federal funds rate by 1/4 percentage point
           at this meeting."
June 17:   12–0 unanimous.
April 29:  8–4 (Miran preferred a cut; Hammack, Kashkari, Logan opposed the
           easing-bias language).

THE HAWK ESCALATION LADDER — the same three names, three meetings, three rungs:
  April 29  → dissent against LANGUAGE (the easing bias)
  June 17   → fall in line once that language is removed (12–0)
  July 29   → dissent for an ACTUAL RATE CHANGE

This is the meaningful progression. In April the hawks were arguing about how
the statement described the future. Today they are voting for a different policy
rate. The identical trio held together across all three meetings, and their
objection escalated from wording to the instrument itself.

DIRECTION: HAWKISH. Unambiguously, and located entirely in the vote.

─── PARAGRAPH 1 — Policy decision + reserves ───
JUNE 17:  "The Committee decided to maintain the target range for the federal
           funds rate at 3-1/2 to 3-3/4 percent, in support of the Federal
           Reserve's dual mandate. The Committee REAFFIRMED its policy of
           maintaining ample reserves in the banking system."
JULY 29:  "The Committee decided to maintain the target range for the federal
           funds rate at 3-1/2 to 3-3/4 percent, in support of the Federal
           Reserve's dual mandate. The Committee IS CONTINUING its policy of
           maintaining ample reserves in the banking system."
CHANGE:   "reaffirmed" → "is continuing". The ONLY textual change in the
          statement.
DIRECTION: NEUTRAL — housekeeping, and it should be read as such. "Reaffirmed"
          was appropriate in June because the ample-reserves framework was being
          actively restated under a new Chair; "is continuing" is the steady-
          state descriptor once the framework is settled. The implementation
          note confirms this reading: every operative provision (IORB 3.65%,
          primary credit 3.75%, standing repo 3.75%, ON RRP 3.5% at a $160bn
          per-counterparty limit, bill purchases to maintain ample reserves,
          full reinvestment) is unchanged from June. Do not over-read this as a
          balance-sheet signal — there is no accompanying operational change.
          Worth one line of monitoring given Warsh's stated "leaner, meaner
          balance sheet" preference and his July 15 framing of the balance sheet
          as one of "two instruments" against inflation risks, but nothing in
          today's documents advances that.

─── PARAGRAPH 2 — Economic activity + labor ───
JUNE 17 and JULY 29 are VERBATIM IDENTICAL:
  "Economic activity is expanding at a solid pace despite elevated uncertainty
   that owes, in part, to the conflict in the Middle East. Productivity growth
   and capital investment are strong. Job gains have kept pace with the
   workforce, and the unemployment rate has changed little."
CHANGE:   None.
DIRECTION: NEUTRAL by text — but note what was NOT done. The labor line "job
          gains have kept pace with the workforce" was the under-appreciated
          hawkish reframing introduced in June (soft payrolls recast as ADEQUATE
          given slower labor-supply growth, rather than as a slowdown). The
          Committee had six weeks of additional labor data and chose to leave
          that framing untouched. Retaining it is a decision, not an oversight:
          the labor market remains formally unavailable as a rationale for
          easing.

─── PARAGRAPH 3 — Inflation ───
JUNE 17 and JULY 29 are VERBATIM IDENTICAL:
  "Inflation remains elevated relative to the Committee's 2 percent goal, in
   part reflecting supply shocks that have driven price increases in certain
   sectors, including energy. The Committee will deliver price stability."
CHANGE:   None.
DIRECTION: NEUTRAL by text — and this is the most consequential non-change in
          the document. The intervening June CPI and PPI prints were benign.
          Williams read them as "inflation has peaked." Goolsbee framed a
          conditional normalization path off them. Had the Committee wanted to
          validate that read, this paragraph is where it would have happened —
          softening "remains elevated," or qualifying the supply-shock
          attribution. It did neither. "The Committee will deliver price
          stability" — the declarative commitment Warsh introduced in June —
          survives intact for a second meeting.
          The Committee has now declined to bank one favorable inflation round.
          That is the hawks' argument (Logan, Jul 16: "one month of relief is
          not enough; it is time to finish the job") winning the drafting
          argument even as it lost the vote.

─── FORWARD GUIDANCE ───
Absent in June. Still absent in July. Confirmed against the official page.
The Warsh minimalist regime holds: the statement describes conditions and the
decision, and pre-commits to nothing. Consistent with his July 15 Senate
formulation, "play the ball, don't play the Fed."

WHAT THE STATEMENT DID NOT SAY — the deliberate omission
════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
Set the frozen text against the June SEP. That SEP was a wholesale hawkish
repricing: 2026 headline PCE +0.9 (2.7% → 3.6%), core +0.6, the median 2026 dot
at 3.8% — ABOVE the current 3.625% midpoint — and 9 of 18 participants
penciling at least one 2026 hike, up from ZERO in March.

Six weeks later, three voters formalized that into dissents. And the statement
language added not one hawkish word.

This is a widening DOTS-VS-STATEMENT GAP, and per the Language Cycle it is a
bottom-up leading indicator that has NOT yet consolidated. The individual
positioning (dots in June, dissents today) is running well ahead of the
collective language. Historically the statement is the LAST place a shift
appears. On that reading, the July statement is a lagging document and the 9–3
vote is the live one.

Two competing interpretations, and the September meeting resolves them:
  (a) WARSH IS HOLDING THE CENTER DELIBERATELY. He gave zero rate-path signal
      across two full days of congressional testimony (Jul 14–15), keeping
      "leave alone, raise, cut" all explicitly open 13 days out. Freezing the
      text is the same strategy: absorb three dissents rather than concede
      language that would pre-commit the Committee. Consistent with a Chair who
      has structurally rejected forward guidance.
  (b) THE CENTER IS SIMPLY NOT THERE YET. The median voter has not moved, the
      hawks are a visible but contained minority, and the language is frozen
      because there is no majority for anything else.
Both are consistent with today's documents. Distinguishing them requires the
minutes (~Aug 19) and the September SEP.

LOGAN VOTED HER BOOK, EXACTLY AS TELEGRAPHED:
  Jul 16 (Houston): hike as CURRENT BASE CASE — "modestly higher interest rates
  would better balance the dual mandate," "one month of relief is not enough; it
  is time to finish the job," "better modest restriction now than severe
  restriction later." She was the only voter with a stated base-case hike going
  into blackout. She dissented for precisely that. No new information in Logan's
  vote — but strong confirmation that her stated positions are votable.

WALLER DID NOT DISSENT — AND THIS IS THE MOST USEFUL DATA POINT IN THE DOCUMENT:
  His Jul 13 decision rule was explicit and dated: a HOT June core CPI/PPI print
  → "the FOMC will need to consider tightening monetary policy in the near
  term"; several months of lower readings → hold. The June prints came in
  benign. The trigger was NOT met. He voted with the majority.
  Waller's stated framework governed his vote. That materially raises the
  forecasting value of his conditionality going forward — he is now the single
  highest-information swing vote on the Committee, and his trigger is a
  published, testable data condition.
