RATE DECISION
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
HOLD: Target range maintained at 3-1/2 to 3-3/4 percent. Fifth consecutive hold.
Not a projection (SEP) meeting — July is an interim meeting; no dot plot released.

DISSENTS: Hammack (Cleveland), Kashkari (Minneapolis), Logan (Dallas) — all three
preferred to raise the target range by 1/4 percentage point (25bp hike).

Waller voted WITH the majority. Note: Waller's July 13 speech ("Monetary Policy at a
Crossroads") had set an explicit hike conditionality on hot CPI/PPI data this week. The
June CPI report (released ~July 15) came in cooler than expected, closing the door on a
July hike per Conference Board analysis — Waller's threshold was not triggered.

──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
¶3 — INFLATION + PRICE STABILITY COMMITMENT
──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
June:  "Inflation remains elevated relative to the Committee's 2 percent goal, in part
        reflecting supply shocks that have driven price increases in certain sectors,
        including energy. The Committee will deliver price stability."

July:  "Inflation remains SOMEWHAT elevated relative to the Committee's 2 percent goal,
        in part reflecting supply shocks that have driven price increases in certain
        sectors, including energy. The Committee will deliver price stability."

CHANGE: "elevated" → "somewhat elevated"

Assessment: DOVISH. This is the sole substantive change in the statement. Adding "somewhat"
signals the committee views inflation as having made progress and no longer at the same
severity level as June. It is a one-word softening. The structural cause language
("supply shocks... energy") and the price stability commitment ("will deliver price
stability") are unchanged. The "somewhat" qualifier appeared earlier in the 2026 cycle
before Warsh's arrival; its re-insertion here is directionally dovish but calibrated —
the committee is not declaring victory.

This is almost certainly what drove the three hawkish dissents: Hammack, Kashkari, and
Logan disagree that inflation warrants the softened characterization and are voting
for active tightening, not merely resisting dovish language as in April.

════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
DISSENT ANALYSIS — ESCALATION FROM APRIL
════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
April 29, 2026: Hammack, Kashkari, Logan dissented AGAINST the easing bias language
  (opposed forward guidance implying a next move would be a cut).

June 17, 2026: Easing bias removed → 12–0 UNANIMOUS. The three hawks were satisfied.

July 29, 2026: Hammack, Kashkari, Logan dissent FOR a 25bp HIKE. This is an escalation:
  they have moved from "oppose the committee's dovish framing" to "demand active tightening."
  The trigger appears to be: (1) addition of "somewhat elevated" language (dovish) and/or
  (2) their view that FFR at 3.50–3.75% is insufficiently restrictive given current
  inflation trajectory.

This is the largest hawkish dissent coalition since the 2022–2023 tightening cycle.
Three simultaneous dissents in favor of a hike from three of the four rotating regional
presidents who are voters this year (Paulson is the fourth, voted with majority).

Waller: Did NOT dissent despite his July 13 signal ("in the near term" hike if CPI/PPI
hot). The cooler June CPI print closed the door on a July hike for Waller. He sits at the
margin — he is the critical swing vote. Hot August CPI (released Sep ~10) could tip him
into the dissent column at the September meeting.

════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
NET READ
════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
The one-word change (elevated → somewhat elevated) signals the committee's majority
believes inflation is slowly moving in the right direction, but the three-vote hawkish
bloc (Hammack/Kashkari/Logan) disagrees — these are the most vocal dissents of Warsh's
tenure. Warsh is threading a needle: softening the inflation language modestly while
holding rates and refusing forward guidance. The 9-3 split shows the committee is more
divided than the June unanimous vote suggested. September is live. The pivot variable is
Waller: if August CPI data is hot, he has pre-committed to near-term tightening
consideration and could tip the September vote closer to a hike.
