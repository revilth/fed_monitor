TOP FINDINGS
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

1. PRESENT-TENSE HIKE CASE (new this meeting)
   "A few participants commented that... there was a case for raising the
   target range for the federal funds rate, but those participants
   indicated that they supported maintaining the current target range at
   this meeting."
   April's equivalent was purely conditional/future ("some policy firming
   would likely become appropriate IF inflation continues persistently
   above 2 percent"). June documents an actual case made and declined —
   not just a future reaction function.

2. THE NEUTRAL-RATE HEDGE PHRASE IS GONE
   March and April minutes both used: "policy rate was within the range
   of plausible estimates of its neutral level" — deliberately vague,
   spanning mildly-restrictive to neutral views.
   June instead: "Several participants remarked that they did not see the
   current policy stance as restrictive, while a few other participants
   commented that they saw the current policy stance as slightly
   restrictive."
   The vague hedge is replaced by an explicit split that resolves
   hawkish — the larger group says NOT restrictive at all; nobody is
   quoted calling policy meaningfully above neutral.

3. THE APRIL CUT CASE HAS NO JUNE EQUIVALENT
   April: "Several participants highlighted that it would likely be
   appropriate to lower the target range... once there are clear
   indications that disinflation is firmly back on track."
   June: no equivalent language anywhere in the monetary policy
   discussion. The near-term dovish case has dropped out, not just
   weakened.

4. COMMITTEE-WIDE NAMED SCENARIO FRAMING (bottom-up language cycle)
   "Most participants" discussed a BENIGN scenario (inflation dissipates
   → maintain or eventually lower) and an ADVERSE scenario (AI demand /
   Middle East / tariffs keep inflation elevated → policy firming
   warranted). This is Waller's Apr 17 named-scenario device, now
   appearing committee-wide in the minutes for the first time — one stage
   short of appearing in the statement itself.

5. LABOR RISK EASED, REMOVING A DOVISH COUNTERWEIGHT
   April: "most participants judged... risks to the employment side...
   tilted to the downside."
   June: "downside risks to achieving maximum employment had moderated a
   bit." Minutes explicitly self-reference the shift: "concerns earlier
   this year about labor market deterioration had eased with recent
   data."

6. DOT POSITIONING MATCHES THE SCORED SEP
   "Many participants... within or slightly below current range at
   year-end. Many other participants... above current range." Matches
   the SEP's 9-of-18 hike dots (0 of 19 in March), median 3.4%→3.8%,
   collapsed dovish tail. Warsh's own Q&A: "half of the members of the
   FOMC expect rate hikes as soon as this year."

GROWTH — brief
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Solid pace, AI investment "showing no signs of slowing." New compositional
flag: higher-income spending supported by stock gains/tax refunds; lower-
income households "increasingly relying on credit," facing "disproportion-
ate pressures" from gas/groceries — sharper than April's single-line
mention, and absent from both the opening statement and the public
statement. Direction: neutral-to-hawkish.

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
INFLATION — brief
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
"Increased further and remained well above" 2% (vs. April's "moved up").
Attribution widened to Strait of Hormuz closure + AI demand, beyond
tariffs. New broad-based sectoral characterization (transportation,
airfares, petrochemicals, agricultural inputs) not present in April. New
demand-side channel: growth exceeding potential (via AI investment) as an
inflation driver — absent from April entirely. Expectations-fragility
concern escalated from "several" to "majority." Direction: hawkish
escalation — the largest move of the three background topics.
