INFLATION
──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
June 17 statement: "Inflation remains elevated relative to the Committee's
2 percent goal, in part reflecting supply shocks that have driven price increases
in certain sectors, including energy. The Committee will deliver price stability."

July 29 statement: "Inflation remains SOMEWHAT elevated relative to the
Committee's 2 percent goal, in part reflecting supply shocks that have driven
price increases in certain sectors, including energy. The Committee will deliver
price stability."

CHANGE: "elevated" → "somewhat elevated" — the sole substantive word change
in the statement.

Verification against June 17 opening: The word "somewhat" was absent from the June 17
opening statement. This is a genuine addition. The "somewhat" qualifier appeared
earlier in the pre-Warsh 2026 cycle (before June); its re-insertion here is confirmed
as new under Warsh.

Opening statement additions (July 29 only):
  "We have begun a new chapter, and we understand that the five-plus years of
  inflation above target cannot be cured in nine weeks, or by a single month of
  modest price decreases. This Fed will not waver. Our credibility rests on
  performing our duties and delivering on our responsibilities."

  "There is no soft inflation target, there is no soft implicit target — not on
  this Committee's watch. There is only a target, and it is 2 percent."

Interpretation: The statement text softens ("somewhat elevated") while the surrounding
framing hardens ("nine weeks," "will not waver," "no soft target"). This is a
deliberate tension: the one-word softening acknowledges modest progress while the
framing sentences explicitly pre-empt any dovish misreading of that softening.
The "no soft target" line is the hardest inflation language Warsh has used in an
opening statement as Chair.

Direction: NEUTRAL-TO-HAWKISH. The "somewhat elevated" word is a dovish gesture
on the formal language metric; the surrounding context is more hawkish than June 17.
Net reading: the committee is acknowledging incremental progress while sharply
narrowing the interpretive space that progress affords.

vs. June 17 opening: HAWKISH LEAN on net — the opening statement framing is
harder despite the one-word softening. In June, the five task forces and the new
statement format were the news; here, the explicit no-soft-target declaration and
"will not waver" are the news.

Q&A BULLET 1 — DISSENTS: FRAMED AS FEATURE, NOT BUG
"I asked for a good family fight, and I got one. Most of our discussions were
on the big questions that matter to the conduct of monetary policy. We didn't
sort of hide from them."

BEYOND STATEMENT: The statement does not address dissents. In Q&A, Warsh
explicitly embraced the three hawkish dissents as evidence of healthy, substantive
deliberation. This is significant: he is not managing the dissents or minimizing
them; he is amplifying them as a credibility signal. The "family fight" framing
was pre-seeded (used 13 times across 5 prior public appearances) — it was not
improvised. Warsh entered this press conference expecting dissents and prepared
a public narrative that treats them as a strength. This is a departure from the
Powell-era practice of describing consensus or noting "participants broadly agreed."

Policy implication: Warsh is deliberately normalizing a more pluralistic and
contestatory committee culture. Three hawkish dissents demanding a hike are not
a defeat for Warsh — they are, in his framing, evidence that the committee is
taking the inflation problem seriously enough to have real disagreements.

Q&A BULLET 2 — BOND MARKET: YIELDS WELCOMED, NOT FEARED
"I think it's a good thing if the bond market moves based on economic data as
opposed to the Fed's policy." "Nominal and real yields are materially higher
across the Treasury curve" since the previous meeting.

BEYOND STATEMENT: The opening statement makes no reference to financial conditions
or the yield curve. In Q&A, Warsh cited higher long-term yields explicitly and
framed them as a positive — a sign that markets are pricing economic reality
rather than waiting on Fed guidance. This is the inversion of the pre-Warsh posture
(where long-rate spikes would be cited as a tightening of financial conditions
warranting a policy response). Warsh treats the 10-year at 4.67% and the 30-year
at 5.21% as a validation of the committee's credibility, not as a headwind.
[Caveat: this is a CNBC reconstruction; verify the exact framing in the official PDF.]

Market reaction: Bond market closed sharply higher in yield (10y +7bp, 30y +12bp)
and stocks sold off (Dow -2.19%, S&P -1.52%). CNBC headline: "Warsh says Fed won't
hesitate to stop inflation, but bond market has doubts." The bond market's response
suggests it did NOT read the "somewhat elevated" softening as a pivot signal and
was not satisfied by the Q&A's credibility framing.

Q&A BULLET 3 — CREDIBILITY SELF-ASSESSMENT: PERSONAL MILESTONE
"Markets broadly saying this FOMC has credibility to deliver on dual mandate."
"Feel better about FOMC's ability to deliver on inflation target than I did
initially."
[Both from CNBC clip excerpts — verify against PDF]

BEYOND STATEMENT: The opening statement carries the institutional commitment ("This
Committee will deliver price stability"). The Q&A adds Warsh's personal progress
assessment — he is explicitly saying the credibility-building exercise is working,
two meetings in. This is the Chair's subjective confidence assessment, not a data
characterization. "Feel better... than I did initially" is a first-person signal
that Warsh's own confidence in the committee's delivery has increased since June 17.
Combined with the "new chapter" framing, this is a Chair who believes the June 17
turning point was real and is consolidating it — not hedging.

Q&A BULLET 4 — HIKE CONDITIONALITY: NO HESITATION LANGUAGE
"I want to stress, of course, that decisions by this committee matter a great deal,
and where necessary and appropriate, we will not hesitate to act."

BEYOND STATEMENT: The statement holds rates and carries no guidance on direction.
The Q&A adds an implicit hike threat — "will not hesitate to act" is the Chair's
personal statement of willingness to tighten if needed. This is consistent with the
three dissents and the committee's internal dynamics (Waller is the swing vote; his
July 13 conditionality on hot CPI/PPI is still structurally in place even though
the June CPI print kept him with the majority this month). The no-hesitation language
is the Warsh Q&A equivalent of what the three dissenters expressed in their votes.

Q&A BULLET 5 — COMMUNICATIONS PHILOSOPHY: OPACITY AS CREDIBILITY SIGNAL
"Market participants are learning to play the ball, not the referee — and market
prices will continue to respond in the direction and magnitude they see fit. This
is, in my view, a change for the better."

BEYOND STATEMENT: This is Warsh's articulation of why a less communicative Fed is
a more credible one. Forward guidance absence is not described as a transitional
awkwardness but as a structural improvement. Markets are re-learning to price
fundamentals, not Fed signals — and Warsh is endorsing that adjustment. This is
Warsh's intellectual framework for the communications reform that was first
announced at the June 17 task forces.

Q&A BULLET 6 — INFLATION TARGET: NO-SOFT-TARGET (Q&A VERSION)
"There is no soft inflation target, there is no soft implicit target — not on
this Committee's watch. There is only a target, and it is 2 percent."

BEYOND STATEMENT: This line also appeared in the opening statement (see above), so
it straddles both sections. It bears noting in the Q&A section because it reinforces
that the "somewhat elevated" softening was NOT a signal that the committee is
drifting toward tolerance for 2.5% or reconsidering the target. The no-soft-target
declaration is the hardest, most explicit 2% defense Warsh has made in any
committee-level document, going beyond the June 17 Q&A's "zero is to the right of
the decimal point" formulation.
