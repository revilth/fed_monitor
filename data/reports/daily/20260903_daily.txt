1.  CHRISTOPHER J. WALLER  |  Governor  |  Just Voter
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

GROWTH
  "Solid pace" (statement language). Real GDP 1.8% annualized in H1; real PDFP
  "a strong 3 percent"; full-year "a bit more than 2 percent." Weak July retail
  sales attributed to Amazon Prime Day moving to June — "Outside of 'non-store'
  sales, retail spending grew." He rejects the AI-composition discount that
  Cook and Daly have raised: "Some would argue that this investment ... is
  misleadingly propping up GDP and should in some sense be discounted. I don't
  agree." Unchanged from July 13; no easing case from growth.
  Direction: neutral

LABOR MARKET
  "Satisfactory shape." Job creation averaging 60,000/month through July,
  "close to and probably a bit above" breakeven "mostly because of much lower
  net immigration"; unemployment 4.1%, "slightly below the median of FOMC
  participants' long-run or equilibrium rate"; 2025's signs of easing demand
  "evaporated"; "employment near its maximum sustainable level." The −23K July
  print is absorbed into the average without being named. Then the gatekeeper:
  "they are not a large factor in my determination of the appropriate setting
  of monetary policy." Third official in a week (Schmid, Warsh, Waller) to
  supply a mechanism for reading low payroll gains as non-signal, the day
  before the August report: "I expect more of the same tomorrow."
  Direction: neutral, unchanged — and explicitly excluded from his decision

INFLATION
  "Elevated significantly above" target, "five and a half years" — then the
  turn: "recent data suggest we are finally seeing some signs of
  disinflation." The supply shocks are conceded as the source of the level and
  denied as ongoing pressure: tariffs "have largely passed through," the
  energy bleed "hasn't come to pass." Twelve-month PCE 3.7% / core 3.3% "are
  not the best guide for where inflation is today"; his measure is momentum:
  "Three-month core inflation is 3.05 percent ... has fallen steadily from
  4.76 percent in February ... the speed of this downward trajectory is
  encouraging" — the momentum answer to Warsh's "at sufficient speed"
  standard, reaching the opposite verdict. New: nonmarket (imputed) services
  were "approximately half of the increase in core prices" — "ignoring this
  one factor, my take is that underlying inflation is doing better than the
  core numbers suggest" — and a pending Commerce Department revision "could
  lower 12-month PCE inflation by a few tenths." Upside risks named (energy
  up again, AI-related tech goods prices, more tariffs); wages consistent with
  2% after productivity; expectations a risk "the FOMC should be prepared to
  act on." July 13's breadth statistic and "upward trajectory" are gone.
  Direction: dovish

MONETARY POLICY
  Explicit, named, dated two-branch conditional: "If this continues in the
  data due over the next two weeks, I would be inclined to support holding
  ... If the incoming data for August show this improvement has been
  fleeting, then it may be appropriate to raise the policy rate when the FOMC
  meets on September 15 and 16." Restated at the close: "if inflation comes in
  hot, I would consider a rate hike." Stance: "I judge that policy is
  currently only slightly restricting aggregate demand, and it may not take
  much acceleration in inflation to nudge me into supporting tighter policy
  ... a small adjustment in our stance would help ensure that it resumes."
  "Only slightly restricting" places him with the "mildly restrictive" hold
  majority, not with Warsh's "hard pressed to describe ... as restrictive" —
  but with a thinner margin and a hair trigger. Cut signal: absent. Hike
  signal: present as a branch, no longer the near-term consideration of July
  13.
  On communication: three types — explaining the current position; the
  reaction function ("not a commitment to a policy action—it is a conditional
  policy statement"); forward guidance (data-independent path, for the lower
  bound). "I agree with Chairman Warsh that forward guidance isn't appropriate
  now or in many other situations. But when it is truly needed, I believe it
  should be used." He defends the reaction function Warsh declined at Jackson
  Hole ("our knowledge just doesn't extend that far"): "I do not let
  perfection become the enemy of the good," and inverts the Chair's idiom —
  "Both want to play the ball, but they cannot do that until they know the
  umpire's strike zone. The strike zone is the umpire's reaction function."
  Direction: dovish lean on the base case; hike branch live

NET SIGNAL: DOVISH LEAN vs. July 13 — the Committee's most explicit hike
  conditional resolved to a hold base case after the trigger did not fire;
  held short of plainly dovish by "only slightly restricting," "may not take
  much," and a named September hike branch keyed to the August print.

vs. PRIOR SPEECH (Jul 13):              DOVISH on the base case — "several
  months of lower readings" → hold is now the inclination; "hot reading →
  FOMC will need to consider tightening in the near term" → hike only if
  August is "fleeting." Growth and labor unchanged. New stance descriptor.
  July 13's anti-overtightening guardrails not repeated.
vs. PRE-FOMC SPEECH (Jul 13):           Same document — July 13 is both
  comparators (no intervening communication).
vs. COMMITTEE SINCE FOMC:              On the trend, with Williams (Sep 2) by
  a different test and against Warsh's breadth verdict. On the stance, with
  Paulson, Collins and Daly ("mildly restrictive"), not with Warsh, Schmid or
  the dissenters. On labor, with Warsh and Schmid in pre-discounting weak
  payrolls. On communication, the first official to align with the Chair on
  forward guidance while defending reaction functions against him.
