GROWTH
  Raised via the bond-yield question: higher yields reflect "a strong U.S.
  economy and a strong economic outlook fueled by big investments in AI and
  data centers." "Strong" is his adjective (statement: "solid"; his own Aug 3:
  "solid, but not more than solid"). He concedes "some crowding out" of
  interest-sensitive sectors and attributes it to the investment surge, not to
  policy. The strength is used to explain yields, not to argue for tightening.
  Direction: hawkish lean on the read; neutral in use

LABOR MARKET
  Not asked. One passing line: "The labor market is solid. It's stable." The
  July 15 wording is back (Aug 3 had "stable" alone). This is his first
  communication since the −23K July payroll and it does not engage it — the
  anchor's lead-in was walking through payroll-by-sector figures and no
  question followed. Per the Type J rule, the brevity is not a signal.
  Direction: neutral, unchanged

INFLATION
  He echoes the statement's supply-shock attribution and puts it first —
  tariffs, Middle East energy effects "and broader commodity prices are the
  biggest drivers of inflation" — the clearest defense of that clause by any
  post-FOMC speaker (Warsh did not use it Aug 28; Waller says the shocks are
  spent). Core "running around 3.3%." His test is attribution: "we're not
  seeing kind of second round effects or broadening of the tariffs. We're not
  seeing unusual broadening of the effects of higher energy prices." Verdict:
  "I am actually ... seeing ... the trend in inflation moving slowly down as
  some of the effects of the tariffs can move into the rear view mirror. But
  we've got to be data dependent." Aug 3 gave this as forecast; Sep 2 gives it
  as observed. Both he and Warsh say "disaggregate" — Warsh counts breadth
  (54% of the basket above 3%) and finds no meaningful improvement; Williams
  tests for broadening and finds a trend moving down.
  Direction: dovish lean

MONETARY POLICY
  Hold-anchored: "I obviously supported our decision not to raise rates in the
  last meeting ... it was let's get some more data." Yields: "We're not seeing
  it in terms of inflation compensation. We're seeing it mostly in real rates
  ... it's not really about financial conditions affecting the economy. It's
  more about the economy affecting financial conditions." Asked whether the
  market can do the work: "That's job number one. So that's our job. And you
  know, nobody else can do that for us" — Aug 3's "we have to do the work
  ourselves," firmer. Markets are "inputs into my thinking," subordinate to
  "the totality of the data." The bar: "not only that we are on that path, but
  we are on that path to do that ... in the foreseeable future" — and the
  clip ends there. Cut signal: absent. Hike signal: absent in the published
  segment; Aug 3's direction-unnamed conditional-to-act does not appear, but
  the truncation means this is not scored as a retraction.
  Direction: neutral (hold-anchored)

NET SIGNAL: DOVISH LEAN (mild) — stance unchanged; the inflation read moves

  while the yield rise is read as a consequence of strong investment rather
