DESCRIPTIVE NOTE (media coverage; not a scored assessment):
  Williams gave a live CNBC "Squawk Box" interview on the morning of
  September 2, conducted by Steve Liesman. Four themes emerged from
  published media coverage (Bloomberg, Reuters, CNBC, FXStreet):

  BOND YIELDS / FINANCIAL CONDITIONS
    Williams characterized the recent surge in Treasury yields as
    reflecting a strong economic outlook rather than market dysfunction:
    "the recent surge in Treasury yields is the product of a strong
    economy, not market dysfunction." He framed rising yields as an
    informational signal rather than a policy complication, and
    separately noted that Treasury debt-management actions "don't
    complicate my job or our job making monetary policy."
    [Reported as slightly hawkish framing: higher yields = strong
    economy = policy stance appropriate. Does NOT echo Warsh's
    Jackson Hole concern about financial conditions being insufficiently
    tight — Williams is less alarmed by financial conditions.]

  INFLATION
    Williams struck a more optimistic tone on inflation than most
    of his recent peers: "I am actually seeing the trend in inflation
    moving slowly down as some of the effects of the tariffs move
    into the rearview mirror." He added that energy prices are not
    spreading into other services sectors and that inflation
    expectations "remain well anchored."
    [Notable contrast: Warsh (Aug 28), Barr (Sep 1), and the July 29
    minutes all emphasize upside inflation risk and the risk of
    de-anchoring. Williams is expressing greater confidence in
    disinflation. This is a Troika-level signal with potential cycle
    significance if it represents a genuine majority-bloc view distinct
    from the hawkish dissenters.]

  MONETARY POLICY / SEPTEMBER MEETING
    On the September rate decision, Williams declined to pre-commit:
    "we have to wait and see." He acknowledged policy uncertainty:
    "There's no clear science that says monetary policy is currently in
    the right position to accomplish the Fed's objectives and lower
    inflation to target in the next year or so." When asked about a
    rate hike, he said the decision "will depend on the data and depend
    on some of the risks to achieve our goals."
    [Data-dependent framing. He did not signal a September hike,
    distinguishing himself from Barr's conditional hike scenario
    (September 1) and the Hammack/Kashkari/Logan base-case hike
    positions. Within the Troika, Williams is signaling patience rather
    than conditionality — a potential moderating signal within the
    current hawkish cycle if confirmed by the official transcript.]

  CANDIDATE SIGNAL (pending transcript confirmation):
    IF these reported quotes are confirmed verbatim, Williams's
