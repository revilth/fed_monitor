1.  KEVIN WARSH  |  Chair  |  Troika
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

GROWTH
  "I am impressed by the overall performance of the economy, which appears to
  have strengthened" — one step beyond the statement's "solid pace." Capex in
  equipment and intangibles up ~9% four-quarter, "its highest growth rate
  since 2021," more than half AI-related; S&P 500 profits up more than 20%;
  PDFP running near 3% this year; consumption above 2%. Credit spreads near
  historical lows and bank C&I standards on the easier end: "Credit and loan
  markets are showing few signs of policy restraint." The one hedge is that he
  will watch "the second derivative" of capex and earnings growth.
  Direction: hawkish lean

LABOR MARKET
  Statement language echoed ("quite stable," 4.1%, "has not changed much for a
  couple of years"), then a completion claim that is new for him: "as of now, I
  believe the labor markets are consistent with full employment." The
  analytically important addition is structural: "When labor supply is barely
  growing, monthly job gains are naturally going to run low." The −23K July
  payroll is not mentioned; the sentence supplies a reason not to read low job
  gains as weakness, ahead of the August report on September 4.
  Direction: hawkish lean

INFLATION
  Levels stated plainly — 12-month PCE 3.7%, six-month 4.1% — with the
  statement's supply-shock attribution left unused: "Inflation is running
  above our 2 percent target. So the Fed's predominant focus right now should
  be on prices." He then discloses the measure behind the "underlying
  inflation" reaction function he gave on July 29: "disaggregate the 199
  individual components of the PCE price measure ... 54 percent of goods and
  services in the PCE basket showed price increases above 3 percent ... well
  above the level of 32 percent in the two decades that preceded the
  pandemic"; 49 percent over six months. A breadth measure, slow-moving by
  construction. The verdict on the summer data: "they do not tell me that
  underlying trends have meaningfully improved." The standard: "We must be
  confident that underlying inflation is moving to our objective, clearly and
  at sufficient speed. Otherwise, we have work to do." Target restated as 2%
  PCE, "firm, fixed," and "nor is inflation necessarily mean-reverting." Wage
  growth dismissed as an indicator; expectations anchored but "they tend to
  look strong and durable until they don't." Duration clock now "65 months."
  Direction: hawkish

MONETARY POLICY
  No decision, no path, no reference to September, the SEP or the dissents:
  "committed to a discipline, not to a decision." Three things do move. (1)
  Stance: "on balance, I would be hard pressed to describe broad financial
  conditions as restrictive" — a level judgment, where July 29 spoke of the
  change ("a material tightening ... in real rates"). The July hold leaned on
  that tightening; he is no longer using the argument. (2) Instrument: "short-
  term interest rates are the predominant tool," unconventional policy "used
  sparingly, if at all" — narrowing the balance-sheet route he had raised on
  July 15 ("two instruments") and July 29 ("how much accommodation are we
  getting from the balance sheet?"). (3) Guidance: forward guidance "has
  overstayed its welcome"; an explicit reaction function declined ("our
  knowledge just doesn't extend that far") five weeks after he volunteered a
  directional one in the July 29 Q&A — a rule-versus-disposition distinction he
  does not draw himself. His summary of the July minutes ("a good majority ...
  thought the wiser course was to await new information") omits the "several
  participants" who favored a 25bp increase and the "many participants" who
  expected tightening if inflation did not decline. AI is set aside from the
  current policy conjuncture entirely. Cut signal: absent. Hike signal: absent
  as a signal — "Otherwise, we have work to do" is undirected in instrument
  and timing.
  Direction: hawkish lean

NET SIGNAL: HAWKISH LEAN — the grounds for patience are addressed one by one
  (growth strengthened; labor at full employment with low payrolls pre-
  explained; conditions not restrictive; wages uninformative; expectations
  anchored "until they don't"; summer prints discounted) and a published
  standard is reported unmet. Only the decision is withheld.

vs. PRIOR SPEECH (Jul 29 press conference):   HAWKISH — the reaction
  function's input is now disclosed and a standard stated; the financial-
  conditions claim moved from change to level; rates named the predominant
  tool. "We will not hesitate to act" became "we have work to do."
vs. PRE-FOMC SPEECH (Jul 14–15 testimony):    HAWKISH — testimony kept
  leave / raise / cut all open and treated benign prints as "imperfect
  measures"; he now supplies his own measure and a verdict.
vs. COMMITTEE SINCE FOMC:               On the diagnosis — restrictiveness,
  breadth, non-mean-reversion — his language now sits with the three
  dissenters, Schmid (Aug 27) and the minutes' "some participants." On the
  instrument he is with the majority: rates, not the balance sheet, and not
  yet. His breadth measure is the direct counterpart to Paulson's Aug 4
  underlying estimate of "2.4, 2.8" (tariff and energy effects stripped out);
  the two methods read the same data in opposite directions.

LANGUAGE TO WATCH:
  • "clearly and at sufficient speed" — the stated standard. DISPERSED (first
    use). If a second official adopts a "sufficient" test, the Committee has
    a shared bar.
  • 199-component PCE breadth (share above 3%) — DISPERSED (Chair only;
    Waller's Jul 13 core-services breadth is the nearest prior). Watch the
    September minutes and SEP discussion.
  • "hard pressed to describe broad financial conditions as restrictive" —
    EMERGING (minutes "some participants"; Hammack, Kashkari, Logan; Schmid;
    now the Chair). The proposition on which a September hold rests.
  • "nor is inflation necessarily mean-reverting" — EMERGING (Kashkari's
    successive-shocks argument; minutes "many participants ... more
    persistently elevated").
  • "65 months" — duration clock, third consecutive document, incrementing.
  • "committed to a discipline, not to a decision" — the no-guidance formula.

ANALYST FLAG: YES
  → (1) The standard is public and unmet by his own account. Whether "work to
    do" means the rate in September or continued observation is unresolved.
  → (2) Breadth barely moves on one soft print. Under this reaction function
    the August CPI/PCE matter less for the Chair than for officials using
    momentum or attribution tests.
  → (3) [Inference] Footnote 7 cites a Board paper ("Anchored to the Dot
    Plot") on expectations adhering to prior SEP projections, three weeks
    before a projection meeting. He does not connect them. Low confidence,
    high consequence if the September SEP is restructured or caveated.
  → (4) The minutes characterization omits the hike caucus — same device as
    July 29 (unity on the objective; dispersion on the path unmentioned).
