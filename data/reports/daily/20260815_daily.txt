DATA CONTEXT — WEEK OF AUGUST 11–15, 2026
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
The data prints released this week constitute the primary
inter-meeting data arc between the July 29 FOMC decision and the
August 19 minutes release. All three prints bear on the dissenter
vs. majority argument:

JULY CPI (released August 12, 8:30am ET):
  Headline:  +0.1% MoM  |  3.4% YoY (down from 3.5% in June)
  Core:      +0.2% MoM  |  2.5% YoY (down from 2.6% in June)
  Read: DOVISH vs. June. Two consecutive soft MoM readings (June
  +0.2%, July +0.1%). Annual core at 2.5% — lowest in the cycle.
  Waller's Jul 13 hike conditionality was explicitly tied to
  whether the subsequent CPI cleared his threshold — the July
  print appears to have done so, consistent with his July 29
  majority vote to hold.

JULY PPI (released August 13, 8:30am ET):
  Final demand: 0.0% MoM  |  4.7% YoY (down from 5.5% in June)
  Core PPI:    +0.2% MoM  |  4.2% YoY (down from 4.7% in June)
  Goods: -0.7% MoM (energy -3.1%, food -0.9%)  |  Services: +0.2%
  Read: DOVISH vs. June. Unchanged headline and sharp annual
  deceleration. Goods decline is energy-driven; services +0.2%
  contained. Leading indicator for PCE — suggests June PCE spike
  (headline 3.7%, core 3.3%) may moderate in upcoming data.

RETAIL SALES — JULY (released August 14, 8:30am ET):
  Headline:  -0.6% MoM (vs. +0.1%–+0.3% consensus)
  Ex-autos and gas: -0.2% MoM  |  YoY: +5.0%
  Read: SIGNIFICANTLY BELOW EXPECTATIONS. Largest monthly decline
  since May 2025. Consumers pulled back on online stores and auto
  dealers. Policy implication: demand-side consumer spending —
  the primary support for the dissenters' "more hikes needed" case
  — is softening. Weakens the Hammack/Logan/Kashkari demand-side
  inflation argument (Hammack Jul 31: "inflationary pressures
  coming from the demand side").

U. OF MICHIGAN CONSUMER SENTIMENT — AUGUST (preliminary, Aug 14):
  Index fell ~8% from July (two consecutive months of improvement
  reversed). Cited driver: Iran war pushing up fuel, energy, and
  food prices. 1-year inflation expectations data: watch for any
  deanchoring — that would be the strongest counter-argument to
  the disinflationary CPI/PPI read.

NET DATA READ — WEEK OF AUGUST 11–15:
  The CPI, PPI, and retail sales trifecta is the softest
  consecutive data week of the post-July-29 inter-meeting window.
  Taken together: inflation momentum is cooling sequentially
  (monthly core CPI and PPI both ≤0.2% for two months), and
  consumer demand is softening faster than expected (-0.6% retail).
  This data set modestly supports the hold majority's position and
  modestly undermines the dissenters' demand-side attribution.
  The data does NOT close the September hike question — annual
  inflation (CPI 3.4%, PPI YoY 4.7%) remains significantly above
  2%, and the Iran-war energy channel could reverse goods
  deflation. The FOMC minutes (Aug 19) and Jackson Hole speeches
  (Aug 27–29, esp. Warsh keynote) are the next inflection points.
