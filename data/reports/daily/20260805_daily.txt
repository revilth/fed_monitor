THE DAY IN ONE LINE
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
The first member of the nine-vote July 29 majority to speak since the meeting
softened the risk architecture underneath an unchanged hawkish verdict — and
declined to defend the statement's supply-shock attribution without qualifying it.

WHY THIS SPEECH MATTERS
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Everything the Committee has said since July 29 came from the three dissenters
(Hammack, Kashkari, Logan, all July 31). Cook is the first majority voter to
speak. She is also the majority-side voter who carried the most explicit hike
conditional into the meeting. Her August 3 flag in this report series — "whether
she renews or retreats is the highest-priority data point of the week" — is now
answered, and the answer is: she did both.

1.  COOK  |  Governor  |  Just Voter
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

GROWTH
  Adopts the July 29 statement's adjective directly — "Overall economic growth
  in the U.S. remains solid this year" — and adds an acceleration call: output
  grew 1.8 percent in H1 and "is on track to grow at a faster pace in the second
  half." Business investment up at a 10 percent annual rate; consumer spending
  near 2 percent; housing the one soft spot, residential investment down about
  3 percent. She is not forecasting demand-side disinflation from slowing
  activity — the opposite — which matters given that all three of her named
  disinflation channels are supply- or base-effect channels.
  Direction: neutral, marginal hawkish lean

LABOR MARKET
  Verdict unchanged: "the labor market appears to be stable, in a low-hire,
  low-fire environment," unemployment 4.2 percent and "barely changed from a
  year earlier." New quantification on the firm side — job growth "picked up
  during the spring months, averaging more than 100,000 jobs added per month in
  the April through June period," comfortably above breakeven against the
  shrinking labor force she describes. Her AI-displacement caveat and her
  low-hire bounding are near-verbatim carry-overs from July 15 and carry no
  signal. Notably, the July 15 sentence that sourced her risk-balance tilt to
  the employment side ("risks on the employment side have diminished") is not
  repeated — the tilt survives, but its driver moved.
  Direction: neutral

INFLATION
  Level verdict recycled intact — "my assessment is simple: Inflation is too
  high," above target "for more than five years," PCE 3.7 percent through June
  (same figure as July 15). Underneath it, the risk architecture inverted. On
  July 15 she named two channels by which risks were "strongly weighted toward
  higher inflation"; today she names three disinflationary forces: tariff
  pass-through dropping out of the 12-month window, oil prices expected to fall
  by year-end, and AI-buildout price pressure easing as supply chains adjust.
  The third is a direct reversal — the AI buildout was her single
  most-emphasized escalation vector three weeks ago ("does not show signs of
  slowing … $1.5 trillion in data center plans, only a small portion of which
  has been realized"). She also concedes June "improved modestly" in her own
  voice, and the striking core-goods breadth alarm from July 15 (goods at a
  "striking 5 percent annual pace") is gone. Offsetting all of it, the
  entrenchment warning is sharpened to a new closing line: "while we might be
  able to afford to wait for longer in a different environment, we do not have
  that luxury in this one."
  Direction: dovish lean on the architecture; verdict unchanged

  ⚠ THE STATEMENT ATTRIBUTION — the week's central question, answered.
  The July 29 statement attributes elevated inflation "in part reflecting supply
  shocks … including energy." Hammack and Logan attacked that attribution from
  the demand side on July 31. Cook, from the majority side, declines to defend
  it unqualified: energy "contributed significantly … but it is not the only
  factor," with core at 3.3 percent as the evidence. She then names her second
  source — the AI capital-spending wave, which "has lifted prices for
  semiconductors, high-tech equipment, software, and utilities." [Inference:
  that is a demand-driven relative price shift, not a supply shock in the
  statement's sense — so she concedes part of the dissenters' premise while
  refusing their framing and their conclusion. She does not characterize it as
  demand-side herself.]

MONETARY POLICY
  The conditional is now explicit in direction: "I am prepared to act by raising
  rates, if necessary" — July 15 read only "I am prepared to act." She remains
  the only majority-side voter carrying a directional rate conditional. But in
  the same passage she adds two things with no July 15 equivalent: a stated cost
  to hiking ("The labor market and output growth are currently stable. I would
  consider how a rate increase could negatively affect that stability") and a
  flat escape clause — "Still, I would support an increase, if it becomes
  necessary to bring inflation down. It may not." Her trigger also moved by one
  word, from "signs of disinflation" to "signs of CONTINUED disinflation,"
  which — read with her concession that June improved — presupposes the process
  has started. She grounds the hold in her three disinflation channels: "For
  these three reasons, I felt it was appropriate not to change rates." No
  characterization of restrictiveness appears anywhere; the wire-reported
  "mildly restrictive" framing from her July 15 Q&A did NOT migrate into
  prepared text.
  Cut signal status:  ABSENT (May 27 labor backstop still dropped)
  Hike signal status: PRESENT, direction named, condition materially weakened
  Direction: dovish lean

EMBEDDED TOPIC — CONSUMER SENTIMENT
  Routes a conventionally dovish input to a hawkish conclusion. Sentiment is
  "lower than one would expect in a solid labor market" and "perceptions of job
  availability have continued to worsen" — the one genuinely soft labor data
  point in the speech, filed under sentiment rather than under the labor market,
  where it does not touch her verdict. Three causes: AI job uncertainty,
  decades-long structural cost increases, and five years of high inflation. The
  conversion: "As a monetary policymaker, I believe that the best thing we can
  do in our roles is to ensure that inflation returns to and stays at target."

NET SIGNAL: DOVISH LEAN — she made the hike conditional more explicit and its
  condition less likely to fire, in the same paragraph.

vs. PRIOR SPEECH (July 15, 2026):        dovish lean — see below; July 15 is
  also the pre-FOMC baseline, so the two comparisons are identical and are
  reported once. This is Cook's first communication of any kind since July 29.
  The verdict ("too high"), the 3.7 percent figure, the five-year duration
  argument and the labor verdict are all carried over intact. What changed:
  three disinflation channels replace two escalation channels; the AI buildout
  crosses from the risk column to the disinflation column; June's improvement is
  conceded; the core-goods breadth alarm is dropped; a cost-of-hiking caveat and
  "It may not" are added; the trigger softens to "continued" disinflation.
  Against that: the hike direction is named for the first time and the
  entrenchment warning is sharpened.

vs. PRE-FOMC SPEECH (July 15, 2026):     same comparison — see above.

vs. COMMITTEE SINCE FOMC:                Cook is the fourth official to speak
  since July 29 and the first from the majority. The three dissenters (July 31)
  all argued the statement understates the problem and the Fed should already be
  hiking. Cook agrees the level is unacceptable and agrees the supply-shock
  attribution is incomplete — then splits from them on the remedy, arguing the
  correction is already in train. The majority's first answer to the 9–3 split
  is "the process is working," not "you are wrong about the level." That is a
  narrower disagreement than the vote suggests.

LANGUAGE TO WATCH:
  • "but it is not the only factor" (on the statement's supply-shock
    attribution) — now the most contested formulation of the cycle, qualified
    by two dissenters from the demand side and by a majority voter from the
    composition side. If Jefferson, Williams or Paulson also decline to defend
    it unqualified, the inflation sentence is the language most likely to change
    in the September statement. Stage: EMERGING.
  • "prepared to act by raising rates" — direction now named. Waller carries a
    data-triggered version (July 13). Stage: EMERGING (two Just Voters).
  • "continued disinflation" — the changed trigger word. Adoption by others
    would signal the majority has collectively accepted disinflation has begun.
    Stage: DISPERSED (one speaker).
  • AI buildout as a source of coming disinflation — reverses the prevailing
    committee treatment of AI capex as an inflationary demand shock. If it
    spreads, the committee's inflation-risk assessment falls with no data
    changing. Stage: DISPERSED (one speaker, new).
  • "we do not have that luxury in this one" — rhetorically close to Hammack's
    July 31 "the longer that high inflation persists, the more challenging and
    costly it can be to bring it back down." A majority voter and a dissenter
    converging on the persistence argument while disagreeing on the action.
    Stage: EMERGING.

ANALYST FLAG: YES
  → PRIMARY: the AI-buildout reversal. Reason #1 for higher inflation risk on
    July 15; a source of coming disinflation on August 5. No obvious data
    between the two dates forces the change — but the July 28–29 meeting sits
    between them. Is this Cook updating on evidence, or the majority position
    she signed onto appearing in her prepared text? The July 28–29 minutes
    (August 19) will show whether the participant discussion of AI capex moved
    the same way. Highest-value check available before Jackson Hole.
  → The trigger word. "Signs of disinflation" → "signs of CONTINUED
    disinflation." Does "continued" lower her bar (she needs only continuation
    from June) or raise it (she demands a sustained run)? The text does not
    settle it. The July PCE print is the first test, and her reading of it
    determines whether the most explicit hike conditional on the majority side
    survives into September.
  → Her three disinflation channels are all supply-side or base-effect while
    her growth call is for H2 ACCELERATION — she expects disinflation with no
    demand moderation. If oil does not fall by year-end as she assumes ("many
    forecasters suggest"), two of the three channels are gone and only the
    tariff base effect remains.
