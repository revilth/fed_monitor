SPEECH 1 — "Initial Findings from Independent Review of Silicon Valley Bank"
Classification: Type B — supervisory reform announcement.
Content: Bowman announced initial findings of the independent Starling Advisory
  Group review of SVB's March 2023 collapse. Seven key findings: supervisory staff
  knew of SVB vulnerabilities as early as March 2022 but did not take timely action;
  root cause was a culture of risk aversion and unclear decision rights, NOT
  regulatory tailoring or direction from the former Vice Chair for Supervision;
  social media did NOT trigger or accelerate the bank run (Charles River Associates
  analysis). Announced remedial actions already underway: Supervisory Operating
  Principles statement; examiners to submit monthly escalation reports to leadership.
Policy signal: none. Regulatory significance: high (first formal independent
  accountability assessment of SVB; frames Bowman's supervisory reform agenda).

SPEECH 2 — "The Final Chapter on Modernizing Bank Regulatory Stress Testing"
Classification: Type B — stress testing framework finalization.
Content: Previewed the upcoming final rules on enhanced transparency and public
  accountability for the bank stress capital buffer framework. Key elements:
  publication of stress test model equations, coefficients, assumptions, and
  scenario-design process; reduction of year-over-year capital requirement
  volatility; introduction of forward-looking stress exercises (results would NOT
  affect capital requirements — informational/supervisory only). Basel III capital
  reforms and G-SIB surcharge revisions expected to be finalized by year-end 2026.
Policy signal: none. Regulatory significance: high (completes the stress-testing
  modernization program Bowman began on confirmation as VCS).

NET SIGNAL (Bowman): No monetary policy content. First post-FOMC day used by
  Bowman for regulatory announcements from a pre-planned London engagement.
  No economic outlook signals to score.

GROWTH
  "Overall economic growth is solid, supported by continued consumer spending
  and strong business investment" — a close paraphrase of the statement's
  "solid pace" and "domestic spending has been resilient." "Growth resilient"
  is his own word since July 16. The Aug 27 AI/data-center crowding-out
  argument is not in this short outlook preface.
  Direction: neutral

LABOR MARKET
  "The labor market appears to be in balance. The unemployment rate, at 4.1
  percent, is in the range of what most observers would consider to be full
  employment." "Full employment" is new for Schmid — Aug 4 used "roughly in
  balance ... neither too tight nor too loose" — and matches the Chair's Sep
  16 opening statement ("consistent with full employment"). Slow job growth
  is "not unexpected given the aging of the workforce and an increase in the
  number of retirements" (Aug 4 cited "a labor force that is barely growing").
  Direction: hawkish lean (small)

INFLATION
  Inflation "has remained too high for too long," above 2 percent "for over
  five years now," with recent readings "trending above 3 percent." The core
  argument carries over nearly verbatim from Aug 4: "our inflation problem is
  not just about energy ... a broad range of goods and services are showing
  price growth inconsistent with our price stability target," and "the Fed,
  through its influence on demand, always has a role to play when it comes to
  keeping inflation in check." The statement's deletion of the supply-shock
  clause moved the committee toward the position he has argued since July.
  Direction: hawkish lean

MONETARY POLICY
  "On Wednesday the Federal Open Market Committee voted to raise the policy
  interest rate and to tighten the stance of monetary policy. I supported this
  decision." Citing Warsh's Jackson Hole remarks: "the Fed has work to do on
  inflation and this week's action was a step in that direction." Inflation is
  his "primary concern as I think about the correct course for monetary
  policy." No stance label for the new range: his Aug 4 "I do not see the
  current stance of monetary policy as restrictive" and Aug 27 "fairly
  accommodative" are neither restated nor withdrawn. No path and no condition.
  [Inference: "a step" implies he sees the job as incomplete.]
  Direction: hawkish lean

NET SIGNAL: HAWKISH LEAN — first-person endorsement of the hike as "a step";
  confirmation of his August position rather than an escalation.

vs. PRIOR SPEECH (Aug 27, Bloomberg Odd Lots interview): unchanged — "We
