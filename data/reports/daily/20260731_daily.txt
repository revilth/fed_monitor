THE DAY IN ONE LINE
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Three voters published formal statements on the same day arguing the Fed should
be raising rates. It is the first time this cycle that a hike case has been made
on the record by a bloc rather than an individual, and — more consequentially —
all three moved their dissent from the Committee's WORDS to the Committee's RATE.

1.  BETH M. HAMMACK  |  Cleveland Fed President  |  Just Voter
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

GROWTH
  No standalone assessment. Growth appears only as the thing a hike is meant to
  restrain — "A higher federal funds rate would help restrain economic activity
  and reduce inflationary pressures" — with no growth-side risk named anywhere.
  Consistent with June 2's resilient read.
  Direction: neutral (hawkish by implication)

LABOR MARKET
  Labor is the permission slip, not a concern: "Given the stability of the labor
  market, with the unemployment rate near my estimate of maximum employment, I
  view high inflation as the more pressing problem." The near-full-employment
  framing is carry-over — it appears in her May 1 statement and her June 2 speech
  in almost identical words, so it is not a shift.
  Direction: neutral-to-hawkish (labor read as headroom to tighten)

INFLATION
  The decisive sentence is an attribution move against the FOMC statement itself.
  The July 29 statement blames "supply shocks"; Hammack answers: "Supply-side
  factors, including energy prices, have boosted inflation this year, BUT I SEE
  INFLATIONARY PRESSURES COMING FROM THE DEMAND SIDE OF THE ECONOMY, AS WELL."
  A pure supply diagnosis implies look-through and argues against hiking; locating
  demand-side pressure is what makes a hike coherent. She adds new district
  evidence — businesses describe pricing pressures "as broadening rather than
  fading," consumers "expressing despair" — and the flat statement "I am not
  confident it will return to our objective on its own." The "more than five
  years" duration frame is carry-over (June 2 used "sixth year").
  Direction: HAWKISH

MONETARY POLICY
  The hedges come off, in two matched pairs:
    Jun 2:  "monetary policy MAY NOT BE sufficiently restrictive"
    Jul 31: "I DID NOT SEE the current policy stance as appropriately restrictive"
    Jun 2:  "it MAY SOON BE APPROPRIATE for policy to act"
    Jul 31: "NOW IS THE TIME for the FOMC to act"
  Conditional and deferred in June; unconditional and present in July. She names
  no path beyond this meeting. Her closing line on the value of "a wide range of
  viewpoints" is her standard dissent-statement formula (near-verbatim from
  May 1) and carries no signal.
  Direction: HAWKISH

NET SIGNAL: HAWKISH — the dissent object moved from statement LANGUAGE (Apr 29)
to the RATE (Jul 29), and every hedge in her June formulation was removed.

vs. PRIOR SPEECH (June 2, 2026):        HAWKISH — conditional hike signal
  converted into an executed hike vote; restrictiveness verdict de-hedged.
vs. PRE-FOMC SPEECH (June 2, 2026):     HAWKISH — same document (no intervening
  appearance; June 2 was her last before the blackout).
vs. HER OWN PRIOR DISSENT (May 1):      The highest-value comparison in today's
  set. May 1 read: "Uncertainty around the economic outlook is elevated, with
  upside risks to inflation AND DOWNSIDE RISKS TO GROWTH AND EMPLOYMENT." The
  July 31 statement contains no two-sided risk language at all — no uncertainty,
  no downside risk to growth or employment. Same author, same genre, same length,
  same purpose: her risk assessment went from explicitly two-sided to entirely
  one-sided in three months.
vs. COMMITTEE SINCE FOMC:               One of three dissenters sharing the full
  argumentative template. Her distinctive contribution is district evidence —
  the only bottom-up corroboration any of the three offers for the demand-side
  claim. Names no path, placing her between Logan (one move) and Kashkari (a
  series).

LANGUAGE TO WATCH:
  • "did not see the current policy stance as appropriately restrictive" — the
    unhedged restrictiveness verdict. Stage: DISPERSED within the bloc.
  • "pressures coming from the demand side of the economy, as well" — the
    supply-shock exit ramp. Stage: EMERGING.
  • "broadening rather than fading" — district pricing evidence. Watch Barkin as
    the natural cross-check.
  • "despair" — first use; affective escalation on the social-cost argument.

ANALYST FLAG: YES — (1) the disappearance of May 1's downside-risk clause is the
cleanest like-for-like evidence of a genuine change in her risk balance; worth
confirming before it is treated as settled. (2) She names no path beyond July,
leaving the September question open.

━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
2.  NEEL KASHKARI  |  Minneapolis Fed President  |  Just Voter
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

GROWTH
  Absent as a topic. The only growth-adjacent content is instrumental: "The
  massive investment in data centers has also added a new demand element to the
  high inflation Americans are experiencing" — AI capex introduced as an
  inflation channel, converging with Logan's July 16 framing.
  Direction: neutral

LABOR MARKET
  No current characterization at all — no unemployment rate, no job gains, no
  verdict. Labor appears only in a comparison to the 1970s and in a list of what
  he will watch. Not scoreable as "unchanged"; there is nothing to compare.
  [Inference: choosing not to characterize the labor market while voting to
  tighten means declining to defend the employment side of the trade-off. This is
  inferred from absence and is not stated by Kashkari.]
  Direction: n/a

INFLATION
  He adopts the statement's own attribution and then dismantles it with two
  modifiers: "The INITIAL cause of that inflation was, in part, A SERIES OF
  supply shocks." Initial — so no longer operative. A series — so not the single
  shock look-through doctrine is built for. Then the doctrinal break itself: "In
  the face of individual supply shocks ... policymakers should 'look through' the
  shock ... I LARGELY SUBSCRIBE TO THAT VIEW, BUT I INCREASINGLY BELIEVE that
  monetary policy does have an important role to play in addressing a series of
  successive supply shocks that might lead to entrenched higher inflation."
  He is not disputing the data; he is withdrawing from the doctrine the data
  would otherwise trigger. New and hard: the 1970s analogy — policymakers then
  "ALSO DIAGNOSED" inflation as successive supply shocks and "ultimately
  concluded that tight monetary policy was necessary ... DESPITE THEIR ORIGINAL
  SUPPLY SHOCK DIAGNOSIS." That is a charge of identifiable policymaker error,
  though he immediately hedges on severity ("the economy today is in a much, much
  better place"). Note "increasingly believe" is a softer epistemic register than
  Hammack's or Logan's flat verdicts.
  Direction: HAWKISH

MONETARY POLICY
  The only multi-step path on the committee: "I would rather tighten policy
  incrementally ... a potential series of small policy moves would be better than
  waiting and eventually concluding that even bolder actions were necessary," with
  a symmetric exit — "if inflation durably fades, a strategy of small policy steps
  would allow the FOMC to slow or pause."
  CARRY-OVER WARNING: "a series of them" is not new language — his May 1 article
  already had "Federal funds rate increases, potentially a series of them, could
  be warranted." But there it sat inside the ADVERSE Hormuz scenario. The change
  is that the series has moved from the tail to the base case with no triggering
  catastrophe required. The relocation is the signal; the phrase is not.
  Direction: HAWKISH

NET SIGNAL: HAWKISH — the largest single-document move of the three, because his
starting point was the least committed. Dissent object moved from FORWARD
GUIDANCE (Apr 29) to the RATE (Jul 29), with 0.25pp named.

vs. PRIOR SPEECH (May 13, 2026):        HAWKISH, major. May 13 was "We just don't
  know right now" — no forward guidance, cut signal already withdrawn. July 31 is
  an executed hike vote plus a conditional sequence.
vs. PRE-FOMC SPEECH (May 13, 2026):     Same document — and note this is a 77-day
  gap with no recovered Type A in between. See analyst flag.
vs. HIS OWN PRIOR DISSENT (May 1):      May 1 dissented against forward-guidance
  language while supporting the hold, and reached "a series" of hikes only inside
  a named adverse scenario. July 31 dissents on the rate and puts the series in
  the base case. The historical parallel also changed — from the Ukraine oil shock
  (a comparison about price mechanics) to the 1970s (a comparison about policy
  error).
vs. COMMITTEE SINCE FOMC:               Path-maximalist of the bloc — the only one
  asking for a sequence — while being the most tentative in stating conviction. He
  offers no current data assessment, which makes his the most theoretical dissent
  and the most vulnerable to a couple of soft prints. Against that, doctrine is
  what the minutes and statement language actually encode, so his argument is the
  one most likely to travel.

LANGUAGE TO WATCH:
  • "a series of successive supply shocks" — the look-through exit ramp stated as
    doctrine. Stage: EMERGING. Highest-priority phrase this cycle; it is the
    direct antagonist of the statement's "reflecting supply shocks" clause and the
    most likely to surface in the Aug 19 minutes.
  • "entrenched higher inflation" — shared with Jefferson's July 16 "inflation
    becomes entrenched." Stage: EMERGING, and notable because Jefferson is Vice
    Chair; a Troika member using a dissent-bloc frame is the classic bottom-up
    precursor.
  • "a potential series of small policy moves" — Stage: DISPERSED (Kashkari
    alone). If either other dissenter adopts it, September stops being about one
    hike.
  • The 1970s analogy — watch for explicit pushback from a Troika member; that
    would tell you it landed.

ANALYST FLAG: YES — (1) the 77-day baseline gap is a genuine coverage hole; his
shift is scored against a stale prior and a targeted June–July sweep of
Minneapolis Fed / YouTube is warranted before the magnitude is treated as settled.
(2) He is the only dissenter arguing for a series; whether the others follow him
past one hike is the difference between a September one-off and a tightening
faction. (3) With no labor characterization on the record, a weak August
employment report leaves nothing holding him to this position.

3.  LORIE K. LOGAN  |  Dallas Fed President  |  Just Voter
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

⚠ This statement comes 13 days after a full Type A speech making the same
argument. Most of its language is recycled from July 16, several formulations
from June 3. Everything below has been checked against the July 16 scored file;
the genuinely new content is small and is isolated explicitly.

GROWTH
  One sentence, and it is carry-over: "Labor, consumption and financial market
  conditions indicate that monetary policy is not restraining the economy" —
  near-verbatim from July 16 ("The labor, consumption and financial data indicate
  that monetary policy is not restraining the economy"). The quantified evidence
  behind it on July 16 (equities +20%, credit spreads near record tights) is
  dropped; the conclusion is retained.
  Direction: neutral-to-hawkish (unchanged)

LABOR MARKET
  "the job market is solid and perhaps strengthening a bit" — the distinctive
  "perhaps ... strengthening a bit" clause is identical to July 16 ("the labor
  market is well balanced and perhaps even strengthening a bit"). What changed is
  placement: on July 16 labor opened its own section; here it is a subordinate
  clause inside the sentence naming the employment mandate. She raises maximum
  employment in order to dispose of it. July 16's two-sided qualifier — "slow to
  fire and slow to hire ... cold comfort for anyone who needs to find a new job" —
  does not survive, though its conclusion (easing is not the remedy) is implicit.
  Direction: neutral-to-hawkish

INFLATION
  Her path verdict is now in its THIRD consecutive document, near-verbatim: "the
  mid-2's, not all the way to 2 percent, and the risks are to the upside" (June 3,
  July 16, July 31). The most durable individual forecast on the committee — and
  promoted here to the opening sentence, so the forecast rather than the vote is
  presented as the reason.
  Genuinely new: a concession-and-dismiss construction — "EVEN AFTER ACCOUNTING
  FOR PRODUCTIVITY GAINS AND TEMPORARY SUPPLY SHOCKS" the forecast still holds,
  which grants the two strongest arguments for holding and asserts they do not
  change the answer. All the measure-by-measure detail from July 16 (core PCE
  3.4%, trimmed mean, the June CPI rebuttal, the Texas CEO wage anecdotes) is
  dropped.
  Direction: HAWKISH

MONETARY POLICY
  The vote formulation is carry-over with a number attached: July 16's "modestly
  higher interest rates would better balance the outlook and risks" becomes "I
  would have preferred to increase interest rates by one-quarter percentage point
  at this week's meeting."
  Two genuinely new elements:
  (1) The unanticipated-shock rebuttal: "Without any policy restraint, inflation
      will likely continue to trend above target UNTIL THERE'S AN UNANTICIPATED
      SHOCK. The FOMC CANNOT COUNT ON UNANTICIPATED SHOCKS to achieve its goals
      and can always adjust policy if unanticipated shocks occur." This names and
      refutes the implicit majority position — that something will come along to
      cool inflation — and characterizes holding as reliance on luck, with
      reversibility built in to pre-empt the overtightening objection.
  (2) The framework invocation: "The FOMC's policy framework, as described in the
      statement on longer-run goals and monetary policy strategy, ALSO CALLS FOR A
      BALANCED APPROACH to our Congressionally established ... mandates." She
      grounds the dissent in the Committee's own standing strategy rather than in
      her forecast — converting "I read the data differently" into "the majority
      is in tension with our stated strategy," and invoking Congress.
  Also notable by absence: July 16's committee-humility close ("I may persuade
  them, they may persuade me") — the passage that led the July 16 score to treat
  her advocacy as positioning-to-persuade rather than a dissent guarantee — has no
  counterpart here. She offers no collegiality close at all.
  Direction: HAWKISH

NET SIGNAL: HAWKISH — but CONFIRMATION, not escalation. Her substantive position
is where it was on July 16; the news is that she executed it.

vs. PRIOR SPEECH (July 16, 2026):       UNCHANGED in direction, sharpened in
  construction. Same forecast, same risk skew; the new material is defensive
  (pre-empting the productivity, transitory-shock and overtightening objections)
  rather than an escalation of the inflation read.
vs. PRE-FOMC SPEECH (July 16, 2026):    Same document — she was the only dissenter
  who had publicly pre-committed before the meeting.
vs. COMMITTEE SINCE FOMC:               The hawkish anchor and the most
  institutionally framed of the three: Hammack argues from district evidence,
  Kashkari from doctrine and history, Logan from the Committee's own strategy
  document. Like Hammack and unlike Kashkari, she asks for one move.

LANGUAGE TO WATCH:
  • "cannot count on unanticipated shocks to achieve its goals" — the cleanest
    one-line attack on the hold position this cycle. Stage: DISPERSED (first use);
    quotable enough to travel.
  • "the statement on longer-run goals ... calls for a balanced approach" —
    framework-fidelity argument. Stage: DISPERSED but high-consequence: a second
    adopter would escalate the disagreement from a policy judgment to a question
    about whether the Committee is following its own strategy.
  • "trending toward the mid-2's, not all the way to 2 percent" — third
    consecutive document; entrenched as her signature.

ANALYST FLAG: YES — (1) the framework invocation, not the vote, is the escalation
to watch; it is harder for the Chair to absorb quietly than a forecast dispute.
(2) Logan's view did NOT move between July 16 and July 31 — treating this
statement as fresh hawkish information would double-count a position already
priced from July 16. The new information today is Hammack's and Kashkari's, whose
baselines were June 2 and May 13.

CROSS-SPEAKER NOTE — three statements, one template
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
These were published the same day by three officials at three banks. The
convergence is close enough to be worth reading as a coordinated case rather than
three coincidences.

1. ALL THREE ESCALATED FROM LANGUAGE TO RATE. On April 29 all three dissented
   about the statement's easing-bias wording while supporting the rate decision.
   That language was removed on June 17 — they won. Having won the wording fight,
   all three moved the objection to the rate itself at the next attended meeting.
   This is the single most important structural fact of the cycle: the dispute has
   exhausted the space the Chair can settle by editing the statement.

2. ALL THREE MADE A DEMAND-SIDE MOVE, BY DIFFERENT ROUTES. The July 29 statement
   attributes inflation to "supply shocks," and a supply-only diagnosis implies
   look-through — which argues against hiking. Each dissenter had to get past it:
     Hammack:  "inflationary pressures coming from the demand side ... as well"
     Kashkari: data-center investment "added a new demand element"; and a series
               of shocks is not the case look-through is built for
     Logan:    "even after accounting for ... temporary supply shocks," the
               forecast still misses
   Three routes, one destination. This is the load-bearing claim for all three and
   the direct contradiction of the statement's own attribution clause.

3. ALL THREE RAN THE SAME COST-OF-WAITING ARGUMENT, near-verbatim:
     Hammack:  "The longer that high inflation persists, the more challenging and
                costly it can be to bring it back down."
     Kashkari: "better than waiting and eventually concluding that even bolder
                actions were necessary."
     Logan:    "Modest action in the near term would reduce the likelihood of
                needing to take sharper action later."
   Stage: COMMITTEE-WIDE WITHIN THE DISSENT BLOC; absent from the statement. The
   most likely of the shared formulations to surface in the Aug 19 minutes.

4. ALL THREE USED THE FIVE-YEAR DURATION FRAME ("more than five years"). This is
   long-established carry-over across Hammack, Logan, Kashkari and Barkin and
   carries no new signal on its own — noted here only so its ubiquity today is not
   mistaken for fresh convergence.

WHERE THEY DIVERGE — the September question:
   Logan and Hammack ask for one move; Kashkari asks for a SERIES. Nothing
   published today resolves whether the bloc converges on a path. That, not the
   fact of the dissent, is what determines whether September is a one-off or the
   start of a tightening sequence.

CALIBRATING THE SIGNAL — what is actually new:
   Logan's dissent was fully pre-announced on July 16 and adds no new substantive
   position; reading it as fresh hawkish information double-counts. The genuinely
   new information is Hammack's (last seen June 2, conditional) and Kashkari's
   (last seen May 13, agnostic). Two of three dissenters moved materially during
   the blackout, unobserved.
