1.  MICHAEL S. BARR  |  Governor  |  Just Voter
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

A financial-inclusion speech with four opening paragraphs on the economy —
Barr's first outlook language since March 26, and the most explicit policy
conditional he has put on record this cycle. Only the outlook paragraphs are
scored.

GROWTH
  Close paraphrase of the statement: "The economy has been growing solidly,
  powered in part by the boom in AI-related business investment ...
  Productivity and new business formation have been strong for a number of
  years." His own qualifier is on the consumer only: "Consumer spending to date
  has been largely resilient." March 26 offered no growth characterization at
  all; growth is now stated as a support for the inflation-first case.
  Direction: hawkish lean

LABOR MARKET
  One sentence, statement-aligned: "The labor market is stable, with
  relatively low unemployment." What is absent matters: March 26's "Low levels
  of hiring likely leave the labor market vulnerable to shocks, so continued
  vigilance ... remains warranted" and "low hire, low fire" do not appear. The
  −23K July payroll is not mentioned.
  Direction: hawkish lean (fragility qualifier dropped)

INFLATION
  "But inflation remains too high—and has been for over five years." ("Too
  high" for the statement's "elevated"; five-year duration carried from March
  26.) Progress "stalled in 2025" under "a series of shocks—from tariffs and
  then the conflict in the Middle East, as well as from the rapid AI buildout"
  — the statement's supply-shock attribution echoed and extended to a demand-
  side source (AI capex) the statement does not name. "Core non-housing
  services inflation remains elevated" (same component as March 26 and the
  minutes). Risk framing moves from expectations (March 26) to broadening:
  "there is a risk of broader price pressures taking hold, a risk I am
  watching closely."
  Direction: hawkish lean — structure unchanged from March 26

MONETARY POLICY
  Explicit and named two-branch scenario for a specific meeting:
    HOLD: "If trends in the data give me some confidence that inflation is
      moderating on a path to 2 percent, then I think we can take a bit more
      time to assess our policy stance."
    HIKE: "However, if inflation appears not to be moderating sufficiently,
      then I think we should act decisively to raise rates."
  Both branches carry a stated policy response; the trigger ("trends in the
  data," "moderating sufficiently") is not quantified. The hold branch is
  framed as "a bit more time" — a deferral rather than a settled stance.
  "Moderating sufficiently" is the same test-form as Warsh's Aug 28 "clearly
  and at sufficient speed." Cut signal: absent. Hike signal: present,
  conditional, named.
  Direction: hawkish

NET SIGNAL: HAWKISH LEAN — the two qualifiers that cushioned Barr's March 26
  hawkish inflation read (labor vulnerability; no growth claim) are gone, and a
  pure hold has become a two-branch conditional with a named September hike.

vs. PRIOR SPEECH (Mar 26, 2026):        HAWKISH — "take some time to assess"
  (hold, no hike branch) → "act decisively to raise rates" if inflation is not
  moderating sufficiently; labor fragility clause dropped; growth affirmative.
vs. PRE-FOMC SPEECH (Mar 26, 2026):     Same document — March 26 is both the
  prior outlook and the pre-FOMC baseline (no outlook language on June 6 or
  July 14). The shift spans two FOMC meetings.
vs. COMMITTEE SINCE FOMC:              With Cook (Aug 5: "I am prepared to act
  by raising rates, if necessary"), Barr is one of two majority-side voters
  carrying a named hike conditional since July 29. His verb is stronger than
  Cook's; his trigger is vaguer than Waller's (Jul 13: a hot core reading). He
  does not adopt the dissenters' base-case hike. On the diagnosis he is
  closest to the minutes' "some participants" on core services and
  broadening.

LANGUAGE TO WATCH:
  • "act decisively to raise rates" — unhedged hike verb from a majority
    voter. DISPERSED (Cook's "prepared to act by raising rates" nearest).
  • "moderating sufficiently" — sufficiency test on the pace of disinflation,
    four days after Warsh's "at sufficient speed." EMERGING (two officials).
  • "a series of shocks ... as well as from the rapid AI buildout" — extends
    the successive-shocks framing (Waller Apr 17; Kashkari Jul 31) and adds AI
    capex to the shock list. EMERGING.

ANALYST FLAG: YES
  → A July hold voter has named a September hike branch six weeks before a
    projection meeting, with an unquantified trigger. Stance shift toward the
    dissenters, or optionality ahead of the SEP? The August CPI/PCE prints
    (inside blackout) are the only data that resolve which branch he takes.
