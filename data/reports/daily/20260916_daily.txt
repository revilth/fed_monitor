════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
The 5:30am ET GitHub Actions collect job checks today's date (2026-09-17)
against the blackout calendar. Since 2026-09-17 is the FINAL day of the
September 16 blackout period (start: 2026-09-05, end: 2026-09-17), the
collect script printed "BLACKOUT DAY (2026-09-17) — skipping collection."
and exited with no data collected. The FOMC statement, SEP projections
table, and press conference transcript — all posted by 2:00pm ET on
September 16 — were never retrieved by the scraper.

In addition, this scoring environment's egress proxy blocks all outbound
connections to federalreserve.gov (error: EGRESS_BLOCKED), so the
python3 main.py refetch fallback and direct WebFetch paths also fail from
within this session.

STRUCTURAL WORKFLOW GAP: The collect job will always miss FOMC-day
documents when the blackout period ends the day AFTER the decision day:
the collect job runs at 5:30am on the day the blackout ends, sees that
day is still within the blackout, and skips. The FOMC materials posted at
2:00pm the prior day are therefore never auto-collected. The September 18
collect run (first non-blackout run) WILL collect the September 16
documents, but no scoring routine is scheduled to process them — the
September 18 run reports on September 17 (BLACKOUT → SKIP) and the
September 19 run reports on September 18. The September 16 canonical FOMC
report will remain pending unless manually triggered.

KEY ANALYTICAL QUESTIONS THE SEPTEMBER MEETING WAS EXPECTED TO RESOLVE:

1. DID THE AUGUST DATA TRIGGER WALLER'S HIKE BRANCH?
   Waller (Sep 3): "If the incoming data for August show this improvement
   [in inflation] has been fleeting, then it may be appropriate to raise
   the policy rate when the FOMC meets on September 15 and 16." Explicit,
   named, dated conditional. The August CPI/PCE landed inside the blackout
   and was determinative. The three-month core trajectory (4.76% Feb →
   3.05% Jul) was Waller's measure. Also: "only slightly restricting
   aggregate demand" — a thin margin, so the August print carried
   asymmetric downside for the hold case.

2. HOW FAR DID THE HIKE CAUCUS EXPAND?
   At July 29: Logan, Hammack, Kashkari confirmed. September added:
   — Barr (Sep 1): "if inflation appears not to be moderating sufficiently,
     then I think we should act decisively to raise rates." Hike trigger
     unquantified but named.
   — Waller (Sep 3): conditioned explicitly on August data (see above).
   — Cook (Aug 5): "prepared to act by raising rates, if necessary"
     (weakest formulation; "It may not [be necessary]").
   Any of these three crossing to the hike side would expand the dissent
   bloc from three to four-plus, a qualitative shift in committee dynamics.

3. UNDERLYING INFLATION: THREE COMPETING FRAMEWORKS.
   Going into the meeting, the Troika held three different measures:
   • Warsh (Aug 28, BREADTH): 54% of 199 PCE components still above 3%
     (12m); 49% (6m); vs. 32% pre-pandemic. Standard: "unmet."
   • Williams (Sep 2, ATTRIBUTION): tariffs in the "rear view mirror"; no
     second-round effects or broadening. Standard: "trend moving slowly
     down."
   • Waller (Sep 3, MOMENTUM): 3m annualized core 3.05% (down from 4.76%
     Feb); imputed nonmarket services ~half of core increase (strip out:
     underlying "doing better than core numbers suggest").
   Which framework governs the September statement language and dot
   discussion is the highest-value open question in the entire cycle.

4. SEP DOT DISTRIBUTION: first Warsh-chaired projection meeting.
   June 17, 2026: 9 of 18 penciled a 2026 hike; median 3.75–3.8%.
   Warsh withheld his own dot at June 17 ("not well suited to current
   conjuncture"). Whether he submitted in September is a structural
   question about the SEP's credibility as a consensus document.
   Three outcomes possible: hike bloc expands (dot distribution shifts
   more hawkish), contracts (August data relieved pressure), or holds
   (near 50/50 maintained).

5. SEP FORWARD GUIDANCE REFORM.
   Warsh's five task forces were expected to report "by fall/year-end."
   Any announced change to the SEP structure, press conference frequency,
   or forward guidance format would be structural, not cyclical.
