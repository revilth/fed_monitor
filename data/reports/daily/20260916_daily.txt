RATE DECISION
  "The Committee decided to raise the target range for the federal funds
  rate by 1/4 percentage point to 3-3/4 to 4 percent." First increase since
  July 26, 2023. Implementation note moves every administered rate 25bp
  (IORB 3.90, standing repo 4.00, ON RRP 3.75, primary credit 4.00); no
  balance-sheet change.

VOTE
  12–0. The three July dissenters (Hammack, Kashkari, Logan) got the 25bp they
  asked for; the nine who held in July joined them. No dovish dissent from
  Bowman, Powell, Williams or Paulson. Voting members [INFERRED from roster;
  the page lists only the tally]: Warsh, Jefferson, Williams, Barr, Bowman,
  Cook, Powell, Waller, Paulson, Hammack, Kashkari, Logan.

GROWTH  (¶2)
  Jul 29: "expanding at a solid pace despite elevated uncertainty that owes,
    in part, to the conflict in the Middle East. Productivity growth and
    capital investment are strong."
  Sep 16: "expanding at a solid pace. While uncertainty remains elevated
    owing, in part, to geopolitical developments, domestic spending has been
    resilient. Productivity growth is strong, and capital investment is
    robust."
  Changes: Middle East → "geopolitical developments" (broadened); NEW
    "domestic spending has been resilient" (first demand-side line in a
    Warsh statement); capex "strong" → "robust."
  Direction: HAWKISH LEAN — resilient demand plus strong supply leaves no
    growth-side case for patience.

LABOR MARKET  (¶2, second sentence)
  "Job gains have kept pace with the workforce, and the unemployment rate has
  changed little." UNCHANGED — third consecutive statement.
  Direction: NEUTRAL by text. The SEP does the work: unemployment median 4.1
  through 2029; upside-risk count 7 → 0.

INFLATION  (¶3 — the document)
  Jul 29: "Inflation remains elevated relative to the Committee's 2 percent
    goal, in part reflecting supply shocks that have driven price increases
    in certain sectors, including energy. The Committee will deliver price
    stability."
  Sep 16: "Inflation remains elevated. Today's policy action will support a
    timelier return to the Committee's 2 percent goal. The Committee will
    deliver price stability."
  Changes: DELETED "relative to the Committee's 2 percent goal" as qualifier;
    DELETED the entire supply-shock attribution clause (in every statement
    since June 17; the framework Williams defended Aug 3 / Sep 2); NEW
    purpose clause with a comparative — "timelier" says the July path was
    too slow; "will deliver price stability" retained (third time).
  Direction: HAWKISH — the Committee stopped explaining inflation and acted
    on it. The breadth test (Warsh, Aug 28) beat the attribution test
    (Williams) in the drafting.

MONETARY POLICY
  Forward guidance: absent (third statement). "Timelier" is a purpose
  clause, not a path. Warsh in Q&A: "I'm not in the forward guidance
  business ... You heard from other people in the dots effectively."
  Direction: HAWKISH — the action.

NET SIGNAL: HAWKISH — action, deleted attribution and new purpose clause all
  point one way; nothing in the text points the other.

vs. PREVIOUS STATEMENT (Jul 29): every change hawkish or hawkish-lean; labor
  and reserves sentences carried verbatim.

THE RATE PATH vs. THE NEW 3.875% MIDPOINT
  2026 dots: 3.875:2 | 4.125:12 | 4.375:4.  16 of 18 project at least one
  more hike by year-end (Oct 27–28 or Dec 8–9); 2 hold; 0 cut — the first SEP
  of the cycle with no participant below the current rate. June, against its
  own 3.625 midpoint, was 9 hike / 8 hold / 1 cut.
  2027 median = 2026 median = 4.125: hike once more, then hold through 2027.
  14 of 18 at or above 4.125 for 2027; the June dovish 2027 tail (8 dots at
  3.375 or below) is down to 1.
  Longer run 3.0625 → 3.25: mode still 3.0; dots at ≥3.5 went 4 → 7. The
  2026-minus-longer-run gap widened +0.69 → +0.875 — higher-for-longer
  against a slightly higher neutral, not neutral-repricing.

THE MECHANISM — why the rate path rose +0.3/+0.5/+0.5 on an inflation
revision of +0.1/0/+0.1
  The inflation forecast barely moved and CONVERGED (2026 PCE: 12 of 18 now
  on 3.7–3.8; June's three dots at 3.9+ are gone). Inflation risk unchanged
  (17 of 18 upside, both SEPs). What flipped is labor and growth: unemployment
  revised down 0.2 at every horizon; participants seeing upside unemployment
  risk 7 → 0 (balanced 10 → 17); downside growth risk 5 → 0. The patience
  that the labor market had justified was withdrawn. The Committee re-priced
  its reaction function, not its inflation view.
  Tension to flag: "timelier return" (statement) vs. median PCE reaching 2.0
  in 2029, one year later than June's path. Warsh: "those aren't my
  forecasts." [Inference: "timelier" is relative to not hiking, not to June.]

PARTICIPANTS: 18 in both SEPs; no composition change. Warsh: "as in June—I
  have not offered a projection of my own." CORRECTION to the June scored
  file: the withheld dot is Warsh's, not Atlanta's. With identical
  composition, the whole move is participants revising their own dots — at
  least nine moved up for 2026, most by two steps.

Direction: HAWKISH. Dots-vs-statement gap: half-closed by the hike,

OPENING STATEMENT (committee-approved language). All "new" items verified
absent from the July 29 official opening statement; Aug 28 origins noted.

GROWTH
  New: "the American economy appears to be strengthening. New hiring,
  private-sector earnings, business capital investment—each of these markers
  has improved ... Credit flows have been robust." "an attitude of optimism
  is exactly what I heard inside the FOMC." The July AI-capex hedge is gone.
  Direction: HAWKISH LEAN.

LABOR MARKET
  New: "jobless rate remains low at around 4.1 percent, and both job openings
  and weekly hours have been increasing. Unemployment claims ... consistent
  with full employment. So, the labor side of the Fed's congressional remit
  is in good shape." (Aug 28's "consistent with full employment" now
  consensus text.)
  Direction: HAWKISH LEAN — full-employment verdict removes the labor case
  for patience.

INFLATION
  New: "our predominant focus is on the price-stability side"; "inflation is
  too high and has been for too long"; "This summer's inflation readings do
  not tell me that underlying trends have meaningfully improved" (Aug 28
  verbatim); PCE "around 3.6 percent in August"; "Too many categories are
  still posting increases above 3 percent, on both a 6- and 12-month basis"
  — THE BREADTH MEASURE, personal on Aug 28, committee text 19 days later;
  commodity input prices "have risen"; and the standard executed: "I defined
  the standard for action: We must be confident that underlying inflation is
  moving to our objective, clearly and at sufficient speed. Today, the FOMC
  decided that this standard has not been satisfied." Four aims enumerated,
  including "that relative price changes in some sectors of the economy do
  not broaden" — the deleted supply-shock clause inverted into a policy aim.
  Absent vs. July: "no soft inflation target" passage (not repeated).
  Direction: HAWKISH.

MONETARY POLICY
  New: "as I said at the policy symposium in Jackson Hole, I would be
  hard-pressed to describe broad financial conditions as restrictive. This
  view was widely shared by the Committee. So we removed a dose of
  accommodation." The July hold retold as timing ("a good majority ... thought
  the wiser course then would be to await new information"). "The committee's
  unanimous vote shows our resolve." Dot withheld again. Global context:
  G-20 Asheville, Basel — "most advanced economies are facing price
  pressures." Absent vs. July: the bond-market/"play the ball" section, the
  four meeting questions, the balance sheet (not mentioned anywhere today),
  "will not hesitate to act" (superseded by acting).
  Cut signal: ABSENT.  Hike signal: the hike; no path.
  Direction: HAWKISH.

Q&A — the unscripted signals beyond the opening statement
  • The path delegated, then disowned. "I'm not in the forward guidance
    business ... You heard from other people in the dots effectively, what
    their forecasts are." And on the SEP's 2029: "those aren't my forecasts.
    Those are the forecasts of my 18 colleagues ... we'll have more to say
    about it. But I'm ill-prepared to pre-judge those future actions."
  • Not the CPI. "I'm not a data point dependent guy." Three things changed
    since July: the economy "has strengthened"; "the inflation summer trends
    weren't passing the test. I've seen very little information since that
    would make me reverse that decision"; and geopolitics — "our judgment
    about what is the most likely, or least likely of the geopolitical
    situation has changed." The July hold: "seven weeks is a good investment
    ... a way to buy time." The judgment was formed at Jackson Hole and
    executed today; Waller's dated August-data trigger reached the same vote
    by a different route.
  • No ceiling. Restrictive? "my colleagues were hard-pressed to describe it
    that way too. We removed a dose of accommodation." Neutral rate? "Do I
    think it has any operational effect on decisions that we make today? No,
    I don't." Against the majority's own pre-hike labels ("mildly
    restrictive" — Paulson, Collins, Daly; "only slightly restricting" —
    Waller), the Chair will not call 3.875% restrictive.
  • Hiking into a supply shock: "what we can do, and will do, is ensure that
    any change in relative prices don't broaden out. Don't have second and
    third order effects." No labor sacrifice: "I don't believe that we need
    to do harm to the labor markets to achieve our objective."
  • Independence: "today was our decision." On the President: "I've got
    nothing for you." "Independence is a two-way street." A unanimous hike
    against explicit pressure for cuts.
  • Yields explained by growth, hyperscaler capital demand and geopolitics —
    July's "curve doing the work" argument is gone.

NET SIGNAL: HAWKISH — a unanimous hike framed as accommodation-removal; the
  Chair's Aug 28 framework (standard, breadth test, level claim) adopted as
  Committee text within nineteen days; the supply-shock excuse retired; no
  ceiling stated; the path handed to a dot plot in which 16 of 18 see another
  hike.

  balance-sheet arguments dropped; dissents cited as "resolve" vs. unmentioned.
vs. CHAIR'S PREVIOUS SPEECH (Aug 28, Jackson Hole):  EXECUTION — standard,
  measure and level claim all migrated personal → Committee; new: "removed a
  dose of accommodation," geopolitics as a decision input, "do not broaden,"
  "inflation is a choice." Nothing retracted. Balance sheet now absent.
vs. COMMITTEE SINCE PREVIOUS FOMC:  Anticipated by seven voters and two

  Cook, Daly, Paulson). Diverged: Williams on inflation attribution (voted
  with the majority anyway). Stance-label collision: four voters said
  "restrictive" pre-hike; the Chair says the Committee was "hard-pressed" to.
