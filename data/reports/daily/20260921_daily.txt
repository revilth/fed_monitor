1.  AUSTAN GOOLSBEE  |  Chicago Fed President  |  Non Voter
    Type B (framework speech on supply shocks) with embedded outlook
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

GROWTH
  Treated only as an overheating risk: "any evidence that AI data center
  construction is spilling out of its own lane and raising aggregate
  output beyond what the economy can absorb" is one of his two demand
  tells, and in Q&A "the bigger the hype the bigger the risk of short-run
  overheating ... I'm still worried about that." Fiscal deficits are
  "part of aggregate demand"; he rejects cutting to cheapen debt issuance
  as "the canonical reason why you want monetary independence."
  Direction: hawkish lean

LABOR MARKET
  No characterization at all — the four-consecutive-appearance idiom
  "stable without being good" is gone. Labor appears only as the absence
  of a problem: "If the unemployment rate is barely 4% and core inflation
  is above three on an extended basis, there's not a conflict of mandates.
  There's just an inflation problem." And: "we do not have an employment
  problem and we do have an inflation problem." The labor cover for
  patience is withdrawn in his own voice.
  Direction: hawkish lean

INFLATION
  The speech is the rationale the statement omitted when it deleted the
  supply-shock clause: "there are some supply shocks that central banks
  should not simply look through—namely, the persistent ones"; "when you
  see a large supply shock, it's usually more accurate to assume it will
  be persistent." His July "tariffs should be one and done" is reversed
  ("nothing like the stylized textbook example ... repeated escalation").
  The peak-inflation date has slipped "Q4 2025 ... Q1 2026 ... Q2, then
  Q3, then Q4—and now sometime in 2027. That's not a comforting pattern."
  Watch list: services inflation and AI-adjacent inflation, both of which
  "correlate more strongly with overheating demand." New caveat: one-to-
  two-year survey expectations show "a lot of movement."
  Direction: hawkish

MONETARY POLICY
  Endorses the hike in the first person as a non-voter: "we know what has
  to happen in that circumstance"; "that's why I supported this decision."
  The rule: "don't do nothing. Looking through won't work"; "the only way
  back is the hard way." Two bounds: supply-shock responses "may not need
  to be as large as" demand-shock responses, and the July conditional cut
  path survives in form — "if we have some convincing evidence that we're
  headed back to 2%, I have no problem with rates ... heading back down."
  But the default has flipped: patience with a cut path attached has
  become "we know what has to happen" with a cut path attached to evidence
  that has not arrived ("show-me state"). Asked about Warsh's "removed a
  dose of accommodation," he declines both stance labels — r-star is his
  "Sasquatch" — and answers on outcomes. Low-end neutral prior stated: "a
  3% rate with 2% inflation and 1% real felt approximately right," which
  AI productivity "raises ... It doesn't lower it." Says he is "among the
  more optimistic" dots. Scenario framing: descriptive, with a named
  evidence bar.
  Direction: hawkish

NET SIGNAL: HAWKISH LEAN — the former dovish wing's marker; a non-voter
  who dissented against the December cut now endorses a hike and supplies
  the theory for why supply shocks no longer license looking through,
  while keeping magnitude bounded and a conditional way back down.

vs. PRIOR SPEECH (Jul 14):     HAWKISH — tariffs no longer one-and-done;
  labor no longer "stable without being good"; the multi-month
  disinflation bar he set is reported unmet; "normalize" demoted from
  operative conditional to exception clause.
vs. PRE-FOMC SPEECH (Jul 14):  same document — HAWKISH. (His Aug 28
  Bloomberg Odd Lots appearance was never collected; see flag.)
vs. COMMITTEE SINCE FOMC:      First regional voice after the hike. Aligns
  with Warsh Sept 16 on the mandates not being in conflict and on the
  summer data not passing the test; adds the persistence argument the
  statement's deletion implied. Declines the Chair's "accommodation" label
  as firmly as the Just Voters' "restrictive" one.

LANGUAGE TO WATCH:
  • "the only way back is the hard way" — personal; closing line and
    repeated in Q&A.
  • "persistent supply shocks ... should not simply look through" —
    rationale for the deleted statement clause; DISPERSED (one regional).
    Williams's attribution test is the opposite pole.
  • "there's not a conflict of mandates. There's just an inflation
    problem" — echoes Warsh Sept 16; EMERGING (Chair + one regional).
  • "time for sniffing" / "show-me state" — his idioms for the evidence
    bar; a shift to "time for walking" would mean a series.
  • DROPPED: "stable without being good"; "one and done" for tariffs.

ANALYST FLAG: YES
  → (1) He rejected both stance labels. If the "mildly restrictive" bloc
        (Paulson, Collins, Daly, Waller) moves to outcome language, the
        stance-label collision resolves by abandoning the label.
  → (2) Coverage gap: Aug 28 Bloomberg Odd Lots (46 min) not collected;
        the July 14 baseline may understate his pre-blackout position.
  → (3) Sept 29 "The Economy Ahead" (Chicago Fed luncheon) promises a
        full outlook — the next Goolsbee document to prioritize.
