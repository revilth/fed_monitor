THE DAY IN ONE LINE
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
The most analytically explicit document of the cycle: Daly endorsed the July
hold, kept the benign case as her modal — and pre-committed to a response
LARGER than any dissenter has requested if the adverse case emerges.

1.  DALY  |  San Francisco Fed President  |  Non Voter
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

⚠ Speaker's own disclaimer, stated mid-speech: "this is my judgment, by the way
… it doesn't reflect the FOMC's views."

THE STRUCTURE — two NAMED scenarios with policy responses attached. Per the
framework, explicit named scenarios are a stronger signal than descriptive risk
language.

  SCENARIO ONE (conventional): "the current mildly restrictive policy that's in
   place in the US is sufficient to gradually return inflation to the 2% target.
   It would just push it out a little bit." → "you might fine-tune the policy
   level, but you don't really need a large change."

  SCENARIO TWO (compounding): the three shocks — tariffs, energy, AI —
   "overlapping at a point in time where inflation's already elevated" produce
   an effect "greater than the sum of its parts," broadening inflation and
   making it persistent. → "policy must be recalibrated and not a little bit but
   probably more substantially … it would be insufficient to then just do small
   calibrations, almost surgical calibrations."

  ⚠ THE KEY SENTENCE — the weighting moved: "I think scenario one is still my
  modal, but if you do the percentages of the modal versus the scenario 2, I
  think they're much closer together than they used to be." Both scenarios are
  now, in her CEO metaphor, "on the middle of the desk … getting equal
  attention." A probability shift, not a verdict change.

GROWTH
  Treated only through the shock decomposition. On AI she is the only official
  to REFUSE to resolve the question: "it remains an open question whether this
  is a relative demand shock and just changes the relative price of technology
  equipment … or if it's something broader … because it's competing for
  resources." Adds a mechanism nobody else has — resource crowding: "what do the
  other businesses feel like they're not getting or they're paying more for
  because the AI investment boom is taking resources."
  Direction: neutral

LABOR MARKET
  Exculpates labor from inflation, with a productivity mechanism the others
  don't supply: wages "not growing beyond 2% inflation and productivity," and a
  gap between measured productivity and pay "suggesting that firms are able to
  handle some of the cost pressure through gains in productivity as opposed to
  raising prices." CEO contacts confirm margin defence through cost-cutting —
  "trying to do more with fewer workers." She files this under disinflation only
  and does not draw the later labor-demand risk.
  Direction: neutral / dovish on inflation pressure

INFLATION
  States the look-through doctrine as textbook — policy "acts with a lag, 12 to
  18 months … by the time you've raised the rate, you haven't affected the shock
  dynamic, but you've definitely affected the economy" — which is exactly
  Paulson's August 4 timing argument. Then asks what Paulson does not: does it
  survive THREE OVERLAPPING shocks? That is Kashkari's successive-shocks
  argument rebuilt analytically rather than by historical analogy, and left OPEN
  rather than asserted — which makes it adoptable by majority members without
  conceding the Burns charge.

  Four named reasons for scenario one: limited pricing power (NY Fed services
  and Richmond Fed manufacturing surveys); inflation expectations tracking
  gasoline rather than broad inflation; no labor cost-push; anchored long-run
  expectations.

  ⚠ Two named reasons for scenario two, one of them QUANTIFIED:
   • The attention threshold — "a forthcoming AER paper … argues that consumers'
     attention to inflation impacts inflation itself … the threshold where
     people's attention focuses on inflation is 4%. And we're very close to 4%.
     We are 3.9 on average and 3.7 of late." A near-breached trip wire; the most
     concrete escalation trigger any official has named this cycle.
   • The producer-consumer gap — "PPI in June is 5.5 and consumer price index
     grows by 3.5 … the cumulative change still leaves firms behind … at some
     point firms want to maintain their margins." With a contagion mechanism:
     "everybody else is raising prices. Why don't I try to? That hasn't happened
     yet … But it's not a risk we can just push aside."

  ⚠ NOTE THE INTERNAL LOGIC: limited pricing power is her reason #1 FOR the
  benign case and compressed margins are her reason #2 FOR the adverse case.
  These are the same fact on two horizons — pricing power is a stock that
  depletes. That is the sharpest analytical point of the week and it is exactly
  what Paulson's version lacks.

  On anchored expectations, comfort explicitly REFUSED (Q&A): "Absolutely not.
  By the time the anchor drifts, it's really hard to get it back." And: "the
  longer inflation expectations remain well anchored through big shocks, the
  less comfortable they are in a way for evidence." Same inversion Cook
  performed July 15, reached independently.
  Direction: hawkish lean

MONETARY POLICY
  "I was completely supportive of the decision to hold rates in July." But the
  hold is justified as SCENARIO DISCRIMINATION, not confidence: "we have a lot
  of information we need to collect if we're deciding between scenario one and
  scenario two. If it was just fine-tuning I thought we were up against, that
  would be a different problem." Materially different from Paulson's "not a
  close call" and Cook's "three disinflation channels in train."
  Named triggers (Q&A): tariff goods-price improvement reversing; whether the
  war stabilizes and energy falls; AI resource-crowding appearing in non-AI
  firms' costs.
  Closing pre-commitment: "be vigilant … but be very prepared to take the action
  if the second scenario in particular emerges."
  Cut signal status:  ABSENT — cuts unmentioned in either scenario
  Hike signal status: PRESENT, conditional on scenario two, and UNUSUALLY
    LARGE — she pre-commits to a NON-INCREMENTAL response
  Direction: hawkish lean

NET SIGNAL: HAWKISH LEAN — verdict unchanged, weighting and pre-commitment
  moved. She endorses the hold, keeps the benign case modal, and promotes the
  adverse case out of the tail while committing to a bigger response than the
  dissenters ask for.

vs. PRIOR SPEECH / PRE-FOMC (July 2, 2026):  hawkish lean — the two-scenario
  architecture is new in this explicit, named form, and the adverse branch is
  promoted from tail risk to co-equal deliberation.

vs. COMMITTEE SINCE FOMC:  Daly sits between the majority and the dissenters and
  is the only official to build a framework in which both are right under
  different states of the world. She shares Paulson's timing doctrine and
  pricing-power evidence, shares Kashkari's overlapping-shocks worry and Cook's
  refusal to take comfort from anchored expectations — and commits to a response
  larger than anything the dissenters have requested, conditional on evidence
  none of them yet claims to have.

LANGUAGE TO WATCH:
  • "compounding effect" / "greater than the sum of its parts" — the analytical,
    adoptable form of Kashkari's successive-shocks doctrine. Watch the Aug 19
    minutes and Jefferson/Williams. Stage: EMERGING.
  • The 4% attention threshold — quantified trigger, readings just below.
    Stage: DISPERSED (first use).
  • "mildly restrictive" — now Paulson, Collins, Cook (Q&A), Daly. Contested by
    Kashkari. Stage: COMMITTEE-WIDE among non-Troika.
  • "policy must be recalibrated and not a little bit" — the only
    non-incremental response on record; contrast Kashkari's "small steps."
    Stage: DISPERSED.

ANALYST FLAG: YES
  → PRIMARY — the pricing-power stock/flow argument. Paulson and Daly cite the
    same survey evidence to opposite ends. Whether pricing power is a level or a
    countdown is the most decision-relevant open question in the cycle. Barkin's
    Richmond manufacturer contacts are the earliest read — and Barkin's July 31
    remarks remain unscored for lack of a transcript.
  → The committee now has two quantified trip wires pointing opposite ways:
    Paulson's shock-stripped 2.4–2.8% underlying, and Daly's lived-headline 4%
    attention threshold. Which is the reaction function keyed to?
  → Daly commits to a LARGER response than any dissenter requests. If compounding
    evidence arrives, the hawkish tail is fatter than the 9–3 implies.
  → Non-voter status and her explicit disclaimer bound how far to read this into
    committee positioning.
