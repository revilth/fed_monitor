MACRO CONTEXT — June PPI (8:30am ET) + Beige Book (2:00pm ET)

JUNE PPI (BLS, 8:30am ET):
  Final demand: -0.3% MoM (SA); consensus was +0.0%.
  Core PPI (ex-food and energy): +0.2% MoM.
  Core ex-trade services: +0.1% MoM.
  Final demand goods: -1.4% (gasoline -12%, accounting for ~2/3 of decline).
  Final demand services: +0.2%.
  YoY final demand: +5.5% (down from +6.5% in May).

  CRITICAL: Under Waller's July 13 decision rule ("if we get another hot reading
  on core inflation this week, the FOMC will need to consider tightening in the
  near term"), a +0.2% core PPI reading is NOT a hot outcome. Combined with June
  CPI (core flat MoM, released July 14), BOTH data points fall below Waller's
  stated tightening threshold. His HOLD branch is activated. July 28-29 hike is
  now off the table under his own framework, absent additional data between now
  and the meeting. Market probability of a July hike fell further; September hike
  probability ~55-65%.

JULY BEIGE BOOK (Type H, released 2:00pm ET):
  Prepared at the Federal Reserve Bank of San Francisco (Twelfth District).
  Reference period: conditions on or before mid-July 2026.

  NATIONAL SUMMARY — KEY DIFF vs. June 3, 2026 Beige Book:

  OVERALL ECONOMIC ACTIVITY:
    July 15: Economic activity increased at a "slight to moderate" pace.
    June 3:  Economic activity increased at a "slight to moderate" pace (10 of
             12 districts; 1 slight decline, 1 no change).
    → Growth descriptor UNCHANGED. Retail sales and demand for services "edged
      down." Uncertainty and elevated fuel costs weigh on sentiment.
    Net: NEUTRAL.

  LABOR MARKETS:
    July 15: "Most regions experienced little to no change in employment levels."
             Employers held head counts steady. AI investment theme added as a
             labor force development. Wages rose "slightly."
    June 3:  "Employment showed little to no change across eleven Districts."
             "Modest to moderate" wage growth; "low-hire, low-fire environment."
    → Labor descriptor ESSENTIALLY UNCHANGED. Wage growth descriptor slightly
      softer (July: "slightly"; June: "modest to moderate"). Low-hire/low-fire
      environment persists.
    Net: NEUTRAL (very slight dovish lean on wages).

  PRICES:
    July 15: Prices increased "moderately overall." "Price growth was the same
             or slower in ALL Districts" compared with the prior period.
             Non-labor input costs still elevated (energy, transportation, raw
             materials). Distribution: 9 districts moderate, 2 robust, 1 slight.
    June 3:  Prices increased at "a moderate to strong pace overall." "Most
             Districts reporting HIGHER inflation than the previous report."
             Energy costs (Middle East conflict) identified as primary driver.
    → KEY DOVISH SHIFT: "moderate to strong, most districts HIGHER" →
      "moderate, same or SLOWER in ALL districts." Every single district
      showed prices either flat or decelerating. The energy cost reversal
      (gasoline -5.7% in June CPI, -12% in June PPI goods) is the proximate
      driver; partially offset by ongoing non-labor input cost pressures.
    Net: DOVISH on prices — first district-wide price deceleration signal
         since the energy price shock began. Corroborates the June CPI/PPI prints.

  BEIGE BOOK NET READ: MILDLY DOVISH — price section is the key signal.
    The all-districts price deceleration is the first collective downshift in
    the Beige Book price descriptor since the onset of the energy price shock.
    Growth and labor sections are essentially unchanged. Taken together with
    the June CPI and June PPI, the Beige Book closes the pre-blackout data
    picture with three consistent dovish-on-prices signals in 48 hours.

Type A (Economic Outlook). Full scored file: data/scored/speeches/20260715_John_Williams_scored.txt

GROWTH
  "Growth in the economy is solid and on trend." GDP: ~2.0–2.25% this year and next
  two years; "modestly above its potential rate." Unemployment edging toward 4.0%
  by 2028. AI investment named as the engine — consistent with June 25 framing.
  Forecast unchanged from June 25. No growth concern flagged.
  Direction: neutral

LABOR MARKET
  "The labor market is likewise solid and stable." Labor NOT adding to inflationary
  pressures — his stable holding formula maintained. Inflation expectations "well-
  anchored," giving the Fed "policy breathing room." Consistent with June 25;
  slight tonal upgrade from "largely stabilized, albeit at low levels" → "solid
  and stable."
  Direction: neutral

INFLATION
  "Inflation is unquestionably too high." Three-driver framework (tariffs + Middle
  East + AI demand) unchanged. KEY NEW LANGUAGE: "There are encouraging reasons to
  expect that inflation has peaked and should edge down in coming quarters." — "peaked"
  is new vs. June 25's "edge down." Williams incorporates the same-morning PPI print
  into his forward inflation call. Year-end 2026 forecast revised down to ~3.25%
  (from June 25's 3.5%), then glide toward 2% by 2028.
  Direction: DOVISH LEAN — "peaked" claim and downward forecast revision are the signal.

MONETARY POLICY
  "The current stance of monetary policy is well positioned to do that." Unchanged
  from June 25. No cut signal, no hike signal, no forward guidance. Does NOT echo
  Cook's "prepared to act" or Waller's near-term tightening conditionality. The
  "peaked" call serves as his justification for the current rate being sufficient —
  not as a dovish pivot toward cuts.
  Direction: neutral (hold-anchored by design)

NET SIGNAL: NEUTRAL on stance, DOVISH LEAN on inflation direction.
  Williams absorbs the benign June data with a "peaked" call — more confident than
  his June 25 "edge down." He does not join the Just Voter conditional hike camp.
  He is the Troika's patient-hold voice: disinflation is coming, rates are right,
  be patient. Explicitly not "not mission accomplished."

vs. PRIOR SPEECH (June 25):             DOVISH LEAN — "peaked" is new, more
  confident than "edge down"; year-end forecast revised down 0.25pp. Stance unchanged.
vs. PRE-FOMC SPEECH (May 4):            MIXED — confident disinflation call new;
  return-to-2% timeline slips 2027→2028 from May 4; "increasing labor slack" from
  May 4 fully replaced by "solid and stable."
vs. COMMITTEE SINCE FOMC:               Williams is the most constructive Troika
  voice on the inflation path. Warsh (Jul 14): "not mission accomplished" (vigilant).
  Williams (Jul 15): "peaked" + "well positioned" (patient). The Troika is speaking
  from both ends of the hold message — Warsh anchors the hawkish constraint, Williams
  anchors the patient disinflation path.

LANGUAGE TO WATCH:
  • "inflation has peaked" — Troika member making an explicit peak call; first use
    in the 2026 scored corpus. If Warsh or Jefferson echo it, it signals the Troika
    is preparing the ground for a future easing bias. Stage: DISPERSED (Williams only).
  • Year-end inflation forecast: 3.5% → 3.25% — data-driven downward revision;
    watch for committee-wide pattern in coming speeches.

ANALYST FLAG: YES
  → Does "peaked" signal a coming Troika split, with Warsh maintaining hawkish
    vigilance while Williams/Jefferson build the dovish-path case for a future
    cut? Or is this coordinated Troika communication (one hawk, one dove, same
    hold outcome)? Jefferson's absence from the pre-blackout window is notable —
    his next speech is the key for resolving this.

Type A (Economic Outlook). Full scored file: data/scored/speeches/20260715_Lisa_D__Cook_scored.txt
Cook's first Type A speech since the June 17 FOMC meeting.

GROWTH
  GDP 2025: ~2.0%; FOMC projects 2026: ~2.2%. Labor productivity: ~2.5% annual
  average over past two years. AI investment boom driving above-trend growth.
  Growth not flagged as a policy concern; treated as stable and positive.
  Direction: neutral

LABOR MARKET
  "Nearly all indicators point to stability." Unemployment at 4.2% (edged
  down from 4.3% in April). KEY: "I see few reasons that today's labor market
  has more risk than a year earlier" — explicit downgrade of employment downside
  risk. Compare May 27: "I view the downside risks to the labor market as being
  elevated." "I see a notable shift in the balance of risks relative to a year or
  so ago, with inflation risks now outweighing employment risks." Formal June-17
  framework adoption: labor is no longer the offsetting risk.
  Direction: HAWKISH LEAN — employment risk explicitly minimized.

INFLATION
  "The initial assessment is easy: Inflation is simply too high." PCE: ~3.7%
  YoY (1.7pp above target). Three-driver framework unchanged: AI demand, tariffs,
  Middle East. "I am fully committed to reaching our inflation target, and this
  commitment is unwavering." "Simply too high" (level verdict) is firmer than May
  27's directional "clearly moving in the wrong direction." Cook does not engage with
  the same-morning June PPI print or the prior day's CPI — no concession to the
  benign data.
  Direction: HAWKISH

MONETARY POLICY
  Rate characterized as "mildly restrictive" — new characterization, adding
  uncertainty about whether policy is sufficiently tight. "The FOMC can take its
  time, I can take my time, to observe more data to understand whether it's really
  restrictive or not." KEY: "If we do not see signs of disinflation soon, I am
  prepared to act." — explicit conditional hike signal, maintained from May 27's
  "prepared to raise rates." Cut backstop language from May 27 ("prepared to
  adjust my policy stance downward should the labor market deteriorate") is ABSENT.
  Hike signal: PRESENT (conditional on absence of disinflation "soon").
  Cut signal: ABSENT (contrast May 27's employment backstop).
  Direction: HAWKISH

NET SIGNAL: HAWKISH. Cook's post-June-17 debut confirms the hike signal,
  drops the employment backstop, explicitly de-emphasizes labor risk, and
  characterizes policy as only "mildly restrictive." She does not soften in
  response to the benign CPI/PPI data — the conditional is set on disinflation
  "soon," not on the next print.

vs. PRIOR SPEECH (May 27 — pre-FOMC baseline):
  HAWKISH NET — hike signal maintained; employment risk explicitly downgraded
  (from "elevated" to "I see few reasons"); cut backstop dropped; "mildly
  restrictive" characterization added; balance of risks formally tilted to inflation.
vs. PRE-FOMC SPEECH (May 27):
  [Same speech — May 27 is both the prior and the pre-FOMC baseline.]
vs. COMMITTEE SINCE FOMC:
  Cook represents the Board's hawkish wing alongside Waller (Jul 13). With
  Waller's hike conditionality now technically not triggered (benign PPI),
  Cook's "prepared to act" carries the conditional hike signal into the
  blackout as the clearest standing hike signal from a voter. She is more
  hawkish than Williams ("peaked," patient hold) and more hawkish than
  Goolsbee's conditional normalization (Jul 14). Consistent with Warsh's
  "not mission accomplished" framing but with an explicit rate signal Warsh
  withholds.

LANGUAGE TO WATCH:
  • "inflation is simply too high" — Cook's own-language level verdict; watch
    for committee adoption. Stage: DISPERSED.
  • "I am prepared to act" — hike conditional, maintained from May 27;
    Cook + Waller are both carrying it. Stage: EMERGING (two Just Voters).
  • "mildly restrictive" — new rate characterization; if adopted by others,
    implies a lower bar for further tightening. Stage: DISPERSED.
  • Absence of cut backstop — deliberate or venue artifact? Monitor next speech.

ANALYST FLAG: YES
  → (1) Cook does not soften despite the benign June CPI/PPI. Her conditional
    is prospective ("soon"), not data-reactive in the backward-looking direction.
    Does she update her framework if July data are also benign?
  → (2) "Mildly restrictive" alongside "prepared to act" creates tension: if
    the rate is only mildly restrictive, the case for action is weaker. This
    may be Cook's deliberate framing to leave flexibility — watch whether this
    exact phrase migrates into other officials' speeches.

   all three mildly dovish. Waller's hike-conditionality data triggers are NOT
   met. The collective data picture is the weakest inflationary signal since

   premature celebration, Williams is describing the trajectory — but they frame
   the same data very differently and send different signals to market participants.

3. JUST VOTER SPLIT: Cook (Jul 15): conditional hike signal maintained, employment
   risk de-emphasized, hawkish. Waller (Jul 13): hike conditionality stated but now
   technically NOT triggered by the data he named.

The net committee picture entering the July 28-29 blackout:
  → HOLD is the virtually certain July 28-29 outcome. Hike is off the table under
    Waller's stated framework. Cut is not on the table given the still-elevated
    inflation and Warsh/Cook/Waller hawkish vigilance.
  → The SEPTEMBER meeting is now the focal point. Williams's "peaked" call
    and 3.25% year-end forecast imply he sees no need to hike in September.
    Cook's "if no disinflation soon" and Waller's "at a crossroads" framing
    leave September open for a hike if inflation re-accelerates.
  → The committee goes into the blackout with an internal tension that the
    July 28-29 statement is unlikely to resolve — the July meeting is a hold
    with debate about the September forward path simmering underneath.
