Type F (Congressional Testimony). Prepared remarks identical to the July 14
House testimony — see the July 14 daily for the prepared-statement analysis.
This section covers the Senate Q&A. Full scored file:
data/scored/speeches/20260715_Kevin_Warsh_scored.txt

GROWTH
  Capex remains the centerpiece: "a capex investment boom that the country
  hasn't seen in more than a generation … the seed corn for productivity and GDP
  improvement over the next decade." On the AI-bubble question he offered a
  compact two-sided test: the Fed wants "economic strength to broaden and the
  inflation … to become more narrow." Supply-side optimism, no rate conclusion.
  Direction: neutral

LABOR MARKET
  "The labor market broadly looks in balance." His cleanest dual-mandate ranking
  to date: "the labor market part of our mandate looks pretty good. The price
  stability part of our mandate looks less good." Acknowledges aggregate data
  can "look better than people feel" during structural transition; the verdict
  is unmoved.
  Direction: neutral (labor de-prioritized as a policy driver — hawkish structure)

INFLATION
  On the CPI/PPI pair: "any central bank would be happy to have the data going
  in the right direction" — then the cap: "These are all imperfect measures of
  the state of underlying inflation." One notch warmer than the House hearing's
  "not mission accomplished," identical vigilance structure. The "63 months"
  above-target clock repeated. To Kennedy: "It's not going to be permanent under
  my watch." Notable analytical split: he discounts AI investment as an
  inflation driver — a one-time price change with a coming supply response is
  "not necessarily inflationary … Whether that's inflationary or not, that's up
  to the Federal Reserve" — explicitly nuancing away from Waller and Williams,
  whom the questioner cited. Monetary aggregates (M2) restored to the MPR:
  "monetary policy has something to do with money."
  Direction: neutral-hawkish (vigilance intact; benign data conceded direction
  only, not conclusion)

MONETARY POLICY
  No rate signal in ~2 hours of Q&A. Pressed for options: leave alone, raise,
  cut — "a non-exclusive list of options." Asked directly about Waller's hike
  conditionality: "There are diversity of views. I wouldn't want to dwell on
  any one of them." Balance-sheet doctrine extended: "As we think about
  inflation risks … we have two instruments that we should be thinking about.
  The balance sheet is one of them" — the first time he has tied the balance
  sheet to the current inflation fight; preference for a "leaner, meaner
  balance sheet," rates as the primary tool. Markets doctrine distilled:
  "play the ball, don't play the Fed." Task forces: FOMC briefings as early as
  September, public conclusions by year-end. Independence: "They chose an
  independent guy to do an independent job"; declined to characterize
  conversations with the President ("I don't want to be in the business of
  sharing discussions that the president and I have") while asserting no
  attempt to influence policy has occurred.
  Direction: neutral by design

NET SIGNAL: NEUTRAL by design, vigilance intact — two days, ~5 hours of Q&A,
  zero rate-path signal, all three options explicitly open. The balance-sheet-
  as-inflation-instrument framing and the AI-inflation split with Waller/
  Williams are the new intelligence.

vs. PRIOR APPEARANCE (July 14 House):     unchanged on substance — same
  prepared text, same vigilance; data acknowledgment one notch warmer
  ("right direction" vs. "not mission accomplished"), capped by "imperfect
  measures."
vs. PRE-FOMC SPEECH:                      N/A — no pre-June-17 baseline as Chair.
vs. COMMITTEE SINCE FOMC:                 Holds the center by refusing to
  engage either wing: no endorsement of Waller/Cook conditional-hike language,
  no echo of Williams's "peaked." Full optionality preserved into blackout.

LANGUAGE TO WATCH:
  • "imperfect measures" — the standing device for discounting favorable
    prints; second consecutive day. Stage: DISPERSED.
  • "two instruments" (balance sheet vs. inflation risks) — watch Logan
    (Jul 16), the committee's balance-sheet specialist. Stage: DISPERSED.
  • "play the ball, don't play the Fed" — likely media pickup as the
    no-guidance regime's shorthand. Stage: DISPERSED.

ANALYST FLAG: YES
  → Balance sheet named as an inflation-relevant instrument goes beyond the

GROWTH
  "Growth in the economy is solid and on trend." GDP ~2 to 2-1/4 percent this
  year and the next two; "modestly above my estimate of its potential rate of
  2 percent"; unemployment edging "very gradually to 4 percent in 2028."
  Composition flagged: "pockets of red-hot growth" (AI/tech investment,
  equity-supported spending) are offsetting declines in residential
  construction, federal spending, and energy-squeezed household spending.
  Forecast unchanged from June 25.
  Direction: neutral

LABOR MARKET
  "The labor market is likewise solid and stable" — his summary verdict.
  Unemployment "within a narrow range of about 4-1/4 to 4-1/2 percent over the
  past year"; soft-data survey measures "largely stabilized, albeit at low
  levels" (same formulation as June 25, applied to the same indicators). Labor
  is "not adding to inflationary pressures" (HPW index, wage data) — his stable
  anchor removing labor as a two-sided risk driver.
  Direction: neutral

INFLATION
  "Inflation is unquestionably too high at about 4 percent" (PCE). Three-driver
  framework unchanged (tariffs, Middle East, AI investment demand). KEY: "There
  are encouraging reasons to expect that inflation has peaked and should edge
  down in coming quarters" — the "peaked" claim goes beyond his June 25 "edge
  down" and is the first peak call by any official in the 2026 corpus. Six
  stated reasons: tariff effects largely played out; modest market rents;
  energy "likely peaked" per futures pricing; AI supply response coming; labor
  not contributing; expectations anchored. Year-end forecast revised down to
  ~3-1/4 percent (from June 25's 3-1/2), still landing on 2% target in 2028.
  Direction: DOVISH — peak call plus downward forecast revision, absorbing the
  benign June CPI/PPI.

MONETARY POLICY
  "It is imperative that we restore [inflation] to the Federal Reserve's 2
  percent longer-run goal on a sustained basis. The current stance of monetary
  policy is well positioned to do that." Identical stance formulation to June
  25. No cut signal, no hike signal, no forward guidance; closes on his
  standing data-dependence formula. Does not echo Cook's "prepared to act" or
  Waller's tightening conditionality — the peak call underwrites patience at
  the current rate, not easing.
  Direction: neutral (hold-anchored by design)

NET SIGNAL: NEUTRAL on stance, DOVISH on inflation direction. Williams is the
  Troika's patient-hold voice: disinflation has crested, the current rate
  delivers it, be patient.

vs. PRIOR SPEECH (June 25):             DOVISH on inflation — "peaked" is a
  stronger claim than "edge down"; year-end forecast cut 0.25pp. Stance,
  growth, and labor unchanged.
vs. PRE-FOMC SPEECH (May 4):            MIXED — confident peak call vs. May 4's
  upside-tilted risks; but return-to-2% timeline remains 2028 (May 4 said
  2027); May 4's "increasing labor market slack" concern remains withdrawn.
vs. COMMITTEE SINCE FOMC:               The most constructive committee voice
  on the inflation path. Same-day contrast: Warsh concedes direction but not
  conclusion ("imperfect measures"); Williams asserts the peak. The Troika is
  speaking from both ends of the hold — Warsh anchors vigilance, Williams
  anchors the disinflation path.

LANGUAGE TO WATCH:
  • "inflation has peaked" — first peak call in the 2026 corpus; if Warsh or
    Jefferson echo it, the Troika is preparing the ground for a future easing
    bias. Stage: DISPERSED (Williams only).
  • Year-end inflation forecast 3.5% → 3.25% — watch for a committee-wide
    revision pattern in the September SEP.

ANALYST FLAG: YES
  → Williams calls the inflation peak one week before blackout while Warsh

Type A (Economic Outlook). Cook's first outlook speech since the June 17
meeting. Full scored file: data/scored/speeches/20260715_Lisa_D__Cook_scored.txt

GROWTH
  "Surprisingly resilient output": GDP 2.0% in 2025, FOMC forecast 2.2% for
  2026 — both ~1/2 point above last year's forecasts. Productivity "booming"
  (~2-1/2%/yr for two years); "the data center buildout has added some heat to
  the economy." Growth strength is deployed as evidence of "less risk to the
  employment mandate" — an argument on the hawkish side of her risk balance.
  Direction: neutral (used hawkishly)

LABOR MARKET
  "Nearly all indicators point to stability." Unemployment 4.2%, in line with
  the natural rate. KEY: "I see few reasons that today's labor market has more
  risk than a year earlier. Therefore, risks on the employment side have
  diminished" — a direct reversal of her May 27 "downside risks to the labor
  market as being elevated." Her seesaw metaphor resolves: "the balance of
  risks has teetered toward the inflation mandate." Low-hire/low-fire strain on
  new entrants acknowledged but bounded (not a downturn signal).
  Direction: HAWKISH — employment risk explicitly downgraded.

INFLATION
  She engages the benign data head-on and dismisses it at the level: "Even
  though this week's consumer price index and producer price index reports were
  softer than expected, they still imply that the price index we target rose
  3.7 percent in the 12 months through June … We have not reached our 2 percent
  target in more than five years." Verdict: "The initial assessment is easy:
  inflation is simply too high" — near the highest since 2023. Core goods
  rising at "a striking 5 percent annual pace": "the recent acceleration in
  inflation is not only an energy price story." Risks "continue to be strongly
  weighted toward higher inflation": the AI buildout ($1.5T+ announced data
  center plans, mostly unrealized) and persistence risk after five years above
  target ("Firms' pricing and wage decisions may depend more on what inflation
  has been rather than its source"). Anchored expectations comfort her "only to
  the extent" they show confidence the Fed will act — "they do not tell us what
  those policy actions need to be."
  Direction: HAWKISH

MONETARY POLICY
  Endorses the June hold as her vote: prudent "to give a bit more time to
  observe how inflation unfolds." KEY: "If we do not see signs of disinflation
  soon, I am prepared to act. I am fully committed to reaching our inflation
  target, and this commitment is unwavering" — her May 27 conditional hike
  signal maintained, with the trigger tightened from "in a timely manner" to
  "soon." The May 27 labor-market cut backstop is absent — the conditionality
  now runs one way. In Q&A (wire-reported): the rate is "mildly restrictive";
  "The FOMC can take its time, I can take my time, to observe more data to
  understand whether it's really restrictive or not."
  Hike signal: PRESENT (conditional on absence of disinflation "soon").
  Cut signal: ABSENT (May 27 employment backstop dropped).
  Direction: HAWKISH

NET SIGNAL: HAWKISH. Conditional hike signal maintained, employment backstop
  dropped, labor risk downgraded, benign June data ruled insufficient at the
  level. Her conditional is prospective ("soon"), not print-reactive — the
  soft data do not disarm it.

vs. PRIOR SPEECH (May 27 — also pre-FOMC baseline):
  HAWKISH NET — employment risk from "elevated" to "few reasons"; cut backstop
  dropped; level verdict ("simply too high") on top of the directional one;
  restrictiveness of the current stance questioned in Q&A.
vs. COMMITTEE SINCE FOMC:
  With Waller's July 13 data trigger technically unmet by CPI/PPI, Cook's
  "prepared to act" is the clearest standing hike conditional carried by a
  voter into the blackout. More hawkish than Williams (same day) and Goolsbee
  (Jul 14); aligned in vigilance with Warsh but with an explicit rate
  conditional the Chair withholds.

LANGUAGE TO WATCH:
  • "inflation is simply too high" — converges with Williams's same-day
    "unquestionably too high" on a shared level-verdict register. Stage:
    EMERGING (two speakers, same day).
  • "I am prepared to act" — Cook and Waller both carry a hike conditional.
    Stage: EMERGING (two Just Voters).
  • "mildly restrictive" (Q&A) — if it migrates into prepared texts or other
    officials' language, the bar for tightening drops. Stage: DISPERSED.

ANALYST FLAG: YES
  → (1) Her trigger is disinflation "soon." If July/August prints extend the

  → (2) "Mildly restrictive" + "prepared to act" implies doubt the current
