━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

GROWTH
  "Economic growth is strong" — GDP 3.4% Q3; Dallas Fed Weekly Economic Index
  consistent with ~3% annualized. Business investment "robust": equipment +14%
  YoY, led by AI data center build (>80% of tech equipment growth) with broadening
  into industrial machinery, semiconductor mfg, defense. "Technological innovation
  and changes in global trade and security alliances appear poised to continue
  driving strong investment in coming years." Consumer spending solid, weighted
  toward wealthier households; growth permissive of further tightening.
  Direction: neutral (hawkish permissive backdrop)

LABOR MARKET
  "Balanced and even tightening a bit"; unemployment "just above 4 percent, down
  slightly from late last year." Conclusion: "achieving maximum employment...
  remains on track." New nuance: "not especially dynamic" — Logan raises the
  same low-hire/low-fire structural concern Waller raises simultaneously, then
  explicitly quarantines it from policy: "Monetary policy addresses cyclical
  mismatches, not structural challenges." "Latest data show hints of increased
  dynamism, including more job openings and more workers quitting."
  Direction: neutral

INFLATION
  PCE 3.6% (12m Sept); core PCE 3.1%; October CPI "higher than expected" this
  morning. Non-housing, non-transportation core services 3.2% — "eight-tenths
  above the pace historically consistent" with 2% — and these categories "aren't
  particularly sensitive to tariffs, conflict in the Middle East or surging demand
  for computer chips... the broader inflationary trend is more likely to persist."
  Notable: Dallas Fed trimmed mean (2.3%) explicitly dismissed as currently
  misleading downward due to composition effects. NY Fed multivariate core trend:
  2.8%. Overall verdict: "trending meaningfully above 2 percent."
  Direction: hawkish

MONETARY POLICY
  Real fed funds rate: 3.88% nominal minus 3.1% expected PCE inflation = 78bp,
  "a quarter-percentage-point below the lowest estimate of neutral" (8-model range:
  1.0–1.85% real). Explicit stance label: "accommodative monetary policy is adding
  to inflationary pressures." Gap to even modest restriction: "at least 50 basis
  points." Path: "the FOMC needs to implement at least two more 25-basis-point rate
  increases." After those: pause to assess. Speed option: "it may be possible to
  move more rapidly this winter than the FOMC has moved so far this fall." October
  skip: "skipping one meeting does not change the monetary policy destination."
  Direction: hawkish

NET SIGNAL: HAWKISH — the most hawkish and most analytically detailed post-FOMC
  outlook speech from any voter this cycle. Key escalation vs. July 31 dissent:
  she asked for one move; she now says "at least two more." The real-rate
  calculation is the most precise policy-gap estimate from any voter.

vs. PRIOR SPEECH (Jul 31, 2026):   HAWKISH — Jul 31 asked for one move (25bp);
  Nov 10 says "at least two more." Inflation verdict upgraded from "mid-2's"

vs. PRE-FOMC SPEECH (Jul 31, 2026): Same — HAWKISH. No intervening scored speech.

  enough"; Waller (Oct 8): "additional hikes... not consecutive"; Schmid (Sep 18):
  "a step in that direction." Logan is well ahead of the committee median on the

GROWTH
  GDP 3.4% Q3, "healthy pace"; consumer spending strong (wealth-effect driven);
  business investment in AI continuing. Q4 growth "backing off a bit" as spending
  normalizes toward income, but "output will continue to grow at a solid pace."
  Growth provides cover for tightening without recession risk.
  Direction: neutral

LABOR MARKET
  "Solid and stable" in September; 4.1% unemployment, payroll gains "in the range
  of breakeven." Extended structural analysis of "low hire, low fire" equilibrium:
  long-term unemployed (27+ weeks) at 27%+ of unemployed (20-year high context);
  new entrant unemployment near 20-year high. Explicit policy disposal: "What is
  important in the near term is that I see the labor market as stable and employment
  at a level close to the FOMC's maximum-employment goal." Labor dynamism concern
  is a "longer-term" question, quarantined from near-term policy.
  Direction: neutral

INFLATION
  "September's inflation data were bad. There was no silver lining." PCE 0.5%
  monthly, 3.7% 12m; core PCE 0.3% monthly, 3.1% 12m; breadth: "more than 70
  percent of the components of core PCE service prices saw 12-month increases above
  3 percent." Three forward risks: (1) ISM textual analysis points to more price
  increases ahead; (2) energy pass-through research finds core inflation "more
  responsive to energy shocks" post-pandemic, could "persist well into 2027";
  (3) AI-buildout inflation in high-tech goods. October CPI: "in a few hours" at
  speech time; Waller pre-committed escalation branch to 50bp if "around 0.3% or
  higher" (Logan confirms data came in hot).
  Direction: hawkish

MONETARY POLICY
  Base case: "even if [October CPI] shows an improvement over September, I would
  still support a 25 basis point increase... at our December meeting." Escalation
  branch: "if the October CPI data... are similar to the past two monthly
  readings—with core CPI inflation around 0.3 percent or even higher—then a
  stronger policy move may be needed. In that case, I believe a 50 basis point
  hike in the policy rate should be considered at the December meeting. Hiking only
  25 basis points would put our credibility at risk, a risk I am not eager to take."
  October hold rationale confirmed as tactical: "it is not necessary to raise rates
  at consecutive meetings."
  Direction: hawkish (escalating)

NET SIGNAL: HAWKISH — most explicit near-term escalation signal from Waller this

On the same day (November 10), two Just Voters independently made the most
hawkish coordinated statement since the July 16 pre-FOMC alignment:

(1) DESTINATION CLARITY: Both explicitly endorse the October hold as a tactical
    skip that "does not change the monetary policy destination." Neither treats
    the skip as a signal of a lower terminal rate.

(2) DECEMBER ESCALATION: Logan says "at least two more 25bp"; Waller says 25bp
    is the floor, 50bp is live if data are hot. Together they bracket December
    at 25bp minimum to 50bp possible — the first public 50bp discussion from
    voters this cycle.

(3) LABOR DYNAMISM PARALLEL: Both independently raise the "low hire, low fire"
    /labor dynamism concern on the same day, then independently quarantine it
    from near-term policy. The simultaneity is notable.

(4) REAL RATE DIAGNOSIS: Logan's "78bp below lowest neutral" and Waller's
    "policy will be focused on the inflation side of our mandate" both point to
    the same operational conclusion: current policy is NOT restricting the economy
    meaningfully, more tightening is required.

(5) OCTOBER CPI: Both reference it. Logan: "higher than expected." Waller:
    pre-committed 50bp to "around 0.3% or higher." The live data point may be
    the most consequential same-day data-and-speech alignment this cycle.
