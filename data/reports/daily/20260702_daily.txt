TIMING NOTE: Made roughly 30 minutes BEFORE the June jobs report landed
  (+57K vs. ~110K consensus, released later that day — see the July 3, 2026
  daily report). Daly explicitly pre-committed to discount that print before
  seeing it — a near-identical move to Warsh's Sintra remarks the day before.

GROWTH
  AI capex is the primary growth engine in her read: "very strong investment
  growth... I don't see any sense in which that's going to start coming
  down." Consumer spending framed as a secondary but real support, not the
  main driver. Echoes and extends the June 17 statement's "productivity
  growth and capital investment are strong" line.
  Direction: hawkish lean

LABOR MARKET
  "The job market... it's steady. We had a little bit of concern last year
  that it might be softening, but now it's stabilized." Then, ~30 minutes
  before the June NFP release: "one data point is not going to make or break
  our analysis of this" — an explicit pre-commitment to discount the
  imminent print on multi-indicator grounds.
  Direction: neutral

INFLATION
  "We continue to have policy in a slightly restrictive position, so
  inflation should come down." Oil's retreat to ~$70/barrel treated as
  "hope for relief" — a fading of the acute oil-shock alarm from her May 8
  "dashboard... starting to see red" framing. Two risk scenarios named
  (persistent inflation / growth pullback) but NEITHER attached to a
  specific policy response — descriptive framing, not a Waller-style
  explicit pre-commitment.
  Direction: dovish lean (vs. her own May 8 baseline; policy stance itself unchanged)

MONETARY POLICY
  No rate-path signal in either direction; self-described "gradualist."
  Declined to speculate on any change to meeting/decision frequency (a live
  question since Warsh's June 17 remarks). NOTABLE: diverges from Warsh's
  forward-guidance rationale — she defends forward guidance "in principle,"
  framing its current absence as a response to unusual uncertainty ("false
  guidance... not forward guidance"), versus Warsh's structural rejection of
  the tool itself. Same practice, different doctrine — worth tracking across
  future speeches to see which framing spreads.
  Cut signal status: absent
  Direction: neutral

NET SIGNAL: neutral-to-slight-dovish lean vs. her May 8 pre-FOMC baseline
  (oil-shock alarm has faded); broadly consistent with, but not doctrinally
  identical to, the June 17 committee stance.
