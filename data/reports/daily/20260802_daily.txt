WHERE THE COMMITTEE STANDS ENTERING THIS WEEK
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
The July 29 statement was frozen — its economic paragraphs are character-for-
character identical to June 17, the only edit being "reaffirmed" → "is continuing"
on ample reserves. Against that unchanged text, three voters dissented for a 25bp
hike. The dispersion is not being absorbed into the language; it is being left
visible in the vote.

The July 31 statements established that all three dissenters have escalated from
objecting to the Committee's WORDS (April 29, on easing-bias language) to
objecting to its RATE — and that all three had to get past the statement's own
"reflecting supply shocks" attribution to do it, each by a different route. That
attribution clause is now the contested ground.

Two of the three moved materially during the blackout, unobserved: Hammack (last
seen June 2, conditional) and Kashkari (last seen May 13, agnostic). Logan's
position was already public from July 16 and did not change.

Not yet heard from since July 29: the entire 9-vote majority. Everything above is
one side of the argument.
