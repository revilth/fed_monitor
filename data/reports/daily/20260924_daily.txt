1.  ANNA PAULSON  |  Philadelphia Fed President  |  Just Voter (MEDIAN)
    First communication since the hike; first prepared text since May 19
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

GROWTH
  "output growth has been solid, perhaps even strengthening somewhat" —
  one step past the statement's "solid pace." The consumer is the engine:
  Q2 real consumption at 3.4 percent annualized, GDPNow above 4 percent for
  Q3, helped by stock-market wealth that is "not ... experienced evenly."
  AI investment "continues to power the economy," and manufacturing
  strength "extends well beyond AI."
  Direction: hawkish lean

LABOR MARKET
  "stable and seem to have improved a bit." For the first time she calls
  4.1 percent unemployment "consistent with what I consider to be maximum
  employment." Hiring has "broadened" beyond healthcare. Wage growth is
  "modest," so labor is "not driving inflation pressures" (carry-over). The
  May 19 downside caveats were untested after the Aug 4 interview. They are
  absent again in a prepared text.
  Direction: hawkish lean

INFLATION
  Her underlying-inflation range moved UP: Aug 4 "somewhere in the range of
  2.4, 2.8" with "a little bit of mild progress" → Sept 24 "about 2.5 to 3
  percent ... that gap has shown little sign of closing." AI is now a direct
  inflation source: "While the impact of AI on productivity remains
  unclear, its effect on prices is not" (computer import prices +24 percent).
  Her Aug 4 defense of looking through supply shocks is not repeated.
  Direction: HAWKISH

MONETARY POLICY
  She explains the vote as her Aug 4 conditional firing: "Underlying
  inflation showed little to no progress ... That's why I supported raising
  the federal funds rate by 25 basis points." The hike "brings policy
  closer to what I believe is needed," so policy was not restrictive enough
  before it. "Mildly restrictive," her label since March, is gone. Forward
  path: "if conditions evolve as I expect, some modest further tightening
  may be warranted." Returning to 2 percent is "non-negotiable." Scenario
  framing is descriptive, a single baseline with no named trigger.
  Direction: HAWKISH

NET SIGNAL: HAWKISH — the median voter's base case now includes a further
  modest hike.

vs. PRIOR SPEECH (Aug 4, CNBC):         HAWKISH — conditional "could be higher
  rates" became an executed hike plus a lean toward more; underlying range
  raised; "mildly restrictive" dropped.
vs. PRE-FOMC SPEECH (Aug 4):            HAWKISH — same document.
vs. COMMITTEE SINCE FOMC:  Joins Barr ("further policy adjustments are
  likely to be needed," Sept 23) and Barkin ("one 'talking-to' might not be
  enough," Sept 22) in leaning toward more. She is the first of the "mildly
  restrictive" bloc (Paulson, Collins, Daly, Waller) to speak after the
  hike, and she left it.

━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

GROWTH
  "output is growing at a solid pace" — statement echo, nothing added.
  Direction: neutral

LABOR MARKET
  "the labor market remains close to my definition of maximum employment"
  — carry-over from Jul 31 and Jun 2. No downside risk named.
  Direction: neutral

INFLATION
  "The inflation outlook continues to be highly uncertain with risks tilted
  to the upside." This echoes the Chair's Sept 16 "Inflation risks are to
  the upside," but she drops his "labor risks are roughly balanced." She
  names the demand shock for the first time: "the boom in capital spending
  related to AI." Jul 31 said only "the demand side of the economy." She
  also argues against looking through supply shocks when "the shocks arrive
  one after another in a period when inflation has been elevated for
  years." "Costly to bring it back down" is verbatim from Jul 31.
  Direction: hawkish lean

MONETARY POLICY
  Nothing. No mention of the September hike, the rate level or the path.
  This was a research-conference welcome. She told the room she will be on
  a policy panel Friday afternoon, which is where a path view would come.
  Direction: no signal

NET SIGNAL: HAWKISH LEAN on inflation; no policy signal.

  carry-over. Policy untested. On Aug 27 she saw "no restriction in policy"
  and a high neutral, and today she did not say whether one hike fixed that.
vs. PRE-FOMC SPEECH (Aug 27):           same document.
vs. COMMITTEE SINCE FOMC:  The dissent bloc's framework is now shared. The
  statement deleted the supply-shock clause, and the median voter made the

  path, not the diagnosis.

CROSS-SPEAKER NOTE
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
The median voter and a July dissenter spoke an hour apart and said the same
thing about AI. Paulson: "The AI buildout is one factor keeping underlying
inflation stubbornly high." Hammack: shocks "from the demand side from the
boom in capital spending related to AI." On Aug 4 Paulson used AI only as a
reason policy might not be restrictive enough. Now both treat it as a direct
inflation driver. If AI capex is a demand shock, looking through it is not an
option, and that removes the last doctrinal argument for pausing after one
hike.

Paulson went further than Hammack on policy. The median voter now has a
base-case lean toward "some modest further tightening." The July dissenter
said nothing on the path today.
