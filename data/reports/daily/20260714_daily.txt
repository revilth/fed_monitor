1.  KEVIN WARSH  |  Chair  |  Troika
    "Semiannual Monetary Policy Report to the Congress (Day 1)"
    House Financial Services Committee
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
Type F (Congressional Testimony) — Humphrey-Hawkins Day 1, House. First HH testimony
as Chair. Coincided with the June CPI release (8:30am ET: headline 3.5% / core flat
MoM). Scored from the full transcript (official prepared remarks + C-SPAN Q&A).

GROWTH
  "Economic activity is expanding at a solid pace, showing resilience." AI investment
  is "the most striking feature of the economy right now" (equipment +8% y/y; high-tech
  ~25%). "The housing sector, however... continues to lag" — but he
  routes housing weakness back into the price-stability case (mortgages high "because
  of inflation"), not toward cuts. KEY Q&A: refused the Greenspan-1990s analogy of
  cutting rates ahead of the data on a productivity boom — "If you've seen one
  productivity boom, you've seen one productivity boom... I can't say it for certain
  as of yet." Blocks the dovish reading of his own supply-side optimism.
  Direction: neutral (with the preemptive-cut door explicitly closed)

LABOR MARKET
  "Broadly stable" — unchanged from Sintra "steady"; weak June NFP (+57K) did not move
  him. Detail: "relatively few layoffs, only slight variance in job vacancies, solid
  growth in nominal wages." KEY Q&A: "The labor markets look to be in pretty
  good balance. We've got some work to do on the inflation front" — explicit
  prioritization. "No cruel choice" between the mandates (repeated 3x): full
  employment is delivered THROUGH price stability. [Inference: labor softening would
  not independently justify easing under this doctrine.]
  Direction: neutral verdict, mildly hawkish prioritization

INFLATION
  Prepared: "no tolerance for persistently elevated inflation"; "resolute commitment";
  "the inflation surge of the last five years will be a thing of the past"; monthly
  fluctuations vs. "underlying inflation over longer time horizons is determined
  largely by monetary policy."
  Q&A on June CPI (used twice): "There might be some that look at this
  morning's data and say, 'Oh, mission accomplished. Everything is swell.' That is
  not my view." / "It's one data point... I don't want to overread or cherrypick data."
  "Sticky prices": "The longer that prices have been above the inflation target,
  it's usually a bit harder to dislodge them... my commitment to you is to take sticky
  prices and to unstick them" — duration makes disinflation harder; raises the bar.
  2% target: "That is our resolution... double down on," PLUS the Volcker-era
  broader definition ("change in prices such that households... don't have to think
  about it") with "we'll see whether there's further reforms to be had."
  Denied the May 31 WSJ trimmed-mean-PCE story: "incorrect... none of those are
  very good measures... we need new measures."
  On the 2020 FAIT: "It was a mistake."
  "Regime change" — in his own words, on the record.
  Sintra's "inflation risks have come down" softening is ABSENT from the full
  3-hour hearing — tactical, now buried.
  Direction: hawkish lean

MONETARY POLICY
  No rate signal anywhere in 3 hours — July 28–29 referenced only as "a continued good
  family fight... in a couple of weeks." "You'll hear from me a lot less forward
  guidance and a lot of listening." "No anguish in central banking here" (rejecting
  Burns): "commitment, ownership, tools — we're three for three."
  BALANCE SHEET DOCTRINE: "The balance sheet is
  part of monetary policy... isn't merely about plumbing"; "In normal times, the
  Federal Reserve should be a price taker, not a price maker"; post-crisis policy
  "driven almost exclusively by interest rate policy" ("interest rates get in the
  cracks"); flagged the duration gap (Fed holdings ~6.5y vs. market ~4.5y — Volcker:
  "edge of its authority"); "better regimes we can go to," changes long, public,
  well-telegraphed. [Inference: preview of the balance-sheet task force landing zone —
  eventual reduction/shorter duration framed as regime reform, not tightening.]
  Independence escalated: "sacrosanct"; "The Fed will remain independent on my watch";
  "I would continue to do my job"; "follow the law and follow the data."
  Task forces: staffed and operating ("discovery mode"); conclusions expected by
  end of year — resolves the Sintra timing question.
  Cut signal: ABSENT. Hike signal: ABSENT by design (no echo of Waller Jul 13).
  Direction: neutral (by deliberate design)

NET SIGNAL: NEUTRAL by design, HAWKISH by framing.
  "Not mission accomplished" + "sticky prices" + "no cruel choice" + refusal of the
  Greenspan preemptive-cut analogy together close every dovish exit from his own
  optimistic supply-side story. Labor is fine; inflation is "the work." He did NOT
  amplify Waller's tightening conditionality — July 28–29 optionality fully open.
  The balance-sheet doctrine is the highest-value intelligence of the hearing.

vs. PRIOR SPEECH (Jul 1, Sintra):        HAWKISH LEAN — Sintra's "inflation risks
  have come down" tactical softening is absent; full June 17 hawkish vigilance
  restored. "Not mission accomplished" directly forecloses the dovish CPI reading.
vs. PRIOR PRESS CONFERENCE (Jun 17):     UNCHANGED substantively — same themes,
  same framing, same no-signal posture. "Not mission accomplished" (absent from
  the Jun 17 transcript) addresses the July 14 CPI context directly.
vs. COMMITTEE SINCE FOMC:               Warsh maintains the orchestrator role:
  "no tolerance" + "regime change" anchors the committee's public stance, while
  Waller (Jul 13) has set the explicit hike-conditionality on the hawk side and
  Goolsbee (below) reacts cautiously to the benign CPI on the less-hawkish side.
  Warsh's refusal to signal holds the center while preserving the right tail.

LANGUAGE TO WATCH:
  • "Not mission accomplished" — first use in this context; high spread probability.
    Watch for Logan (Jul 16) and Senate testimony (Jul 15) echoes. Stage: DISPERSED.
  • "Sticky prices → unstick them" — Warsh own-language, first use; hawkish duration
    framing. Stage: DISPERSED (single speaker).
  • "Price taker, not price maker" (balance sheet) — doctrine formulation for the
    balance-sheet reform debate. Stage: DISPERSED.
  • "No cruel choice" / "not an either-or" (dual mandate) — used 3x in this hearing
    and at the June 17 press conference. Stage: EMERGING (Warsh-consistent).
  • "63 months" running clock of above-target inflation — watch for committee pickup.
  • "No tolerance for persistently elevated inflation" + "resolute commitment" —
    committee-level language (prepared remarks = MPR consensus). Stage: COMMITTEE-WIDE.
  • "Regime change" — in Warsh's own words, on the record; watch whether it migrates
    into technical policy speeches or stays audience-specific.
  • Absence of "inflation risks have come down" (from Sintra) — absent from the full
    3-hour hearing; tactical softening buried. Watch Senate Day 2.

ANALYST FLAG: YES
  → (1) CRITICAL DATA TRIGGER: June core CPI flat MoM is not a "hot reading" under
    Waller's July 13 rule — his stated framework should push toward hold. But Warsh's
    "not mission accomplished" reframes the print as insufficient progress, not as

  → (2) Balance-sheet doctrine: does Logan (Jul 16 — ex-NY Fed markets desk, the

  → (4) Senate testimony (Jul 15) Q&A — watch for whether Warsh updates the Sintra
    "inflation risks have come down" line or continues to bury it.

Type B (fireside chat — roughly half the event is Fed-explainer and community
content) with embedded economic outlook, scored per Type A methodology. Moderated
by KABA President Nicole Ryf. Same-day June CPI reaction. Full scored file:
data/scored/speeches/20260714_Austan_Goolsbee_scored.txt

GROWTH
  No cyclical growth read — long-run optimism boilerplate only (Buffett/Dow-
  since-1900 story). Housing treated as structural affordability with a supply
  answer ("you got to build more houses"), not a demand signal. The Jun 25
  AI-productivity overheating frame was not reprised in this venue; AI appears
  only as a possible offset to the Midwest demographic drag.
  Direction: neutral

LABOR MARKET
  Verdict verbatim for the third consecutive appearance: "remarkably stable
  without being good. So it's not a super boom, but it's not falling apart."
  Payrolls have "broken down as the leading indicator of the business cycle"
  (population/immigration measurement uncertainty); the rates-based measures —
  "the hiring rate, the layoff rate, the vacancy rate, the unemployment rate...
  have been largely flat. And I think those are the best indicators." [Inference:
  this is his look-through of the weak June NFP; he does not cite the print
  directly.] Same low-hire/low-fire paradox, attributed to uncertainty; "on the
  job side we're still going to see that" — stability expected to persist.
  Direction: neutral

INFLATION
  June CPI "surprisingly benign... a bit cutting the other way from what things
  have been for the last six months" — but "one month is no months," and, in
  closing: "I'm heartened by this CPI today, but we need a lot more than one
  month to think that it's going well." Services persistence remains the named
  worry ("services inflation has been way too high — historically services
  inflation is quite persistent... That number has been making me nervous"),
  with today's services component "very encouraging." The persistence question
  ("is inflation going to be more persistent than what we wanted it to be...
  the thing I've been trying to grapple with") remains his stated central
  concern. Names PPI (Jul 15) and PCE as the next gates.
  Direction: neutral — framework unchanged; favorable print acknowledged, not banked

MONETARY POLICY
  Declined the direct rate-path question: "I have never been a huge fan of
  forecasting here's what the interest rate is going to do even at the next
  meeting. I don't like even implicitly tying our hands" — consistent with the
  guidance-free regime he endorsed Jun 25. KEY own-language: "if we got PCE
  versions of inflation that look like what the CPI inflation was today and we
  got that for several months and it was clear we're back on a path toward
  2%... the sooner we can show that we're headed back to 2%, I think the sooner
  we can normalize" — a data-conditional normalization path, the dovish-side
  counterpart to Waller's Jul 13 hike conditionality, keyed to the same
  near-term inflation data. Scenario framing: descriptive-conditional (a data
  path with a policy direction, no named scenarios, no timing pre-commitment) —
  weaker than Waller's explicit rule. On Warsh: "levelheaded... I've been
  impressed"; independently corroborates the task-force topics (inflation,
  AI/productivity, balance sheet). No engagement with Waller's tightening
  conditionality; no hike language anywhere.
  Cut signal: CONDITIONAL — several months of CPI-like PCE prints → "normalize."
  Direction: dovish lean (conditional)

NET SIGNAL: DOVISH LEAN vs. Jun 25, entirely data-conditional. Labor and
  inflation frameworks are unchanged; the move is the conditional normalization
  path, which Jun 25 lacked. Nothing here resists a July 28–29 hold.

vs. PRIOR SPEECH (Jun 25):     DOVISH — volunteers a conditional normalization
  path where Jun 25 had no cut language; the productivity-overheating risk is
  not reprised.
vs. PRE-FOMC SPEECH (May 12):  HAWKISH — the cut signal survives only in
  weakened form ("normalize" on a multi-month PCE condition vs. May 12's "rates
  can come down still a fair amount"), consistent with the intervening June 17
  hawkish consensus.
vs. COMMITTEE SINCE FOMC:      The most receptive official to the benign June
  CPI and the only one to sketch the dovish exit ramp — bracketed by Warsh's
  "not mission accomplished" (no progress declared) and Waller's Jul 13 hike
  conditionality (the opposite branch, keyed to the same data).

LANGUAGE TO WATCH:
  • "the sooner we can show that we're headed back to 2%, the sooner we can
    normalize" — conditional normalization; dovish counterpart to Waller's
    conditionality. Watch whether any voter adopts a symmetric two-sided
    conditional frame. Stage: DISPERSED.
  • "one month is no months" — Goolsbee idiom, second consecutive appearance,
    here applied symmetrically to a favorable print.
  • Payrolls "broken down as the leading indicator of the business cycle" —
    rates-based measures preferred; consistent with Daly (Jul 2) and Warsh
    (Sintra) discounting single prints.

ANALYST FLAG: YES
  → The committee now has explicit conditional branches on both sides from
    different officials — Waller (Jul 13): hot core reading → consider

    both keyed to the same near-term inflation data (PPI Jul 15, then PCE).
    Assess whether Goolsbee's branch is a genuine reopening of his cut path
    after its Jun 25 absence, or venue-driven softness.
  → "Normalize" implies above-neutral policy — tension with his Feb 24 "not
    obvious that our interest rate policy is even restrictive." Probe at his
