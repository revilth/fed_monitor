The July 2026 MPR is the first under Chair Warsh (sworn in May 2026).
Released July 10 at 11:00am EDT ahead of his debut Humphrey-Hawkins
testimony (House, July 14; Senate, July 15). The MPR is a committee
document, not a personal speech; assessment is relative to (a) the
June 17, 2026 FOMC statement — the most recent committee language —
and (b) the prior MPR (June 20, 2025, last under Powell — there was NO
February 2026 MPR). Full diff: data/scored/mpr/20260710_MPR_scored.txt.

NOTE ON SOURCING (REVISED): The MPR PDF returned HTTP 403 on the original
run and the first pass was written from media coverage. The PDF was
subsequently retrieved with a browser user-agent and this section was
re-scored directly from the source document (data cutoff: noon July 8,
2026; monthly series through May, labor data through June). All quotes
below are verbatim from the report.

GROWTH
  Q1 real GDP +2.1% ("moderate pace… similar to last year's pace"),
  "capital investment rising considerably but household consumption
  increasing only very modestly" (+1.3% annualized over five months).
  "Overall, economic activity is expanding at a solid pace despite
  elevated uncertainty that owes, in part, to the conflict in the Middle
  East" — verbatim from the June 17 statement. "Labor productivity growth
  is strong." Compositional caveat: growth is investment-led (AI/data
  centers) while consumption is soft — a demand-breadth flag, not a
  headline concern.
  Direction: NEUTRAL — stable vs. June 17 consensus.

LABOR MARKET
  "The labor market has stabilized, with demand and supply roughly in
  balance. The unemployment rate, at 4.2 percent in June, was low and has
  been little changed since last summer… private payroll gains have
  picked up." Labor-supply growth slowed (immigration + aging/LFPR).
  Echoes June 17 verbatim: "Job gains have kept pace with the workforce,
  and the unemployment rate has changed little."
  CORRECTION: the MPR is NOT blind to June data. It cites the June UR
  (4.2%) and the July 2 payroll read; the information cutoff is noon
  July 8. The "stale May-cutoff vs. weak June jobs" tension asserted in
  the first pass does not hold. (The "117k vs. 10k jobs/month" figures in
  the first pass are not in the document.)
  Direction: NEUTRAL — stabilization framing removes the spring's dovish
  labor floor; no fresh hawkish push.

INFLATION
  Headline PCE +4.1% and core PCE +3.4% (12 months ending May; up from
  2.5% / 2.8% a year earlier). Energy +24%. Inflation "stepped up further
  this spring." Three drivers, verbatim: "earlier tariff hikes… a surge
  in energy prices associated with constraints on oil supplies following
  the start of the Middle East conflict in late February… and increased
  demand for some high-tech products that support artificial intelligence
  (AI) applications." (NOT "the Iran war" — the report says "Middle East
  conflict"; Iran appears only re: U.S.–Iran oil negotiations.)
  Echoes June 17 verbatim: "Inflation remains elevated relative to the
  Committee's 2 percent goal, in part reflecting supply shocks that have
  driven price increases in certain sectors, including energy."
  PARTIAL OFFSET (real, quantified): Dallas Fed trimmed-mean PCE DECLINED
  from 2.6% (last May) to 2.4% (this May) even as headline/core rose —
  the Committee distinguishes shock-driven headline from underlying trend
  (footnote 2 adds a caveat that trimmed-mean can mislead when the price-
  change distribution shifts). Long-run inflation expectations remain
  anchored near 2%.
  Direction: NEUTRAL — reaffirms June 17 structural-shock narrative;
  declining trimmed-mean + anchored expectations prevent a hawkish read
  despite the 4.1% headline.

MONETARY POLICY
  The interest-rate-policy paragraph is a VERBATIM reproduction of the
  June 17 statement, closing with "The Committee will deliver price
  stability." No forward guidance, no easing bias (consistent with the
  June 17 redesign). No new policy ground vs. June 17 — the MPR's
  function is to formalize that posture on the congressional record.
  Market rate path (report's own framing): the expected funds-rate path
  has "moved above the levels prevailing at the start of the year,"
  reflecting the Middle East conflict's inflation impulse and "increased
  confidence in the stability of the U.S. labor market" — reported as an
  observation, not a Committee endorsement.
  BALANCE-SHEET PIVOT: the Fed ended runoff (Dec 2025) and is now BUYING
  short-term Treasuries — ~$250bn in T-bills since January, balance sheet
  back to $6.7tn — the mirror image of the June 2025 MPR's active runoff.
  Plumbing, not stance, but note the direction flip.
  STRUCTURAL / WARSH FEATURES (the genuine novelty of this report):
    (1) FIVE independent task forces on (1) communications, (2) balance
        sheet policy, (3) data quality, (4) productivity/jobs, (5)
        frameworks for the drivers of inflation. Task force (1) will
        review the SEP itself "with the possibility of suggesting changes
        to current arrangements" — the dot-plot format is under review.
    (2) M2 / "stock of money" appears, but NEUTRALLY, not as a hawkish
        monetarist signal: "it is difficult to measure the stock of
        money… 12-month rates of increase in M2 were moderate and broadly
        similar to the pace typically observed during the 2010s." Moderate
        growth = no inflationary money signal.
  Direction: NEUTRAL vs. June 17 (verbatim echo, no new signal).

NET SIGNAL: NEUTRAL vs. June 17 committee language; HAWKISH-IN-TONE vs.
the true prior MPR (June 2025).
  The MPR breaks no new policy ground vs. June 17 — its rate-policy
  paragraph is reproduced verbatim and "will deliver price stability" is
  carried over, not new. Its function is formalization ahead of Warsh's
  debut testimony. The only material analytical addition is the trimmed-
  mean offset; the structural novelty is the five task forces (incl. an
  SEP review), NOT the money-supply box (neutral).

vs. JUNE 17 STATEMENT:   CONSISTENT — the interest-rate-policy paragraph
  is a verbatim reproduction; "will deliver price stability" carried over.
  No new directional signal. Trimmed-mean detail is a context addition.

vs. JUNE 2025 MPR (Powell): HAWKISH SHIFT IN TONE — from June 2025's
  symmetric, conditional register ("strongly committed to supporting
  maximum employment and returning inflation to its 2 percent objective…
  will carefully assess incoming data… well positioned to wait for more
  clarity") to July 2026's terse, unconditional "The Committee will
  deliver price stability" with no dual-mandate balancing sentence and no
  forward guidance. CAVEAT: over the same span the FFR was CUT 75bp
  (4.25–4.50% → 3.50–3.75%) and inflation roughly doubled (PCE 2.1% →
  4.1%), so this is a communication/tone hardening on an easier rate
  level against a firmer inflation backdrop — not a signal of intended
  additional tightening.

LANGUAGE TO WATCH:
  • "The Committee will deliver price stability" — committee consensus now
    on the congressional record. Watch for Warsh to use it verbatim in
    oral testimony (Jul 14–15) and for adoption by other officials.
  • Five task forces — esp. (1) communications reviewing the SEP and (5)
    frameworks for inflation drivers. Watch the July 28–29 statement/
    minutes and Warsh speeches for any procedural or framework change.
  • Trimmed-mean PCE (2.4%, declining) — watch whether Warsh uses it as a
    dovish hedge or distances from it under questioning (footnote pre-loads
    the caveat).
  • Balance-sheet flip (now buying T-bills, $6.7tn) — watch for questions
    tied to task force (2), balance-sheet policy.

ANALYST FLAG: YES
  → Live-data gap is NARROWER than first reported: the MPR already
    incorporates June UR (4.2%) and the July 2 payroll read. The remaining
    live inputs Warsh must address are June CPI (Jul 14, hearing morning)
    and June PPI (Jul 15) — not a stale-vs-June-jobs gap.
  → "Will deliver price stability" with NO dual-mandate balancing sentence
    is a deliberate communication choice. Confirm whether to treat the
    dropped symmetric "supporting maximum employment / returning inflation
    to 2 percent" language as a durable framework change (consistent with
    the June 17 redesign) rather than a one-off.
  → Task force (1) reviews the SEP "with the possibility of suggesting
    changes": the September SEP could arrive in a revised format — a
    forward process risk to the dot-plot diff methodology.
  → Market is pricing ~30% probability of a July 28–29 hike (CME
    FedWatch). The oral testimony will validate or compress this. Watch the
    2-year reaction to the oral statement vs. the written MPR.
