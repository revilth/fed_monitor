━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

The July 2026 MPR is the first under Chair Warsh (sworn in May 2026).
Released July 10 at 11:00am EDT ahead of his debut Humphrey-Hawkins
testimony (House, July 14; Senate, July 15). The MPR is a committee
document, not a personal speech; assessment is relative to (a) the
June 17, 2026 FOMC statement — the most recent committee language —
and (b) the prior MPR (February 2026, last under Powell).

NOTE ON ACCESS: The MPR PDF (federalreserve.gov) returned HTTP 403
during this run. The analysis below is drawn from contemporaneous
media reporting (Bloomberg, The Globe and Mail, American Banker,
Yahoo Finance / AP) and verified web search results. Quote marks
indicate language attributed to the report by multiple sources;
paraphrase is labelled as such.

GROWTH
  Growth characterized as "solid" with "productivity as strong" and
  "little sign of risk in the banking system." Upbeat framing. Consistent
  with June 17 statement language ("Economic activity has continued to
  expand at a solid pace") and the June minutes' characterization of AI
  investment "showing no signs of slowing." No material change in growth
  narrative from the June committee documents.
  Direction: NEUTRAL — stable vs. June 17 consensus.

LABOR MARKET
  Labor market described as having "stabilized after weakening in 2025."
  Data through May 2026: private employers added an average of 117,000
  jobs/month vs. an average of 10,000 jobs/month across 2025. This is
  a material improvement in the month-count narrative — the June minutes
  noted "several participants remarked that their concerns earlier this
  year about labor market deterioration had eased with recent data." The
  MPR's stabilization framing is directionally consistent with the June
  minutes' shift from April's dovish-labor-risk framing.
  CAVEAT: The MPR data cutoff is May 2026. The June NFP (+57K, released
  July 2) came in well below expectations and is NOT in the MPR. Warsh
  will face direct questions about June labor data in oral testimony
  July 14. The gap between the MPR's "stabilized" read and the weak June
  print is the primary labor tension heading into the hearing.
  Direction: NEUTRAL-TO-HAWKISH — stabilization framing (through May)
  removes the dovish labor floor present in April; June NFP complicates
  this and will be addressed in testimony.

INFLATION
  Inflation described as having "moved higher over the past year due to
  the impact of the Iran war, tariffs and soaring costs for technology
  goods." Multiple sources characterize the MPR's inflation language as
  "stepped-up inflation." Attribution is three-channel (Iran/Hormuz,
  tariffs, AI-tech demand) — matching the June minutes' expanded
  attribution beyond the earlier tariff-only story.
  PARTIAL OFFSET: The MPR notes that "other inflation gauges, including
  a so-called trimmed-mean measure of price gains, had declined over the
  past year." This is a meaningful concession not present in the June 17
  statement or statement redline; it suggests the committee's analytical
  framework distinguishes headline/shock-driven inflation from underlying
  trend. The trimmed-mean caveat is a potential dovish anchor if supply
  shocks retrace.
  Direction: NEUTRAL — reaffirms June 17 structural-shock narrative with
  a trimmed-mean offset. "Stepped-up inflation" is a new register vs.
  prior MPR language; trimmed-mean caveat prevents a pure hawkish read.

MONETARY POLICY
  The MPR uses "The Committee will deliver price stability" — verbatim
  from the June 17 FOMC statement's structural rewrite (hawkish departure
  from the Powell-era "committed to returning inflation to" phrasing).
  Now formalized in the congressional semiannual report, this is the
  committee's official posture heading into the July 28–29 meeting.
  NEW STRUCTURAL FEATURES under Warsh:
    (1) MONEY SUPPLY featured for the first time in a Fed MPR. Reflects
        Warsh's stated monetarist orientation and the data task force's
        early influence. This is a committee-document-level formalization
        of a Warsh personal methodological preference. Language cycle
        stage: Warsh personal → MPR/congressional document. Not yet in
        FOMC statement or minutes.
    (2) No forward guidance language. Consistent with June 17 statement
        redesign removing easing bias and forward guidance entirely.
  Direction: HAWKISH — "will deliver" unconditional commitment elevated
  to congressional-record level; money supply inclusion signals a
  structural methodological shift.

NET SIGNAL: NEUTRAL-TO-HAWKISH
  The MPR does not break new ground vs. the June 17 statement and
  minutes. Its primary function is formalization: "will deliver price
  stability" is now a congressional commitment, not just an FOMC
  statement phrase. The trimmed-mean caveat is the only material addition
  vs. June 17 committee language. Money supply inclusion is the structural
  novelty. Net: the document anchors the committee's hawkish posture
  ahead of the July 28–29 meeting without signaling a pivot in either
  direction.

vs. JUNE 17 STATEMENT:   CONSISTENT — MPR formalizes statement language
  at the congressional document level. "Will deliver price stability"
  appears. No new directional signal; trimmed-mean caveat is an addition.

vs. FEBRUARY 2026 MPR (Powell): HAWKISH SHIFT — "Will deliver" is new
  and unconditional. Money supply addition is structural. "Stepped-up
  inflation" vs. prior MPR's more measured register. No forward guidance
  or easing-bias language. Chair is Warsh (Powell's first year as Chair
  was the Feb MPR comparator).

LANGUAGE TO WATCH:
  • "The Committee will deliver price stability" — formalized in a
    congressional record. Watch for Warsh to use verbatim in oral
    testimony July 14–15 (language cycle: FOMC statement → MPR/Congress).
  • Money supply — new in an MPR. Watch for reference in July 28–29
    FOMC statement, July minutes, or post-meeting Warsh speech.
  • "Stepped-up inflation" — new register in official documents. Watch
    for adoption by other officials post-July 14 testimony.
  • Trimmed-mean inflation — watch whether this appears in oral testimony
    as a dovish hedge, or whether Warsh distances himself from it under
    Senate questioning.

ANALYST FLAG: YES
  → The MPR is dated through May 2026. June NFP (+57K, well below trend)
    and June CPI (released July 14, same morning as the House hearing)
    will be the live data Warsh must address. The "stabilized" labor
    narrative in the MPR vs. the weak June print is a key tension that
    could produce a dovish adjustment in oral testimony — or Warsh could
    apply the trend-over-print discipline he signaled at Sintra ("I tend
    to look at it over rolling periods" re: single data points).
  → Money supply inclusion: is this a Warsh personal view being introduced
    into committee documents, or a formal committee data-framework change?
    If Warsh explicitly cites M2 growth in oral testimony, it becomes a
    committee-level analytical anchor for how the Fed assesses inflationary
    pressure — with significant implications for how the committee would
    react if money supply growth reaccelerates.
  → Market is pricing ~30% probability of a July 28–29 hike (CME
    FedWatch). The oral testimony will either validate or compress this
    probability. Watch the 2-year yield reaction to the oral statement
    vs. the MPR written language for a market-based read on the delta.
