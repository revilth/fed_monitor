DATA CONTEXT — August 14, 2026
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Three data prints were released this week. Together they constitute
the primary inter-meeting data arc between the July 29 FOMC decision
and the August 19 minutes release. All three prints bear on the
dissenter vs. majority argument:

JULY CPI (released August 12, 8:30am ET):
  Headline:  +0.1% MoM  |  3.4% YoY (down from 3.5% in June)
  Core:      +0.2% MoM  |  2.5% YoY (down from 2.6% in June)
  Read: DOVISH vs. June. Two consecutive soft MoM readings (June +0.2%,
  July +0.1%). Annual core at 2.5% — within 50bp of the 2% target and
  the lowest in the cycle. The sequential cooling lowered September
  hike odds (per Kiplinger, Bloomberg). Waller's Jul 13 hike
  conditionality was explicitly tied to whether the next CPI reading
  cleared his threshold — the July print appears to have done so,
  consistent with his July 29 majority vote to hold.

  Key speaker implications:
  • Waller: his Jul 13 hike conditionality required June CPI to miss;
    June cleared his bar, he held; July also came in below his
    implicit threshold → consistent with continued hold posture.
  • Daly: her Aug 6 ESRI compounding-shocks scenario carried ~40%
    probability; July CPI is evidence against compounding → watch for
    scenario probability update in next appearance.
  • Dissenters (Hammack, Logan, Kashkari): their "inflation not yet
    controlled" dissent argument is modestly weakened by two
    consecutive soft monthly readings; core 2.5% YoY narrows the gap
    to target. Watch whether their next Type A appearance walks back
    or doubles down on "modestly higher interest rates."
  • Barkin: his Aug 13 "Will the Fed need to raise rates further?"
    language was framed as an open question, not a prediction. July
    CPI is evidence for the disinflationary side of his steelman.

JULY PPI (released August 13, 8:30am ET):
  Final demand: 0.0% MoM (unchanged; below +0.2% consensus)
  Final demand YoY: 4.7% (down from 5.5% in June)
  Core PPI: +0.2% MoM  |  4.2% YoY (down from 4.7% in June)
  Goods:  -0.7% MoM (energy -3.1%, food -0.9%)
  Services: +0.2% MoM
  Read: DOVISH vs. June. Unchanged headline for the month and a
  sharp annual deceleration (5.5% → 4.7%). The goods decline is
  energy-driven and may partially reverse if Middle East tensions
  sustain; services +0.2% is contained. PPI is a leading indicator
  for PCE (the Fed's preferred measure) — two consecutive soft
  PPI/CPI prints suggest the June PCE spike (headline 3.7%, core
  3.3%) that Barkin cited Aug 13 may moderate in upcoming PCE data.
  Barkin noted on Aug 13 that "B2C firms...have little ability to
  pass on costs" — this PPI goods print is consistent with that
  intelligence.

RETAIL SALES — JULY (released August 14, 8:30am ET):
  Headline:  -0.6% MoM (vs. +0.1% to +0.3% consensus)
  Ex-autos and gas: -0.2% MoM
  YoY: +5.0% from July 2025
  Read: SIGNIFICANTLY BELOW EXPECTATIONS. Largest monthly decline
  since May 2025. The miss vs. consensus is 70–90bp depending on
  the expected figure used. Consumers pulled back on online stores
  and auto dealers (the two largest category contributors to the
  decline). The YoY +5.0% indicates the level remains elevated; it
  is the monthly sequential weakening that is the new signal.
  Policy implication: consumer spending is the demand-side support
  for the "more rate hikes needed" dissent argument. A sharp
  pullback weakens that case — if demand is already softening, the
  Fed may not need to tighten further to complete disinflation.
  Cross-check: Barkin Aug 13 said consumers are "highly price
  sensitive"; this retail print is consistent with his B2C
  pass-through intelligence.

U. OF MICHIGAN CONSUMER SENTIMENT — AUGUST (preliminary, August 14):
  Index: fell ~8% from July (two consecutive months of improvement
  reversed). Cited driver: Iran war pushing up fuel, energy, and
  food prices. Inflation expectations data from the same survey
  (not yet in search results) — watch the 1-year ahead number,
  as any deanchoring would be the strongest counter-argument to
  the soft CPI/PPI disinflationary read.

NET DATA READ — WEEK OF AUGUST 11–14:
  The CPI, PPI, and retail sales trifecta constitutes the softest
  consecutive data week of the post-July-29 inter-meeting window.
  Taken together: inflation momentum is cooling sequentially (monthly
  core CPI and PPI both ≤0.2% for two months), and consumer demand
  is softening faster than expected (-0.6% retail). This data set
  is modestly supportive of the hold majority's position and modestly
  undermines the dissenters' "demand-side inflation" argument
  (Hammack's specific July 31 language). The data does NOT close
  the September hike question — annual inflation (CPI 3.4%, PPI YoY
  4.7%) remains significantly above 2%, and the Iran-war energy
  channel could reverse the goods deflation embedded in the PPI
  headline. The FOMC minutes (Aug 19) and Jackson Hole speeches
  (Aug 27–29, esp. Warsh keynote Aug 28) will determine how the
  committee interprets this week's prints.
