1.  CHRISTOPHER J. WALLER  |  Governor  |  Just Voter
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

TYPE: B with embedded outlook. The first half reviews the economic rationale
for the September hike and the current conditions read (Type A methodology);
the second half argues the SEP functions as a "signaling option" — between
silence and full forward guidance — that is working as designed.

GROWTH
  Waller echoes the September 16 statement's "domestic spending has been
  resilient" with "economic activity is strengthening in the second half of
  this year." Growth remains the permissive backdrop for further tightening:
  "I am not greatly concerned that tighter monetary policy threatens a
  damaging slowdown in the economy." No new data; the directional adjective
  "strengthening" is stronger than Sep 3's "solid pace."
  Direction: neutral (growth clears the path for hikes; not a concern in
  either direction)

LABOR MARKET
  September jobs report described as "solid and stable"; the unemployment
  rate "near the median of policymakers' projections of its longer-run
  level"; payroll gains "in the range of estimates of breakeven." He notes
  the overall count was "down" but maintains the same "satisfactory" verdict
  as Sep 3. Labor remains explicitly excluded from his decision: "policy
  will be focused on the inflation side of our mandate."
  Direction: neutral

INFLATION
  The three-month disinflation signal he highlighted on September 3 did not
  survive the August data. He reports August core PCE at 0.25% monthly and
  3.0% 12-month — "not showing sufficient progress." He traces the pivot
  decision to three persistent forces: (1) Middle East conflict keeping oil
  prices elevated "through 2027"; (2) AI buildout "significantly driving up
  high-tech consumer prices"; (3) the threat of additional tariffs. The hot
  August CPI was "impossible to deny." New emphasis vs. Sep 3: expectations
  risk elevated from contingent to active — "I am concerned that the recent
  acceleration in inflation... will lead consumers, investors, and price-
  setting businesses to revise up their expectations." The three-month-core
  momentum measure (4.76%→3.05%, "encouraging" on Sep 3) and the nonmarket-
  services adjustment are both absent — silently retired by the August data.
  Direction: hawkish lean

MONETARY POLICY
  Base case is now explicit additional hikes: "If the economic data continue
  to come in as expected, I anticipate additional hikes to support a timelier
  return of inflation to our 2 percent goal." Verbatim adoption of the
  September 16 statement phrase "timelier return." Timing is flexible — "do
  not need to come at consecutive meetings, but they should be in place in an
  acceptable period of time." No explicit stance label (the Sep 3 "only
  slightly restricting aggregate demand" is not repeated). Cut scenario:
  absent entirely.

  SEP framework: He defends the dot plot as functioning as designed — a
  "signaling option" that communicates the likely terminal rate range without
  committing to pace. He cites: 16/18 participants expecting at least one
  more hike this year; 4 expecting two; 8 expecting rates 50bp higher by
  end-2027. Market pricing (as of Oct 7): 85% chance of at least one hike
  by December; ~80% chance at least two by March 2027. This is the most
  explicit defense of the SEP's usefulness since Chair Warsh began signaling
  possible reform (Jul 1: "for a short time, at the very least"; dot
  withheld twice). Waller does not address the Chair's reform signals directly.
  Direction: hawkish

NET SIGNAL: HAWKISH LEAN — the September 3 conditional (hold if disinflation
continues; hike if hot) has resolved: August was hot, September hiked, and
additional hikes are now anticipated. The base case has moved from hold-with-
hike-branch to hike-with-timing-flexibility.

vs. PRIOR SPEECH (Sep 3, 2026):
  HAWKISH — Sep 3 hold was the stated inclination; Oct 8 additional hikes
  are the base case. The three-month disinflation read reversed by August
  data. Expectations risk elevated from contingent to active.

vs. PRE-FOMC SPEECH (Sep 3, 2026):
  Same comparison — hawkish.

vs. COMMITTEE SINCE FOMC (Sep 16):
  Waller joins Paulson (Sep 24), Barkin (Sep 22), Schmid (Sep 18), and
  Goolsbee (Sep 21) in post-hike endorsement and a base case that includes
  at least one additional move. His formulation is the most explicit: where
  Barkin said "one 'talking-to' might not be enough" and Paulson said "some
  modest further tightening may be warranted," Waller says "anticipate
  additional hikes." The only post-FOMC speakers not explicitly endorsing
  more: Logan and Hammack have not spoken publicly since September 24–25
  (Hammack's Sep 25 panel appearance unscored; no Hammack or Logan prepared
  remarks since the hike).

LANGUAGE TO WATCH:
  • "timelier return of inflation to our 2 percent goal" — verbatim from the
    Sep 16 statement; confirmed as committee-level language. Stage: IN-STATEMENT.
  • "anticipate additional hikes... not consecutive... acceptable period of time"
    — Waller's most explicit near-term path statement. Stage: DISPERSED (first
    use of this framing; watch for Logan/Hammack adoption).
  • "impossible to deny" (re August CPI) — frames September hike as evidence-
    forced, not discretionary. Stage: DISPERSED (first use).
  • "five and a half years" duration clock — third use across Waller, Barkin;
    watch for Logan and Paulson. Stage: SPREADING.
  • SEP "signaling role" / defense of the dot plot — Stage: DISPERSED. First
    explicit public defense of the SEP mechanism post-Warsh's reform signals.
    Key fault line to watch.

ANALYST FLAG: YES
  → (1) "Acceptable period of time": does this include the October 27–28
    meeting, or does Waller's flexibility point only to December? Market
    pricing (85% for December, not October) implies October is not priced.
    Clarify on next appearance.
  → (2) SEP defense vs. Warsh reform: the explicit "SEP is serving that
    signaling role" framing is the first public counter to the Chair's
    communications-reform trajectory. Does Warsh respond, or is this a
    permitted public debate within the task forces?
  → (3) Three-month core momentum and nonmarket-services adjustment (Sep 3's
    two dovish analytics) are silently dropped. Confirm what August three-
    month core read was after revisions.
