One speech appeared on July 13: Vice Chair for Supervision Michelle W.
Bowman at the Bank Policy Institute London Conference ("Modernizing
Financial Regulation"). The speech was reviewed in full and classified
as Type B — FSB regulatory modernization — with no embedded economic
outlook section and no current conditions assessment.

Speech coverage: Bowman outlined the FSB Standing Committee on
Supervisory and Regulatory Cooperation's (SRC) modernization framework
— she chairs the SRC — including four guiding principles (material
risk focus, proportionality, transparency, forward-looking innovation
support). U.S. implementation highlights: (1) the 2026 Basel III
capital framework proposal, simplifying requirements and recalibrating
the G-SIB surcharge with an index to nominal economic growth; (2) newly
published Supervisory Operating Principles (first public disclosure of
Fed supervisory approach); (3) updated asset thresholds indexed for
economic growth; (4) FSB AI sound-practices consultation report, open
for comment through July 22.

No rate-path, inflation, labor market, growth, or monetary policy
forward guidance content of any kind. This speech carries zero signal
for the July 28–29 FOMC decision.

Context: July 13 is the first day of the final pre-blackout week (blackout
begins Saturday July 18). Bowman's most recent scored economic outlook
speech remains her May 29 Reykjavík appearance ("A Framework for Practical
Monetary Policy Decision Making," Type B-with-embedded-outlook), where she
described policy as "moderately restrictive" and expressed preference for
"proactive" cuts. That view has not been publicly updated since the June 17
FOMC restructure (easing bias removed, SEP turned hawkish). Bowman's
pre-blackout policy stance remains TBD — any update would be high priority.
