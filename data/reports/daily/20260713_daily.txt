━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
Type A economic outlook delivered the day before June CPI (July 14) and
two days before PPI — Waller explicitly conditions his policy path on
this week's data. First post-June-17 outlook speech from him (June 22
and July 6 appearances were Type B, no outlook).

GROWTH
  "Economic activity continues to be solid"; private domestic final
  purchases "likely rose strongly"; AI investment expected "at a strong
  pace in the near term and most likely next year as well." The May 22
  income-bifurcation and April discretionary-softness caveats are not
  repeated — "the real side of the economy is in good shape" clears
  growth out of the policy decision.
  Direction: neutral (marginally firmer than May 22)

LABOR MARKET
  Verdict: "stability and a balance between supply and demand";
  employment "close to its maximum sustainable level, neither a source
  of concern... nor a source of inflationary pressure." Explicitly looks
  through the weak June NFP (57K + downward revisions): 3-month average
  111K/month "reflects a strong level of labor demand relative to
  supply" under his recalibrated low-labor-supply break-even; prime-age
  participation drop dismissed as likely noise. Gatekeeper line: "Unless
  I see evidence of a significantly weakening labor market, my focus
  will be on inflation."
  Direction: neutral (hawkish in effect — weak June print did not move him)

INFLATION
  Core PCE "steadily moved up" 3.0% (Dec 2025) → 3.4% (May); "we are
  past the point where we can attribute large price increases to
  earlier tariff hikes" — he dismantles the June 17 statement's supply-
  shock attribution (tariff effects "mostly over," energy pass-through
  "greatly diminished"), leaving an unexplained core uptrend. Breadth:
  ~70% of core services categories above 3%. New third driver named: AI
  demand (chips/servers/memory feeding retail goods prices). One dovish
  update: expectations concern RETIRED — 2y/5y TIPS at 2.1%/2.3%,
  "down a bit in recent weeks," "anchored near our 2 percent target" —
  but he blocks the dovish inference: anchored expectations mean hikes
  can be deliberate and less persistent, not unnecessary ("Sternly
  staring at inflation until it melts before our withering gaze is not
  an option").
  Direction: hawkish lean

MONETARY POLICY
  Explicit, named scenario framing with a dated trigger (June CPI/PPI
  this week):
  HOLD branch — needs "several months of lower readings" on core to
    hold at current range.
  TIGHTEN branch — "If we get another hot reading on core inflation
    this week, then the FOMC will need to consider tightening monetary
    policy in the near term."
  Escalation arc: Apr 17 hold-only → May 22 "can no longer rule out
  rate hikes further down the road" → Jul 13 committee-level "near
  term" tightening consideration. With the July 28–29 meeting 15 days
  away, a July hike consideration is formally on the table. New
  two-sided guardrails (verified new vs. May 22): "determined to avoid
  overtightening policy and risking a recession"; "avoid... fighting
  the last war"; labor not tight (V/U ~1) and anchored expectations
  argue for deliberate, less persistent tightening — calibration of HOW
  a hike would proceed, not whether. Cut signal: ABSENT (second
  consecutive outlook speech).
  Direction: hawkish

NET SIGNAL: HAWKISH lean — hike moved from tail risk (May 22) to live
  near-term consideration with a named data trigger; real economy
  cleared out of the decision; core inflation the sole variable.

  → "near term"; personal ("I can no longer rule out") → committee ("the
  FOMC will need to consider"); expectations worry retired but its

  bound the size, not the likelihood, of tightening.
vs. COMMITTEE SINCE FOMC:                Most explicit near-term hike

  supply-shock framing on core. Sits hawk-side of Warsh (Sintra Jul 1)
  and Daly (Jul 2), who discounted the June NFP but attached no
  near-term tightening conditionality. Pre-blackout reads still to come:
  Warsh testimony (Jul 14–15), Logan (Jul 16).

LANGUAGE TO WATCH:
  • "the FOMC will need to consider tightening monetary policy in the
    near term" — first voter to put tightening on the near-term table
    with a dated trigger. Stage: DISPERSED. If Warsh (testimony) or
    Logan (Jul 16) echo post-CPI, a July 28–29 hike becomes a live
    scenario.
  • "at a crossroads" — binary-fork/turning-point framing; watch for
    pre-blackout spread.
  • "avoid overtightening... and risking a recession" / "fighting the

  • Anchored expectations ≠ no response ("any serious policy rule calls

    anchored-expectations argument; watch for counter-echo (Bowman,
    Goolsbee).
  • AI-demand inflation channel — first voter articulation; check the
