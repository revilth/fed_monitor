WHERE THE COMMITTEE STANDS — ENTERING THE WEEK
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
The July 29 meeting produced a 9–3 vote with three dissenters (Hammack, Kashkari,
Logan) each calling for a 25bp hike. Their July 31 dissent statements were
retrieved and scored (see 20260731_daily.txt, redrafted August 2); all three
moved their object of dissent from the statement's LANGUAGE (April 29) to the
RATE itself (July 29), and each contested the statement's supply-shock attribution
as insufficient to explain current inflation. The full 9-vote majority has not
been heard from since the meeting.

Key open questions heading into the week:
  • The "demand-side" attribution claim (Hammack and Logan), vs. the statement's
    "supply shock" framing — whether majority speakers affirm or contest it is the
    week's central analytical question.
  • Cook's hike conditional ("prepared to act if disinflation does not appear
    soon" — July 15, scored) is the most hawkish language from a majority-side
    voter on record. Whether she renews or retreats on Wednesday is the highest-
    priority data point of the week.
  • Bowman (speaking Tuesday August 4, this event runs to tomorrow's report)
    was the explicit dovish outlier as recently as May; her first post-July 29
    public appearance will establish whether she is moving toward or against the
    dissenting bloc.
  • Williams (NY Fed, Troika): last scored July 15 ("inflation has peaked"),
    which is the sharpest challenge yet to the dissenters' core claim. No
    confirmed post-FOMC appearance.
  • Warsh (Chair, Troika): no scheduled speech before Jackson Hole (Aug 27–29).
    His only cycle-relevant public framing post-July 29 was the press conference.

Unconfirmed but plausible this week and next:
  • Hammack, Kashkari, Logan — dissenting officials typically follow a written
    statement with a full outlook speech within 1–2 weeks. None has a confirmed
    event. Kashkari is the highest-priority unconfirmed: his July 31 statement
    contained no unemployment or labor-market assessment, leaving his growth

  • Waller (Board) — voted with the majority July 29 after the June CPI cleared

  • Williams (NY Fed, Troika) — last scored July 15. Typical pattern is one to
