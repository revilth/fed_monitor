1.  JOHN C. WILLIAMS  |  NY Fed President  |  Troika
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
Type J — on-record interview. No official transcript exists; the NY Fed
published only a Teller Window summary, so the outlet's verbatim Q&A is the
primary source. Scored with the Type A per-topic structure.

TIMING: conducted July 31, the morning the three dissent statements appeared.
It PRE-DATES the July payroll report (-23,000), the July CPI (Aug 12) and the
July PPI (Aug 13). Nothing below is a reaction to August data.

COVERAGE CAVEAT: topics were set by the interviewer. Oil inventories, September
market pricing, long-term yields, the forward-guidance pullback, FOMC
deliberation tenor and AI bubble/leverage risk were all Derby's questions. The
absence of a topic carries no signal in this document.

GROWTH
  "Growth has been solid, but not more than solid… GDP growth over the last year
  was 2%, it's a little less than that in the first half of this year… we're not
  seeing the economy show any signs of overheating." Growth is now "more or less
  at trend," a step down from July 15's "modestly above my estimate of its
  potential rate of 2 percent." The read is also deployed differently: it is the
  first plank of the case against hiking rather than a descriptive passage.
  Direction: dovish lean

LABOR MARKET
  "the labor market is, been stable" — continuity with July 15's "solid and
  stable," and again framed as a non-source of inflation pressure ("no signs of
  overheating in terms of labor market"). That framing is the structural rebuttal
  to the dissenters: with no tightness channel, a pre-emptive hike loses its
  transmission mechanism. This read pre-dates the -23K payroll print.
  Direction: neutral (unchanged)

INFLATION
  The three-driver decomposition is intact — tariffs, Middle East energy, AI
  demand — with two changes and one demotion. Tariffs: the pass-through claim
  hardens ("we should not see large effects from tariffs on inflation rates in
  the coming months anymore"). Energy: the peak call survives but the level is
  marked up, and he says so explicitly — "relative to some of my earlier
  comments, oil prices are higher, and… the future path of oil prices is also
  somewhat higher." AI demand: demoted from a co-equal driver to "not a big
  driver of inflation right now" — the direct negation of the dissenters'
  demand-side attribution. Overall risk "similar to the June FOMC"; the 2%-by-
  2028 base case is reaffirmed; his own stated test is "core inflation data over
  the next several months."
  Note: he does not restate the July 15 headline "inflation has peaked" as a
  standalone claim. The peak is asserted for the tariff and energy components
  and the disinflation path is given in forecast form. Substance intact, form
  unrepeated.
  Direction: mixed — hawkish on the energy level, dovish on AI, net unchanged

MONETARY POLICY
  Asked directly why the Committee did not hike, Williams gave the majority's
  reasoning on the record for the first time: no overheating; inflation driven by
  identifiable, fading, supply-side specifics; "monetary policy currently is well
  positioned in where we are today to support that disinflationary path." He
  frames the majority as forward-looking against a backward-looking case for
  hiking. New relative to July 15: a conditional-to-act — "if the economy is not
  on a trajectory that will bring inflation back down to 2%… it would absolutely
  be appropriate to act." Direction unnamed, but asked in a hiking context. It is
  materially weaker than Cook's July 15 "prepared to act" and than Waller's
  July 13 named trigger.
  On market pricing of September he declines to validate ("I don't opine on
  whether they're right or wrong because I don't know") and rejects the idea that
  yields substitute for policy: "I never like the phrase that the markets are
  doing the work for us." He endorses the forward-guidance pullback as correct
  for present conditions while explicitly bounding it as situational, not
  doctrinal.
  Cut signal: ABSENT (unchanged since April 16). Hike signal: ABSENT; a
  direction-unnamed conditional now sits alongside "well positioned."
  Direction: hawkish lean, marginal

NET SIGNAL: NEUTRAL (hold-anchored). Williams is not moving; he is defending.
  The stance formula is verbatim July 15. The value of the document is that a
  Troika member states, plank by plank, why the nine did not hike — two days
  after three regional presidents said publicly that they should have.

  solid"), AI demoted as an inflation driver, energy level marked up, a
  conditional-to-act added. Stance unchanged.
vs. PRE-FOMC SPEECH (July 15, 2026):  same document; July 15 is both the prior
  speech and the pre-FOMC baseline.
vs. COMMITTEE SINCE FOMC:  Williams anchors the majority's supply-shock
  attribution against the dissenters' demand-side attribution. Two days later
  the split widened from inside the majority: Cook (Aug 5) reframed AI capex as
  a demand-side inflation impulse — the opposite of Williams's demotion.

LANGUAGE TO WATCH:
  • "not a big driver of inflation right now" (AI demand) — the counter-claim to
    the dissent bloc's central mechanism. Stage: DISPERSED, and contested inside
    the majority by Cook. The July minutes (Aug 19) are the resolution point.
  • "not more than solid" (growth) — capped characterization from the Troika's
    number two. Stage: DISPERSED. Watch Jefferson.
  • "it would absolutely be appropriate to act" — the majority's first
    conditional-to-act. Stage: DISPERSED.
  • "I never like the phrase that the markets are doing the work for us" —
    institutional, not directional; relevant to the Chair's market-signal
    doctrine and to Jackson Hole.

ANALYST FLAG: YES
  → The AI-attribution split is the cycle's central analytical question:
    Williams says AI demand is not a big inflation driver; Cook and the
    dissenters locate the problem on the demand side. The July minutes on
    August 19 test which view had the room.
  → Every read here pre-dates the -23K payroll, the July CPI and the July PPI.
    Williams's next appearance is the highest-priority document of the open
    window remaining before the September 5 blackout.

WHERE THE COMMITTEE STANDS — ENTERING THE WEEK
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
The July 29 meeting produced a 9–3 vote with three dissenters (Hammack, Kashkari,
Logan) each calling for a 25bp hike. Their July 31 dissent statements were
retrieved and scored (see 20260731_daily.txt); all three moved their object of
dissent from the statement's LANGUAGE (April 29) to the RATE itself (July 29),
and each contested the statement's supply-shock attribution as insufficient to
explain current inflation. Williams's interview above is the majority's first
answer to that charge.

Key open questions heading into the week:
  • The "demand-side" attribution claim (Hammack and Logan), vs. the statement's
    "supply shock" framing — whether majority speakers affirm or contest it is the
    week's central analytical question. Williams contests it.
  • Cook's hike conditional ("prepared to act if disinflation does not appear
    soon" — July 15, scored) is the most hawkish language from a majority-side
    voter on record. Whether she renews or retreats on Wednesday is the highest-
    priority remaining data point of the week.
  • Bowman (speaking Tuesday August 4, this event runs to tomorrow's report)
    was the explicit dovish outlier as recently as May; her first post-July 29
    public appearance will establish whether she is moving toward or against the
    dissenting bloc.
  • Warsh (Chair, Troika): no scheduled speech before Jackson Hole (Aug 27–29).
    His only cycle-relevant public framing post-July 29 was the press conference.
