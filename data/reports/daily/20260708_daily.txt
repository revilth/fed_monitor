The June 16–17 FOMC Minutes were released at 3:00pm ET. They were scored
and an alert was pushed the same evening (6:33pm ET, commit 806656c).
The key findings are summarized below. See the full scored file and the
alert for complete three-way comparison analysis.

━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
FOMC MINUTES — June 16–17, 2026  |  Type D
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

FFR: Hold at 3-1/2 to 3-3/4%. Vote: 12–0 unanimous. (April 29 vote was
8–4 with Miran for cut and Hammack/Kashkari/Logan against easing bias;
Miran departed May 2026, June is Warsh's first meeting as Chair.)
Prior minutes: April 28–29, 2026.
Prior SEP: June 17 (decisively hawkish; 2026 median dot 3.4%→3.8%; 9 of
18 participants pencil a 2026 hike; entire March dovish tail is gone).

METHOD: Three comparisons per CLAUDE.md Type D — (1) vs. June 17 press
conference opening statement; (2) vs. April 29 minutes; (3) vs. June 17
statement.

GROWTH
  Solid pace; AI investment "showing no signs of slowing." New
  compositional detail not in the opening statement or public statement:
  higher-income households supported by stock gains and tax refunds;
  lower-income households "increasingly relying on credit," facing
  "disproportionate pressures" from gas and groceries. Sharper two-tier
  characterization than April's single-line mention of energy-price
  strains. A watch item, not yet a policy driver.
  Direction: NEUTRAL-TO-HAWKISH

LABOR MARKET
  "Participants generally" (not a qualified minority as in April) observed
  payroll gains "roughly consistent with underlying labor force growth" and
  unemployment near the longer-run level. Minority caveats remain (low job-
  finding rate; low dynamism in surveys). Key shift: "Several participants
  remarked that their concerns earlier this year about labor market
  deterioration had eased with recent data." April: "most participants
  judged risks to the employment side tilted to the downside." June: "downside
  risks to achieving maximum employment had moderated a bit." The April
  fragility framing ("a few pointed to the possibility that low job growth
  signaled labor market fragility") has no June equivalent.
  Direction: HAWKISH SHIFT — the insurance-cut rationale for labor is fading.

INFLATION
  "Increased further and remained well above the Committee's 2 percent
  longer-run objective" (vs. April's "moved up"). Attribution broadened
  to Strait of Hormuz + AI demand (beyond April's tariff-only story).
  New broad-based sectoral characterization: "transportation, airfares,
  petrochemical products, and agricultural inputs." New demand-side
  channel: growth exceeding potential via AI investment — absent from
  April entirely. Expectations-fragility risk escalated from "several"
  to "the majority" of participants. Partial offsets: housing disinflation
  expected to continue; AI productivity may eventually reduce costs
  (though "would likely take time to materialize").
  Direction: HAWKISH ESCALATION — largest directional move of the four topics.

MONETARY POLICY
  Four high-signal findings:

  1. PRESENT-TENSE HIKE CASE (new this cycle):
     "A few participants commented that... there was a case for raising the
     target range for the federal funds rate, but those participants
     indicated that they supported maintaining the current target range at
     this meeting." April's equivalent was purely conditional/future ("some
     policy firming would likely become appropriate IF inflation continues
     persistently above 2 percent"). June documents an actual case made and
     declined at the meeting — not just a future reaction function.

  2. NEUTRAL-RATE HEDGE PHRASE GONE:
     March and April both used: "policy rate was within the range of
     plausible estimates of its neutral level" — deliberately vague.
     June: "Several participants remarked that they did not see the current
     policy stance as restrictive, while a few other participants commented
     that they saw the current policy stance as slightly restrictive."
     The larger group says NOT restrictive at all; nobody is quoted calling
     policy meaningfully above neutral. Resolves hawkish.

  3. APRIL CUT CASE HAS NO JUNE EQUIVALENT:
     April: "several participants highlighted that it would likely be
     appropriate to lower the target range... once there are clear
     indications that disinflation is firmly back on track."
     June: no near-term dovish cut case anywhere in the monetary policy
     discussion. The minority that provided a dovish counterweight one
     meeting ago has dropped out entirely.

  4. COMMITTEE-WIDE NAMED SCENARIO FRAMING:
     "Most participants" discussed a BENIGN scenario (inflation dissipates
     → maintain or lower rates) and an ADVERSE scenario (AI demand / Middle
     East / tariffs → policy firming warranted). This is Waller's Apr 17
     named-scenario device — now appearing committee-wide in the minutes for
     the first time. Language cycle: dispersed (Waller Apr 17) →
     committee-wide (June minutes). One stage short of the statement.

  Also: dot positioning matches the SEP quantitatively (many within/below
  current range vs. many above; SEP: 9 of 18 pencil a 2026 hike). Statement
  redesign: "a majority... preferred not to repeat the language... that had
  suggested an easing bias" — validates April minutes' prediction. Five
  Warsh task forces (confirmed as discussed at the meeting, not just
  announced after).

  Direction: HAWKISH — strongest signal of the four topics; most consequential.

vs. JUNE 17 PRESS CONFERENCE OPENING STATEMENT:
  Minutes are more hawkish than both the opening statement and Warsh's
  unscripted Q&A. The opening statement carries neutral forward language;
  the Q&A quantified the hike risk ("half the committee") but did not
  disclose that an actual hike case was made and declined at the meeting,
  or that the "not restrictive" framing had become the larger camp.

vs. APRIL 29 MINUTES:
  Net hawkish across all four topics — hike case escalated (conditional →
  present-tense); restrictiveness resolved hawkish (hedge phrase gone);
  dovish cut case disappeared; named scenario framing new; labor risk eased
  removing the April dovish counterweight.

vs. JUNE 17 STATEMENT:
  Widest comparison-3 gap of any document this cycle. The 130-word public
  statement omits: the hike-case discussion, the restrictiveness split, the
  named scenario framework, the dot positioning, the income-composition
  growth detail, and the five Warsh task forces.

NET SIGNAL: HAWKISH — consistent across all three comparisons.
  The most hawkish minutes document in this cycle. The June 17 statement
  and press conference understated how hawkish the internal deliberations
  were; the minutes close that gap.

LANGUAGE TO WATCH:
  • "Case for raising the target range" — first present-tense hike advocacy
    in minutes this cycle. Watch for Just Voter speeches to use this
    framing explicitly before the July 28–29 meeting.
  • Named benign/adverse scenario framework — now committee-wide (Waller
    Apr 17 → June minutes). Watch for it entering the July 28–29 statement.
  • Disappearance of "within the range of plausible estimates of its neutral
    level" — gone from June; if absent from July minutes, treat as durable.
  • Disappearance of the "firmly back on track" cut threshold — no successor
    dovish phrase appeared anywhere.

ANALYST FLAG: YES
  → Is the "case for raising" minority on track to become the median view
    by July 28–29, given the SEP already shows 9 of 18 penciling a 2026
    hike with the median dot above the current midpoint?
  → Is the disappearance of the near-term dovish cut case driven by the
    Middle East/AI channel (potentially transitory — staff review notes a
    Strait of Hormuz agreement was reached by the end of the intermeeting
    period), or does it reflect a more durable hawkish consolidation? This
    distinction matters for how quickly the committee could pivot back
    toward cuts if energy prices retrace.
