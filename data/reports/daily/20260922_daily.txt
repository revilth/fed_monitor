━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
Both Troika members chose an implementation venue for their first
post-hike appearance and gave no outlook language. Williams covered the
LIBOR transition, central clearing and the ample-reserves framework;
Jefferson covered discount-window modernization and its role in Treasury
market resilience. Neither engaged the September 16 decision.
Significance: Williams is the official whose inflation framework
(attribution, "not seeing broadening") the September 16 statement
deleted; Jefferson is the Vice Chair with no outlook communication since
July 16. Both signals remain outstanding. Their next outlook speeches are
the two highest-priority documents of the inter-meeting period.

3.  TOM BARKIN  |  Richmond Fed President  |  Non Voter
    Type A — first explicit defense of the hike by a participant
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

GROWTH
  "The economy and the labor market remain on solid footing. We hear from
  businesses that economic conditions are, if anything, firming" — one
  step past the statement's "solid." Consumers "still spending" ("a
  blowout retail sales report last week"), top-heavy by wealth, with a
  longer coping list at the low end (trading down "from beef to chicken
  to even cheaper proteins," dropping insurance, "living a little closer
  to the edge"). Investment "booming," and "momentum outside of data
  centers, too" (defense, manufacturing, bank pipelines). Uncertainty is
  "the new baseline"; firms "can't afford to wait." Hiring remains "the
  big gap on the demand side." Verdict: "the demand picture hasn't
  changed much."
  Direction: hawkish lean

LABOR MARKET
  "The labor market continues to get good grades": unemployment 4.1%,
  "the longest streak in recorded history ... at or below 4.5 percent,"
  claims "testing historic lows," layoffs "muted," August payrolls
  "surpassing 160,000" after July's "scare." The Aug 13 softer-under-the-
  headline read (−23K, the fragile supply/demand offset, the CFO hiring
  pause) is withdrawn; the slower-workforce mechanism survives as one
  explanatory sentence.
  Direction: hawkish lean

INFLATION
  "Today, inflation is our troublemaker." PCE 3.7 / core 3.3. The
  BREADTH TEST, in his own decomposition: "It is tempting to try to blame
  high inflation on a handful of categories with particularly high
  exposure to the Middle East conflict or to tariffs. But more than 60
  percent of the PCE index is rising faster than 3 percent year over
  year" (180 categories). That is Warsh's Aug 28 measure (54% of 199)
  reproduced by a second official — and the reasoning behind the
  statement's deleted attribution clause. The "return to target on its
  own" argument, which he steelmanned on Aug 13, is named as the argument
  that lost: "the 'passing' shocks aren't proving to be short-lived, or
  one-off events." Firm expectations quantified on the hawkish side:
  Richmond surveys' prices received averaging 3.5% since late 2023,
  "nearly double" pre-pandemic; CFO Survey 2027 price growth 4.1%, "more
  than double the 2019 average ... 'double' is not a word I want to be
  using." Pass-through flips back to hawkish: firms "rediscovered the
  pricing lever" and are "finding less resistance than they did
  prepandemic, especially from the wealthy and from business customers."
  "The net of all this is more inflationary pressure."
  Direction: hawkish

MONETARY POLICY
  "The risks to inflation outweigh the risks to maximum employment.
  That's why we raised rates." Why now: "What's changed? My answer: not
  that much. But that is precisely why it was appropriate for the FOMC to
  adjust our stance" — the wait expired; the same rationale as Warsh
  Sept 16 and Goolsbee Sept 21. What next: "Will additional hikes be
  required, and how many? We'll see." Two descriptive risk lists, no
  named scenarios, no pre-commitment — but the only directional image
  points to a second move: "Like in child rearing, one 'talking-to'
  might not be enough." No stance label at all ("restrictive" and
  "accommodation" both absent). Cut signal: absent.
  Direction: hawkish

NET SIGNAL: HAWKISH — a defense of the hike on the Chair's three pillars
  (full employment, breadth, the expired wait), with every dovish element
  of Aug 13 withdrawn and his on-the-ground pricing and expectations
  intelligence now on the hawkish side.

vs. PRIOR SPEECH (Aug 13):     HAWKISH — hike-as-open-question becomes
  hike-delivered-and-defended; "already on a path down" dropped; labor
  from "delicate balance" to "good grades"; pass-through reversed back;
  "many think ... still restrictive enough" gone.
vs. PRE-FOMC SPEECH (Aug 13):  same document — HAWKISH. (His Aug 31
  Bloomberg Odd Lots appearance was never collected; see flag.)
vs. COMMITTEE SINCE FOMC:      Second regional voice after the hike, one
  day after Goolsbee, same conclusion by a different route (business
  intelligence vs. theory). Both reject the attribution framework; both
  decline a stance label; both leave the path open with a lean toward
  more. The "mildly restrictive" bloc has not yet spoken.

    percent" — the breadth test, second official (Warsh 54%). Stage:
    EMERGING. A third user makes it committee-wide.
  • "The risks to inflation outweigh the risks to maximum employment.
    That's why we raised rates." — cleanest one-line rationale on record.
  • "one 'talking-to' might not be enough" — personal; second-hike image.
  • "the 'passing' shocks aren't proving to be short-lived" — pairs with
    Goolsbee's "persistent ones" (Sept 21). Stage: EMERGING.
  • "firming" — direction added to the statement's "solid."

        outlier? Their first post-hike speeches decide it.
  → (4) Williams and Jefferson: outlook signals still outstanding.

CROSS-SPEAKER NOTE (Sept 21–22)
  Goolsbee and Barkin, non-voters from opposite reputational poles,
  delivered the same argument on consecutive days: supply shocks have
  proven persistent, the labor market gives no reason to wait, the hike
  was warranted and the path is open with a lean toward more. Neither
  used "restrictive" or "accommodation." Two Troika members spoke the same
  day and said nothing about the outlook. The post-hike consensus is so
  far being articulated from the regional banks, not the Board.
