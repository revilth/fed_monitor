WILLIAMS — "DO YOU REMEMBER?" (TYPE B, NO EMBEDDED OUTLOOK)
Williams opened the 12th annual U.S. Treasury Market Conference with
remarks entirely about monetary policy implementation: the completed LIBOR
transition, the ongoing central clearing rollout, the ample-reserves
framework (ON RRP, SRP), and three principles for effective implementation
(interest rate control, low opportunity cost of reserves, elasticity). The
speech is a companion to Jefferson's same-day remarks (also Treasury market
infrastructure). There is no growth, labor market, inflation, or rate-path
content. This is therefore not a post-FOMC policy signal.

Significance flag: Williams is Troika. His July 15 scored speech ("Stability
of Thy Times") — his last pre-blackout and pre-hike communication — framed
the labor market's overall characterization as "solid and on trend" (growth)
and inflation as still above 2% with a data-dependent path. The key fault-
line entering the post-hike cycle is whether Williams defends or modifies
his attribution-based inflation framework (his "not seeing broadening /
second-round effects" logic) in light of the September 16 statement's
deletion of the supply-shock attribution clause. THAT signal did not come
on September 22 — Williams chose a technical implementation venue for his
first post-FOMC appearance. His next economic-outlook speech is the one
to watch.
