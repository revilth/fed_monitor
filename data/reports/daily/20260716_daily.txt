GROWTH
  No explicit GDP growth section in available remarks. Growth is addressed
  implicitly through the "not restraining" judgment: "The labor, consumption
  and financial data indicate that monetary policy is not restraining the
  economy." Same verdict as June 3; updated labor data (92K jobs/month vs.
  ~50K in June 3) makes the "not restraining" case even more concrete.
  Direction: neutral-to-hawkish — no demand-side risk; "not restraining" maintained.

LABOR MARKET
  Unemployment averaged 4.3% in H1 2026 — unchanged from June 3's read.
  Average job gains: ~92,000/month (upgraded from June 3's ~50,000/month);
  Logan does not characterize the increase as a new concern — the labor market
  remains a stable, non-dovish anchor. The three-category "not restraining"
  judgment (labor, consumption, financial) explicitly removes the dual-mandate
  obstacle to tightening: "The labor, consumption and financial data indicate
  that monetary policy is not restraining the economy."
  Direction: neutral-to-hawkish — stability confirmed; upgraded job gains
  remove any residual softening argument.

INFLATION
  Logan explicitly dismisses the June CPI/PPI data: "One month of relief is
  not enough. It is time to finish the job of restoring price stability." This
  is a direct rebuttal of the Williams "peaked" reading (July 15) and counter
  to Waller's trigger-unmet inference. Her quantitative case:
    - Core PCE: 3.4%, RISEN since December (vs. 3.3% in June 3 speech)
    - NY Fed persistent component: 3.4%
    - Non-housing core services inflation: no 12-month progress since mid-2024
  Verdict: "Inflation has been too high, for too long, and does not appear to
  be on track all the way back to 2%." Duration argument sharpened: "Every
  month of above-target inflation has compounded the strain on Americans'
  budgets." Path forecast maintained from June 3: heading toward "mid 2's" —
  not all the way to 2 percent.
  Direction: HAWKISH — above-2% forecast maintained; measures deteriorated
  on the margin since June 3; June CPI/PPI explicitly insufficient.

MONETARY POLICY
  KEY ESCALATION — first voter under Warsh to advocate publicly for a rate
  increase: "I currently believe modestly higher interest rates would better
  balance the outlook and risks for the FOMC's dual mandate goals."
  Pre-emptive dual-mandate argument: "If higher inflation becomes entrenched,
  we'd need sharper rate increases to bring it back to target, with a larger
  cost for the labor market. Better modest restriction now than severe
  restriction later." The logical chain is fully explicit: inflation not on
  track to 2% on its own → restriction needed → cost-of-waiting argument
  (entrenchment risk justifies acting now, not later).
  Logan stopped short of committing to a July 28-29 hike specifically;
  no preferred magnitude was stated.
  Hike signal: PRESENT — escalated from conditional concern to current
  conviction ("currently believe … would"). The shift from "could be
  necessary later this year" (June 3) to "currently believe would better
  balance" is the single most important change in this speech: the hike
  has moved from a tail risk to Logan's stated base case.
  Cut signal: ABSENT.
  Direction: HAWKISH

NET SIGNAL: HAWKISH — significant escalation from June 3 baseline.
