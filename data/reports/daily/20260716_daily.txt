━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

Type B (Special Topic — a framework speech on how policymakers classify and
respond to economic shocks) with an embedded economic-outlook / monetary-policy
section ("Challenges Posed by the Current Juncture" and "On the Current Stance
of Monetary Policy"). Jefferson's FIRST post-June-17 speech with policy content.
Full scored file: data/scored/speeches/20260716_Philip_N__Jefferson_scored.txt

GROWTH
  No GDP forecast — this is a framework speech. Growth enters only via the AI
  discussion: "optimism about AI may boost investment and consumption today,
  even before these productivity gains fully materialize." He flags the timing
  asymmetry as the risk: if AI demand effects arrive before supply/productivity
  effects, "AI could exert upward pressure on inflation."
  Direction: neutral — no scorable growth verdict.

LABOR MARKET
  Labor characterized as at/near maximum employment: the unemployment rate "is
  near a level that most observers view as consistent with maximum employment"
  (footnote: 4.2% vs. CBO's 4.4% noncyclical estimate). But Jefferson elevates
  the employment DOWNSIDE into one pole of an explicit dual-mandate tradeoff:
  "we must be mindful of employment potentially moving below its maximum
  sustainable level." He frames the current juncture as a genuine tension where
  the two mandate goals are "not aligned but rather in tension."
  Direction: slight dovish lean — protects the employment side more visibly than
  his May 27 baseline.

INFLATION
  Echoes the elevated framing with a duration note: inflation "has already been
  above the FOMC's target for some time." KEY hawkish escalation is the risk
  language: "The quick succession of shocks raises the risk that inflation
  becomes entrenched and inflation expectations become unanchored." This is the
  sequence-of-shocks framing (energy + trade/tariffs) that raises the bar for
  looking through inflation. Base case remains a two-shock pass-through
  disinflation ("as the effects of past tariffs and energy prices pass through
  completely"); energy demand effects expected "muted" (U.S. net oil exporter).
  Direction: hawkish lean — risk framing escalated to "entrenched"/"unanchored."

MONETARY POLICY
  Base case = patient hold: the June stance "should continue to support the
  labor market while allowing inflation to resume its decline toward our 2
  percent target." Then the SIGNAL — a named conditional pointing hawkish:
  "in a scenario where actual inflation does not start to cool down soon, I
  believe that it could be appropriate to reconsider our current policy stance
  to ensure we fulfill our commitment to deliver price stability."
  Scenario framing: EXPLICIT and NAMED (he labels the scenario and attaches a
  policy response), but the verb "reconsider" is deliberately softer than "raise"
  or "prepared to act" — an openness to revisiting the hold, not a pre-commitment.
  r* note (heavily hedged, symmetric): if AI raises the neutral rate, "for any
  given level of the federal funds rate, policy effectively becomes more
  accommodative" — a latent structural consideration, NOT a rate call.
  Cut signal: ABSENT.  Hike signal: ABSENT as base case; a NAMED conditional
  toward reconsidering (tightening) is present.
  Direction: hawkish lean.

NET SIGNAL: HAWKISH LEAN (with a protected employment side) — Jefferson's first
  post-June-17 policy content escalates inflation-risk language to "entrenched"/
  "unanchored" and replaces May 27's open optionality with a named conditional to
  "reconsider" the stance if inflation does not cool. But the base case is a
  patient, pass-through hold and he elevates the employment downside into an
  explicit tradeoff — so he sits dovish of Logan and to the patient side of the
  hawkish voter wing.

vs. PRIOR SPEECH [May 27 — also pre-FOMC baseline]:
  HAWKISH LEAN — May 27's policy signal was pure optionality ("I have not
  prejudged the next meeting"); July 16 replaces it with a directional named
  conditional ("reconsider our current policy stance" if inflation doesn't cool).
  Inflation risk language also escalates from "moved notably higher" to
  "entrenched"/"unanchored" driven by the quick succession of shocks.
vs. PRE-FOMC SPEECH [May 27]:
  As above — May 27 (BOJ-IMES Tokyo) is both prior and pre-FOMC baseline; no
  post-June-17 Jefferson speech before today.
vs. COMMITTEE SINCE FOMC:
  Jefferson becomes the Troika swing. With Logan advocating a hike today and
  Williams counseling patience (Jul 15), Jefferson's conditional bar — a
  near-term inflation disappointment — places him between the two. As Vice Chair,
  his conditional is the Troika's clearest signal that the hold is not open-ended.

LANGUAGE TO WATCH:
  • "inflation becomes entrenched … expectations become unanchored" — the
    sequence-of-shocks hawkish frame, now at Troika level. Stage: EMERGING —
    Jefferson AND Logan use the "entrenched" frame the same day (see cross-speaker
    note). Watch Williams/Warsh; Troika convergence on entrenchment would be a
    leading indicator for hawkish statement language.
  • "reconsider our current policy stance" — Jefferson's first named policy
    conditional since June 17; softer than Cook's "prepared to act," harder than
    Williams's patience. Stage: DISPERSED.

ANALYST FLAG: YES
  → SWING TROIKA VOTE. The July 28–29 meeting turns on whether Jefferson reads
    the June CPI (which eased) as inflation "starting to cool" (Williams's view →
    comfortable hold) or as insufficient (Logan's view → conditional near live).
    His speech does not resolve which — and his simultaneous elevation of the
    employment downside argues for restraint, not a hawkish lean. Flag for the
    analyst: is "reconsider" deliberate Troika equidistance or a genuine tilt?

Type A (Economic Outlook — prepared remarks before a moderated conversation).
Logan's first post-June-17 economic outlook speech.
Full scored file: data/scored/speeches/20260716_Lorie_Logan_scored.txt

GROWTH
  "Strong consumer spending, soaring corporate profits, accommodative financial
  conditions and AI investment continue to drive economic activity" (equities
  +20% y/y; credit spreads near record tights). She states directly — no longer
  as an inference — that "the labor, consumption and financial data indicate
  that monetary policy is not restraining the economy. And that's a problem for
  sustainably achieving the FOMC's inflation goal."
  Direction: neutral-to-hawkish — "not restraining" upgraded to a direct claim.

LABOR MARKET
  Unemployment averaged 4.3% in H1 (unchanged from June 3). Job gains revised up:
  "an average of 92,000 jobs per month in the first half of this year, modestly
  outpacing growth in the size of the labor force" (June 3 cited ~50,000/month as
  merely in line with labor-force growth). Verdict nudged more constructive:
  "well balanced and perhaps even strengthening a bit." She concedes worker-level
  frictions ("slow to fire and slow to hire") but rules out easing: "not ones
  that easier monetary policy can fix."
  Direction: neutral-to-hawkish — job gains and verdict stronger than June 3.

INFLATION
  Explicitly dismisses the June CPI relief: "one month of relief is not enough.
  It is time to finish the job of restoring price stability" — a direct rebuttal
  of Williams's "peaked" read (Jul 15) and below Waller's hot-print trigger.
  Quantitative case: PCE 4.1% (12mo through May); core PCE 3.4% ("risen four-
  tenths since December," vs. 3.3% on June 3); NY Fed core trend 3.4%; market-
  based non-housing core services "picked up a bit this spring." New duration /
  cost-of-waiting framing: inflation above target "more than five years" and
  "every month … has compounded the strain on Americans' budgets." Path forecast
  maintained: "heading toward the mid 2's—not all the way back to 2 percent."
  Direction: HAWKISH — above-2% forecast held; measures marginally worse; June
  data rejected.

MONETARY POLICY
  Outright base-case HIKE ADVOCACY, stated twice bookending the speech: "I
  currently believe modestly higher interest rates would better balance the
  outlook and risks for the FOMC's dual mandate goals." Deductive core: "If
  inflation is not heading all the way to 2 percent on its own, then at least
  some policy restriction is needed." Pre-emptive case: "If higher inflation
  becomes entrenched, we'd need sharper rate increases … Better modest
  restriction now than severe restriction later." Escalation from June 3's
  conditional/deferred worry ("could be necessary later this year") to present
  conviction ("currently believe … would"). Committee-humility close tempers the
  read: "I may persuade them, they may persuade me" — positioning to persuade,
  not a pre-committed dissent. No magnitude beyond "modestly"; July 28–29 not
  named.
  Hike signal: PRESENT (base case).  Cut signal: ABSENT.
  Direction: HAWKISH.

NET SIGNAL: HAWKISH — significant escalation from June 3. Hike moved from tail to
  base case; June CPI explicitly dismissed; "not restraining" upgraded to a
  direct, named claim with job gains revised up.

vs. PRIOR SPEECH [June 3 — also pre-FOMC baseline]:
  HAWKISH — (1) conditional "could be necessary later this year" → current
  conviction "currently believe … would better balance"; (2) June CPI dismissed
  as insufficient; (3) core PCE risen 3.3%→3.4%, confirming her above-2% forecast.
vs. PRE-FOMC SPEECH [June 3]:
  As above — June 3 is both prior and pre-FOMC baseline.
vs. COMMITTEE SINCE FOMC:
  Most hawkish stated voter position entering blackout — to the right of Cook's
  conditional (Jul 15) and Waller's unmet trigger (Jul 13), and beyond
  Jefferson's "reconsider" conditional (today). No Troika endorsement of a hike.

LANGUAGE TO WATCH:
  • "modestly higher interest rates would better balance the dual mandate" —
    base-case hike advocacy. Stage: DISPERSED (Logan alone as a base case).
  • "If higher inflation becomes entrenched" — shared with Jefferson's
    "entrenched"/"unanchored" today. Stage: EMERGING (see cross-speaker note).
  • "Better modest restriction now than severe restriction later" — clean
    pre-emptive one-liner; watch for uptake by Kashkari/Hammack or a dissent
    rationale. Stage: DISPERSED.
  • "One month of relief is not enough" — direct rebuttal of the June CPI; the
    counter-signal to Williams's "peaked" call. Stage: DISPERSED.

ANALYST FLAG: YES
  → (1) Dissent risk at July 28–29 is elevated — Logan is the hawkish anchor. But
    her committee-humility close argues she is positioning to persuade rather than
    pre-committing; treat dissent as probable, not certain.
  → (2) "Monetary policy is not restraining the economy," if accepted by the
    committee, mandates tightening — Logan's most consequential claim. Jefferson's
    view on whether current policy is restrictive is the swing (his speech leaves
    it open).

CROSS-SPEAKER NOTE — July 16
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Two officials spoke the same day and converged on one frame: INFLATION
ENTRENCHMENT. Logan (Just Voter): "If higher inflation becomes entrenched, we'd
need sharper rate increases." Jefferson (Vice Chair / Troika): "The quick
succession of shocks raises the risk that inflation becomes entrenched and
inflation expectations become unanchored." A Just Voter and the Vice Chair using
the entrenchment argument on the same day is a potential bottom-up leading
indicator — the entrenchment frame is one step from the "sequence of supply
shocks" language already in the June 17 statement. Watch whether Williams or
Warsh adopt it.

They diverge sharply on the policy conclusion drawn from it. Logan: act now
("better modest restriction now than severe restriction later") — a base-case
hike. Jefferson: patient hold with a soft conditional ("reconsider … if inflation
does not cool soon") and an explicit counterweight for the employment side.
Same risk, opposite policy prescription. The committee enters the July 28–29
blackout with the June CPI read (eased) interpreted three ways — Williams
"peaked"/hold, Logan "not enough"/hike, Jefferson conditional/swing — and Warsh
holding the center.
