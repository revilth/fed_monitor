1.  SUSAN M. COLLINS  |  Boston Fed President  |  Non Voter
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

GROWTH
  Collins characterizes growth as "expanding at a near-trend pace" —
  a subtle step down from the July 29 statement's "solid pace" and from
  her own May 13 "resilient" framing. Growth is positive but not a
  policy concern. She does flag the AI build-out as generating demand-
  side goods inflation (addressed under Inflation), which implies AI
  capex is a growth tailwind with an inflationary shadow.
  Direction: neutral

LABOR MARKET
  Strong positive pivot vs. May 13: Collins now characterizes labor as
  "broadly consistent with maximum or full employment," leading with
  unemployment "just above 4 percent" in a "narrow range since mid-2024,"
  limited layoffs, and hiring that has "picked up relative to late 2025"
  and become "somewhat more broad-based." She retains the "unusual
  balance" framing from May 13 (low-hire, low-fire; immigration-adjusted)
  but converts the risk framing from downside-skewed to "two-sided" —
  upside (stronger growth tightens labor further) and downside (AI
  repricing/displacement, low job-finding rate → discouraged workers).
  May 13's AI skill-mismatch alarm is absent. Net labor read is the most
  constructive from Collins in 2026.
  Direction: neutral (improved vs. May 13 → mild hawkish offset for policy)

INFLATION
  Price-stability-first framing leads the piece: "inflation still too
  high," "running above the FOMC's 2 percent target for over five years."
  Modal scenario requires limited additional tariff increases AND some
  Hormuz reopening — neither assumed. Collins lists three disinflation
  factors: (1) balanced labor not adding price pressure; (2) mildly
  restrictive policy PLUS "the recent rise in longer-term interest rates"
  (new — echoes Warsh July 29 bond-yield-as-credibility framing); (3)
  productivity growth "if it continues." Upside inflation risks include
  supply shocks and stronger demand; new AI channel: "the AI build-out
  appears to be putting upward pressure on core goods inflation" (aligns
  with Waller's July 13 AI-goods analysis). Data: "June and July inflation
  reports were mildly encouraging" — most positive data characterization
  from Collins in 2026, but immediately qualified: "monthly readings can
  be volatile... remains to be seen whether the recent improvements will
  be sustained."
  Direction: neutral (unchanged from May 13 with new AI upside risk added)

MONETARY POLICY
  Validates the July 29 hold: "comfortable with July's FOMC decision to
  maintain the current target range." Characterizes stance as "mildly
  restrictive" (same as May 13's "slightly restrictive" — stable Collins
  vocabulary). Critical condition: "maintaining the current federal funds
  rate target range will require continued evidence that inflation is
  indeed coming down." Then the escalation: "Should evidence of sustained
  inflation progress not materialize, I believe it will be appropriate to
  tighten policy soon to ensure we deliver price stability in a reasonable
  time frame."

  "TIGHTEN POLICY SOON" is the key phrase. Compare to May 13: "I can
  also envision a scenario that requires some policy tightening" (hypothetical).
  Aug 25 upgrades to a pre-commitment with time pressure. The gap between
  "I can envision" (May 13) and "I believe it will be appropriate... soon"
  (Aug 25) is the policy signal. Cut signal: absent. Hike signal: conditional
  and time-pressured.
  Direction: hawkish (escalation vs. May 13)

NET SIGNAL: HAWKISH LEAN — The primary move is in monetary policy: Collins
  pre-commits to tighten "soon" if sustained progress does not materialize.
  Labor has improved (removing a dovish offset), and the AI goods-inflation
  channel is a new hawkish add. "Mildly encouraging" June/July data is the
  only dovish note and is explicitly qualified as volatile and uncertain.

vs. PRIOR SPEECH (May 13, 2026):       HAWKISH — "Tighten policy soon" is
  meaningfully more urgent than May 13's "I can also envision a scenario."
  Labor improved (two-sided risks vs. downside-skewed), removing a softening
  argument. AI inflation channel added. Net escalation is clear.
vs. PRE-FOMC SPEECH (May 13, 2026):    HAWKISH — Same comparison (May 13
  is both the prior speech and the pre-FOMC baseline given no scored speech
  in the intervening period). The arc from neutral-patient (Mar 6) → conditional
  tightening scenario (May 13) → time-pressured pre-commitment (Aug 25) is
  consistently and incrementally hawkish across the post-April cycle.
vs. COMMITTEE SINCE FOMC:              Collins sits inside the hold-but-primed
  camp, directionally aligned with the three July 29 dissenters (Hammack,
  Kashkari, Logan) without joining their immediate-hike preference. As a
  non-voter, her signal is informational but high-quality. The "soon" framing
  means that absent a decisive string of soft prints, Collins would support
  a September hike — a posture that, if replicated among Just Voters, resolves
  into a live hike meeting.

LANGUAGE TO WATCH:
  • "tighten policy soon" — first Collins use of time-pressured tighten.
    Language cycle stage: EMERGING (dissenters in hike camp; Collins now
    in conditional-soon camp from non-voter bench). Watch for spread to voters.
  • "AI build-out appears to be putting upward pressure on core goods
    inflation" — AI demand channel for goods. DISPERSED: Waller Jul 13,
    Collins Aug 25. Not yet Troika-level. If Warsh echoes at Jackson Hole,
    it enters the committee-wide vocabulary.
  • "the recent rise in longer-term interest rates" as a policy co-tightening
    factor — echoes Warsh July 29 bond-market framing. EMERGING from Chair
    downward; Collins' adoption suggests it is propagating.

ANALYST FLAG: YES
  → Collins publishes her pre-Jackson Hole posture: hold for now, but
    "tighten soon" if inflation progress stalls. Her own read of June/July
    data is "mildly encouraging" but not decisive. If August CPI/PCE
    disappoints, Collins' "soon" language would reach the September FOMC
    with full pre-commitment force. Highest-value action: track whether
    Warsh's Aug 28 Jackson Hole keynote (Type G — highest priority)
