THE DAY IN ONE LINE
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
The median voter put a number on underlying inflation for the first time — 2.4

⚠ BASELINE GAP: her last communication was May 19 — 77 days, spanning TWO FOMC
meetings (June 17 and July 29). Every comparison below measures a two-meeting
drift and cannot isolate which meeting moved her.

GROWTH
  Echoes the statement's "solid pace" as "an economy that's pretty strong," and
  discounts the soft Q2 headline in favour of the internals: "the top line
  number wasn't so awesome, but when you look at investment, you look at the
  consumer, it looks really strong." That is an upgrade of the consumer, the one
  component she flagged as moderating on May 19. Subdued housing is used as
  evidence the stance is restrictive rather than as a downside risk; AI capex,
  the investment boom and robust equities are weighed on the other side.
  Direction: hawkish lean

LABOR MARKET
  "A labor market that's stable," wage growth "very consistent with 2%
  inflation," low-hire/low-fire, and — carried forward unchanged from May 19 —
  "you're not seeing pressures on inflation in the labor market." Labor is now an
  input to her restrictiveness assessment rather than a mandate risk.
  ⚠ Three May 19 caveats are absent: the soft-data divergence, the 1948
  historical anomaly, and the AI boardroom layoff flag. She was never asked about
  labor downside in a seven-minute interview and volunteered the topic only as
  evidence for restrictiveness. Do NOT score the absence as a retraction — treat
  as UNTESTED until her next prepared text.
  Direction: neutral

INFLATION
  "You've got inflation that's too high" — verbatim carry-over from May 19. But
  the substance is more benign than that sentence suggests, because she supplies
  the number underneath it for the first time in the corpus:

    "I'm trying to measure the depth of water in a pool and there's rocks being
     thrown into the pool — tariffs, energy shocks. So what's the level of the
     pool? When I look at underlying inflation, when you try to strip out the
     effects of those things, I see it somewhere in the range of 2.4, 2.8."

  Against a 3.7 percent headline, her working estimate is 0.4–0.8pp above target,
  not 1.7. That is the analytical foundation of the median voter's hold, and it
  is a far smaller problem than the dissent bloc describes. She concedes "maybe
  there was a little bit of mild progress over the last several months" and wants
  "more progress."

  ⚠ SHE DEFENDS LOOK-THROUGH, AND DOES IT AGAINST KASHKARI BY NAME. Asked about
  his view that the Fed has a role in addressing supply shocks: "I have definitely
  been of the view that you should look through supply shocks. I think the June
  PCE inflation data kind of validate that … when we had the lull in the conflict
  in the Middle East, oil prices came down, we got inflation relief. If I had
  tried to affect that through monetary policy, I would have been too late."
  Note the form — a TIMING argument, not a level argument. She does not claim the
  shocks are small; she claims policy cannot arrive when they do. Bounded by
  second-round effects: "that's where monetary policy has a real role to play, to
  keep those shocks in their lane."

  New ground-level evidence: CEOs "selling directly to consumers … don't feel
  like they can pass along cost increases because consumers aren't in a position
  to be able to absorb it" — a demand-side ceiling on pass-through, filed as
  evidence for restrictiveness.
  Direction: dovish lean on substance

MONETARY POLICY
  "Mildly restrictive" is unchanged — her label since March 6, now four
  consecutive uses across five months. What moved is the conditional attached to
  it. On May 19 she validated OTHERS' pricing of tightening ("it is healthy that
  market participants have taken on board … scenarios where further tightening
  becomes necessary"). On August 4, asked directly whether no progress means
  higher rates: "Could be higher rates, could be same rates for longer. I'm
  keeping an open mind." First person, her own conditional. Cuts are not
  mentioned anywhere in the interview.

  On the 9–3: "for me, it was not a close call." The median voter says her own
  vote was not near the line.

  On the neutral rate — Liesman puts the dissenters' challenge to her directly
  (investment may have raised neutral, "maybe you're not as restrictive as you
  think you are"): "that's one of the things I am assessing constantly. I think
  that's the key question." She GRANTS it is the key question and does not
  resolve it — she converts it into an empirical test: does the pool level fall
  from here. Her evidence for "mildly restrictive" is subdued housing, benign
  wages, and the pricing-power ceiling; against it, AI capex and equities.

  Forward guidance explicitly withheld: "I can't provide any forward guidance
  about where I'm leaning because I'm weighing the evidence along with everybody
  else."
  Cut signal status:  ABSENT
  Hike signal status: PRESENT — conditional, first-person, no trigger, no
    magnitude
  Direction: hawkish lean

INSTITUTIONAL — THE WARSH REFORM AGENDA
  Non-committal on all three items, which is itself the finding: the median voter
  is not pushing back publicly. She narrows "forward guidance" to a ZLB tool
  rather than defending Fed communication generally; is "open-minded" on six
  versus eight meetings; and on the dot plot values it internally but doubts its
  external use — "do people get too obsessed with the median dot versus the
  entire constellation of dots? There's 19 people around that table." Carry that
  caution into the September SEP (Type I).

NET SIGNAL: HAWKISH LEAN on the policy option set; DOVISH on the data read. Both
  are real and they do not cancel — she is more willing to name a hike while
  believing the inflation problem is smaller than the dissenters do.

  and Cook (Aug 5) are the only two majority voters on record since July 29, and

    an adjective, from the median voter. Nothing else on the committee is this

    arithmetic rather than doctrinal. Stage: DISPERSED (first use).
  • "mildly restrictive" — her stable label, also Collins (May 13) and Cook's
    July 15 Q&A. It is the CNBC headline phrase, so expect amplification. Direct
    antagonist next day: Kashkari, "what evidence do I have that monetary policy
    is particularly restrictive right now?" Stage: EMERGING → COMMITTEE-WIDE.
  • "could be higher rates, could be same rates for longer" — two-sided-hawkish

  • Look-through defended on TIMING ("I would have been too late") — a distinct

    timing form, the majority has an answer to Kashkari's 1970s analogy.
    Stage: DISPERSED (first use).
  • Pricing-power ceiling — the mechanism blocking second-round effects. Barkin's
    contact intelligence is the natural corroborator. Stage: DISPERSED.

PIPELINE NOTE — A STANDING COVERAGE HOLE
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Paulson's interview was missed by every automated layer and by a manual coverage
review the following day. The cause is structural, not a run failure:

  • The regional-bank scrapers crawl speech indexes. A CNBC interview is not
    posted to philadelphiafed.org in any form.
  • The Fed Board calendar lists Board events. It does not list regional
    presidents' media appearances.
  • The YouTube scraper scans official Fed and institutional channels. CNBC
    Television is a media channel and is only reached by a per-speaker search,
    which nothing runs on a schedule.

CLAUDE.md already anticipates this — it directs searching media channels "by
speaker name and date" — but that step is manual and was not performed. Two
communications were lost to it in two days (Paulson Aug 4, Kashkari Aug 5), both
from voting members, one the median voter.

  RECOMMENDED FIX: add a scheduled per-speaker YouTube search across CNBC,
  Bloomberg and Fox Business for all twelve voters plus the five non-voters, run
  daily as part of collect. The search that found both of these took seconds:
    yt-dlp --dump-json --flat-playlist --playlist-end 10 \
      "ytsearch10:[Speaker] Federal Reserve [Month] [Year]"

  Not implemented — flagged for your decision, since it changes what the pipeline
  treats as a source.
