THE RECOVERED DOCUMENT — WILLIAMS TO REUTERS (Troika)
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Conducted July 31 (the morning the three dissent statements landed), published
August 3 by Reuters. No official transcript exists — the NY Fed posted only a
Teller Window summary — so under the Type J rule the outlet's verbatim Q&A is
the primary source. Raw and scored files are now in the repo.

Why it matters: it has been the ONLY Troika communication of the entire
post-July-29 cycle other than the Chair's press conference, and it sat outside
the corpus for eleven days while three daily reports and the August 7 synthesis
were written around a gap where it should have been.

What Williams said:
  • He gives the majority's anti-hike case on the record, plank by plank, two
    days after three presidents said publicly that the Committee should have
    hiked: no overheating ("growth has been solid, but not more than solid";
    "the labor market is, been stable"); inflation driven by identifiable,
    fading supply-side specifics; "monetary policy currently is well positioned
    in where we are today to support that disinflationary path."
  • He DEMOTES AI demand as an inflation driver — "not a big driver of inflation
    right now." On July 15 it was one of three co-equal drivers. This is the
    direct negation of the dissenters' demand-side attribution.
  • He marks the ENERGY level up while keeping the peak call: "relative to some
    of my earlier comments, oil prices are higher, and the future path of oil
    prices is also somewhat higher." The one explicitly acknowledged hawkish
    revision.
  • He adds a conditional he did not have on July 15: "if the economy is not on
    a trajectory that will bring inflation back down to 2%… it would absolutely
    be appropriate to act." Direction unnamed; asked in a hiking context.
  • His own stated test: "core inflation data over the next several months" — a
    multi-month bar, implicitly high for a September move in either direction.
  • On markets pricing September, he declines to validate, and rejects the idea
    that yields substitute for policy: "I never like the phrase that the markets
    are doing the work for us."

Net signal: NEUTRAL, hold-anchored. He is not moving; he is defending.

CRITICAL CAVEAT: every read above pre-dates the –23,000 July payroll print, the
July CPI and the July PPI. Williams has not been heard from since any of them.

SINCE THE JULY 29 FOMC — WHERE THE COMMITTEE STANDS
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Eight scored communications now exist for this cycle. The organising fact is not
majority-vs-dissenters; it is that the NINE ARE NOT UNIFIED, and they disagree
about the mechanism, not just the call.

TROIKA
  Williams (NY Fed) — Jul 31 interview, above. Supply-shock attribution; AI
    demoted as an inflation driver; no overheating channel; "well positioned";
    conditional-to-act added. NEUTRAL.
  Warsh (Chair) — silent since the July 29 press conference. No speech scheduled
    before his Jackson Hole keynote (Fri Aug 28). Outside commentary this week
    (Hoenig, Kaplan, Bloomberg's Trumponomics) converged on the same complaint:
    three months in, the market still cannot read his reaction function.
  Jefferson (Vice Chair) — NO post-July-29 appearance at all. The longest Troika
    silence of the cycle and the single largest coverage gap in the corpus.

JUST VOTERS
  Paulson (Philadelphia — MEDIAN VOTER, Aug 4): hawkish on the option set,
    dovish on the data. Puts underlying inflation ex-tariffs-and-energy at
    "somewhere in the range of 2.4, 2.8" — the only falsifiable number any
    official has offered — while saying in the first person that it "could be
    higher rates." Defends look-through on timing grounds. Vote "not a close call."
  Cook (Board, Aug 5): still the most hawkish majority voter in absolute terms,
    but DOVISH vs. her own July 15 baseline. Names the hike direction explicitly
    for the first time while making its trigger less likely to fire. Declines to
    defend the statement's supply-shock attribution — "but it is not the only
    factor." Her AI capex read crossed to the DISINFLATION side, i.e. the exact
    opposite of the move Williams made.
  Kashkari (Minneapolis, DISSENTER, Aug 5 CNBC): "now is the time to start
    slowly moving" rates up, and three hikes by year-end is "not impossible" —
    the only official arguing for a sequence. But materially softer in conviction
    than his written dissent: he was UNDECIDED going into the meeting and
    concedes most of the inflation IS supply-driven, with demand only "on top of
    it."
  Hammack (Cleveland, DISSENTER) and Logan (Dallas, DISSENTER): nothing since
    their July 31 dissent statements — now 14 days. Both are overdue against
    their own post-April-29 pattern (full Type A within 7–14 days).
  Bowman, Waller, Barr, Jefferson: silent as voters this cycle. Bowman's August 8
    fireside chat has never been published (see notes).

NON VOTERS
  Daly (San Francisco, Aug 6, Tokyo): the cycle's most analytically explicit
    document. Two named scenarios, both "on the middle of the desk," with the
    adverse compounding-shocks scenario now "much closer" to the benign one. Her
    response to scenario two is not a hike but a substantial recalibration —
    "surgical" adjustments explicitly ruled out as insufficient. Names a
    quantified trip wire nobody else has: a 4% inflation attention threshold
    against a 3.9% average and 3.7% latest. HAWKISH LEAN.
  Barkin (Richmond, Aug 13 — the week's only speech): NEUTRAL / HAWKISH LEAN.
    Growth materially stronger than May (H1 nonresidential fixed investment
    +9.5% annualised); the "fog" metaphor inverted — "high uncertainty is the new
    baseline… they can't afford to wait any longer." Labor softer than the
    headline (unemployment 4.1%, but –23K payrolls, resolved via a structural
    supply-demand balance). And, first in his 2026 vocabulary: "Will the Fed need
    to raise rates further, or is inflation already on a path down to target?"
    Offsetting that, his pass-through intelligence REVERSED — "many
    business-to-consumer firms tell us they have little ability to pass on
    costs" — the opposite of his May 21 "renewed determination to pass on cost
    increases."
  Goolsbee, Collins, Schmid, Musalem: nothing scored this cycle. Musalem's
    August 6 AEI remarks remain unrecovered.

SHARED LANGUAGE SINCE THE JULY 29 FOMC
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
1. THE ATTRIBUTION FIGHT — what is actually causing the inflation.
   This is the cycle's central question and it now splits the majority itself.
     • Supply-shock attribution (the statement's own framing): Williams defends
       it outright and demotes AI demand to "not a big driver of inflation right
       now." Paulson defends look-through and cites the June PCE relief as proof.
     • Demand-side attribution: Hammack and Logan (dissent statements), and Cook
       from INSIDE the majority — "but it is not the only factor," with AI capex
       reframed as an inflation impulse on Aug 5.
   Stage: EMERGING, contested, and unusually — being contested from two
   directions at once. Two Troika-adjacent majority voters read the same AI
   buildout through opposite mechanisms one day apart.
   RESOLUTION POINT: the July 28–29 minutes, Wednesday August 19, 2:00pm ET.

2. "A series of successive supply shocks" (Kashkari) / "compounding effect…
   greater than the sum of its parts" (Daly). The look-through exit ramp. Daly's
   version is the adoptable one because she states it as an open question rather
   than a verdict. Stage: EMERGING.

3. The hike question moving OUTWARD from the dissent bloc. Waller (Jul 13,
   conditional), Cook (Jul 15/Aug 5, named), Paulson (Aug 4, first person), and
   now Barkin (Aug 13, named as a live question by a non-dissenting Non Voter).
   Williams's conditional-to-act is the weakest version of the same move.
   Stage: EMERGING → approaching committee-wide as a QUESTION, though not as a
   position. Nobody outside the three dissenters has advocated a hike.

4. "Well positioned" — the stable holding formula. Unchanged in Williams's
   interview. Flag only if it disappears.
