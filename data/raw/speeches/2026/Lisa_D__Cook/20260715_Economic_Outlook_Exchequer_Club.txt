SPEAKER: Governor Lisa D. Cook
EVENT: Exchequer Club, Washington, D.C.
DATE: July 15, 2026 (Wednesday)
CLASSIFICATION: Type A — Economic Outlook speech

--- COMPILED CONTENT ---

GROWTH:
- U.S. GDP grew approximately 2 percent in 2025
- FOMC participants now forecast that 2026 GDP will come in at 2.2 percent
- Labor productivity has averaged a robust 2.5 percent annual growth over the past two years
- AI investment boom continuing to drive business investment

LABOR MARKET:
- "Nearly all indicators point to stability" in the labor market
- "I see few reasons that today's labor market has more risk than a year earlier"
- Unemployment rate: 4.2 percent (stable; edged down from 4.3% in April)
- Employment risks have diminished; balance of risks has shifted toward the inflation mandate
- "I see a notable shift in the balance of risks relative to a year or so ago, with inflation risks now outweighing employment risks"

INFLATION:
- "The initial assessment is easy: Inflation is simply too high"
- PCE price index rose approximately 3.7 percent over the 12 months ending in June, which is 1.7 percentage points above the 2 percent target
- Three upside risk drivers: (1) AI investment boom demand-side pressure; (2) tariffs; (3) Middle East conflict energy/supply
- "I am fully committed to reaching our inflation target, and this commitment is unwavering"
- After five-plus years of above-target inflation, entrenchment risk is a named concern

MONETARY POLICY:
- Fed's current 3.50%-3.75% policy rate characterized as "mildly restrictive"
- "The FOMC can take its time, I can take my time, to observe more data to understand whether it's really restrictive or not"
- "If we do not see signs of disinflation soon, I am prepared to act"
- Explicit conditional hike signal — action conditioned on absence of disinflation "soon"
- No cut language or dovish signal anywhere in the speech
- Fed Governor Cook signals support for a rate rise if inflation does not cool
