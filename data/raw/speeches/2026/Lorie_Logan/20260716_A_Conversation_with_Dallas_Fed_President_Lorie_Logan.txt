EVENT: "A Conversation with Dallas Fed President Lorie Logan"
       Listening in 360° tour — Houston Branch, Federal Reserve Bank of Dallas
       1801 Allen Parkway, Houston, TX
       11:30 a.m.–1:00 p.m. CDT, Thursday July 16, 2026
       Format: Prepared remarks followed by moderated community conversation.

SPEAKER: Lorie K. Logan, President and CEO, Federal Reserve Bank of Dallas
CLASSIFICATION: Just Voter (2026 rotating member)
NOTES: Logan is the first FOMC policymaker under Chair Warsh to publicly
       advocate for an interest-rate increase ahead of the July 28-29 FOMC meeting.

--- PREPARED REMARKS (key quotes verified via wire coverage) ---

INFLATION:
"Inflation has been too high, for too long, and does not appear to be on track all
the way back to 2%."

"Every month of above-target inflation has compounded the strain on Americans'
budgets."

[On June CPI/PPI data:] "One month of relief is not enough. It is time to finish
the job of restoring price stability."

Inflation measures cited:
  - Core PCE: 3.4%, risen since December 2025
  - NY Fed multivariate core trend model (persistent component): 3.4%
  - Market-based non-housing core services inflation: no progress on a 12-month
    basis since mid-2024
  - Logan's path forecast: inflation heading toward "mid 2's" — not all the way
    back to 2 percent

LABOR MARKET / GROWTH:
  - Unemployment rate averaged 4.3% in the first half of 2026
  - Average job gains: approximately 92,000 per month
  - "The labor, consumption and financial data indicate that monetary policy is
    not restraining the economy."

MONETARY POLICY:
"If inflation is not heading all the way to 2 percent on its own, then at least
some policy restriction is needed to help get it there."

"If higher inflation becomes entrenched, we'd need sharper rate increases to bring
it back to target, with a larger cost for the labor market. Better modest
restriction now than severe restriction later."

"I currently believe modestly higher interest rates would better balance the
outlook and risks for the FOMC's dual mandate goals."

NOTE: Logan stopped short of committing to a hike at the July 28-29 meeting
specifically. No preferred magnitude was indicated.
