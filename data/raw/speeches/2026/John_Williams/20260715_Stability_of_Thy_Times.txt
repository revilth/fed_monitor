SPEAKER: John C. Williams, NY Fed President and CEO
TITLE: "Stability of Thy Times"
EVENT: Partnership for New York City, New York City, New York
DATE: July 15, 2026 (Wednesday)
CLASSIFICATION: Type A — Economic Outlook speech

--- COMPILED CONTENT ---

GROWTH:
- "Growth in the economy is solid and on trend"
- Real GDP growth projected at "around 2 percent to 2.25 percent this year and over the next two years"
- Growth running "modestly above its potential rate of 2 percent"
- AI-related investment continues to be the primary engine of business investment
- Unemployment rate expected to edge "down gradually to 4 percent in 2028"

LABOR MARKET:
- "The labor market is likewise solid and stable"
- Labor market is NOT a source of inflationary pressure
- Inflation expectations "well-anchored" — giving the Fed "policy breathing room"
- "we are not seeing evidence that the labor market is adding to inflationary pressures"

INFLATION:
- "Inflation is unquestionably too high" at approximately 3.7-4 percent
- "There are encouraging reasons to expect that inflation has peaked and should edge down in coming quarters"
- Five reasons cited for expected disinflation (specific list from Teller Window key-takeaways; details to be confirmed from full text)
- Three inflation drivers: (1) tariffs on imported goods; (2) Middle East conflict supply/energy disruptions; (3) robust AI investment demand for technology goods and electricity
- Inflation expected to decline to approximately 3.25 percent by year-end 2026 (updated from June 25's 3.5% forecast)
- Then continue on a glide path toward 2 percent goal in 2027, "land on target" in 2028
- Medium-term inflation expectations remain well-anchored

MONETARY POLICY:
- "The current stance of monetary policy is well positioned to do that" [restore 2% inflation]
- "well positioned" — stable holding formula; no cut signal, no hike signal
- No forward guidance; pure data-dependence
- "my views, as always, will be based on the evolution of the totality of the data, the economic outlook, and the balance of risks"
- Williams supports holding rates steady; does not echo Waller's/Cook's near-term hike conditionality
