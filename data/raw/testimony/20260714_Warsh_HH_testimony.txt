July 14, 2026
Testimony on the Semiannual Monetary Policy Report to the Congress
Chair Kevin Warsh
Committee on Financial Services, U.S. House of Representatives, Washington, D.C.

[This is Warsh's first Humphrey-Hawkins testimony as Chair. Senate testimony (Day 2)
follows July 15, 2026. The hearing coincided with the 8:30am ET release of June CPI:
headline 3.5% YoY (down from 4.2%), core flat MoM / 2.6% YoY — well below the
consensus of 3.8% headline / +0.2% MoM core.]

════════════════════════════════════════════
PREPARED REMARKS — EXTRACTS FROM MEDIA COVERAGE
════════════════════════════════════════════

Inflation commitment:
"The members of our Committee have no tolerance for persistently elevated inflation.
And we share a resolute commitment to restoring price stability."

"If we get policy right — and we will — the inflation surge of the last five years
will be a thing of the past."

"While monthly price fluctuations are inevitable – especially in an unsettled world –
underlying inflation over longer time horizons is determined largely by monetary policy."

AI / growth:
"[AI investment is] the most striking feature of the economy right now."
(Warsh said the Fed is "monitoring the implications" for inflation and jobs.)

Labor:
Assessed labor market as "broadly stable."

Economy:
Economy "expanding and has proven to be resilient to a variety of headwinds."

Policy framework changes:
Task forces (announced June 17, 2026): communications; balance sheet policy; economic
data; productivity and jobs; inflation frameworks.
"Regime change" language: Warsh described the Fed's approach as a "regime change"
to rid inflation as a "tax" on Americans.

════════════════════════════════════════════
KEY Q&A EXCHANGES — from media coverage
════════════════════════════════════════════

On June CPI (released same morning, headline 3.5% / core flat MoM):
"There might be some that look at this morning's data and say, 'mission accomplished.'
That is not my view."
[Additional context: Warsh noted the figures represented just one month of data and
said he does not see inflation as defeated.]

On July FOMC rate path:
No signal given. Declined to indicate whether rate increases would be necessary.
Consistent with stated no-forward-guidance policy ("not well suited to the current
policy conjuncture," June 17).

On Fed independence:
"We're an independent central bank."
"My commitment to you is to follow the law and follow the data. Follow our very best
judgment."
[Context: Rep. Nydia Velázquez asked whether he "works for Trump."]
