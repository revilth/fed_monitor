## Troika

**Warsh** — Chair as of May 2026; first FOMC press conference June 17, 2026. This is now the primary Troika calibration entry. Key signals from June 17:

INFLATION: Hard 2.0% target — "zero is to the right of the decimal point." Explicitly rules out target reconsideration before 2% is hit. The "five years above 2%" framing is in the opening statement (committee-approved). Not a symmetric AIT framework.

LABOR: Deliberately reframed labor as adequate ("kept pace with the workforce"), rejecting Powell's "labor demand has clearly softened" framing. In Q&A, explicitly rejected Phillips-curve tradeoff: "I don't believe we have a cruel choice... we can make strong growth, low prices and strong employment mutually compatible." Will NOT concede a labor sacrifice in pursuing price stability.

FORWARD GUIDANCE: Removed entirely ("not well suited to the current policy conjuncture"). Academic critique of forward guidance is now operational policy. "At least as currently structured" re: SEP signals reform in progress — Warsh withheld his own dot. Likely reducing press conference frequency from 8/year (one per meeting) to fewer — "when you have one, you want to make sure you have something important to say."

HIKE RISK: Explicitly quantified — "half of the members of the FOMC expect rate hikes as soon as this year." The opening statement is neutral; the Q&A amplifies the hawkish tail. Warsh is anchoring a unified 12–0 hold consensus while leaving the right tail visible.

INSTITUTIONAL REFORM: Five task forces launched (communications, balance sheet, data, productivity/jobs, causes of inflation). The data task force signals Warsh views current official statistics as methodologically inadequate — raises the bar for reacting to individual prints. Most to conclude by fall/year-end.

TRACKED LANGUAGE: "The recent past need not be prologue" (opening statement; committee-level). "The Committee will deliver price stability" (statement; committee-level). "Zero is to the right" (Q&A; personal). "We can make strong growth, low prices and strong employment mutually compatible" (Q&A; personal).

## Just Voters

**Waller** — Gave the most hawkish Just Voter speech of 2026 on Apr 17. Key new framework: "sequence of transitory shocks" — explicitly warns against reflexive "look through" when shocks stack. First voter to say he would hold even if the labor market weakens, if inflation risks dominate. Critical arc: dissented at Jan 28 preferring a cut; Feb 23 speech ("Labor Market Data: Signal or Noise?") frames it as a "coin flip" on labor with look-through tariffs intact and a conditional cut signal. By Apr 17 — after the February jobs disappointment and late-February energy shock — reversed to the committee's most hawkish stance. The Feb 23 scored speech is the essential pre-FOMC baseline for understanding this reversal. May 22 ("Policy Risks Have Changed"): first voter to advocate removing the easing bias (realized June 17) and first to say hikes "can no longer be ruled out." **July 13 ("Monetary Policy at a Crossroads"): escalated to "the FOMC will need to consider tightening monetary policy in the near term" if this week's core CPI/PPI print hot — the most explicit near-term hike conditionality of any 2026 voter.** Decision rule on record: several months of lower core readings → hold; hot reading → near-term tightening consideration. Expectations concern retired (TIPS 2y/5y at 2.1%/2.3%, "anchored") but explicitly rebutted the dovish inference — anchoring calibrates hike speed/persistence, not whether. New symmetric guardrails: "avoid overtightening... and risking a recession," "fighting the last war." Cut scenario absent since May 22. New tracked language: "at a crossroads"; AI-demand inflation channel (chips/servers/memory → retail goods). **September 3 ("The Economic Outlook and Some Comments on My Policy Communication," Reuters NEXT; first communication since Jul 13; scored at data/scored/speeches/20260903_Christopher_J__Waller_scored.txt): the July 13 trigger did not fire and the base case is now HOLD — "recent data suggest we are finally seeing some signs of disinflation. If this continues in the data due over the next two weeks, I would be inclined to support holding ... If the incoming data for August show this improvement has been fleeting, then it may be appropriate to raise the policy rate when the FOMC meets on September 15 and 16." Measure is MOMENTUM: three-month core 4.76% (Feb) → 3.05% (Jul), "the speed of this downward trajectory is encouraging" — the momentum answer to Warsh's "at sufficient speed," opposite verdict; July 13's breadth statistic dropped. New: nonmarket (imputed) services were ~half of the core increase — "ignoring this one factor ... underlying inflation is doing better than the core numbers suggest"; pending Commerce revision could cut 12m PCE "a few tenths." Stance: "policy is currently only slightly restricting aggregate demand, and it may not take much acceleration in inflation to nudge me into supporting tighter policy" — first stance descriptor since May 22's "restrictive setting"; sits with the "mildly restrictive" hold majority, not with Warsh/dissenters, but with a hair trigger and "a small adjustment" (one move). Labor "satisfactory," "not a large factor in my determination"; lower net immigration as the breakeven mechanism. Tariffs "largely passed through"; energy bleed "hasn't come to pass." Communication: "I agree with Chairman Warsh that forward guidance isn't appropriate now ... But when it is truly needed, I believe it should be used"; defends reaction functions against the Chair — "The strike zone is the umpire's reaction function," inverting "play the ball, not the referee." Q&A not published. Calibration takeaway: his September vote is a stated function of the August inflation print; "hot" is not numbered.

**Paulson** (median voter) — Has set an explicit inflation-first bar: "If inflation is above 2 percent and has been for some time, I would be more cautious." Needs 2% inflation before considering patience on growth signals — more restrictive than Jefferson's symmetric dual-risk framing.

**Logan** (Dallas) — Most hawkish framing of any voter; highest analytical credibility of any regional president (former NY Fed markets desk head). "Policy may be very close to neutral" (Feb 10). Dissented on Apr 29 against easing bias language, alongside Kashkari and Hammack. Hawkish anchor of the 2026 voter coalition. **Jul 29: dissented in favor of a 25bp HIKE** — exactly as telegraphed in her Jul 16 Houston remarks (hike as CURRENT BASE CASE, "modestly higher interest rates would better balance the dual mandate," "one month of relief is not enough; it is time to finish the job"). Calibration takeaway: Logan's stated base case is votable — she does not hold a position in text and then vote the consensus. Treat her stated stance as a direct read on her vote. **Jul 31 dissent statement** (scored: data/scored/speeches/20260731_Lorie_Logan_scored.txt): CONFIRMATION, not escalation — her substantive position is unchanged from Jul 16 and most language is verbatim carry-over ("mid-2's, not all the way to 2 percent" now in its third consecutive document; "not restraining the economy"; "perhaps strengthening a bit"). Do not double-count it as fresh hawkish information. Two genuinely new elements: (1) "The FOMC cannot count on unanticipated shocks to achieve its goals and can always adjust policy if unanticipated shocks occur" — the sharpest one-line attack on the hold case, with reversibility pre-built against the overtightening objection; (2) she invokes the **statement on longer-run goals and monetary policy strategy** and "Congressionally established" mandates — grounding the dissent in the Committee's own framework rather than her forecast. That is an institutional escalation and the thing to watch. Also notable: her Jul 16 committee-humility close ("I may persuade them, they may persuade me") is GONE. Asks for one move, not a series.

**Hammack** (Cleveland) — Dissented Apr 29 against easing bias language; voted with the majority June 17 once that language was removed; **dissented July 29 in favor of a 25bp HIKE.** Gave no post-June economic outlook speech — the July 16 read carried her as "presumed sympathetic but silent," and the July 29 vote converts that inference to fact. **Jul 31 dissent statement** (scored: data/scored/speeches/20260731_Beth_Hammack_scored.txt) resolves the text gap. Escalation from Jun 2 is in the hedges: "monetary policy MAY NOT BE sufficiently restrictive" → "I DID NOT SEE the current policy stance as appropriately restrictive"; "it MAY SOON BE APPROPRIATE for policy to act" → "NOW IS THE TIME for the FOMC to act." Key analytical move: explicit demand-side attribution ("inflationary pressures coming from the demand side of the economy, as well") — the exit from the statement's supply-shock framing, which is what makes hiking coherent. Distinctive contribution is district evidence: pricing pressures "broadening rather than fading," consumers "expressing despair" (first affective escalation). **Highest-value calibration fact:** her May 1 dissent statement carried "upside risks to inflation AND DOWNSIDE RISKS TO GROWTH AND EMPLOYMENT"; the Jul 31 statement has NO two-sided risk language at all — same author, genre and length, so the one-sided risk balance is a real change, not compression. Names no rate path beyond July. Closing line on "a wide range of viewpoints" is her standard dissent boilerplate (near-verbatim from May 1) — no signal.

**Kashkari** (Minneapolis) — Dissented Apr 29 against easing bias language; voted with the majority June 17 once that language was removed; **dissented July 29 in favor of a 25bp HIKE.** Like Hammack, gave no post-June outlook speech — the "presumed sympathetic but silent" read is now confirmed by the vote. **Jul 31 dissent statement** (scored: data/scored/speeches/20260731_Neel_Kashkari_scored.txt) resolves the gap and is the largest single-document move of the three dissenters, because his May 13 prior was the least committed ("We just don't know right now"). Substance is doctrinal, not data-driven — no unemployment rate, no inflation print, **no labor-market characterization at all**. Core: an explicit revision of look-through — "I largely subscribe to that view, BUT I increasingly believe that monetary policy does have an important role to play in addressing a series of successive supply shocks that might lead to entrenched higher inflation" — plus a new **1970s analogy** charging that policymakers then held a supply-shock diagnosis too long. Note "increasingly believe" is a softer register than Hammack's or Logan's flat verdicts. He is the ONLY dissenter asking for a **series** of small hikes rather than one move, with a symmetric exit if inflation fades.
